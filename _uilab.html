CurrentThirteen bordered plates, ragged widths, 7–11px tracked Consolas, saturated cyan with an 8px glow. The ship and the spectacle get whatever is left in the middle.

ProposedFour anchors, no plates — soft vignettes and hard text-shadow carry legibility. Speed is 64px and readable at a glance. A swarm of seventeen becomes one number, three names and a count.

CorrectionThe Massline ring is drawn at screen centre here to show how loud the tether should be while latched. Its placement is already settled elsewhere — design/HUD_FLIGHT_ATTENTION.md (active, authorized 2026-08-13) gives the latched Massline bottom-centre. That ruling wins; this frame is illustrating volume, not position.

CurrentEvery node says what it costs and whether it is locked. None say what you would be able to do. The 🔒 is drawn straight onto the canvas, and the whole chart paints in the browser fallback font — the code asks for var(--mono), which Canvas 2D cannot read.

ProposedEight capabilities phrased as verbs, each stating what you can do now and what the next rung buys — in tonnes and metres, never percentages. The prereq chart still exists underneath as the rule engine; it just stops being the picture. Progression is shown on the ship it modifies.

Accent

The one open art-direction call. Everything else above is fixed by the plan.

Type does the work

Saira SemiCondensed for anything read at speed, IBM Plex Sans for words, IBM Plex Mono for numerals only. All three already ship in styles/fonts/. Nothing below 11px.

No plates in flight

Borders and drop-shadows over a 3D scene are what read as a web page. Legibility comes from a soft vignette behind each anchor plus a hard text-shadow.

Colour is a signal, not a coat

One hot accent, spent on danger and on what you can do right now. Everything at rest is steel. The world supplies the colour.

Consequence, not cost

"You can tow a 40-tonne hauler" beats "+18% tractor strength". Numbers stay available for players who want them — they just stop being the headline.
