## 1. Obvious defects from the Works view

- The silhouette reads as a rectangular gray platform trapped between two paperclip-shaped loops, not a purpose-built industrial rover.
- Both tracks read as hollow wire loops. The dark belt bodies and tread mass are too weak to establish two solid stadium-shaped rubber tracks. The bright outlines dominate and resemble tubes rather than thin raised steel outer lips catching light.
- The central body reads as a flat gray card or plate. There is insufficient tub depth, sidewall thickness, or layered structural mass.
- The cab house does not resolve as a clear dark volume sitting on a mid-value steel tub.
- The windshield band is not visibly established. The black and brown fragments near the upper-right center resemble stickers or scattered blocks rather than one coherent band of glass.
- The yellow feature reads as a roof-mounted postage stamp or vertical plate. It does not read as a thin visor line extending beyond an already-visible windshield.
- The hopper area at left reads as a dark trapezoidal pane laid on the gray deck. It does not convincingly read as a deep open well with a rim, walls, and visible darkness below the surrounding skin.
- The hopper and windshield/cab regions are not sufficiently distinct. Both resolve as dark graphics placed on the same flat body.
- The boom is a plain gray rectangular bar. Its offset mounting and mechanical articulation are poorly communicated.
- The required dark arm and raised steel spine are not separately readable.
- The boom-to-tool connection lacks a convincing joint, collar, bearing, or reinforced load path.
- The tool body does not read as a manufactured steel bit housing. The yellow end reads as a tiny hook or arrowhead rather than a bright working tip emerging from a steel bit body.
- The tiny black shapes around the cab look like stickers or loose block greebles. They do not form convincing controls, hinges, fasteners, hydraulic hardware, or structural interfaces.
- Materials collapse into muddy gray, brown, black, and yellow blocks. Steel, rubber, glass, painted metal, and the working tip lack sufficiently different value and specular responses.
- The entire rover retains an obvious toy/clay/card/box construction: paperclip tracks, a gray plate chassis, a pasted dark hopper shape, a yellow cab plate, and a rectangular boom.

## 2. Visible MTX defects at play size

- **MTX-01 — Mid-poly edges and weighted normals:** Major body edges do not carry controlled, narrow manufactured highlights. The chassis reads flat while the track outlines read excessively tubular.
- **MTX-02 — Authored cross-section loft:** The central structure and boom are dominated by simple rectangular sections without meaningful taper, shoulders, or changing cross-sections.
- **MTX-03 — Skin-breaking boolean with wall thickness:** The hopper does not visibly prove a genuine opening. It reads as a dark plate or painted polygon.
- **MTX-04 — Readable volume in the opening:** The hopper has no clear depth hierarchy, inner walls, or darker floor separated from its rim.
- **MTX-05 — Panelization with gaps:** The body appears assembled from flat cards and boxes rather than fitted panels with thickness, overlaps, and seam channels.
- **MTX-07 — Thin glass over a cut tub:** The cab lacks a coherent framed dark windshield band and darker cavity. The yellow plate currently competes with or replaces the glass read.
- **MTX-11 — Cables and hoses:** No mechanically useful service routing or terminated hydraulic lines are readable around the boom.
- **MTX-12 — Fasteners at interfaces:** The small cab-side details look scattered rather than deliberately placed at functional joins.
- **MTX-31 — Substance classification:** Rubber, steel, paint, glass, and dirt are not clearly separated.
- **MTX-33 — Authored ORM:** Roughness and metallic variation do not survive at this size; most surfaces share a dull, muddy response.
- **MTX-35 — Machined metal:** The boom spine, joints, and tool housing do not read as machined or load-bearing metal.
- **MTX-36 — Thin glass:** No unmistakable glass response is visible on the windshield band.
- **MTX-39 — Cavity dirt:** Darkness does not consistently describe real cavities; the hopper darkness resembles a flat color patch.
- **MTX-40 — Curvature-driven wear:** Edge treatment does not reinforce believable contact and wear zones.
- **MTX-45 — Recessed emission/bright fixtures:** The bright tool tip lacks a substantial non-emissive fixture or bit body behind it.
- **MTX-46 — Forbidden reads:** The supported view still reads as plastic toy, clay blockout, stacked boxes, gray card, and paperclip loops.
- **MTX-55 — Role silhouette at 40 px and 120 px:** “Tracked rover” is weakly present, but “Asteroid Works industrial excavation rover” is not. Identity relies on the track-loop shorthand and tiny yellow mark.
- **MTX-56 — Value hierarchy:** The dark wells, mid steel body, glass band, and controlled yellow accent do not form a clean hierarchy.
- **MTX-57 — Visible shell thickness:** The hopper rim and central tub do not show convincing wall thickness.
- **MTX-59 — Reads at play distance:** Tracks, cab, hopper, and tool do not resolve as four clearly designed functional systems.
- **MTX-61 — Joint language:** The boom and body interfaces lack readable welds, gaskets, collars, bearings, or countersunk connections.
- **MTX-63 — Two-scale surfaces:** There is neither strong meso-scale construction nor useful surviving micro-detail; the result is broad flat plates plus visual specks.
- **MTX-65 — Role-specific massing:** The massing does not yet communicate the heavy structure, protected operator volume, material-handling well, and reinforced excavation mechanism expected of the role.
- **MTX-66 — Specular by manufacture:** Rubber, glass, paint, and bare steel do not exhibit distinct highlights.
- **MTX-67 — Designed accent:** Yellow appears as a plate and a hook-like tip rather than one controlled visor line plus a purposeful working-tip accent.

## 3. Concrete Blender actions

1. Replace each track outline with a solid stadium-shaped belt mesh. Give the rubber belt visible width and thickness, add restrained tread segmentation, then place a much thinner steel outer lip slightly proud of the belt so only that lip catches the rim light.
2. Rebuild the chassis as a recessed steel tub with real sidewalls and beveled load-bearing rails. Remove the large flat gray-card read.
3. Establish a distinct dark cab house on the tub. Cut a continuous windshield opening, add a thin framed dark-glass shell, and place a narrow yellow visor line only beyond the visible glass edge.
4. Remove the current yellow roof/postage-stamp shape.
5. Boolean the hopper through the upper skin. Add a clearly visible rim, four inset walls, and a deeper floor so it remains an unmistakable hole at this camera scale.
6. Separate the hopper material and depth treatment from the windshield so they cannot collapse into similar dark pasted shapes.
7. Rebuild the boom with an offset pivot housing, a dark tapered arm, and a narrower raised steel spine running along it. Use bevelled manufactured sections rather than one rectangular bar.
8. Add visible pivot collars, hinge gaps, load plates, and terminated hydraulic hoses at the boom interfaces.
9. Build the tool end as a substantial steel bit housing with a collar and tapered working body. Restrict the bright yellow value to the actual exposed tip; remove the hook/arrowhead silhouette.
10. Replace scattered cab-side blocks with a small number of functional interface details—hinges, access-panel fasteners, a sensor housing, or hydraulic fittings—positioned where components genuinely meet.
11. Apply scale, use narrow two-segment bevels, shade by angle, and restore weighted/custom normals. Keep the large steel faces flat while allowing thin edge highlights.
12. Author visibly distinct materials: near-black matte rubber, mid-value painted steel tub, darker cab body, low-roughness dark glass, brighter brushed steel on the boom spine and bit body, and a limited yellow safety accent.
13. Re-light the Works view so the belt bodies remain dark but visible, the steel track lips catch only a thin highlight, the windshield band separates from the cab, and the hopper floor remains darker than its walls.

## 4. Verdict

**REVISE**

The framing is valid, but the rover remains below Hitch-plus. At play size it is still a gray box/card with sticker-like dark regions, paperclip tracks, a yellow roof plate, and a rectangular boom ending in a yellow hook-like mark.
