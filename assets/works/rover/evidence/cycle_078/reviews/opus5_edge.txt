1. DEFECTS VISIBLE IN THIS WORKS-EDGE VIEW

Side walls (the aft flank) -- the only vertical face this camera reveals is ~7 px wide and it is three
flat vertical bands: saturated yellow, a blank near-white rectangle, saturated yellow. Nothing else.
No fender, no step, no grab rail, no bolt course, no hose, no plate seam, no overlap. The strong-yellow
columns run x22-34 with up to 44 saturated pixels per column -- a large saturated yellow field on a
vertical face, which is exactly the "yellow cab sides" read the brief forbids. The flank carries zero
manufacture.

Track height / solid belts vs wire loops -- the belts are solid; there is no hollow, no daylight, no
wire read. That is not the problem. The problems are:
  * The top run is a smooth uniform plane. A belt seen from above must show discrete cross-cleats
    marching down its length. Here the cleats exist only as a 2 px dashed line on the inboard edge;
    the belt face itself is unbroken. Nothing breaks the top plane.
  * The silhouette is a perfect stadium -- fully rounded both ends, no drive sprocket bulge, no idler,
    no return rollers, no track frame. Two identical mirrored loaves.
  * The material is wrong. Track top measures RGB (42,30,13), B/R = 0.30, an ochre-olive. The bill
    says #3a3530 neutral dark rubber (B/R = 0.83). These read as clay loaves, not rubber composite.
  * The aft end of each lozenge has a bright pale wedge crossed by a dark diagonal streak -- a shading
    artifact, not a component.
  * Track HEIGHT itself I will not grade: this camera shows about 3 px of side face and cannot carry
    that verdict. The fix belongs in the actions list.

Dark cab house, yellow only as a thin visor past glass -- half right, and the wrong half is the loud
one.
  * The house IS dark relative to the deck (roof sum ~78 vs deck 244) and it is not a yellow slab.
  * But the house is a plain rectangular box with a flat unbroken lid. No hatch, no rib, no step, no
    bevel, no roof rack, no antenna. Nothing on it reads as folded plate.
  * The yellow is thin (3 px) and it does sit outboard of the glass -- the geometry of the
    relationship is right. What is wrong is VALUE: the bar peaks at RGB (186,150,58), sum 513, against
    a next-brightest of 364 anywhere on the vehicle. The bill says yellow stays a paint, not a light.
    This bar is the brightest object in the frame by a wide margin, with no thickness cue, no shadow
    under it, no return at either end. It reads as an emissive stripe, not a steel brow.
  * On the roof: a 4x4 px pale disc at value 245 on a surface at value 78. Whether that is meant as
    the lamp or a vent, it is a bright disk sitting on a plane with no hood, slit, or can.

A dark pane that is actually visible -- YES. Columns x106-111, rows y57-74: a 6 x 17 px block at RGB
~(6,3,5), effectively black. This requirement is met and it survives at play size. Note it is a perfect
rectangle with square corners, no frame, no gasket, no reveal, so it reads as a dark decal rather than
laminated glass in a welded frame -- but it IS visible and it IS dark.

Hopper well -- an open well is present and the interior is dark. The failures:
  * Deck 244 -> wall 110 happens across ONE pixel. There is no rim thickness at all. The deck plate
    has no visible turn-down at the mouth, so the opening reads as a hole cut in paper.
  * The interior is dead flat: 18 px of unvarying value ~54. No liner break, no fill, no rubble, no
    burnished mouth, no scale.
  * Ribbing exists on one wall only; the far and near walls are blank.
  * The dark floor is a 6 px wedge along one edge -- a shadow sliver, not a floor. The well never
    establishes depth.

Darker boom with a raised steel spine into a steel bit body -- INVERTED ON EVERY COUNT.
  * The boom slab is a constant-width rectangle from root to tip. No taper, no truss, no cylinder
    section, no hydraulic ram, no pivot at the root. It just abuts the cab side.
  * The "spine" is a 2 px line at sum 283-309 running along the MIDDLE of a slab at sum 62-75. A 4x
    brightness jump, dead straight, no taper, no end return, only a weak value drop on one side. It
    reads as a white stripe PAINTED on the boom, not a raised steel rib. The brief asks for a DARKER
    boom with a raised spine; the boom is dark and the spine is white, which is the opposite
    relationship.
  * The "bit" is a 5x9 px irregular white blob at peak sum 364 -- the same white as the spine, so it
    does not separate as a distinct forged body. No disc, no cone, no carbide teeth, no heat tint
    anywhere (the bill calls for #848b93 running to #9a6f4a / #ff6242). It reads as the arrowhead
    terminating the stripe.
  * The tip overhangs the pad edge with pure black behind it, which flattens it further.

Scar plate -- THIS IS THE LEAD DEFECT, because the material bill names this exact still as its proving
view ("Flank, readable at works_edge"). What is on the flank is a blank near-white rectangle, ~7 x 42
px, value sum 228 -- the second-brightest large surface on the whole vehicle. No soot. No gas-breach
chip. No bolts, no bevel, no edge wear, square corners, zero texture. Either that rectangle IS the scar
plate, in which case it is value-inverted from "sooted steel" and carries no damage story at all; or
the scar plate is ABSENT from the flank and this is an unrelated blank plate occupying its place. Both
fail the same row, and both fail it in the one view the contract nominated.

Toy / clay / card / box / gray plate -- FOUR OF THEM:
  1. THE DECK IS A CARD. It is the largest surface on the vehicle and it is a flat, featureless,
     near-white plate: RGB (99,81,65), sum 244. It is BRIGHTER than the ground it sits on (169) and
     LESS saturated (B/R 0.63 vs the pad 0.53). The bill puts safety-yellow #ffd23f livery here. This
     is not an exposure artifact -- the cab roof faces the same way, sits higher, and would catch more
     of the weak overhead rim, yet it comes back darker (78) and far more saturated (B/R 0.10).
     Different material, not different light. The deck has gone bare gray-white plate, and it carries
     no plate steps, no seam channels, no bolt courses, no chevrons, no wear.
  2. THE FLANK PANEL IS A WHITE STICKER (above).
  3. THE CAB IS A BOX -- rectangular prism, flat lid, painted stripes on the front face.
  4. THE TRACKS ARE CLAY LOAVES -- smooth ochre stadiums with no mechanism.

Two further absences from this view: NO CHEVRON PLATES anywhere (the bill wants separate #161008 bars
reading as dark bars from above; there are none), and NO CONTACT SHADOW -- the pad immediately above
the track reads 163 against a far-corner 172, about 5% darker. The rover looks decaled onto the pad
rather than standing on it. That last one is a scene/key observation, not a mesh defect.

2. MTX ROWS FAILED AT THIS PLAY SIZE

  * MTX-46 forbidden reads stay forbidden -- four separate blank white plates (deck, flank panel, boom
    stripe, bit blob) plus a bright disc on a lid gives a plastic-toy read. This row fails the leaf on
    its own, regardless of everything else.
  * MTX-56 value hierarchy -- the hierarchy exists but is assigned BACKWARDS. The tracks, which the
    bill calls the darkest mass, sit at 84 -- lighter than the boom (62), the well (54), the well floor
    (28) and the glass (19). The deck, which should be mid paint, is the lightest surface in the shot.
    The "accents" are not saturated colour beats; they are four unrelated whites.
  * MTX-57 visible shell thickness -- the hopper mouth turns from deck to wall in one pixel.
  * MTX-45 recessed emission -- a 4x4 px disc at value 245 sitting flat on a lid. The row named
    forbidden fake is "a bright disk on a surface".
  * MTX-05 panelization with gaps -- the deck, the flank, the cab and the boom are single unbroken
    faces. No stepped plates, no seam channels anywhere in this view.
  * MTX-42 / MTX-43 stencil as spray / decal -- the boom spine and the visor bar are functioning as
    decals doing a structural job. Markings may have no thickness; ribs and brows must.
  * MTX-12 fasteners at interfaces -- the only bolt heads in frame are on two small bracket clusters.
    Deck-to-flank, cab-to-deck, boom-to-cab, track-frame-to-hull: all bare butt joints.
  * MTX-31 / MTX-38 classify the substance -- the track belt is ochre paint-value, not rubber. Nothing
    in frame separates paint from steel from rubber by value or specular.
  * MTX-61 joint language -- no weld bead, gasket, or countersink at any plate meeting.
  * MTX-14 / MTX-60 broken symmetry from service -- the two tracks are exact mirrors; the deck is
    symmetric; the only asymmetry is the boom, which is design, not service. No authored repair,
    stencil, or replaced plate.
  * MTX-55 (principle, at the works analogue of 120 px/cell) -- of the five planform shapes the asset
    owes, this view resolves two cleanly (stadiums, offset boom), one weakly (the well), and two not at
    all (raised cab as a distinct mass, steel bit as a disc/cone).

3. BLENDER ACTIONS

Ordered by how much each buys at 120 px/cell. None are cabin furniture, and none reinstate what cycle
77 ruled out -- no lip ring, no well steel, no boom cap.

  1. REPAINT THE DECK. It is the biggest surface and it is the wrong material. Assign the livery plate
     #ffd23f dielectric to the deck, hopper outer walls, cab sides and boom as the bill specifies.
     Target a mid value clearly BELOW the pad ground, not above it. The flank yellow already reads
     correctly -- match the deck to it.
  2. FIX THE SCAR PLATE. Give the flank plate sooted steel: dark base, roughness 0.55-0.70, metallic
     0.50-0.70. Model the gas-breach chip as real geometry -- a torn edge or punched crater with wall
     thickness, not a texture -- and add the bolt course around its perimeter. It must read as the
     darkest thing on the flank, not the brightest.
  3. CLEAT THE BELTS. Model discrete cross-cleats that break the top plane of each track -- extruded
     bars across the belt width, spaced so they resolve as repeating marks from directly above.
     Retexture the belt to neutral dark #3a3530 so it becomes the darkest mass in the value ladder.
     Break the stadium: sprocket at one end, idler at the other, so the outline is no longer a pill.
  4. FOLD THE HOPPER LIP. Turn the deck plate down into the well as a folded lip 2-3 px deep at play
     size, in the SAME yellow paint -- thickness reads by value break and AO, not by a bright metal
     ring. Then give the interior something: a stepped liner course on the far wall and one hopper fill
     volume so the well has a floor instead of a shadow wedge.
  5. REBUILD THE BOOM AS A SECTION, NOT A CARD. Loft a tapered box beam -- thick at the pivot, thinner
     at the tip. Model the spine as an actual raised rib on the crown with side faces, in STEEL value
     (mid-dark metal), not white -- it should read by its shadow and its silhouette bump, not by
     brightness. Add a pivot yoke and one hydraulic ram at the root.
  6. BUILD THE BIT AS A BODY. A forged drum or cone with a real diameter larger than the boom spine,
     carbide inserts as small solids, and a heat gradient #848b93 -> #9a6f4a -> #ff6242 toward the tip.
     It must separate from the boom by both form and colour.
  7. GIVE THE CAB VOLUME. Bevel the roof edges, break the lid with a hatch and one rib, and put a frame
     with visible thickness around the dark pane so the glass sits in a reveal rather than on a face.
     Bring the yellow brow down from 513 to a paint value -- brightest PAINT, not brightest OBJECT.
  8. RECESS THE ROOF FIXTURE. Sink the disc into a hood or a slit so the fixture reads with emission
     off.
  9. PANELIZE. Inset and solidify plate courses on the deck and flank with 1-2 cm seam channels, and
     bolt heads at the four real interfaces (deck-to-flank, cab-to-deck, boom-to-cab,
     track-frame-to-hull). Add the separate #161008 chevron plates on the deck -- bolted-on plates, not
     painted stripes.
 10. AUTHOR ONE SERVICE ASYMMETRY. One replaced plate or one patch, off-centre, so the mirror breaks.
