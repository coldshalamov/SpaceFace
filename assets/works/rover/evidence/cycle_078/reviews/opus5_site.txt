0. CYCLE 78 vs CYCLE 77 -- THE SAME, PROVABLY

Both site 1:1 crops carry exactly 484 lit pixels; identical luminance min/median/max
(15.0 / 53.2 / 124.4); identical value buckets to within 2 px; identical accent hues. The entire pixel
difference is confined to an 11x22 sub-box with a maximum per-channel delta of 7/255 -- invisible.
Every defect cycle 77 named that survives to this camera (tan card body, yellow bar not a visor line,
boom spine too faint) is still on camera unchanged. This is the literal Section 0 anti-gaming row:
five iterations with the same stills defect.

REVERT is wrong here only because there is nothing better to go back to -- cycle 77 is pixel-identical
and was itself REVISE, so reverting is a no-op.

1. DEFECTS FROM THIS SITE VIEW AT PLAY SIZE

A. IT IS A CARD -- the headline. At play size the rover is a flat tan rounded-corner CARD with two dark
bands ruled across it and a small yellow blob near the middle. The massing under it is a BOX. That is
the toy/card/gray-plate read, and it is the dominant read, not a detail.

B. Silhouette separates, but into the wrong shape. The rover separates cleanly against the surround
(lum 15-124 vs 7-9) -- separation is not the problem. The shape it separates into is a SQUARE, 22x22
px. Nothing overhangs, nothing notches, no negative space. Only the boom nub breaks the outline, by
about 5 px. A square is not a mining rover.

C. THERE IS NO MINE ON CAMERA. 99.98% of the 1920x1080 frame sits at luminance <=12; outside a single
22x22 box the maximum luminance anywhere in the frame is 9, and row-mean varies only 7.79 -> 8.64 top
to bottom. Boosted 6x, the surround is flat noise: no rock face, no bench, no muck pile, no berm, no
other machines, no contact shadow, no horizon. I cannot distinguish "no site geometry was placed" from
"a ground plane is there at near-zero exposure" -- the faint row-mean gradient hints a plane exists.
Either way the still cannot answer "working machine in a mine vs toy on a texture" in the machine
favour, because there is no mine to be parked in.

D. Tracks -- NOT BELTS. Two dark horizontal SMEARS ruled across the top and bottom of the card,
INBOARD of the card own edge. They do not carry the machine: the deck corners stick out past them, so
the card sits on top of the tracks rather than the tracks defining the machine width. No stadium shape
survives. No thin raised steel outer lip exists at all -- the entire dark bucket is 5 pixels across the
whole rover, and two lipped belts cannot live in 5 pixels.

E. Cab house -- ABSENT. No dark house on a mid steel tub. The cab does not separate from the deck in
value or outline. Measured: 60.7% of the lit rover in one light band, 36.8% in one mid band, 1.0%
dark; max/median luminance ratio 2.34. One value with a wobble.

F. Windshield / visor -- WRONG ELEMENT. There is no dark windshield band. There is a small
yellow-orange BLOB (7 px, 1.4%) with no dark glass for it to sit past. Every saturated pixel on the
machine falls in hue 26-31 degrees, so the accent shares the body own hue family and reads as a hot
spot on the tan, not a visor line.

G. Hopper -- NOT A HOLE. Nothing reads as an opening from this camera. A short dark mark left of
centre could be anything; no rim, no interior darkness distinguishable from the track smears. Cycle 77
was told the mouth was a raw cut; from the site camera it is now not even a rectangle.

H. Boom -- the only thing working, and it is a nub. A ~5 px dark stub with a bright tip pokes off the
right edge. It is the sole asymmetry and the sole feature breaking the square -- KEEP IT. But there is
no dark arm -> raised steel spine -> steel bit body -> bright tip chain; there is a stub and a dot.

I. Material separation does not survive. Steel, painted, glass, rubber are indistinguishable. Mean
saturation 0.56 with every hue inside a 5-degree band means one paint on everything. The belts read as
slightly darker tan, not rubber. Glass and bare steel are simply absent.

3. CONCRETE BLENDER ACTIONS

Ordered by measured leverage -- the failure is exactly three things: no dark values, one hue, square
plan.

  1. DARKEN THE BELTS HARD. Rubber must be the darkest value on the machine by a wide margin, well
     below the deck albedo. This alone moves the dark bucket from 1% toward 20-30%.
  2. DESATURATE THE TUB. Move the deck/tub off hue 28 at saturation 0.56 to a low-saturation steel
     gray. Saturation is reserved for the accent only. This is why it currently reads as one plastic
     toy.
  3. MOVE THE TRACKS OUTBOARD. The belts must be the widest points in plan, past the deck sides, with
     daylight between deck underside and track top. The tracks carry the machine; the deck does not sit
     on top of them.
  4. RAISED OUTER LIP AS GEOMETRY, NOT TEXTURE: a thin rail 3-5 cm proud along the OUTER face of each
     belt, shaded steel. At 22-38 px each belt then reads as a dark band with a light edge -- a
     two-value read in one pixel. (This is the TRACK lip. It is not the steel lip ring cycle 77 banned
     from the hopper.)
  5. BREAK THE SQUARE IN PLAN. Push the boom arm out to 40-50% of hull length and extend the
     counterweight the other way so the outline is L-shaped and asymmetric at stamp size. The recorded
     0.1466 offset buys nothing visible.
  6. Cab as a genuinely darker house on a mid tub: separate body, real gap at the base, tall enough to
     break the top edge of the silhouette. Value separation makes it a house at 2 px, not modelling.
  7. Windshield: recess a dark band across the cab front with real shell thickness, and put the yellow
     line only on the lip PAST the glass. No yellow on cab sides, no yellow roof patch.
  8. Hopper as a real hole: through-cut with wall thickness, mouth large enough to be 3-4 px at play
     size, positioned to break the deck top edge in plan so it is seen against the ground rather than
     swallowed inside the plate. Per cycle 77 standing instruction: KEEP the interior dark, NO steel
     lip ring, DO NOT steel the well walls -- thickness only.
  9. Boom chain: dark arm, a proud steel spine rail along its top (geometry, not a painted stripe),
     into a chunkier steel bit body with a small hot machined tip as the one bright accent.

NUMERIC GATE FOR CYCLE 79 -- re-measure on the same 38 px crop and require: dark bucket >= 20%,
light+accent <= 35%, max/median luminance >= 4, and accent hue separated from body hue by more than 5
degrees. Without a gate the next cycle can be another zero-delta and nobody will notice.

SEPARATE FROM THE BLENDER WORK -- THE STILL ITSELF IS NOT EVIDENCE. Light the site and put the rover
in contact with something (bench, muck pile, rim light, contact shadow), then re-cut the 1:1 at the
same offset so cycles stay comparable. As shot, no site verdict is possible next cycle either. Also
note the bundle is mixed: works_beside_flight.png places the TOP 1:1 beside the flight ship, not the
site one, so the "does it hold up beside the real game" comparison was never made for this camera. In
that beside-flight frame the courier reads as a manufactured ship with panel breaks, dark wells and
coloured accents; the rover beside it reads as a tan card.
