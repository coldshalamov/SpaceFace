- The rover reads as a small brown rectangular box between two bars, not as a convincing industrial mining rover. The silhouette lacks strong, role-specific massing beyond the tracks and side attachment.
- Both tracks are extremely dark, but their construction is unclear. They resemble flattened segmented bars more than two heavy stadium-shaped solid-rubber belts.
- The thin raised steel outer lips do not catch enough light to remain legible in shadow. Portions risk reading as wire-like outlines around indistinct dark shapes rather than steel-edged rubber tracks.
- The central body lacks a clear material hierarchy. Cab house, steel tub, deck, and mounted equipment merge into nearly the same muddy brown value.
- The large dark rectangle on the left is ambiguous. At this size it could be a windshield, hopper, inset plate, or black sticker.
- The dark windshield band is not clearly established as glass. There is no unmistakable dark-glass strip followed by a separate thin yellow visor line.
- The visible yellow element is a small vertical block near the right side of the body. It reads as a yellow postage stamp or plate, not a thin visor line extending only beyond visible glass.
- The hopper well does not read as a real hole with depth, wall thickness, and a distinct relationship to the windshield. The main dark pane and any intended hopper cavity compete as similar flat rectangles.
- The offset boom is not mechanically readable. Its silhouette becomes a compact tan mushroom or blunt hook attached to the rover’s right side.
- The boom lacks an obvious dark arm, a raised steel load-bearing spine, and a separately readable steel bit body.
- The tip is tiny and pale, but without the arm/spine/bit hierarchy it reads as a stray highlight rather than the bright working tip of a mining tool.
- The central chassis reads as stacked cards and boxes. Several details look like thin plates laid on a rectangular base rather than interlocking manufactured assemblies.
- The broad flat panels and uniform brown response create an obvious clay/toy read. It is not a gray plate specifically, but it has the same unresolved blockout quality: flat slabs, dark stickers, weak construction depth, and little material separation.
- Small top-deck marks resemble stickers or arbitrary greebles. They do not materially improve the play-size read.
- The overall lighting is too dim and monochromatic to prove the required surfaces. Most potentially useful edge work disappears into brown-on-brown shadow.

3. Concrete Blender actions

- Rebuild the central body as a mid-steel tub with visibly thick sidewalls, stepped shoulders, and a darker cab house seated inside or above it. Avoid a single rectangular chassis slab.
- Shape each track as a genuinely solid stadium-section rubber belt. Give it thickness and tread mass, then add a narrow raised steel lip along the outer upper edge. Light the steel lip selectively; do not outline the entire track with a luminous line.
- Separate the track supports, bogie region, and tub with readable gaps and mounting interfaces. Add only the pivots, brackets, and fasteners large enough to survive this camera.
- Cut the hopper well through the body using an actual Boolean opening. Add a thick rim, dark interior walls, and a floor placed deep enough that the opening reads as a hole.
- Rebuild the windshield as a broad, framed, dark-glass band over a dark cab cavity. Ensure it remains distinct from the hopper at 120 px.
- Place a very thin yellow visor line immediately beyond the visible glass edge. Remove the current yellow rectangular plate and keep yellow off the cab sides, tracks, and boom structure.
- Reconstruct the boom as separate functional masses: an offset dark arm, a raised steel spine running along it, a pivot/flange at the chassis, a machined steel bit body, and a small bright working tip.
- Lengthen or angle the boom enough that its arm and spine remain separately readable in the top view. Eliminate the current mushroom/hook silhouette.
- Add believable boom joints: pivot pin, bearing housing, gussets, hydraulic or power runs with end fittings, and a clear connection from the spine into the bit body.
- Apply scale before beveling. Use narrow, scale-correct bevels and weighted/custom normals so the tub, cab, steel lips, boom spine, and bit catch different controlled highlights without becoming inflated.
- Replace card-like surface plates with inset or shrink-wrapped panels that have real thickness, small standoffs, and dark seam channels.
- Establish distinct materials: near-black matte rubber belts, dark painted cab, mid-value worn steel tub, low-roughness dark glass, brighter machined steel lips/spine/bit, and one restrained yellow visor accent.
- Author roughness and metallic variation so the steel edges and boom catch light while rubber remains broad and dull. Keep the windshield’s specular response visibly different from both.
- Raise overall key and rim readability enough to expose the silhouette and material hierarchy at play size without turning every outer edge into a continuous glow.
- Validate the result at the same 120-pixel crop. The glass band, yellow visor line, hopper hole, two rubber belts with steel lips, and four-stage boom construction must all remain independently recognizable.

**REVISE**

The frame is valid, but the rover remains below the Hitch-plus floor. It reads as a muddy clay/card/box assembly with two dark track bars, an ambiguous dark pane, a yellow plate, and a tan mushroom-like boom.
