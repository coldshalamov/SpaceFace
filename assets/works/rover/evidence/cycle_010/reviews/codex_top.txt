Framing is valid: this is the Works top camera, and the rover fills the 1:1 crop.

1. Obvious defects at play size

- The overall silhouette reads as a compact yellow box between two tracks with a narrow attachment. It lacks convincing industrial massing, structural hierarchy, and a distinctive heavy mining-rover profile.
- The two dark stadium tracks are recognizable, and steel-toned end caps are present, but they read as thin toy tank treads. The repeated tread blocks are soft and uniform; the end caps resemble small circular buttons rather than load-bearing wheels, hubs, or tensioning hardware.
- The chassis between the tracks is a flat rectangular gray/brown plate. It visibly produces the “gray plate under boxes” construction failure.
- The hopper well reads as a dark rectangle, but only barely as a real hole. Its interior is nearly featureless black, while the yellow safety coaming is thick, square, and uniformly bright. Together they resemble a black tile inside a yellow toy box rather than a deep steel hopper.
- The hopper lacks visible inner-wall depth, a sloped or reinforced floor, structural ribs, abrasion wear, and shadow variation that would establish volume.
- The raised cab does not resolve cleanly as a cab. Its roof pane is too small and visually interrupted by surrounding yellow blocks. The frame does not form a strong, continuous dark-roof outline at play size.
- The cab and hopper compete as adjacent rectangular black/yellow shapes. Their functions are not clearly differentiated through massing or material.
- The yellow body consists of several blunt cuboids with similar bevels. It reads as a LEGO-like stack or yellow plastic toy, not yellow safety panels fitted to a steel machine.
- Several yellow panels appear excessively thick and slab-like. They look glued onto the top rather than supported by an underlying steel frame.
- The central gray components read as flat cards or plates. Their interfaces with the yellow blocks lack hinges, welds, gaskets, countersinks, or visible mounting brackets.
- The offset boom is visible, but it reads as a thin gray rectangular bar bolted to the rover’s side. It lacks a substantial shoulder pivot, actuator, gussets, hoses, or a tapered load path.
- The boom’s exposed gray material reads as neutral plastic rather than machined or fabricated steel.
- The steel bit is visible at the boom tip, but its silhouette is a small pale-gray bulb or spade. It does not read decisively as a mining bit, cutter, drill, or breaker.
- The boom and bit are too light and visually detached from the rover’s industrial structure. Their connection looks appended rather than engineered.
- The top surfaces are too clean and uniform. There is little evidence of abrasion, chipped safety paint, exposed steel, grease, tracked dust, or concentrated service wear.
- The dominant surfaces share a soft, clay-like response. Paint, bare steel, track rubber/steel, glass, and hopper interior do not separate convincingly by highlight or roughness.
- The broad brown chassis and soft lighting flatten the rover against the ground tile. Important construction edges disappear at this scale.
- There is no readable authored asymmetry beyond the boom itself. The body otherwise appears mechanically mirrored and generated.
- The result remains a yellow box with plates.

3. Concrete Blender actions

- Rebuild the center chassis as a fabricated steel tub or ladder frame with tapered crossmembers, recessed track mounts, and visible load paths. Remove the broad flat gray plate silhouette.
- Give the body at least three planform transitions: narrower cab shoulder, wider hopper/load frame, and a shaped boom-mount quarter. Avoid a single rectangular envelope.
- Cut the hopper through the upper skin with an actual Boolean opening. Add 4–12 cm rim thickness, sloped inner walls, a darker abrasion-marked floor, and two or three structural ribs visible from above.
- Replace the thick yellow hopper box with a narrower yellow safety coaming mounted over dark steel. Break the coaming at joints and corners so it reads as fitted guard structure.
- Raise and clarify the cab as one coherent steel module. Cut a roof tub, add a thin dark glass shell, and surround it with a continuous steel frame that remains readable at 120 px.
- Reduce yellow coverage. Use yellow on replaceable safety panels, guards, step edges, and hazard zones; expose a darker fabricated-steel primary structure beneath them.
- Reshape yellow panels as thin formed sheets with flanges and controlled bends. Add narrow shadow gaps rather than stacking beveled cubes.
- Rebuild each track as a substantial dark stadium assembly: clearly sized drive and idler wheels with steel hubs/end caps, supported road wheels, a deeper tread belt, and track guards tied into the chassis.
- Vary tread shape and spacing enough to avoid the bead-chain or toy-tread read while retaining clarity at play size.
- Build a real boom shoulder: large pivot pin, paired cheek plates, gusseted base, hydraulic cylinder, and a visible gap around the rotating member.
- Taper or loft the boom from a deep root to a narrower tool end. It should carry bending load rather than resemble a rectangular card.
- Route one or two dark hydraulic hoses along the boom using beveled curves. Terminate every hose in visible steel fittings at the chassis and actuator.
- Replace the pale bulb tip with an unmistakable industrial tool silhouette—faceted breaker, conical drill, or toothed cutter—with a darker tool-steel body, replaceable wear tip, collar, and mounting flange.
- Add interface-specific construction: countersunk bolts at removable panels, weld beads at permanent frame joints, gaskets around the roof pane, and pins at articulated joints.
- Apply scale before assigning bevels. Use smaller bevels on sheet edges and hardware, with larger radii only on cast housings or impact-safe corners. Repair weighted normals afterward.
- Author separate materials for yellow coated steel, dark structural steel, brushed boom steel, hardened bit steel, roof glass, and track material. Ensure each produces a distinct highlight.
- Concentrate chipped paint and exposed primer on hopper lips, step edges, boom shoulders, track guards, and leading corners. Add grease at pivots and brown-gray dirt inside recesses.
- Add one purposeful service asymmetry, such as a replaced dark hopper panel, a worn warning stencil, or a bolted repair patch. Do not add random greebles.
- Recheck the exported rover at exactly this Works scale. The hopper must read as a deep hole, the cab as a framed dark roof pane, the boom as fabricated steel hardware, and yellow as safety cladding rather than the machine’s entire substance.

4. Verdict

**REVISE**

The rover is recognizable, but this supported Works view still reads as a yellow plastic box assembled over a gray plate with toy tracks and an appended gray boom. It does not meet the Hitch-plus floor.
