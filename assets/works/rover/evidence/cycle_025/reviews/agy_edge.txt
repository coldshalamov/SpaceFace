### Asteroid Works Rover Visual Review (PQ-131.01 Cycle 25 ΓÇö Works Edge)

### (1) Obvious Defects (Works-Edge View)

* **Toy / Clay / Card / Box / Gray Plate Aesthetic**: The rover continues to read as a primitive cardboard/clay mockup and "yellow box with plates" rather than a functional, heavy-duty asteroid mining machine. The hull is composed of flat, unfilleted gray planar sheets, sharp 90┬░ card-like edges, and unarticulated solid blocks.
* **Side Walls**: The lateral side walls are paper-thin, flat vertical gray sheets tapering toward the front. They completely lack realistic structural plate thickness, rolled/reinforced top lips, exterior vertical stiffener ribs, gussets, fastener patterns, weld seams, and heavy load-bearing structural brackets connecting to the track frames.
* **Track Height & Track Assemblies**: The upper and lower crawler track assemblies are monolithic dark bands with a low vertical profile and minimal relief. Detail is limited to primitive circular idler/roller dots and basic stepped pads. They lack modeled drive sprockets, idler wheels, track tensioners, roller bogies, suspension linkages, and rock/debris scrapers.
* **Cab House with Solid Yellow Roof Patch**: The starboard cab structure remains a basic square gray box with a flat, solid yellow rectangular roof patch. While the hollow frame from cycle 24 is now filled in as a solid patch, it reads as a flat, untextured yellow plastic plate or card dropped into a box. It exhibits an irregular notch on its forward-left edge, lacks chamfered bevels, perimeter weld flanges, an armored visor/brow, external grab handles, or floodlight/antenna mounting bosses.
* **Hopper Well**: The cargo well is an unarticulated, cavernous rectangular cutout with a simple sloped, ribbed forward ramp. It lacks a heavy-duty grizzly sorting grate across the top opening, replaceable interior wear liners, internal reinforcement gussets, hydraulic ejector/dump cylinder mechanisms, and material deflection baffles.
* **Boom / Front Implement**: The forward implement is a static, low-poly gray wedge with a blunt beige/tan nose facet. It is rigidly attached to the cab nose without articulated boom arms, pivot yokes, hydraulic lift/tilt cylinders, armored hydraulic hose runs, or excavation/cutting teeth.
* **Scar Plate & Pivot Mounts**: The mid-deck interface between the hopper well and front assembly consists of two disjointed, isolated black rectangular blocks with tiny protruding yellow pegs. These read as fragile plastic toy snap-connectors rather than a unified, heavy cast-steel scar plate or structural bulkhead with reinforced pivot trunnions.

---

### (2) MTX Defects Visible at Play Size

* **Flat Diffuse Shading & Lack of Specular Response**: Hull plates, roof patch, and nose wedge display flat, uniform diffuse shading without realistic metallic reflectance, surface roughness variation, or contact ambient occlusion in seams, corners, and recesses.
* **Toy-Like Color Blocking**: High-contrast, unweathered separation between flat neutral gray hull plates, pitch-black track loops, saturated yellow roof plate, and beige nose facet. There is no industrial weathering, edge paint chipping, primer exposure, regolith/dust accumulation, grease seepage, or wear gradients.
* **Absence of Micro-Surface Relief & Normal Definition**: At 1:1 play scale, surfaces lack normal/bump detail for structural weld beads, heavy bolt patterns, panel seams, cast steel grain, and track link pins, leaving sharp low-polygon silhouette edges exposed.

2. **Cab House & Solid Yellow Roof Patch Detailing**:

3. **Hopper Well Upgrades**:

4. **Articulated Boom & Tool Geometry**:

   * Model the forward tool head as a reinforced excavation bucket or cutting drum with carbide wear teeth.
5. **Unified Scar Plate & Bulkhead**:

6. **PBR Materials & Weathering Pipeline**:
