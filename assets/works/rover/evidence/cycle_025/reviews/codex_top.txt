3. Concrete Blender actions

- Rebuild the central chassis as a welded or bolted steel tub with at least three changing cross-sections: narrower machinery nose, broader hopper/working mass, and reinforced boom shoulder.
- Break the rectangular planform with functional shoulders, track-mount cut-ins, hopper rim geometry, and a substantial asymmetric boom-root housing.
- Cut the hopper through the chassis using an Exact Boolean. Add a visibly thick rim and four separately modeled sloped steel walls descending to a dark floor. Keep the mouth open and readable from this exact camera.
- Replace superficial gray rectangles with inset or overlapping steel panels. Add narrow seam channels and visible thickness; remove plate-like pieces that do not represent a real access panel or structural member.
- Rebuild each track as a layered assembly: thin steel outer lip, dark belt, clearly raised transverse grousers, and small recessed hubs/idlers visible inside the stadium path.
- Increase grouser width, height, and contrast enough that each reads as a steel cleat rather than a pale dot. Keep their spacing mechanically regular.
- Give the track mounts visible brackets or suspension arms connecting them to the chassis so the belts no longer appear pasted beside the body.
- Make the yellow cab-roof patch an inset face within a steel perimeter: recess it slightly, retain a narrow metal rim, and use painted-steel roughness rather than smooth plastic. Do not split it into bars or a hollow frame.
- Replace the thin beige projection with a fat offset fabricated-steel boom using a tapered box or lofted section. Add a reinforced pivot clevis at the chassis and a separate joint at the tool end.
- Add one or two readable hydraulic cylinders and properly terminated hoses between the boom and its pivot housing. Keep them large enough to survive 120-pixel presentation.
- Rebuild the bit as an attached tool assembly: shaft, mounting collar, thicker cutting head, and a few large teeth or cutting facets. Give the wear surfaces a darker machined-tool-steel response.
- Apply scale before beveling. Use narrow two-segment bevels on chassis plates, wider controlled radii on cast housings, weighted/custom normals, and shade-by-angle rather than global smooth shading.
- Author separate material responses for painted steel, bare/machined steel, dark track material, hopper interior, and cutting-tool steel. Add restrained cavity dirt and contact wear around the hopper, grouser roots, boom pivots, and panel seams.
- Preserve broad rest areas, but cluster mechanical detail at the track drives, hopper rim, boom root, hydraulic joints, and tool mount.
