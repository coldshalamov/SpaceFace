1. Obvious defects from this Works view at play size

- Silhouette reads as a compact rectangular box placed between two parallel bars. It lacks the strong industrial massing and negative space needed to read immediately as a mining rover.
- Tracks are brown/tan and weakly separated from the ground, not two clearly dark stadium-shaped solid-rubber belts.
- The thin raised steel outer lips do not resolve convincingly as steel catching light in shadow. The repeated bright edging makes parts of the tracks resemble outlined loops.
- Track tread structure is too fine and low-contrast to survive at play size.
- The cab house and mid-steel tub are not clearly separated as distinct masses. The center reads as stacked rectangular plates.
- No unmistakable dark windshield band is visible. The large dark square reads more like an inset panel or empty box than glass.
- The yellow treatment is a thick vertical side bar/post. It does not read as a thin visor line appearing only beyond visible dark glass.
- The hopper well is not clearly identifiable as a real hole distinct from the windshield/pane. The large square recess is ambiguous and visually flat.
- The boom is too small and indistinct. Its dark arm, raised steel spine, joint into the bit body, and functional articulation do not survive at this scale.
- The boom tip reads as a small pale/tan knob or mushroom-like attachment, not a steel bit body ending in a deliberate bright working tip.
- The rover has an obvious toy/card/box read: rectangular cab, rectangular inset, flat roof/tub plates, and thin appended boom.
- Materials collapse into brown-gray clay. Rubber, painted cab, structural steel, glazing, and tool steel do not separate convincingly.
- Broad flat faces and uniformly soft lighting create a gray/brown plate appearance. There is little evidence of load-bearing construction, track suspension, joints, shell thickness, or manufactured interfaces.
- At the scale shown in `works_top`, the central square and yellow post dominate more strongly than the rover’s mining function.

3. Concrete Blender actions

- Rebuild the center as a coherent mid-steel tub with changing cross-sections, beveled shoulders, and a lower load-bearing chassis—not stacked cubes.
- Shape two solid, dark stadium belts with real width and sidewalls. Add narrow raised steel outer lips as separate geometry, using restrained metallic roughness so only portions catch the light.
- Increase track-to-ground and track-to-body value separation. Keep the belts dark rubber; do not brighten their complete perimeter.
- Cut the hopper with an actual Boolean Difference. Add a thick rim, four inner walls, and a visibly lower dark floor so it reads as a hole from this camera.
- Separate the hopper opening spatially from the glazed cab area.
- Cut a cab opening and fit a thin dark glass shell into a metal frame. Make the windshield a broad, unmistakable dark band at play size.
- Replace the yellow side/post treatment with a thin visor line located only beyond the visible windshield band. Remove yellow from cab sides and track-adjacent bars.
- Rebuild the boom as offset articulated machinery: substantial dark arm, visible pivot bodies, raised steel spine, and grounded end fittings.
- Model the bit as a tapered or stepped steel tool body joined directly to the spine. Give only its terminal working point a small bright highlight; eliminate the tan knob/mushroom shape.
- Add readable bearings, hinge gaps, pins, hose curves with end fittings, and fasteners only where the boom transfers load into the tub.
- Apply scale before beveling. Use narrow two-segment bevels, angle-based shading, weighted normals, and post-Boolean normal repair.
- Replace flat card-like plates with inset or solidified panels having controlled gaps and thickness.
- Establish distinct materials for rubber belts, painted cab/tub, bare steel lips and boom spine, dark glass, and hardened tool steel.
- Author roughness and metallic variation so these substances remain distinct under the Works lighting.
- Adjust the Works lighting or material values enough to preserve the dark windshield, deep hopper, rubber tracks, steel lips, and bit tip without turning edges into continuous luminous outlines.
- Do not add cabin furniture.

4. Verdict

**REVISE** — the framing is valid, but the rover still reads as a brown-gray box with plates, ambiguous openings, weak track material separation, an incorrect yellow side bar, and an under-resolved mining boom.
