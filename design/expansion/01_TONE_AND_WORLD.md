# 01 — Tone and world

Read [`00_README.md`](00_README.md) first. This file holds four things: the map of the Helios Reach,
its history, the voice, and the look. The canon files it builds on are listed at the end. Lines
marked **PROPOSED CANON** are new and wait for the narrative lane (see the README).

---

## 1. The Helios Reach — the map

The playable system is the **Helios Reach**: one star, its worlds and belts, and the jump-gate web
the Verge-Layers still maintain. The main menu's orrery already calls it "the Orrery of the Helios
Reach". The 25 sectors fall into eight regions. Each region has one physical identity, so a player
can tell where they are from thirty seconds of motion, not from a label.

| Region | Sectors | What moves here | What you throw here | Air |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Core** | Helios Prime, Dione Lane | licensed freight, customs, patrols, tourists | cargo pods, buoys, lane furniture | clean, metered, too bright |
| **The Belt** | Ceres Belt, Vesta Forge, Hyperion Cut, Rhea Cinder, Nereid Shoal | ore, slag, refinery plate, tugs | ore rock, slag bergs, ice, hopper lids | yellow-white, slipping |
| **The Junction** | Tethys Junction, Pallas Drift, Io Reach, Kepler Scar | convoys, black market, mail, surplus | containers, wreck plate, reef debris | sodium-red, graffiti-thick |
| **The Haven** | Sker Haven | stolen cargo, press gangs, the Cull broadcasts | everything that isn't bolted down | smoky, loud |
| **The Deep Belt** | Charon Expanse, Haumea Rift | scorched ore, bounties, survey readings | radiation-cooked rock, ice spires | sold by the canister |
| **The Veil** | Veil Nebula, Orcus Shadow, Phoebe Echo, Triton Wake, Eunomia Gulf, Sedna Dark | Vael research couriers, salvage under licence | instrument buoys, resonant crystal, licensed plate | the best in the outer sectors, and it is a contract |
| **The Margin** | Eris Margin, Nyx March, Proteus Well | contraband, findings sold before they are filed | dead drops, decoy pods, well-sediment | none on record |
| **The Deep** | Ashfall Reach, The Bolus | the Bloom, the Iron Maw, the Witness | Bloom hulks, Vethari wreck-bone, the dreadnought itself | smells like the Pit |

The **Pit** (canon "Sector 0") is not a flyable sector. It is the place every story points home to.
It is seen in the opening, in memory and in the epilogue. Its absence from the map is the point.

## 2. History

Years are counted **from the Release** (Year 0), as Pit survivors count them. Contract 47-A (B0) is
**Year 17**. Everything in **bold** is canon from R1/R12. Everything else is PROPOSED CANON.

| When | Event | What it left in the world (things the player can touch) |
|---|---|---|
| Deep time | **The makers of the Verge-Layers** build the gate web and leave. Spacers call them **the First Clerks**: their machines measure, audit, witness and hear appeals. *The oldest civilization in the Reach was an honest bureaucracy.* | The gates. The 14 machine kinds. The 17 site types (Quarantine Pylon Field, Machine Ossuary, Sleeping Jury…). **Measure Engine** fragments, which are the root of the Line's technology |
| Deep time | **The Vethari**, an advanced spacefaring species, are colonised by the coupling fungus. *Connection is the catastrophe.* Their carriers drift on, crewed by the colony. | Vethari wreck-bone. Colonised carriers (the Bolus is one). Echoes and stains in Bloom memory. **Vethari stay off-frame**: hulls and traces only, never a living Vethari on screen |
| ~200 years before | Humans find the gates working and settle the Reach through them. | Core stations. Old survey graffiti. The first lane buoys |
| ~60 years before | The **Pit** opens as a debtors' and labour prison-colony under the Custodial Authority (the wardens). | Intake stencils, quota ledgers, the count. Every faction's grammar comes from here |
| **Year 0 — the Release** | The Pit's charter is dissolved. *The release changed nothing essential.* Wardens become clerks; prisoners become crews. The factions form along prison roles. | Faction cultures. Graffiti mottos. The habit of counting |
| **Year 3** | **The Pit's recycler catalyst grid (12.4 t) leaves legally under `VALE-ALA-47A`. Slate welds the Shaft 7 hopper shut.** The Pit's air begins to die: **the Slow Gray**. | Helios Bay 7's rack. The smell ticket. The REF 44-C investigation |
| Year 6 | **The Fulfillment** goes autonomous. A logistics combine dissolves in a merger, and its routing fleet keeps fulfilling every order ever placed, forever. | White re-router haulers on fixed routes. Spilled decades-old orders |
| **Year 8 (nine before B0)** | **The vault job. Wren is arrested at 22** and ships on **CPV-2214, "the Can"**. | Wren's record. Callum's ledger |
| Year 11 | **The First Tide.** A colonised Vethari hulk drifts through the Ashfall gate. Bloom swarms reach human lanes in Charon. The Understory's rot-gardens hold the line; three gardens die doing it. | Understory memorial gardens. Bloom-scarred wrecks in Charon and Eunomia. Pitborn learn to cull for money |
| **Year 12 (five before B0)** | **Wren is revived at 26** and settles a debt with the Tessera. | The Tessera, a tug with too many names |
| Year 13 | Pitborn yard crews turn culling into sport: **the Cull**, a broadcast blood-league in wrecked arenas. The Crucible circuit is born. | The Crucible arenas. Cull broadcasts on the band. Kill counts on the ticker |
| Year 16 | **The Gate Audits begin.** The Directorate starts closing outer gates "for fiscal consolidation". Each closure strands a community and wakes a little more Verge-Layer law. | Sealed gates. Longer lanes. Woken Gate Auditors. Stranded homesteads |
| **Year 17 — B0** | **Contract 47-A. THEY KNEW THE MASS.** | Everything in the campaign |

**The present tense (PROPOSED CANON).** Three pressures move the map during the campaign. Each one
is physical, persistent and visible on the chart (`build_map.md` §34 rows 520–523):

1. **Bloom tides.** Infestation spreads along lanes where wrecks are left to rot. Cleared wrecks and
   fed Understory gardens push it back. A neglected sector greys over.
2. **The Gate Audits.** The Directorate closes gates on a schedule the player can read and disrupt.
   Each closure reroutes traffic and wakes Verge law around that gate.
3. **The Slow Gray.** The Pit's air index falls on the Meridian ticker every cycle. It is the
   campaign's clock. It never improves (the epilogue is canon). The player's violence moves the
   price, never the air.

## 3. The voice — "deadpan ledger, loud physics"

### 3.1 The rule

The **words** stay in the canon register: flat declaratives, inventory language, no adverbs, no
speeches (`vibe-CANONICAL.md`, `FACT_Voice_Bible.md`). The **picture and motion** are loud: bright
Neon Industrial colour, fat impacts, ships pinwheeling through cargo. **The comedy is the gap between
them**: a clerk's voice filing a catastrophe the player made with their hands.

The canon comedy law decides where jokes may live (`CREATIVE-DIRECTION.md` §5):

- **Shop floor, loud and funny.** Bars, docks, yards, scales, Cull arenas, swarm rounds, the kill feed,
  Pitborn broadcasts, Fulfillment boarding.
- **Terminal zones, airless.** The Vethari, the Witness's room, Shaft 7, Elroy, the endings, the Pit's
  lower decks. No jokes, no bright colour, no music stings.
- **Hard cuts, never blends.** The game's signature move is a Cull-arena roar that cuts straight to
  the Slow Gray ticker.

### 3.2 The kill feed is the narrator of physics

The kill feed and the Tessera's log carry most of the game's comedy, because they describe
slapstick in the language of filing. Each line obeys the voice law: short, declarative, a confession
if it is about death. These samples set the house register for every physics outcome
(the LOG feed is `build_map.md` §34 row 502, and INFERENCE EXP-12 fills its copy):

```
REAVER-2 RECLASSIFIED: PROJECTILE.
INCIDENT FILED: WASP-4 STRUCK BY ASTEROID AST-355. ASTEROID OWNER: YOU.
CARGO POD 7 DELIVERED. RECIPIENT: CORSAIR-1. SIGNATURE NOT OBTAINED.
TONNAGE LOGGED: 412 T. CORRECT.
TWO HULLS, ONE TRAJECTORY. FILED AS ONE INCIDENT.
MITE COUNT REVISED DOWNWARD. REVISION: 31.
LIABILITY FOR THE STATION DENT: PENDING.
THE ROCK WAS NEUTRAL. IS NOW EVIDENCE.
YOUR LINE HELD 2,180 T. THE MANIFEST SAID 1,900. VARIANCE LOGGED.
```

The ship AI that speaks these, the canon's "voice like a bored hangman", is named here: **LOG**. LOG
never jokes on purpose. LOG is always correct. Whether a line is funny depends on what the player
just did.

### 3.3 Words a player reads in flight

Player-facing flight text follows plain-verb rules (`build_map.md` §33 row 496):

- Say what a thing **does**: "Pull — drags light ships and shots toward it". Never what band it is
  in.
- Numbers carry units a person knows: "tonnes", "seconds", "credits". Never `wu/s`, `REF`, `V-MAX`
  or `LINE 5.5k`.
- Debug provenance (`Reach·Fighter·L1`, `VULN E— K— X—`, `ASSIGNED INTERDICTION`) never reaches
  the flight HUD.
- The insider poetry of the canon voice lives on walls, in barks and on paper. Instructions are
  plain. *"Light ships are ammunition. Swing a rock. Keep the speed."* is a graffiti line, not a
  tutorial line.

Use these when naming people, ships, crews and places. Every new name must sound like it came from
inside one of these cultures.

| Drift (DMC) | injury or tool nicknames | tool names | Nine-Finger Abe · *Ironsong*, *First-Notch* |
| Reach | epithet names | trophy names | Yara No-Cut, Rake Veyra · *Nestbreaker* |
| Quiet | no names, relay codes | none (transponder strings) | "Relay Eleven" · `QX-0` |

## 4. The look

The look law already exists and wins: `docs/visual-assets/LOOK.md` ("Neon Industrial": bright arcade
with a cyberpunk vibe). Hulls stay Lacquer & Starlight (`build_map.md` §26), and the UI stays the
ORRERY. This bible adds three laws that a swarm-physics game needs on top of them.

1. **Work lights tell you what is dangerous.** Amber means it will move. Red means it will hurt. Cyan
   means it is yours or on your Line. Green means it is alive (Bloom or Understory). Violet means it
   is Verge or Vael. One colour per meaning across hulls, VFX and HUD.
2. **Scale must be readable at a glance.** The Katamari ladder needs size cues on every big body:
   windows, ladders, hazard stripes, running lights, ship-sized doors. A 50,000 t station chunk must
   *look* fifty thousand tonnes next to a 20 t rock.
3. **Silhouette before surface.** Every role (02_FACTIONS) and every Bloom tier (03) is identified
   by shape and engine colour with no marker. Surface detail comes after the silhouette reads.

Region moods (palette classes already in `STORY_SECTOR_MAP.md`): **Core** is cyan and steel,
**Belt** is rust and amber, **Junction** is sodium-red, **Haven** is smoke and neon, **Deep Belt**
is functional wrong-light, **Veil** is violet and green dissonance, **Margin** is unlit, and **Deep**
is Pit-temperature grey with Bloom green.
