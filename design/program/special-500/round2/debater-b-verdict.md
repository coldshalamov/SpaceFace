# Round 2 — Debater B verdict (the Curator)

Lens: player experience, variety, pacing, reward sanity. Evidence: all 600 round-1 proposals read
in full; tone bar from `design/GDD_2_0.md` §1–§3 and `design/VISION.md`; quality bar calibrated
against shipped encounters (`src/data/encounters/1207-the-witnessed-price.js`,
`100-distress-call.js`, the 1200-series, the depth series). I am the voice of the player who has
to live inside these 500 things for a hundred hours: I kill filler without apology.

## Critique highlights

- **The eclipse family is seven entries playing one phenomenon.** SP3-002 (umbra minute), SP3-015
  (station view), SP3-019 (bulletin), SP3-028 (moonrise), SP3-172 (shadow bands), SP1-158 (moon
  shadow crossing the sector), SP1-162 (Pallas occultation + shadow cache). One of these is a
  signature scheduled event; seven is a weather channel stuck on the same forecast. I keep SP3-002
  as the anchor with SP3-019 as its teach, and merge the rest into the chain — the merged entry
  must carry the station window-wave and "umbra in sixty" PA (SP3-015), the thermal-tell shadow
  cache (SP1-162), the running-light traffic etiquette (SP1-158), and the shadow-band ripple
  (SP3-172). SP3-028 is the tail I kill outright.
- **Named cross-lens dupes** (both ids, merge into the stronger): SP1-149 / SP3-024 are literally
  both titled "Ring Rain"; SP1-165 / SP2-091 are two Choir confessionals; SP1-175 / SP2-086 are
  two lost-luggage installations; SP1-084 / SP2-141 are two hermits living in wrecks who trade;
  SP1-030 / SP2-142 / SP2-158 are one insurance-fraud story in three acts; SP1-017 / SP1-103 are
  two lifetime-milestone counters (SP2-116 and SP2-189 are the same idea landed better as
  character, so they live); SP3-063 / SP3-065 are the same paired boulders; SP3-118 / SP3-182 are
  the same market chime; SP3-179 / SP3-151 are the same year-end cadence; SP2-087 / SP3-183 /
  SP3-144 are three voices for one shift turn.
- **Clone families** — keep the family's best, fold the rest in as honed legs, kill the tail:
  glints (SP3-001 / 013 / 036 / 164: keep 001 + 036, fold 164 into 001); rock collapses
  (SP1-063 / SP1-148 / SP3-064 / SP3-076: two survivors, each absorbing one); silences
  (SP1-093 / SP3-046 / SP3-148: 093 wins, 046 dies, 148 folds in); bells and chimes (nine
  entries: SP3-085, 100, 144, 149, 151, 182 survive as distinct — the rest merge);
  auctions (SP1-004 / 020 / 088 / 105 / 143: 143's jettison-bid escrow folds into 004; 020, 088,
  105 earn their keep by being different verbs); imitators (SP1-048 / 188 / 199: 188's lesson
  folds into 199); weddings (SP1-159 / SP1-169: 169 folds in — SP2-132 stays because its joke is
  paperwork, not ceremony); numbers stations (SP2-013 / SP2-014 die; SP3-168 is the one keeper
  because it forecasts rather than recites).
- **Content that already ships.** SP1-098 (The Negative) is a second copy of
  `290-depth-h8-echo-of-player.js`, which is live in `src/data/encounters/` — kill. SP2-013 says
  outright it "mirrors the Vesta numbers-station loop with new ids" — a palette swap is exactly
  the filler this debate exists to kill. A player who meets both the shipped echo and SP1-098
  within one save will conclude the world has one trick.
- **Reward sanity flags:** SP2-190 pays "real credits (large)" for a 100-day dock — hone to a few
  hundred; the point is the codex tense change. SP1-013's "rich" stash and SP1-032's "beats a
  delivery contract" need caps. Hull-prize entries (SP1-004, SP1-088, SP1-136, SP1-143) must pay
  in scarred, used hulls with a history — never in stat upgrades. Everything else I checked
  quotes plausible numbers against a working-space economy where a genuine rescue pays ~120 cr
  and a posted kill ~1,400.
- **Pacing:** the comms/mail triggers are well-gated (once-flags everywhere, long cooldowns); the
  real spam risk is at shift turn, where SP3-086 + SP3-100 + SP3-144 + SP2-087 + SP3-183 would
  all speak at once — a pillar-3 violation. My merges collapse that stack to one visual cascade,
  one bell, one station voice. SP2-042's every-8-day spam is the one recurring sender and it is
  funny enough to keep.
- **The first hour is nearly empty.** The overwhelming majority of triggers are counters — 10+
  dockings, beat 3+, 50 deeds, 20 hours. A fresh save meets almost nothing in this pool for its
  first two hours, which is exactly when a player decides whether the world is alive. My
  SPX-B-01/02/03 are first-hour content; the consensus should protect a first-hour slot.
- **Concentration:** Helios / Ceres / Tethys / Vesta carry the pool; Dione, Eris, Nereid, Haumea
  and Proteus are nearly bare. Killing the duplicate families frees budget for exactly those —
  merged entries should prefer a thin sector for their second venue, and my SPX do.
- **Determinism and seams are in good shape.** All three lenses kept to simTime / proximity /
  flags; both seam maps are honest about dirty files. One flag: SP3-016's payoff assumes a photo
  mode — hone it to the scan pulse so it does not lean on a surface that may not exist.
- **Tone:** the pool is overwhelmingly on-register (dry, receipted, physical — "the pod,
  notably, has opinions" could ship tomorrow). Two flags: SP1-111's "Brood Mare" needs a rename;
  SP2-028 designs a moment only a screenshot can hold — kept, but only because its bar-pad
  fallback already solves that.

## New proposals

Filling genuine holes: the first hour is nearly empty in the pool, pure physics-toy encounters and
quiet rest stops are under-served, and the thin sectors (Dione, Ashfall rim, Eunomia, Sedna) need
residents. Effort mix kept cheap on purpose — fillers should be data, not bespoke.

### SPX-B-01 — The Arrival Stencil
- Kind: visual-setpiece
- Where: sector_helios_prime, starter lane rocks (one rock per core sector thereafter)
- Trigger: first-hour proximity — first pass down the starter lane in a fresh save; uniqueOnce per sector
- The player sees: Stenciled hull-paint on the lane's biggest rock, renewed by dockhands between shifts: "WELCOME TO THE LANE. MIND THE TANKERS." Under it, a chalked list of the three dumbest ways to die here, honest and current ("3. the scan ring is NOT a suggestion"). One line is scratched out and corrected in different paint.
- Payoff: Pure first-hour texture; the correction is a real hint (the scratched line names a hazard the tutorial skips). Locals add a small mark each time you pass clean.
- Build seam: story-prop decal on an existing lane rock + one flag; bark line in ENCOUNTER_BARKS
- Effort: data
- replaces: none (fills the first-hour hole)

### SPX-B-02 — The Rookie Scratch
- Kind: easter-egg
- Where: sector_helios_prime, starter station launch rack
- Trigger: first docking after the player's first hull damage (any source) within the first 2 sector-days of a save
- The player sees: The launch rack's fender beam carries generations of scored hull marks, each initialed and dated. As you dock, a dockhand torch adds yours — one line, your initials, today's date — and says nothing. It is already there when you look.
- Payoff: Permanent ship-screen line ("rack mark, {date}"); the dockhand who added it nods at your hull forever after; a veteran with forty marks on the same beam buys your first drink.
- Build seam: dock vignette (902-scene precedent) + damage-event flag; shipScreen dressing row
- Effort: data+glue
- replaces: none (fills the first-hour hole)

### SPX-B-03 — The Rookie Rate
- Kind: comms-event
- Where: sector_io_reach, the first Reach toll of a save
- Trigger: the player's first toll payment ever (persistent counter); uniqueOnce
- The player sees: The receipt prints one line no other receipt has: "ROOKIE RATE — FIRST TOLL ON THE HOUSE. THE LANE WANTS YOU BACK." The collector, if hailed, is embarrassed about it: "It's a promotion. Don't make it a thing."
- Payoff: Zero-cost first contact with the toll system, taught as welcome instead of extortion; the collector remembers you got the rate ("You're the one the promo worked on").
- Build seam: toll receipt variant keyed to a one-shot flag; line in ENCOUNTER_RECEIPTS
- Effort: data
- replaces: none (fills the first-hour hole)

### SPX-B-04 — The Deck Chair
- Kind: anomaly
- Where: sector_dione_lane, a seeded empty patch off the lane
- Trigger: first proximity; uniqueOnce
- The player sees: A patch of nothing — no rocks, no traffic, no salvage — with one welded deck chair bolted to a bare plate, aimed at the sector's best view of its star. A small placard: "RESERVED FOR WHOEVER NEEDS IT. WIPE IT DOWN."
- Payoff: A rest stop the game never marks: hull heat bleeds at double rate while parked here; wiping the chair (a scan interaction) leaves it clean for the next pilot and chalks your initials on the plate's underside, which nobody is supposed to find.
- Build seam: zone record (sectorZones, no presence) + one prop; heat exemption via the same predicate pattern as SP3-026
- Effort: data+glue
- replaces: none (fills the quiet/lonely moment hole in a thin sector)

### SPX-B-05 — The Sprung Hold
- Kind: encounter
- Where: any sector, derelict_field
- Trigger: proximity to a seeded wreck whose hold is under tension (scan tell: hull bowed, one strap singing); cooldownS 3600
- The player sees: A dead freighter's hold is a loaded spring — cargo strapped under a bent beam with one frayed strap holding all of it. Cut it wrong and the pallet launches through the field like a comet, shedding cargo the whole way. Cut it right — latch the beam with the massline first, then the strap — and it settles.
- Payoff: A physics save or a physics chase: catch the flying pallet on a line mid-flight for the whole hold plus the belt's respect; miss and it scatters salvage across a kilometer of field, first-come.
- Build seam: numbered selfRegistered encounter with pre-latched attachments (1210-the-deadweight pattern) and a breakable constraint chain
- Effort: data+glue
- replaces: none (fills the pure-physics-toy encounter hole)

### SPX-B-06 — The Meteor Fishery
- Kind: world-oneoff
- Where: sector_vesta_forge, sector_io_reach (meteor-season surge paths)
- Trigger: always present during meteor_shower seasons; the fishery is a fixed net-rig on the surge path
- The player sees: Crews have strung foil net-lines across the meteor surge path like a river salmon run — stones hit the nets, sparks walk the lines, a skiff hauls the catch. A shingle sign reads the day's take in stones and one honest loss count ("NETS: 3. METEORS: EVERYTHING ELSE").
- Payoff: You may string ONE net-line of your own (capped, licensed for the season — the license is 40 cr and a signature); your line catches seeded stones on the public schedule. Unlicensed lines get cut by the fishery, politely, once.
- Build seam: weatherMeteors slot-book read + prop line + a capped yield flag in story.flags
- Effort: data+glue
- replaces: none (fills the participatory weather-economy hole)

### SPX-B-07 — The Three Liars' Cache
- Kind: easter-egg
- Where: sector_ashfall_reach, radiation field rim
- Trigger: proximity to a locked cache with a plain question on the face ("WHAT IS THE MASS OF THE ROCK BESIDE IT?"); uniqueOnce
- The player sees: Three prior attempt-notes are welded beside the lock, each with a different confident answer, all different, all wrong. The lock accepts ANY answer — it stamps the cache's log with yours and opens. The argument continues inside: the contents include the receipt from the original sale, which settles it, and nobody who found it ever checked.
- Payoff: Modest contents (one module and the receipt) plus the codex page of the four-way argument, now including you.
- Build seam: POI + scan-answer interaction + codex overlay
- Effort: data
- replaces: none (fills the low-stakes puzzle hole)

### SPX-B-08 — The Shadow Ring
- Kind: visual-setpiece
- Where: sector_eunomia_gulf, one flat-faced rock off the trade lane
- Trigger: pure geometry — once per sector day your hull's shadow can land inside a hand-painted target ring on the rock face; no prompt, no marker
- The player sees: A faded painted ring on a rock face, with a tiny dead lamp at its center. Fly so your shadow fills the ring at the right minute and the lamp lights for the day. On your tenth hit, fresh paint appears under the ring overnight: "STILL GOT IT."
- Payoff: A pure-flying ritual with no reward but the lamp, the paint, and the quiet bragging rights of knowing why the lamp is sometimes on.
- Build seam: shadow-geometry predicate (player pos vs sun bearing math) + one lamp channel + a hit counter in story.flags
- Effort: data+glue
- replaces: none (fills the pure-skill, zero-text hole in a thin sector)

### SPX-B-09 — The Anchor Light
- Kind: encounter
- Where: sector_sedna_dark, deep lanes
- Trigger: player hull below 25% integrity with heat at zero, more than 800 WU from any station; once per save
- The player sees: A dead tug you have never seen before settles alongside without a hail, takes your line if you offer it, and tows you — fast, competent, silent — to the nearest station ring. It carries one anchor light and no transponder. At the ring it casts off and is gone before you can pay or thank it.
- Payoff: Survival, and a standing mystery: its registration, checked later, lists a crew of zero and a home port that closed years ago. It can find you once per save. That is all it ever was.
- Build seam: numbered selfRegistered encounter keyed to a hull-state gate; tow via massline physics; registry line in contactHail copy
- Effort: data+glue
- replaces: none (fills the late-game rescue-mystery hole)

### SPX-B-10 — The Hand-Me-Down Route
- Kind: oneoff-mission
- Where: sector_tethys_junction, station bar
- Trigger: offered by a retiring hauler regular after the player has flown 10+ deliveries in the sector; uniqueOnce
- The player sees: She sells you her route — a sealed envelope of berths, timings and contacts, thirty years of it — for her retirement fund, exact change, no haggling. One page is torn out. "One of these berths will cheat you. Finding which one is the tuition. I never did."
- Payoff: A real route bonus (queue priority at the listed berths) plus the seeded cheat berth, which stings once and then becomes your best contact when you catch it; she retires to a berth you can visit, and asks how the route is doing.
- Build seam: numbered dock encounter + berth-priority flags read by docking; cheat berth seeded from programSeed
- Effort: data+glue
- replaces: none (fills the mid-game career-milestone hole)
