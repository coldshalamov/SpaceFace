## The experience to produce

Make the first run feel like a real arcade game immediately. Gunfire is useful, mass is tempting, threats are readable, and the first upgrades create visible possibilities. A player should lose because they understand a mistake, not because they never discovered the game’s controls or spent six menus getting to it.

## Round-by-round authored questions

These are experience targets over the existing planner, not ten new mission systems. Inspect the actual roster and behavior before binding a role; preserve supported identities and meaningful unlock timing. Do not invent an unadmitted specialist solely because a row uses a role word.

### Round 1 — Agency immediately

**Construction:** A useful gun target, a nearby physical opportunity and clear room to move. Keep native cohort/pressure; no tutorial-only parallel battle.

**What must be perceptible:** A clean kill and one understandable physical cause; no idle chain credit.

### Round 2 — The room is a weapon

**Construction:** Existing banks/anchors create an obvious flank or throw solution. Preserve moved terrain.

**What must be perceptible:** A reposition or bank action makes a visible difference before the round ends.

### Round 3 — Read a commitment

**Construction:** Debut one currently admitted charge/commitment role cleanly, then combine with familiar pressure.

**What must be perceptible:** The player sees a tell, moves deliberately and can punish the recovery.

### Round 4 — Choose an approach

**Construction:** A supported ranged role contests the easy route while another useful route stays open.

**What must be perceptible:** Flanking, cover or a physical displacement—not simply more HP—solves the pressure.

### Round 5 — First champion

**Construction:** Stage the native first boss with a readable arrival, first action, counterplay and death/result.

**What must be perceptible:** Its key behavior survives long enough to read; rewards follow the actual defeat.

### Round 6 — Use what you earned

**Construction:** Let the chosen improvement change a familiar problem; avoid immediately negating the build.

**What must be perceptible:** The player can explain how the upgrade affected this fight.

### Round 7 — Two familiar problems

**Construction:** Combine already learned approach roles without two simultaneous unreadable debuts.

**What must be perceptible:** Positioning separates the threats and creates a useful opening.

### Round 8 — Spend the environment

**Construction:** Ask the player to reposition/use limited surviving mass rather than reset a perfect court.

**What must be perceptible:** An alternate gun/position solution remains viable if a prop is gone.

### Round 9 — A build with an identity

**Construction:** Composition lets at least two current styles exploit different strengths.

**What must be perceptible:** No offered build is secretly invalidated by a compulsory specialist.

### Round 10 — A confident continuation

**Construction:** Consolidate learned rules, settle the existing zone/refit/record behavior and show the next reachable goal.

**What must be perceptible:** No invented extra boss or duplicate milestone reward; the player can continue or leave clearly.
