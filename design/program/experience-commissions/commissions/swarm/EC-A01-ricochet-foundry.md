## The room and the feeling

A working industrial room has been emptied just enough to let somebody do something irresponsible with its remaining machinery. Steel banks catch the light. A loose plate is immediately useful. When the first heavy impact lands, the room explains itself in a single ricochet. This is the front door to Swarm: direct, confident and generous with physical possibility.

**Native ladder seed at the reviewed baseline:** `73311`. Reconcile current source rather than overriding a deliberate later revision. This room is one of the seven ladder arenas; its EC label is not an arena ID.

## Build the physical room

- Compose a broad launch pocket, two angled bank corridors, an exposed center and a side refuge joined by at least two return paths. Start around 2–3 default combat-screen widths of meaningful fighting space, then tune to actual speed, camera and native spawn distance. Do not scatter bodies around a vast featureless annulus.
- Preserve and improve existing opening terrain work: baseline rounds 1–3 already specify growing authored throwing courts. Keep actual survivors and moved banks; do not replace them merely to satisfy a pristine layout. Existing eight/eleven/fourteen slot intentions are source context, not permission to exceed current caps.
- Use roughly four to six primary readable structural banks with a few useful loose masses inside the native terrain budget. The first nearby throwable must have an obvious target surface and enough clearance to use it. Leave a recognizable open traversal axis, but do not promise dynamic bodies can never cross it.
- A bank communicates its bounce behavior through normal/material/shape and actual contact. A fixed column and a loose plate should not look interchangeable. Match the room’s public preview; remove unrelated open-sector scenery/notifications only through existing mode presentation gates.

## The first fight and its development

- Opening: demonstrate a gun kill and invite a first throw without requiring a scripted trick.
- Development: a ranged enemy behind a bank creates a reason to use the room rather than chase into a blind corner.
- Pressure: crossing approaches force repositioning while the same bank becomes cover and a liability.
- Boss: use the native scheduled boss and its actual weak/counterplay structure. Keep enough useful mass and a retreat route; do not spawn a decorative giant that dies before its action reads.
- Aftermath: a clear gives a clean upgrade choice and a quieter room that retains meaningful physical history.

## Required physical opportunities

- A thrown body banks into a second threat and the score reflects the real cause.
- A repositioned plate changes both cover and the next useful bank shot.
- A powerful fit can traverse/clear the court faster, but does not become required for wave one.

## Presentation at gameplay scale

Hard industrial forms, warm process/worklight and cool navigation accents. Strong material response and impact direction, not orange fog. Keep gunfire and line/field silhouettes above decorative motion. The room must remain recognizable when the HUD is hidden.

## Shared mechanics back into Adventure

The native `adventureArenaSites.js` mapping places this law at the Forge in Vesta. Carry useful bank/material behavior there without copying arcade waves or rewards.

## Arena-specific acceptance

- Ordinary first launch and rounds 1–3; no auto-credit while idle.
- A bank leaves the fight boundary and returns: original identity survives, respecting current returning-bank work.
- A repair pickup is not consumed when unused; preserve the existing repair-cell fix.
- The last living enemy remains reachable and discoverable; the round does not time out by deleting it.

## Reject

Open space with a flat planet and a few rocks; a first-wave tutorial made of jargon; resetting thrown banks each round; art that promises a different room.
