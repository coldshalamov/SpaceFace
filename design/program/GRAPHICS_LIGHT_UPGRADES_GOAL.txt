Job: make SpaceFace look great with graphics work that costs the frame nothing, using the open
Blender (MCP) to design, the repo's Forge kit to build, and the live renderer to judge. Four
workflows, in ROI order. Each is the loop that gave the player hulls their lit identity trims on
2026-09-30 (`git log --grep="feat(forge)" --since=2026-09-30`): design on the real body, write the
recipe, look at the real picture, fix, publish, commit per body. Finish every body you can; if one
fights back, publish the others and name the exact problem for that one.

Read first: docs/visual-assets/LOOK.md (the look), tools/blender/forge/FORGE.md (the kit, look bar
items 5 and 7), .claude/skills/forge-graphics/SKILL.md, design/program/GRAPHICS_PROGRAM.md §3 (rules)
and §6 (how a change is judged). Reference results: tools/blender/forge/ships/kestrel.py (`glow_cyan`
band on 'Spine'), wasp.py (leading-edge channels), pelican.py (a lit rail on a sloped edge:
`lit_rail`), bastion.py (a lit outline along a plate edge with `region`), leviathan.py (flight-deck
edge lights and the lit hangar maw: the station vocabulary). Blender axes: +X nose, +Y port, +Z up.

=====================================================================================================
THE PERFORMANCE CONTRACT (why the trim pass was free, and what keeps the next passes free)
=====================================================================================================
A forge body is one draw call per finish (~10 for a ship), one shared 1024 texture set, no
per-frame code. The runtime never swaps a live ship to a lower LOD file (owner ruling 2026-09-29,
src/render/wholeShipLodPolicy.js: the frame is sacred), so LOD0 is what ships at EVERY zoom; the
in-file LOD toggles and greeble hiding still shave places when zoomed out. Therefore:
1. Cut into existing parts (`F.band`, `F.panel`) or add parts in finishes the body already uses.
   No new finish = no new draw call. A glow variant (`'glow_cyan.<name>': '#hex'`) is one extra
   material = ONE extra draw call for that body; allowed once per body (twice for a station),
   never more. Prefer the stock glows (`glow_cyan`, `glow_amber`, `glow_warm`, `glow_red`,
   `glow_green`) when the identity colour is close enough.
2. Never a new texture. Never noise. Never a decal. Everything is geometry plus a finish.
3. Nothing per frame. No animation, no scripts, no runtime edits under src/ (that is a separate
   packet with its own frame gate; do not open it here).
4. Triangle budgets hold at LOD0 (that is the shipped cost): ships 10–45k, props ≈15k, stations
   30–70k (trade hub 90k), landmarks 40–100k; LOD1 ≈ 40%, LOD2 ≈ 18%. `band()` re-slices the
   whole part: two hull rings cost the Drifter +5k, so read the `[forge] ... tris=` line every build.
   Mark greebles `s.detail = 1` (dropped at LOD2) or `s.detail = 2` (LOD0 only).
5. Lamps are thin: strips ≤ 0.2 m, rings ≤ 0.16 m, window boxes ≈ 0.3 × 0.2. Bloom spills tens of
   pixels on its own; a broad glow area reads as fog. Window rows stay `glow_warm` (lifted least).
6. Nothing floats, nothing clips. The chase camera finds every gap.
7. Gate per batch, not per body: before the first publish of a batch run
   `node scripts/probe-frame-solid.mjs --headless` on a quiet host and keep the .json; after the
   last publish run it again with `--compare=<that .json>`. Longest frame and p99 must not move.
   Per body, `node scripts/look-bench.mjs --cost --bodies=<id> --moods=arcade` prints GPU ms/frame
   for that body; a lit trim moves it by ~0.

=====================================================================================================
THE LOOP (every body, no exceptions)
=====================================================================================================
0. Preview: `blender -b --python tools/blender/forge/ships/<id>.py` (writes
   assets/ships/forge/preview/<file>.glb; <file> is `file` in tools/blender/forge/fleet.json).
1. Design in the OPEN Blender through the MCP. Do NOT call the recipe's build() there (it wipes
   the scene). Load the kit and the preview:
       exec(open(r'<repo>/tools/blender/forge/mcp_design_kit.py').read()); load('<file>.glb')
   `surface_z(x, y)` gives the skin height and normal under a point; `strip(...)`/`rail(...)`
   drop emissive candidates and report the gap to the skin (> 0.02 m = floating);
   `chase_view(path)` renders the 60° chase angle: open that PNG and look. Read positions off
   the placed objects (matrix_world) and copy them into the recipe. Skip the mock only for a plain
   `band` around a loft or along a flat plate: the renderer picture is the judge anyway.
2. Write it into the recipe (`F.band`, `F.boxes`, `F.box(..., rot=)`, `F.light`, `F.windows`).
   Add the glow variant to COLORS only when a stock glow is wrong for the identity. Keep every
   existing paint band. `band()` traps: the point must sit INSIDE the part (an offset on the wrong
   side of an edge cuts nothing and the build still succeeds; for a port edge wound bow→stern the
   outboard normal is (dy, -dx)/L); `region` bounds are shared with the mirrored cut, so never
   y-bound a `mirror=True` band; `facing=(0,0,1)` for anything the top camera must see.
3. Look at the real picture on the GPU:
       blender -b --python tools/blender/forge/ships/<id>.py
       SF_GL=d3d11 node scripts/fleet-look.mjs --file=assets/ships/forge/preview/<file>.glb --name=<id> --views=close,inspect --out=.devshots/gfx-<id>
   (`--views=place,close` for stations/landmarks; add `--sector=<sector_id>` to judge under a
   sector's rig). `close` is the gameplay camera. Fix until it reads as one confident line or one
   confident lit structure, never a christmas tree. Two or three cycles. The picture decides;
   the exit code proves nothing (Bastion cycle 1 built green and showed no line).
4. Publish: `BLENDER="C:/Program Files/Blender Foundation/Blender 5.1/blender.exe" node tools/blender/forge/publish.mjs <id>`
   (~2.5 min; rewrites EVERY render package because they embed the pilots hash — expected).
5. Checks: node scripts/check-parts-manifest.mjs · node scripts/check-render-package-pilots.mjs ·
   node test/model-truth-census.test.mjs. Player hulls only (workflow C): silhouettes + posters +
   hull tests as in GRAPHICS_PROGRAM.md §2.3.
6. Commit per body with exact pathspecs right after its publish and BEFORE the next publish
   (the next publish rewrites the packages again): the recipe, assets/ships/**,
   src/data/modelTruthCensus.json, src/render/renderPackageManifest.js (+ for player hulls:
   assets/ui/renders/hulls, src/data/shipSilhouettes.js). Check .git/MERGE_HEAD does not exist.
   Never run tree-wide reset/checkout/stash/clean; the tree carries other agents' live work.
7. Push after every few bodies. If master diverged, build the merge with
   `git merge-tree --write-tree HEAD origin/master` + `git commit-tree` and push the sha; never
   pull into the dirty tree (memory: push-diverged-master-without-touching-tree).
Machine notes: background shell jobs die at 10 minutes — chain publishes as single gated jobs,
detach poster renders with `Start-Process pwsh -File` (a -File script gets ONE positional arg:
pass a comma list). Never run two renders of the same body at once. `SF_GL=d3d11` is seconds per
frame; the default SwiftShader path is minutes.

WORKFLOW A — lit identity trims on the 25 NPC hulls (highest ROI; a straight repeat)
=====================================================================================================
Every contact should be readable by its lights before its colour, the way the player hulls now
are. Bodies (tools/blender/forge/ships/<id>.py; all `layout: npc`, one file with LOD0/1/2, no
posters or silhouettes):

      helios_lark helios_cradle helios_span helios_arclight apron_shuttle inspection_cutter
      helios_span_dmc helios_span_mts helios_span_reach
  Ashline raiders (angular, rust/black; identity = sodium orange `#ff8a2a`-ish, ONE variant
      `glow_cyan.sodium` shared by the family so the faction reads as one light):
      ashline_dart ashline_lode ashline_rig ashline_rig_corsair_blade
  Work fleet (chunky, safety colours; identity = hazard yellow, cf. the Yard Tug's T):
      ore_barge repair_tender salvage_cutter survey_pin rescue_lifter prospector_skiff
      scrap_sweeper volatiles_tanker   (yard_tug is done)
  Wasp faction variants (wasp_scn_patrol wasp_mts_escort wasp_free_militia): they are
      `build_variant` files over wasp.py, so the strips already exist — override the `'glow_cyan'`
      hex in the variant's colour dict to the faction's colour (one line each) and shoot to confirm.
Per hull: 1–3 thin strips or one lit ring where the design has an edge the top camera reads
(spine, sponson edge, intake lip, drive collar, cargo-rail edge). Faction rule: a family shares
one trim colour and one placement idea (raiders: a blade edge; civil: a cabin trim line; work
fleet: a hazard bar across the working end). Contrast rule from the Yard Tug: a lit colour on
the same paint colour vanishes — pick the family colour that contrasts with the surface it sits
on. Done when `SF_GL=d3d11 node scripts/fleet-look.mjs --fleet --views=close` shows a lit trim
on all 25 at the chase camera. One sitting per family; one commit per hull.

     place_dead_hulk place_ceres_bait_wreck place_ceres_grave_shard place_47a_rescue_capsule

  Skip: rocks (place_asteroid_*), place_debris_chunk, place_cargo_pod_standard, pod_cargo_container

Per place, in this order, stopping when it reads:
  a. Window rows: `F.windows(...)` / `F.boxes(..., 'glow_warm')` in the hundreds on anything
     crewed; one mesh per row (one draw). Dark hulks and derelicts get a few dead-cold windows and
     one live one.
  b. The dock mouth: a lit threshold bar and a static approach chase (alternating `glow_amber`
     and `glow_cyan` boxes, 0.2 × 0.12, every ~0.9 m) running into the maw, exactly as the
     Leviathan's flight deck does (`EdgeLight`/`CenterLight`/`Threshold`). Static boxes only;
     the runtime chase animation is another packet.
  c. One lit trim line in the place's occupation/faction colour along its biggest plan edge or
     ring (a refinery's stack collar, the gate ring's inner lip, a buoy's equator).
  d. Red port / green starboard `F.light` at the extremities, one amber beacon on the highest
     point, `work_lamp(..., halo=True)` on anything that works (halo, because aimed-down lamps
     show only their backs to the top camera).
  Places must keep using the base `paint` somewhere (the station check fails otherwise) and stay
  inside the live bounding box so the sockets land. Done when `--fleet --views=place` shows every
  place in tiers 1–3 lit and readable at the chase tilt; tier 4 as far as the sitting allows.

WORKFLOW C — close-zoom hero detail on the 15 player hulls (LOD0-only detail, no new finishes)
=====================================================================================================
At close zoom (58 WU, ship ≈ 450 px) the hulls read a little plain. Add a detail layer in finishes
the hull already draws (gunmetal, dark, paint2), so it costs triangles and nothing else:
  - hatch and access-panel outlines: `F.panel(..., inset=0.02, depth=-0.02)` in `dark`;
  - fastener/rivet rows along armour edges: `F.boxes` (one mesh) of 0.05 m studs in `gunmetal`;
  - a cockpit interior: a `dark` tub under the canopy with one thin `glow_warm` instrument strip
    (that one uses an existing glow, still no new draw);
  - replaceable-plate seams: thin `dark` bands with `region` so they do not wrap the whole loft;
  - pipe runs and cable trays along sponson roots: `F.beams` / `F.sweep` in `gunmetal`.
Mark all of it `s.detail = 2`. Budget: ≤ +6k triangles per hull and the hull stays under 45k
(runtime LOD swap is off, so this ships at chase zoom too — that is why the cap). The no-noise
rule still rules the chase picture: shoot `close` AND the default chase view; at 170 px the detail
must vanish into the form, not read as speckle. Then the player-hull tail: silhouettes, posters
(`SF_POSTER_SAMPLES=24`, ~3 min per hull, detached), `node --test test/hull-integrity.test.mjs
test/j07-hud-contract.test.mjs`. Bodies: hornet wasp pelican mule drifter ranger ironback bastion
atlas warden colossus leviathan saucer kestrel massline_express_liner.

=====================================================================================================
WORKFLOW D — sector mood pass (data, judged with the same picture; zero runtime cost)
=====================================================================================================
The same forged bodies should look different under each sector's light. Every mood value is
owned by src/data/lookMoods.js (LOOK_MOODS, LOOK_MOOD_BY_PROFILE) and every sector rig by
src/data/sectorVisualProfiles.js. These are uniforms; changing them costs nothing per frame.
Targets: Vesta forge hot (forge_heat), Pallas drift cold (cold_drift), Sker Haven sodium
(sodium_yard), Ceres belt dusty gold (dust_gold), Tethys/fringe neon noir, the anomaly void_signal.
Judge with `node scripts/look-bench.mjs --moods=<a,b> --bodies=kestrel,hornet,leviathan
--view=close` (one sheet, values side by side) and `SF_GL=d3d11 node scripts/fleet-look.mjs
--fleet --views=close --sector=<sector_id>`. Rules: a mood changes the colour relationship and the
darkness, never what a hull is made of (LOOK.md §5); two colours fight in every frame (key hue vs
edge hue); shadow sides take the mood's shadow colour, never grey; space stays black; nav
red/green and the HUD stay legible in every mood (accessibility contract). One commit per mood
with the two sheets named in the message; delete the sheets after (owner capture rule).

=====================================================================================================
Order, parallelism, done
=====================================================================================================
Run A first (it is the same trick that just worked, on more than twice the bodies), then B tiers
1–2, then C, then D, then B tiers 3–4. Parallel agents own one recipe file each and never touch
the kit, fleet.json, src/, manifests, publish or git; the lead looks at each agent's PNGs itself,
publishes, commits by pathspec, pushes (tools/blender/forge/briefs/). Three render-heavy agents is
the ceiling on one machine. Done when: every body in the workflow shows its light at the chase
camera in the fleet sheet, every publish check passes, the frame-solid compare shows no
regression, and everything is committed and pushed. Report per body in one line: what was lit,
where, the LOD0 triangle count before and after. Never report "blocked" without naming the exact
missing file.
