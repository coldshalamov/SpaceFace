## What is playable here

One neighboring Ceres service cut at sector-local (700, 1600). Dessa Kroll's radio stands by a receiving apron. Two separately colliding packaged rocks form a passable throat. One original canonical Ironback barge waits inside, with passive crew. One loose rock beside it can actually be displaced by a poor pull. Three original packaged worklights sit outside. No new model is made.

The pilot uses the ordinary Line to tow the barge straight through the physical throat, or takes the worklights in first. The opposite end remains open for reversal. A single rescue entry requires the original barge's current native pose inside the apron, released from all attachments, below the speed/spin limits for two continuous seconds, with the original radio still alive. Moving a UI pose or clicking a button cannot complete it. No credit, cargo, general mission completion or fictitious traffic count is awarded.

Each worklight remains a movable native body. Tow it to its labeled service position, release the Line, settle it and choose Seat. Seating changes fixed/dynamic posture at the actual position, without snapping, explicit velocity writes, repairing or replacing the original entity. The existing entity-physics-index and activity-reclassification APIs publish both posture changes to native physics; changing the body specification alone is insufficient. Unbolt reverses that posture. Existing warm point lights follow the originals. A rescue with three installed surviving lamps leaves a persistent rescue-tag fact at the radio; the current light count still falls if a lamp is lost. The visible rescue record is text/marker presentation, not a newly modeled tag.

The receiving apron and three lamp positions use the existing HUD goal-diamond style and explicitly say SERVICE MARK. They are navigation locations, not fake physical sockets. Text remains the primary, mute-friendly interaction. No new voiced performance is claimed.

## Verification and remaining acceptance

The production headless fixture begins with a Pelican outside the pocket, then uses ordinary raw input to fly in, aim and latch the original Ironback, tow and brake. The successful route moves that original 312.70 world units into the apron and records one released, slow arrival. It does not set body position or velocity after fixture setup. The route uses the normal Ceres simulation, including neighboring services; it is not a start-menu-to-site or rendered acceptance claim.

Separate staged Rapier force fixtures prove actual throat clearance, contact between the wrongly pulled barge and loose rock (22.93 units of rock displacement), the reverse opening, and all three physical lamp service positions. They are geometry/contact proofs, not ordinary player lamp-towing acceptance. Unit tests cover missing/dead/replaced/duplicate originals, counterfeit receipts, null tombstones, stale actions, in-place offer updates, native-vs-presentation pose, and repeated callbacks. Worker-channel tests cover initial and incremental lights and offer marks.

The native lamp regression separately verifies ordinary raw-input Line acquisition and motion, then deliberately stages a settled lamp at its service position to test Seat. It checks the real fixed collider over repeated normal ticks, full Continue, Unbolt back to a real dynamic collider, subsequent ordinary Line motion, and another Continue. The staged placement is not evidence of a complete ordinary lamp-seating route. This regression was added after independent review found that the initial implementation changed the specification but omitted the physics-membership publication, losing the collider after Seat and retaining the fixed body after Unbolt.
