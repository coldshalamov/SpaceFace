## Player-facing changes

- The selected original's source and destination stay visible when the radio is compacted. Seat remains available nearby, without reopening the long briefing.
- Bound original Line custody controls focus. A released load keeps its focus; native speed, spin, position, other custody, and missing native state determine the next instruction. Selection changes guidance only, not a physics or aim target.
- The existing Line instrument uses matching original-load guidance instead of generic throw-at-an-exit-target advice. Other contexts retain their instrument.
- A fast released lamp teaches a player-driven counter-pull: pass around its path, move behind its course, and reattach near the mark to brake. The existing Line physics does all work.
- A slow released lamp on a native constant-velocity intercept gets a conditional coast ETA. This predicts neither collisions nor success. Course changes revoke the estimate; Seat requires the actual original inside the original 8 m mark, <=1 m/s and <=0.2 rad/s, released and nearby.
- Telemetry updates do not regenerate decision IDs. Readiness, body life, and choices still revoke old callbacks. Live keyboard/gamepad bindings are used; touch uses action words.
- Temporary mode/venue interruption republishes a fresh decision instead of leaving the radio permanently blank.

No geometry, model, mass, placement threshold, physics force, pose, or velocity changes. Eight authored originals retain their current and saved poses. Rescue remains optional without lamps, and lamp work cannot award a barge receipt.

## Pacing: a verified faster physical route

Measured simulation times: pocket arrival 16.083 s; lamp attachment 23.867; brisk release 26.083; rear latch 39.250; load below 1 m/s 39.483; Seat 45.933; barge received 78.000. Pocket arrival to actual Seat is 29.85 s, versus 142.05 s for the original gentle-coast approach, a 79% reduction. The full single-light-and-barge route takes 61.917 s after pocket arrival, versus 173.85 s, about 64% faster. This is a measured raw-input route, not a hardware frame-performance or human-playtest claim.

The rejected gentle-coast method took 20.117 s of attached pull and 114.15 s of released coast. Nearly two minutes of passive waiting was not accepted as satisfactory pacing. Tight minimum-length reeling also produced sustained ~7.5 m/s orbit. Braking the tug alone cannot stop an undamped payload, and a single transport clamp has the same radial limitation. The better route uses the player's actual flight to change the braking direction; no new automatic receiver, invisible force, weakened gate or success snap was added.

The pilot must move behind early, avoid crossing the moving light, and attach against its course rather than from the side. Safe overshoot/correction remains part of the handling challenge. Headless raw-input automation establishes native feasibility and measured pacing, not comfortable human controls, visual legibility or all-three-light completion.

The earlier 163.23-second pull concerns a different 1,800-mass quarry plate. It is not Ninth Mouth lamp pacing.
