# Independent frame-only review

Native Claude Opus 5, max effort. Session `e8e4840e-f71a-49d4-b777-b3d4d050e16f`; Read/Glob only, no source or author notes. Reviewer inspected A-D, not the later shield supplement. Original wording follows; controller dispositions are in the linked PQ-190 receipt.

## A
Holding a sustained main-engine burn on a straight leftward heading through a debris field, with the ringed planet off to starboard. **Cue:** a continuous white-cored blue exhaust column trailing aft from the tail in all five frames while the hull's attitude never changes and the surrounding rocks drift steadily rightward.

## B
Running a surface pit-mining operation: a small yellow mining unit has driven out of the base cluster to the ore row on the right and is extracting iron. **Cue:** the "+1 Fe" floater inside an orange harvest ring over an ore node in frame 01, and the stock chip on the labelled node dropping from "Fe 8" to "Fe 6."

## C
Sitting at low relative speed inside a multi-ship engagement near the planet — the presumed player craft holds screen centre, drifting and slowly rotating, while other craft manoeuvre around it. **Cue:** a magenta muzzle flash with a dotted tracer string on the craft below the planet in 01, plus a red ring detonation lower-centre in 01–02.

## D
Under thrust with a line run out from the ship to a small circled point off to starboard, among scattered cargo containers and a loose formation of small craft. **Cue:** the beaded cable that is slack and wavy in 02 and pulls straight, evenly beaded and amber by 04, ending in a ring marker.

## Defects (highest impact first)

**1. You cannot tell which ship is yours, or how it is doing.** In C and D the player craft is one of a dozen near-identical grey-white hulls, 60–120 px, unlit against black, with no ownership ring, no facing indicator, no health or heat readout, and no HUD anywhere in the frame. In C the only usable clue is "the one nearest centre." B does have a HUD, but its eight bottom-bar buttons are unlabelled monochrome glyphs and the two meters (top-right capsule, bottom-right green bar) carry no legend or number. Across the set, player state is either absent or unnamed.

**2. Rock is the biggest surface in every shot and it carries no depth or value separation from gameplay objects.** In A, C and D the debris is hundreds of near-identical bright white low-poly chunks held at roughly the same screen size and the same brightness at every distance, so the field reads as scattered paper rather than volume — and in D the cargo containers sit at the same ~20 px size and nearly the same value as inert rock, so the objects that matter disappear into the noise. C and D compound it: the huge foreground boulders are matte, untextured grey-brown with a soft, unrelated key, so two rock materials share one volume. In B the opposite failure — one photographic granite tiled at building scale with hard straight seams every ~130 px, which reads as a stone wall, not an excavated asteroid.

**3. Effects read as flat 2D decals laid over the image, not lit volumes in it.** A's exhaust is a constant-brightness beam roughly four times the hull length that runs off the right edge and paints across the planet's disc at full intensity, with no taper, no falloff and no visible change across five frames — so throttle state is unreadable and depth collapses. Same overdraw at smaller scale in D. C's combat language is a flat red donut sprite for the detonation and a dotted string of points for weapon fire, neither of which reads as an event at this scale.

## What I cannot infer from these frames
Whether the HUD was deliberately hidden for the A/C/D captures. Whether the player fired or was hit in C — nothing visually ties the flash or the detonation to the centre craft. What D's line is: tow cable, tractor beam, drawn flight route and target-lock line are all consistent with what is shown, as is the amber burst on the lower-right craft being either a kill or a hard burn. Whether A/C/D are three style candidates or one world at three camera distances — the planet, rings, galaxy sprite and debris look identical, which argues for the latter, but B is a different scene entirely and is not comparable to them. Also uncertain, so not counted as a defect: in B the counter that changes (−2 on the labelled node) is about four tiles from where the "+1 Fe" credit pops, so the numbers may not tie to the visible action — that could equally be a selected-node readout I am misreading.
