## Outcome

A player recognises Ceres from thirty seconds of activity and Helios from thirty seconds of
different activity — not from a colour grade. Between incidents the world does boring jobs on
screen (inspections, transfers, repair, waiting, a tug moving something enormous), so that when
something goes wrong the change is legible. A handful of memorable one-offs make the universe
specific.

## Source

`design/VISION.md` Part II, "Every place needs a reason to exist", "The game needs ordinary life",
"Not everything needs to be systemically important." Existing seams: `src/systems/npcJobsRuntime.js`

`src/data/PLACE_REGISTRATION.md`, the everyday-space-kit dressing (`PQ-136`).

## How agents get this wrong

### Senior implementation direction for `.01` (2026-09-06)

Keep ordinary jobs as the source of life. Existing workers must visibly arrive, wait for a resource
or berth, transfer or repair, and depart; a tug must move a real load the player can interrupt.
Do not spawn a checklist of decorative actors to satisfy the five-minute capture. Observe the
reference site through its existing job states and preserve each job across departure/return.
The capture must distinguish quiet useful work from an incident without requiring HUD narration.

Read this before touching code. The integrator rejects a unit that matches any line (§1.6, §19).

- Identity by palette: a sector is recognised from activity with labels hidden.
- Filling quiet with events: ordinary life is the point of the quiet phase.
