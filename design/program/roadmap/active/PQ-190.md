## Outcome

The game's WORLD look — hulls, rocks, machines, effects, the sky — is stated in one sentence and proven
on one small scene before any fleet-wide pass: **solid, readable industrial forms with expressive
luminous machinery and effects** — less polished metal, more authored light with a reason. The
starter, one contrasting enemy, one useful solid object, one landmark, one industrial machine and a
representative set of action effects are approved together at the shipping camera in quiet flight,
dense combat and the rover, within the measured budget; then material families, effect recipes, LOD
rules and regional composition profiles are derived from what was approved, so the next asset task
executes an observed style instead of re-interpreting "premium". Screens, HUD and type belong to the
frontend direction (`design/FRONTEND_DIRECTION.md`, `PQ-187`/`PQ-188`), not to this packet; the two
must read as one game, so this packet's approval sheet is reviewed beside the direction lock's.

## How agents get this wrong

These are the ways a lazy first try fails this packet. Read them, then do the work as if the owner will play it tonight.

- Treating "glowy" as permission for transparent spheres or a radial-gradient blob as the whole effect: a stand-in is still a stand-in.
- Turning up a global bloom constant and calling it art direction: design the source radiance first (a thin bright core with structured falloff), reserve headroom for peaks.
- Flattening the rover or hiding detail to "stylize": stylization is authored form and light, not fewer polygons.
- Judging on an asset turntable: the proof is the mixed scene at the shipping camera, in motion, at density, with normal HUD.
- Restarting the whole catalog: six things, approved together, then derived rules. A whole-fleet remaster is what this packet prevents.
- Buying the look with performance: the witness runs before and after; no new top bucket.
- Touching screens or type: that is the frontend direction's.
