## Outcome

Every flyable player and NPC ship **except Hitch/Kestrel** looks like a manufactured 2026
A-list craft — Hitch-plus is the floor, not the finish. One leaf is one ship. Each leaf
runs the full-job review loop in
[`MODEL_ADVERSARIAL_REVIEW_WORKFLOW.md`](../../../../docs/visual-assets/MODEL_ADVERSARIAL_REVIEW_WORKFLOW.md)
at least five times (three valid full-model stills + three subagent reviews per cycle).
An overnight or “non-INFERENCE work in the build map” goal **keeps going** through every
remaining leaf. Hitch stays untouched. No ship self-promotes to accepted.

## Why one ship per leaf

Do not run a stage across the whole fleet (promote everyone, then rebuild everyone, then
review everyone). That ships unfinished work and forgets what the last ship taught.

Finish Hornet. Then Drifter. Then the next name on the list. Shared factory helpers may
improve, but only the claimed ship is rebuilt, judged, and wired in that leaf.

## A hardware ceiling on texture density, found 2026-08-18

MTX-17 wants 256-512 px/m at LOD0. On a 10.7 m ship that needs roughly a 2740 px map. The factory
builders shipped 512, which is about 34 px/m - an order of magnitude under, and the physical reason
every reviewer reads these hulls as unpainted plastic.

Hornet now **crashes Blender in `igc64.dll`, the Intel graphics compiler, during render setup**,
after all three LODs have already exported cleanly — so it is the still-render pass, not the export.

**Resolved by control run: it is the machine, not the build.**

This was first recorded as a texture-size ceiling — 2048 renders on a sparse body, crashes on the
full one. Wrong. The sequence:

| Run | Map sizes | Export | Render |
|---|---|---|---|
| cycle 57 | 2048 / 1024 / 512 | ok | **ok** |
| cycle 58 | 2048 / 1024 / 512 | ok | crash |
| cycle 58 retry | 2048 / 1024 / 512 | ok | crash |
| cycle 58 | 1024 / 512 / 512 | ok | crash |
| cycle 59 control | **512 / 512 / 512** — cycle 54's exact config | ok | crash |

The control is decisive. Cycle 54 rendered on that exact configuration a few hours earlier the same
day. A setup that worked, then stopped working, with no change between, is the environment. The
GPU had by then been rendering more or less continuously for hours alongside browser and game
sessions.

**Every run exported all three LODs correctly before dying**, so the fault is confined to the
still-render pass and never reaches the shipped asset.

**Do not lower texture sizes to chase this.** That was the wrong inference once already. Take the
stills on a fresh GPU state — a restart, or simply a machine that has not been rendering all day.

Two consequences for the campaign, not just for Hornet:

1. **The contract's density target is not currently reachable on this machine through this build
   path.** That is a hardware and pipeline fact, not an art decision, and no amount of authoring
   effort changes it. Either build on a machine that survives 2048 with full geometry, or split the
   texture bake out of the still-render pass so the two never hold memory at once.
2. **Texture size must ladder per LOD.** A flat raise inflated all three levels together to about
   192 MB for one ship - against 15.3 MB for the entire live player ship release, and larger than
   `place_station_trade_hub`, which already fails admission at 75 MB. `create_materials()` was
   called once and shared across LODs, which is what coupled them.
## The gate rule, learned the expensive way (2026-08-18)

Cycle 54's reviewers replaced opinion with a numeric gate. That was right: numbers caught the rod,
the cage and the capped drive that 52 cycles of adjectives had not. Cycle 56 then hit almost every
number **and produced a worse ship**, and the pattern in how it did that is the durable lesson:

> **It passed every gameable row and failed the one ungameable row.**

Each metric was a scalar measured on a projection, and any scalar on a projection can be satisfied
by adding mass anywhere in the frame — so mass arrived where it was cheapest, never where the ship
needed it:

| Metric | The shape that satisfied it | Why that defeats it |
|---|---|---|
| Silhouette height ≥240 px | a tall black tent parked on the spine | wanted three-dimensional massing, got a foreign object |
| Drive bell ≥95 px | a large flat annulus capping the nozzle | diameter rose while the readable engine went away |
| ≥3 openings with rims | topless boxes set **on** the skin | a box added on top is an attachment, not an aperture |
| Bore darker than casing | a pinhole in the flat plate | contrast *ratio* was gated, contrast *area* was not |
| Value split | — | **failed.** The only row that resisted gaming |

Nothing in the gate protected what already worked, so the build paid for the new mass by deleting
77% of the hull's triangles and the entire panel-seam vocabulary with them.

**Therefore every future gate on this packet, and on any PQ-050 leaf, must:**

1. **Anchor each number to its purpose.** Height is measured on the hull loft alone with dorsal
   attachments excluded, and no single element may contribute more than 25% of it. An opening's
   aperture plane must sit *below* the surrounding skin and show at least two interior depth steps.
   A bore must be ≥25% of mouth area, not merely darker. A bell must show ≥4 radial vanes.
2. **Constrain distribution, not just totals.** No single contiguous dark region may exceed 30% of
   all dark pixels — that is what turns "more dark" into shadow and recess across the airframe
   instead of one black lump.
3. **Floor against regression, hard.** `hullTriangles` and dorsal panel-seam count may not fall
   from the prior cycle. LOD0 bytes may not rise more than 25% without written justification.
4. **Keep the override.** Numbers are a floor, never a pass. No cycle promotes on numbers alone if
   any reviewer's plain read of `play_size.png` is worse than the prior cycle. That clause alone
   would have stopped cycle 56.

## Work breakdown

### One-ship contract

Every leaf does all of this for **exactly one** ship, then stops.

The technique law is
[`ADVANCED_MODEL_TECHNIQUE_CONTRACT.md`](../../../../docs/visual-assets/ADVANCED_MODEL_TECHNIQUE_CONTRACT.md).
The look-and-iterate law is
[`MODEL_ADVERSARIAL_REVIEW_WORKFLOW.md`](../../../../docs/visual-assets/MODEL_ADVERSARIAL_REVIEW_WORKFLOW.md).
A lofted hull with boxes, a tinted shared sheet, a successful factory script, a zoomed
gray crop, or “I did five iterations” by splitting the plan **does not close the leaf.**

1. Freeze identity. Read Hitch stills and that ship’s refs.
2. Attempt the **entire** MTX job (form, UV, bake, surface, raised-bar MTX-55–67, LOD).
3. Export. Take **three valid full-model** stills (three-quarter, starboard, rear). If
   you cannot see bow, stern, and most of the ship, recapture. Zoomed plates do not count.
4. Spawn three read-only subagents, one per still, with the workflow prompt. They must
   list every **obvious** defect without being asked “was anything obvious?”
5. Implement every REVISE plus anything obvious they or you still see.
6. Repeat steps 2–5 at least **five** times. Each repeat is a full attempt, not the next
   chapter of the plan. Continue toward ten if A-list is not met.
7. Fill `TECHNIQUE_LEDGER.json`. Clean up old cycle stills and leftover iter folders.
8. Wire **only this ship**. Commit. Campaign goals take the next ship immediately.

Automatic fail (do not wire):

- Factory loft + parented boxes as the shipped hull
- Shared Wasp/other-ship sheet with a MixRGB tint as the only albedo
- `shade_smooth()` on the whole object
- Bay cutter that never leaves the skin, or a liner that fills the hole
- Solid transmissive canopy brick
- Imagen / concept pixels used as normal, AO, or ORM
- Any mandatory MTX row missing, `claimed`, or `blocked`
- Fewer than five cycles with three valid stills and three subagent reports each
- Work split across cycles instead of a full-job attempt each time
- Old iter folders / failed-cycle stills left in the tree
- Self-review used instead of subagents

Wasp extra: hull ≥800 triangles on every LOD, or keep the previous live file.
