## Outcome

Every flyable body in the game — Hitch, the purchasable player hulls, NPC hostiles, traffic and the
work fleet — is built in one kit, [`tools/blender/forge/FORGE.md`](../../../../tools/blender/forge/FORGE.md),
so the fleet reads as one high-quality game: distinct plan silhouettes, three-value paint with one
identity colour, layered construction, lights, manufactured (never noisy) surfaces, nothing
floating, nothing bolted on.

## What changed (owner, 2026-09-27)

The owner reviewed the fleet: "simply ok", inconsistent, surfaces reading as leather or scraped metal,
a box stuck on Hitch's nose, some ships better than others. The earlier packet (one ship per leaf,
five adversarial cycles, MTX ledgers, Hitch frozen) spent dozens of cycles on Hornet without closing
it. The owner lifted the Hitch freeze and asked for the whole fleet to be brought to one bar.

Causes found and fixed at the system level:

- **Leather / scraped tin**: legacy exports without maps got runtime procedural noise normals and
  roughness (`proceduralPbrFallback.js`), UV-scale roughness noise (`installRoughnessBreakup`) and
  family multipliers on top of baked grime textures. Forge hulls carry `spacefaceFinish: forge-v1` and
  skip all of it.
- **Box on the nose**: fitted modules/weapons were bolted onto whole ships as generic kit parts at
  sockets, poking past the nose. Mounts now stay behind the authored nose, skip jobs the body already
  models, and forge bodies (integrated hardpoints) take no bolt-ons.
- **Inconsistency**: every hull now shares one texel density, one texture set (6 images for the
  whole fleet) and one material vocabulary.
