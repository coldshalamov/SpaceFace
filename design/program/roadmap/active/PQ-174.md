## Outcome

A stranger launches and immediately gets the arcade fantasy: bank a bright stream of shots, lead a
pack through a gap, drop a repulsion trap, web pursuers together and smash them into the room.
Clear a quick round, collect its money, buy a specific toy or save, then launch the next round.
The challenge is the farthest round. Fast failure and retry reveal new strategies. Difficulty grows
through numbers, mass, geometry, coordinated arrivals and specialists; existing enemy hull values
stay stable. Strong shooting and clever physics reinforce each other.

The [2026-09-10 arcade revision](../../../vision/CRUCIBLE_SURVIVAL_MASTER_PLAN.md#owner-directed-arcade-revision--2026-09-10)
is the product direction. Swarm accelerates acquisition; Adventure requires earning the same toys.

## How agents get this wrong

Read this before touching code. The integrator rejects a unit that matches any line (§1.6, §19).

- Making it 'fun' by adding enemies or waves: the count is a symptom lever; the fix is pacing, verbs and consequences (bars .00–.02).
- Making physics appear useful by nerfing direct fire or inflating stunt points. Its spatial consequence must earn its place.
- Treating a benchmark ranking as fun. Use a repeatable seed when diagnosing a specific change, and play the actual result.
- Reading 'difficulty' as hit points: a wave that is harder because enemies take longer to kill fails leaf .07 on sight.
- Shipping a boss with an invulnerable phase to force the mechanic instead of making the opportunity attractive.

## Non-goals

Make the five arenas express their laws before multiplying near-identical rooms. Armory upgrades may
improve useful stats as well as add verbs: cadence, handling and capacity can make a build distinctive.
Do not substitute a wall of tiny percentages for toys. Escalate through enemy combinations and
pressure rather than inflating hull points; retain one shared combat path for Crucible and Adventure.
