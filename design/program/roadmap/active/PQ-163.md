## Outcome

The onboarding rail already teaches thrust, brake, latch, winch, cut and dock in-world with a verb-then-silence rhythm; it never teaches boost, draw-to-fly or wells, and the first shove is impossible with the starter gun. This packet re-cuts the opening around the store-page sentence — light ships are ammunition, swing a rock, keep the speed — as a scripted-but-honest rescue: you are a tow rig, a scout is chasing a tug, you swing a rock into a derelict for scrap, fling the scout into an asteroid, grab the pod and run. Then the 47-A scene plays as the climax it already is.

## Appendix — a candidate sequence (audit 2026-09-05 §1.5; a candidate, never a script)

Integrate with the current opening, never a second one. The player first controls the actual starter
Hitch (`ship_kestrel` stays the internal id) somewhere with enough visible geometry to understand
movement; one nearby recoverable object offers a safe, useful first attachment — the goal is to take
an object somewhere and feel how the relation changes the ship, not to read a control chart. The first
complication admits at least two genuinely usable responses shown in geometry and behaviour (avoid,
negotiate through the existing interaction, or physically displace; cut across a route at a handling
cost or follow it), not two menu options with one outcome. The undertaking pays off visibly: hand over
a payload, see a local operation resume, receive an intelligible payment, try one meaningful
modification (a loaner or reversible early fitting can introduce a different Massline behaviour before
a large hull; the starter's Small utility slot against Medium heads is a real access constraint to
evaluate). Then an optional industrial opportunity with a near-term extraction choice and a
longer-term productive face — the player may keep flying. The opening succeeds when the player can
say what they did, why it worked, and one thing they want to try next; completion flags are supporting
evidence.
