## Player-visible result

After a witnessed civilian kill creates a wreck or survivor pod, a response group with at least two
live patrols now makes a stable visible choice. The nearest patrol holds over the victim anchor in a
defensive loiter while the remaining responders keep chasing the attacker. Motion alone does not
swap roles, repeated wreck or pod signals do not restart the order, and a real membership change can
produce one deterministic reassignment.

The production real-path scenario met B10a on seeds **4242** and **8008**: the split appeared in
**1.2 s** against the **10 s** limit, with three responders, one holder, two chasers, and exactly one
`law:witnessChoice` event. The silent control opened no incident and made no choice. This establishes
the runtime law; it does not substitute for the remaining headed capture of the visible maneuver.

## The driver the 2026-09-07 note said was owed now exists

## The law fires. Measured on the live route, seed 4242

- `law:incidentOpened` and `law:dispatchStarted` both fire.
- **Exactly one `law:witnessChoice`**, at **+1.63 s** after the kill, against the bar's 10 s.

That is B10a's *number* met on the adventure route, not only in the bench.

## And at the shipping camera you cannot see it

| What the bar asks to see | What the frames show |
|---|---|
| one responder **holds** at the wreck | the holder is on screen in every frame — but the **wreck it is holding at is 145–193 WU away from the player and drifting further**, so what a viewer sees is one small patrol pottering in empty space, not a ship standing over a body |
| one responder **pursues** the attacker | **never in frame, at any moment of a 23-second capture.** The two chasers are **2043 WU and 1955 WU** from the wreck when the choice is made and are still **947 WU and 894 WU** out at +22.7 s |

**THE EXACT REMAINING DEFECT, and it has a file and a line.** The two "pursuers" are not patrols
that were there and chose to leave. `_respondersFor` **spawns** them
(`src/systems/lawSecurity.js:999–1019`), and it places them with
`reserveArrivalPoint`, whose radius is

```js
// src/law/authorityResponse.js:45
const radius = Math.max(2000, Math.max(0, Number(jurisdictionRadius) || 0) + 700);
```

**A reserve responder is born no closer than 2000 WU from the incident.** That is the measurement,
exactly: at the moment the choice was made the two chasers stood at **2043 WU and 1955 WU** from the
wreck. At the 41–65 WU/s they actually fly, 2000 WU is **thirty to fifty seconds** of travel, against
a bar written in **ten**, and against a chase camera that shows about **200 WU**. The "pursue" half of
the split is therefore a ship that cannot be in the picture at any point in the window the bar
allows — not because the world is empty, but because the law's own reinforcement spawn radius is ten
times the camera and four times the clock.

Underneath that sits the second fact: Helios's reference pocket holds exactly **one** live lawful hull
(measured: nearest lawful hull 82 WU from the victim, and the only one in the sector at that moment),
so the *present* group cannot split at all — one ship cannot both hold and leave.

The two together say what a fix has to do: the hold/chase split must be made by hulls that were
already inside the incident's own `RESPONSE_CLEARANCE` (320 WU) when it opened, with spawned reserves
counted as reinforcement rather than as the visible choice — and the station's door needs a second
patrol on the same beat for there to be a choice to watch. That is a behaviour change to a live law
system that would move the bench numbers this receipt is built on, so it was **measured and reported,
not quietly changed**: `src/systems/lawSecurity.js` was not edited in this session (it also carries
~916 lines of another lane's uncommitted WANTED-net work, which is preserved untouched), and
`src/law/authorityResponse.js` was read only.
