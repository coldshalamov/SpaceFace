DONE PQ-176.01 — the drive gets you there, the thrusters do everything else.

DONE WHEN (verbatim): Two builds of one hull: fast-clumsy and nimble-slow, both viable on the Crucible bench; numbers printed.

WHAT I FOUND — Under the live V3 flight system, **nothing a player could fit changed how a hull turned.** `buildDerivedPropulsion` resolved the profile purely from the hull's authored `driveId`; the engine module moved the legacy stat block and the Travel Burn ceiling and stopped there. So there was no thruster half at all, and the drive half never reached the kernel: "how does my ship handle" was one authored profile with no player input in it.

WHAT I CHANGED — Split the propulsion budget in two. The DRIVE — the hull's own `driveId`, advanced by the engine module fitted to it — owns forward thrust and the speed the governor holds you at. A new MANOEUVRING BAY owns turn torque, strafe and brake. Every hull authors a stock set that is exactly neutral, and both references are the baseline tier-1 parts, so an unfitted hull, every NPC and the shipped starter fit are bit-identical. Three fittable sets ship with it: a Stripped RCS (the racer's choice, 0.68× turn for one tonne), a Vernier Cluster and a Gimbal Array. The bay is the LAST slot in build order, so a save written before it existed is simply one entry short and pads to the stock set — no migration, no save-version bump.

The yaw-rate CEILING follows the square root of the bay's authority rather than scaling with it: the bay owns torque, and the rate ceiling is the balance between that torque and the hull's own damping. Scaling it linearly turned an interceptor into a cursor.

WHAT YOU WILL FEEL — Two Hornets that are not the same ship. One arrives first and cannot do anything when it gets there; the other cannot run from a thing and never loses a knife fight. The fit screen names the part responsible on the line it changed: "Vernier Cluster M in the manoeuvring bay. That is why you turn 42 % harder than this hull's stock set."
