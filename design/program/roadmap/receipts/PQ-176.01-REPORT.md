DONE PQ-176.01 — the drive gets you there, the thrusters do everything else.

DONE WHEN (verbatim): Two builds of one hull: fast-clumsy and nimble-slow, both viable on the Crucible bench; numbers printed.

WHAT I FOUND — Under the live V3 flight system, **nothing a player could fit changed how a hull turned.** `buildDerivedPropulsion` resolved the profile purely from the hull's authored `driveId`; the engine module moved the legacy stat block and the Travel Burn ceiling and stopped there. So there was no thruster half at all, and the drive's thrust half never reached the kernel: "how does my ship handle" was one authored profile with no player input in it.

The second thing I found is the one that nearly shipped a regression, and it is worth the orchestrator's time. My first draft gave the drive the whole speed axis, `combatSpeed` included. That is the GOVERNED FIGHT CAP, and FEEL_CONTRACT B3 measures the screen crossing at exactly that cap ("at cruise the hull needs ≥ 1.2 s to cross the visible depth"); the camera is only allowed to open ABOVE it. A Warp Coil that lifts the cap therefore hands the camera a ship it cannot hold — and not hypothetically: it took the already-shipped `massline_rig` kit from 1.21 s to 0.89 s per crossing and the new Bolt kit to 0.74 s, re-breaking the fix PQ-137.03 landed. **Top speed is above the cap, not at it.** The kernel already says so in its own words at `resolveTravelCeiling`: "drive-tier upgrades hang off this."

WHAT I CHANGED — Split the propulsion budget in two. The DRIVE — the hull's own `driveId`, advanced by the engine module fitted to it — owns forward thrust, the boost ceiling and the Travel Burn ceiling: everything above the fight cap. It does not touch the fight cap, the precision envelope, or anything the bay owns. A new MANOEUVRING BAY owns turn torque, strafe and brake. Every hull authors a stock set that is exactly neutral, and both references are the baseline tier-1 parts, so an unfitted hull, every NPC and the shipped starter fit are bit-identical. Three fittable sets ship with it: a Stripped RCS (the racer's choice, 0.68× turn for one tonne), a Vernier Cluster and a Gimbal Array. The bay is the LAST slot in build order, so a save written before it existed is simply one entry short and pads to the stock set — no migration, no save-version bump.

Two guards ship with the law so the next drive tier cannot undo it: a catalog sweep asserting that **no engine, on any of the thirteen hulls, moves the governed fight cap**, and a bench bar that fails if the two split kits ever stop sharing it.

The yaw-rate CEILING follows the square root of the bay's authority rather than scaling with it: the bay owns torque, and the rate ceiling is the balance between that torque and the hull's own damping. Scaling it linearly turned an interceptor into a cursor.

The compatibility flight model gets the bay and only the bay. It already built `maxSpeed` from `eng.topSpeed` and `cruiseFrac` from `eng.accelMult`, so re-applying the drive multipliers there counted the same engine twice — a Warp Coil read 1.86× on top of a `maxSpeed` that was already 130-based. That is fixed; the legacy path's drive behaviour is exactly what it always was.

WHAT YOU WILL FEEL — Two Hornets that are not the same ship. One runs away from anything and cannot turn when it arrives; the other cannot escape a thing and never loses a knife fight. Both fight at the same speed, because the speed you fight at is the speed the screen was drawn for — what the drive buys you is everything above it. The fit screen names the part responsible on the line it changed: "Vernier Cluster M in the manoeuvring bay. That is why you turn 42 % harder than this hull's stock set."
