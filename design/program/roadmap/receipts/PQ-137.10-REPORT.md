# PQ-137.10 (flight half) — the flight bars now print real numbers

**Leaf:** PQ-137.10, FLIGHT lane, fun-convergence campaign, 2026-09-03.
**Bars touched:** B1, B2, B3 (`design/FEEL_CONTRACT.md` §B). **Instruments only — no game behaviour changed.**

---

## WHAT I FOUND

The three bars that describe how the ship *flies* had almost no real measurement behind them.

- **B2 "Nimble regime"** printed one of its three sentences. "From rest to cruise" and "turn radius at
  cruise" had no number at all — nothing in the game measured them, so no one could tell whether they
  were bad or how bad.
- **B3 "The fight stays on screen"** printed one number, and that number was **not a measurement**. It
  came from a bench scenario that types the ship's speed in by hand (195) and draws the rest of the
  curve with a sine wave. It never touches the game.
- **B1 "Earned speed is kept"** printed nothing from the game at all.
- The same fake scenario is also what makes **B8 "Draw-to-fly rips"** read as passing. It is not
  passing. It is unmeasured. (Fixing that is my leaf 3; the campaign master approved it.)

So the single most important thing in the game — the owner's *"it's like driving a bus"* — had one
honest number against it and six missing ones.

## WHAT I CHANGED

Nothing about how the ship flies. I built the **instruments**: three new bench scenarios that fly the
**real game** — the real physics, the real flight system, the real starter ship — on a fixed seed, and
print all seven sentences as numbers a person can read.

Every number now comes with proof that the real game produced it (the bench records which physics
backend was live and whether it actually came up). The screen size is read out of the real camera
code rather than typed in, and the "earned speed" test shoves the ship with a real physics impulse
instead of setting its speed by hand.

## WHAT YOU WILL FEEL

Nothing yet — on purpose. This leaf changes no gameplay. What it buys is that the next leaf
(PQ-137.03, the nimble regime) can say *"this number was 3.05 seconds and now it is 1.1"* instead of
*"it feels better"*.

What the instruments immediately told us is worth reading, though:

- **The starter ship takes 3.05 seconds to get up to fighting speed.** Three seconds. The contract
  asks for one and a half.
- **Its turning circle at fighting speed is nearly nine screens wide.** You cannot see your own turn.
- **It crosses the whole visible screen in 0.59 seconds.** A fight cannot stay on screen at that speed.
- **The camera barely opens when you go fast.** At three times fighting speed it has widened by 31 %;
  the contract asks for 150 %.
- **Earned speed is genuinely kept now** — 100 % of it, ten seconds later, hands off *and* with the
  throttle held down. That is the one thing in this group that is already right, and this is the first
  time it has been proven on the real game rather than in a unit test.

# Engineering appendix

**Hypothesis line.** *The bus-feel is a ratio problem (thrust : governed cruise : visible screen
depth). It cannot be fixed until all three sides of the ratio print a number from the real path on a
fixed seed; today only one of seven clauses does, and that one is derived from a fabricated scenario.*

**Files added**

| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `scripts/lib/bench/scenarios/feel.reversal_course.mjs` | B2's three clauses, Hitch + Wasp. Rest→cruise and turn radius run their own real-path tapes through `bootRealPath`; the 180° clause delegates to Motion Lab `runM3` so the number stays bit-comparable with the flight bench's existing 4.483 s. |
| `scripts/lib/bench/scenarios/feel.screen_crossing.mjs` | B3's five clauses. Owns `screenDepthWuAtSpeed`, which composes the live `CHASE_ZOOM_DEFAULT`, `resolveSpeedZoomFactor`, `resolveExceptionalSpeedZoomFactor` and `resolveExceptionalSpeed` exactly as `src/render/camera.js` does at its dynamic-zoom call site. |
| `scripts/lib/bench/scenarios/feel.earned_speed_kept.mjs` | B1's two clauses. Exits the cap at 2× cruise with a real `queuePhysicsImpulse` along the ship's own velocity, then holds for 600 ticks hands-off and again with forward held. |
| `test/fun-bench-flight-scenarios.test.mjs` | Shape, determinism (same seed → identical metrics), and "every B3 clause prints a finite number with a unit and a verdict". Assertion messages quote the vision sentence. |

**Clause definitions** (so a later leaf measures the same thing):

- *rest→cruise* — after an 18-tick settle, full forward held 900 ticks; `cruiseSpeed` is the speed on
  the final tick (the governed asymptote); the clause is the time from the first hold tick to the
  first tick at ≥ 0.95 × `cruiseSpeed`. Convergence is checked (speed rising < 0.5 WU/s over the last
  60 ticks) and reported.
- *180° velocity reversal* — `runM3({ seed, hullId }).metrics.hulls[hullId].velocity180TimeS`,
  i.e. `motionTelemetry.reversalBoxMetrics`' own definition, unchanged.
- *turn radius at cruise* — at cruise, full forward + full turn; the velocity heading is accumulated
  with `wrapAngle` until 90° is swept; `r = meanSpeed / (sweptRad / sweepS)`, then divided by the live
  at-cruise screen depth.
- *screen depth* — `2 · tan(fov/2) · CHASE_ZOOM_DEFAULT · speedZoomFactor · 0.72`, with `fov` from
  `state.settings.video.fov` and the speed-zoom factor from the live camera functions. Verified
  against the audit: at 195 WU/s with reference 195 it returns 114.10 WU → 0.5851 s, reproducing
  FEEL_CONTRACT §A7's "crosses the whole screen in 0.6 s".
- *camera band* — both exit speeds are **reached** by `queuePhysicsImpulse` (2× and 3× cruise), the
  depth at each is read from the live camera with `physicsEarned: true`, monotonicity is sampled over
  61 points between cruise and the 3× exit, and the hull share uses the live entity's `radius × 2`
  against `depth × 16/9` (the camera's own default aspect — a headless bench has no window).
- *earned speed kept* — fraction of the post-impulse exit speed still held 600 ticks later. Neither
  tape touches the brake.

**The load-bearing camera fact this leaf uncovered** (verified in source, not inferred, and now the
spine of `PQ-137.03`): `resolveExceptionalSpeedZoomFactor` returns
`SPEED_ZOOM_MAX + (PHYSICS_EARNED_SPEED_ZOOM_MAX − SPEED_ZOOM_MAX) · smoothstep(…)`, so the *largest
possible* depth growth above the cap is exactly `PHYSICS_EARNED_SPEED_ZOOM_MAX / SPEED_ZOOM_MAX` =
1.55 / 1.18 = **1.31×**. The measured 1.3136× at 3× cruise is therefore not a tuning miss — the
camera is already pinned at its arithmetic ceiling, and B3's "≥ 2.5× at 3× cruise" is unreachable
until that constant moves. Raising `SPEED_ZOOM_MAX` alone would make it *worse*.
