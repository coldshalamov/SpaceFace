## Honesty gaps

### 1. The kill is computed above a hard engine bound (this is why B6 is NOT MET)

`src/core/sg02DynamicBodyOwner.js:107` sets `MAX_CONTACT_DV = 40`. Every solver contact is clamped
to `min(dynamic mass) x MAX_CONTACT_DV` at `:1153`, and the per-pair merge re-clamps to the same
minimum at `:1196`, before `payload.impulse` ever reaches `collisionConsequences`. This is the same
bound `design/FEEL_CONTRACT.md` A6 documents in words.

For a Wasp (16) into a Warden (150) the ceiling is `16 x 40 = 640`. The proof constructs `1791.3`
from a hand-written elastic exchange at 105 WU/s closing — **2.80x the ceiling**. Side by side:

| | constructed 1791.3 | live ceiling 640 |
|---|---|---|
| light Δv | 111.96 | 40.00 |
| light impactDamage | 475.00 | 54.07 (tree) / 34.41 (HEAD) |
| light vs durability 260 | **dies** | **survives — ~5-8 committed contacts to kill** |
| heavy impactDamage | 7.69 | 0.00 (Δv 4.27 is under the `damageDeltaV` 8 threshold) |

The heavy half of the claim is therefore *stronger* than reported — at the real bound a heavy takes
literally zero from a light. The light half is not true at all.

The previous wording — "a constructed `direct_contact` receipt, not a headed Crucible rope-swing" —
reads as a statement about *capture method*. It is not. The input is unreachable, so no capture of
any kind would have reproduced it.

### 2. `TERRAIN_CRUMPLE_LAW` does not rescue it

`PQ-137.06` added a pre-solve closing-speed damage path so a bounded contact can still kill on
terrain. `src/combat/impulseKernel.js:281-282` gates it on
`worldSurface = surface === 'terrain' || surface === 'structure'`. Ship-into-ship contact is
`surface: 'craft'` and stays on the bounded-Δv energy proxy. There is no craft escape hatch.

### 3. The intended throw verb routes around this law and is weaker still

"Throw lights into it" is the massline whip. When a whip claims a contact,
`collisionConsequences.js:122` resolves it with `suppressCraftDamage = true`, and
`impulseKernel.js:268-271` zeroes `surfaceDamageMultiplier` for **both** bodies. The plain-contact
law this proof exercises is switched off on exactly the verb the leaf names. Two authored packets
replace it, and both are small:

| packet | goes to | law | on a Wasp thrown at ~60 WU/s |
|---|---|---|---|
| `combat.js:494 onWhipImpact` | the **struck** body (the heavy) | `min(45, momentum/1600)` | ≤ 45 of 1100 hull |
| `masslineImpactDamage.js:55 _onWhipImpact` | the **thrown** ship (the light) | `min(300, momentum x 0.65/250)` | **~2.5 of 260** |

Whip `momentum` is `mass x relSpeed` (`combat.js:39`: a 640-mass rock at 60 WU/s → 24 damage). A
thrown Wasp is `16 x 60 = 960` momentum, so its recoil is `960 x 0.0026 ≈ 2.5`. For recoil alone to
kill it the throw would need `relSpeed ≈ 6250 WU/s` — roughly fifty times a hull's top speed.

Both flags are on by default (`runtimeProfiles.js:15` `combat.whipDamage`, `:27`
`massline2.impactDamage`; both false in the deterministic golden profile). The third path,
`_onPhysicsImpact` tumble damage, early-returns on the shipped SG-02 route because
`consequenceKernelVersion === 1` (`masslineImpactDamage.js:71`) — it is a legacy fixture fallback.

So the thrown light takes about **1%** of its durability, not the ~180% the proof implies. Correcting
an earlier draft of this receipt: that recoil is small, not zero.

### 4. Number provenance: the printed numbers came from a dirty constant

`src/combat/impulseKernel.js` is dirty in this tree: `energyDamageScale` is `0.011` in the working
tree and `0.007` at HEAD. The `475` and `7.69` in the first pass were tree numbers labelled
"leftover" (= already in HEAD). HEAD gives `363.1` and `4.90`.

Separately, `475` is not an energy result at all — it is the mass-relative ceiling
(`maxDamage 190 x clamp(60/16) = 190 x 2.5`). It is the same 475 at any closing speed above the cap,
so it could never have shown how committed the contact was.

The masses and Pulse 84 *are* honest HEAD values: the dirty `ships.js` hunk touches `cargo` only and
the dirty `weapons.js` hunk removes `ammo` fields only. The test is green at both `0.007` and
`0.011`, so nothing here pins the dirty constant.

### 5. The player almost never meets a heavy

`src/data/enemies.js` has exactly one archetype on a mass-150+ hull: `dreadnought_boss`
(`ship_leviathan`, 600), used as the arena/Crucible boss and one world spawn. The two archetypes
that *sound* heavy are not: `bruiser_brawler` and `warden_escort` are both `ship_bastion` at
**mass 80**, under the leaf's own 150 floor. `heavyMotion` and the shrug law therefore never fire in
an ordinary fight. Faction presence rosters (`src/data/factions/*.js`) do list Warden/Atlas/Colossus
for ambient roles; whether those reach the player as combatants is not verified here.

### 6. The player never takes this damage (by ruling, not by defect)

`collisionConsequences.js:160` skips `state.playerId` before routing `impactDamage`. Consistent with
the owner's 2026-09-03 "my ship never knocked around" ruling, and correct — but it means a heavy is
an **obstacle** to the player and a **hazard** only to NPCs. "Hide behind it" has no supporting law.

### 7. Still absent entirely

No rope-swing capture, no Crucible scenario, no wake, no turn-radius consequence, no headed strip.
The turn-carry test asserts `shaped.forceLocal.forward >= turnCarryForward` where the function
assigns exactly `turnCarryForward` — it proves the policy is reachable and mass-gated, not that a
heavy reads as terrain in motion.

# Completion 2026-09-12 — the B6 half is now true on the route

NOT DONE PQ-140.01 — A heavy is moving terrain for the throw. The rope-swing half of the
done-when is still unmeasured, so this is `implemented`, not `done`.

DONE WHEN (verbatim): A rope-swing around a heavy works in the Crucible; a light thrown into it dies (B6).

WHAT I FOUND — The kill was real arithmetic the route could never produce. `test/pq-140-01-heavy-terrain.test.mjs` had already pinned the gap in its own header: the collision law would kill a light at a committed momentum, but the solver bounds every per-contact exchange to `mass x MAX_CONTACT_DV` before the kernel sees it, and craft-vs-craft damage was computed from that bounded number. So a Wasp flown into an Atlas took **the identical 54.07 points of damage at 52 WU/s, at 80 WU/s and at 105 WU/s** and survived all three — the exact saturation PQ-137.06 removed for rock, still in place for the biggest hull in the game. A heavy was terrain in mass and not in consequence.

WHAT I CHANGED — Two things, both small, both in code that already owned the behaviour.

**One line of law** in the kernel: a craft of mass 150 or more is terrain for whatever meets it, so its contact reads damage from the pre-solve closing speed instead of the solver's rate limit. 150 is the roster's own heavy boundary — every mass-150+ hull carries a `heavyMotion` turn-carry profile and nothing lighter does. The solver bound is untouched and still bounds momentum; the law is inverse in the victim's mass, so the heavy on the other side of the same contact takes nothing. No drag, no clamp on given momentum, no NPC gyro, no hit points moved, no new damage rule.

**And the whip's craft-damage suppression, which is honesty gap 3 above.** `collisionConsequences._onWhipImpact` zeroed baseline craft damage for both bodies so a whip contact carried exactly the two authored whip packets — and on the thrown hull that packet is `momentum x 0.65 / 250`, about **2.5 points on a 260-point Wasp**. That made "throw lights into it", the packet's own headline verb, the one route where a heavy stayed soft. Terrain contact is never a suppression case: a hull that meets rock at speed dies whether or not a rope put it there. So when either body is a heavy, the whip no longer suppresses. The two whip packets are untouched, and the heavy's own side of the contact still computes zero because its Δv sits under the damage threshold, so the only thing this admits is the thrown light's crumple.

WHAT YOU WILL FEEL — Shoving a light hostile into a heavy is now a way to kill it, the same way a rock is. Brushing a heavy still costs nothing, and two lights bumping is exactly what it was, so nothing you fly changed shape — the biggest hulls just stopped being soft.
