# PQ-139.01 — Sound tells weight

Lane IMPACT · fun-convergence campaign · seed 4242 · 2026-09-04

## WHAT I FOUND

Every collision in the game played **the same sound**. Literally one recipe, at one fixed pitch,
with a volume that only cared how hard the hit was and never once asked how *heavy* the things were.

A scout brushing a rock and a loaded freighter putting its shoulder through a station dock were the
same noise. The physics knew both masses. The audio never asked.

## WHAT I CHANGED

Collision sound is now decided by **weight and by momentum, separately**:

- **Pitch is set by the heavier of the two things involved.** Heavy sounds low. Light sounds high.
  Stations and asteroids do not have a mass in the sim because nothing can move them — so they are
  given a heavy acoustic mass instead, and now read as the immovable objects they are rather than as
  weightless props.
- **Loudness is set by the momentum actually traded**, on a square-root curve so a kiss is quiet and
  a broadside is enormous without the middle of the range going flat.
- Four tiers — kiss, knock, slam, broadside — each riding a sound that **already exists in the
  game**. No new audio content was invented. (Recorded impact samples are a later packet's job; this
  is the ladder they will slot into.)

## WHAT YOU WILL FEEL

You will hear how big the thing you hit was, and how hard you hit it, without looking. A scrape
against a rock ticks; a freighter hitting a station is a low, loud, heavy event that sounds like
tonnage.

## THE NUMBERS

The bar (`design/FEEL_CONTRACT.md` B9): a scout kissing a rock and a freighter broadsiding a station
must differ by **at least one octave of pitch** and **at least 12 dB of loudness**.

| case | sound | pitch | volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| scout kisses a rock (mass 16, momentum 40) | `sfx_dock_clunk` | 1.057 | 0.087 |
| freighter broadsides a station (mass 200, momentum 24000) | `sfx_explosion_large` | 0.500 | 0.950 |

**Pitch difference: 1.08 octaves** (bar wants ≥ 1.00) — was **0**.
**Loudness difference: 20.8 dB** (bar wants ≥ 12) — was **0**.

Both were exactly zero before: one recipe, one fixed pitch of 0.8, for everything.

(The receipt JSON beside this file reports the same pair as **1.08 octaves / 18.4 dB**. Same
function, slightly gentler kiss: the bench scenario's scout touch trades more momentum than the
test's, so its clunk is 0.114 loud instead of 0.087 and the gap narrows by 2.4 dB. Both clear the
bar; the bench number is the conservative one and is the one to quote if only one is quoted.)

Pitch also falls **strictly** as mass rises across 16 / 32 / 90 / 200 / 400, and volume never falls
as momentum rises across 40 / 400 / 4000 / 24000 — so it is a real ladder, not two hand-picked
endpoints.

## Engineering appendix

**Hypothesis.** `audio._onCollision` plays one recipe at `rate: 0.8` with
`gain: clamp(impulse * 0.3, 0.15, 0.7)` and reads neither participant's mass, so bar B9's audio
clause reads 0 octaves / 0 dB; deriving playback rate from the heavier participant's acoustic mass
and gain from the exchanged momentum `dp` should move it past ≥ 1 octave and ≥ 12 dB.

**Files changed**

- `src/audio/audioSystem.js` — new pure exported `resolveCollisionCue(input)` and a frozen
  `COLLISION_CUE` tuning table; `collisionAcousticMass()` maps immovable `station`/`asteroid` bodies
  to nominal heavy masses; a four-tier recipe ladder over existing recipe ids
  (`sfx_dock_clunk` / `sfx_mining_impact` / `sfx_explosion_small` / `sfx_explosion_large`);
  `_onCollision` now resolves entities from `state.entities` and plays the resolved cue. No
  `Math.random`, no `Date.now`, no DOM, no `this` in the pure function.
- `test/audio-collision-weight.test.mjs` (new) — the bar case (≥ 1 octave, ≥ 12 dB, printed to
  stdout), strict monotonicity of pitch in mass, monotonicity of gain in momentum, bounded under
  absurd input, every tier resolves to a recipe that exists in `AUDIO_RECIPE_BY_ID`, determinism,
  and `_onCollision` routing including the missing-entity and null-payload paths. Every assertion
  message quotes the vision sentence "Sound tells weight".
