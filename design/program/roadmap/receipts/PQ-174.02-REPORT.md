## Controller amendment, 2026-09-10

Verified before integration. Three notes, one of which corrects this receipt's headline.

**1. The gun was genuinely not nerfed, and that is the load-bearing check here.** The Pulse's
per-seed score list is byte-identical before and after — 165 median, the same twenty rows. Its
damage, rate of fire and impulse are untouched in the catalogue. A player who prefers shooting has
exactly the run tonight that they had yesterday; only the leaderboard moved. That is what the
packet's rejection line asks for, and it holds.

**2. `DIRECT_SHOVE_SCORE_CAP = 4` is a SCORE factor, not a force clamp.** It is used as
`Math.min(DIRECT_SHOVE_SCORE_CAP, forceTableScoreFactor(weaponId))`. Nothing clamps momentum, adds
drag, or touches a hull number. Checked because "capped at 4x" is the exact phrasing that would
describe a law violation if it were applied to force instead of score.

**3. Prefer the LIVE number to the estimator's 13.15x.** Re-run at integration, the live board gives
**physics median 1040 against gun 410 — a ratio of 2.537** on the two seeds that loaded, which the
test asserts against its `>= 2` bar and passes. The 13.15x figure comes from
`estimateBoardScore` over bench kill-cause traces, and this receipt's own UNPROVEN section says why
it runs hot: the estimator pays every physics-attributed kill at the shove table because the kit
carries a shove, where live play requires `playerCaused` or recent shove memory.

**2.5x is the better headline and it is also the better outcome.** At 13x a gun run would not be a
choice at all; at 2.5x mass clearly wins while shooting stays a real way to play, which is exactly
the bargain the packet asks for. The estimator stays useful as a directional dashboard; it is not
the measurement.
