# PQ-137.03 — the nimble regime: the ship stops flying like a bus

**Leaf:** PQ-137.03, FLIGHT lane, fun-convergence campaign, 2026-09-04.
**Bars:** B2 (nimble regime) now fully met; B3 (the fight stays on screen) partly met, with the
remaining two clauses blocked on one line in another lane's file; B1 (earned speed kept) unchanged.

---

## WHAT I FOUND

The complaint was *"it's like driving a bus"* and *"I don't feel like I'm in control of anything."*
That is not a missing feature. It is three numbers that were wrong relative to each other:

- the ship's **fighting speed** was 195 units a second,
- its **push** was 44 units a second squared,
- and the **screen** showed about 114 units of world.

A ship's turning circle is speed squared divided by push. 195 x 195 / 44 = **870 units** — nearly
**nine screens wide**. You could not see your own turn. The ship crossed the entire visible screen in
**0.59 seconds**, took **3.05 seconds** to get up to fighting speed, and **4.48 seconds** to turn
around. Every one of those is the same ratio problem wearing a different hat.

The camera was making it worse in a way nobody had noticed. It sizes the frame from a *legacy*
statistic (`player.maxSpeed`, 172 for the starter) rather than from the speed the ship is actually
governed at. The frame was therefore already fully open at cruise — so simply slowing the ship down
would have made the camera **close in** exactly where the fight happens.

## WHAT I CHANGED

**One rescale of the whole ship catalogue, plus the camera that frames it.** No new content, no new
mode, no flag, and nothing that takes away speed the player earned.

- Every drive's **fighting speed roughly halved** and its **push went up about 2.6x**. The starter
  (Hitch) now cruises at 95 with 115 of push; the Wasp at 105 with 143. Every other hull moved with
  them, so a freighter is still a freighter and a fighter is still a fighter.
- The **throttle servo** was the hidden brake on "response starts instantly": it is a first-order
  lag, and at 0.95 s it needed nearly three seconds to settle no matter how much thrust the ship had.
  It is now 0.30 s.
- **Cross-map travel is untouched.** Halving fighting speed would have halved travel too, because the
  travel ceiling was derived from it. Every drive now states its travel ceiling explicitly at exactly
  today's number. **Combat got tighter; getting somewhere takes exactly as long as it did.**
- The **camera now sizes the frame from the ship's governed speed**, and the frame opens further
  when you go fast (the at-cruise frame is ~14 % wider; the above-cap opening ceiling went from 1.31x
  to a designed ~2.6x).

## WHAT YOU WILL FEEL

- **The turn is on screen.** The starter's turning circle went from nine screens wide to **0.95 of
  one screen**. You can see the whole manoeuvre you are flying.
- **It answers immediately.** Rest to fighting speed: **3.05 s → 0.67 s**. Turning around:
  **4.48 s → 1.58 s**.
- **The fight stays in frame.** Crossing the visible screen at fighting speed: **0.59 s → 1.37 s**.
  You have more than twice as long to read what is happening.
- **The Wasp still feels like a different ship** — it gets going 43 % quicker, turns around 42 %
  faster, and turns 21 % tighter than the starter.
- **Earned speed is still yours.** Shove the ship to twice cruise and ten seconds later, hands off
  *or* with the throttle held down, it still has **100 %** of it.

### The two clauses that did not pass, and exactly why

The camera does **not** compute its own "you are going faster than your engines can push you"
signal. It reads a shared record, and the only writer of that record — `src/render/feel.js:421` —
still keys it to the same legacy statistic the camera used to use. So after this change the frame
sizes correctly at cruise but barely opens above it: at twice cruise it opens **1.3 %** where the
contract asks for 50 %.

It is **one line and one import** in a file this lane does not own. It is filed as a cross-lane
request, and the bench now models it honestly — the instrument reads two references because the live
camera reads two, and it prints the failing number rather than the flattering one. An earlier draft
of this leaf had the camera compute the signal itself; that turned
`scripts/check-chase-camera-juice.mjs` cycle 15 red (it explicitly forbids exactly that, to keep one
owner for the record), so it was reverted. **The instrument was made honest before the leaf was
committed, not after.**

**A stale instrument this leaf uncovered.** `motionTelemetry.velocityHeadingChangeTime` starts its
clock at the first sample above 2.2 WU/s after the turn-burn mark, and M3's turn phase yaws with no
thrust. The old sluggish hull coasted through the turn well above that floor; the rescaled hull kills
its own speed during the yaw, so every in-turn sample is skipped and the metric returns `null` — it
reports "no reversal" for a ship that reverses in 1.58 s. `motionTelemetry.js` belongs to another
lane, so the fix here was to measure the same definition (velocity heading rotated ≥ 0.88 π,
speed floor 2.2 WU/s) on this lane's own tape with the clock starting when the reversal is commanded.

**`check-chase-camera-juice.mjs` cycle 15** is a source-text guard:
`/readOwnedExceptionalSpeed\(state\)/.test(camSrc) && !/_flightFrame\.governor[\s\S]*physicsEarned/.test(camSrc)`
— the camera must consume the owner-bound record and must not derive its own physics-earned
provenance. The first implementation violated it; it was reverted rather than the check relaxed.
