# PQ-137.06 step 1 — terrain is lethal? Measured on the real path. It is not lethal at all.

**Bar B6** — *"A light hostile meeting rock at ≥ 50 % of cruise loses ≥ 60 % of hull and its helm;
at ≥ 75 % of cruise it dies. A heavy at the same speed loses ≤ 15 % and keeps its helm."*

This step builds the instrument and prints the honest BEFORE. It changes no game code.

## WHAT I FOUND

**The old B6 number came from a spreadsheet cell, not from the game.** The bench's terrain-slam
"scenario" was a block of arithmetic: multiply a typed-in ship mass by a typed-in speed, times a
typed-in damage scale, against a typed-in hull total, then declare the bar met. No ship, no rock, no
collision, no physics. B6 has been reported as passing its helm clause on that basis.

Run on the real game, the bar does not merely fail — **it cannot be reached.**

**Nothing ever loses any hull to a rock.** A light fighter flown into an asteroid at half its cruise
speed, the same fighter at three-quarters, and a heavy hauler at the same speed: all three lose the
helm, none loses a single point of hull, and none dies. The collision does produce real damage —
65.9 points for the fighter, 95 for the hauler — and **the shields absorb every point of it.** The
fighter's shield goes from 110 to 55.9 and its hull never moves.

**And it is the same crash at every speed.** The collision solver has a safety limit that caps how
much velocity one contact may exchange in a tick. That capped number is then handed to the damage
rule *as if it were the speed of the crash*. So a fighter that hits rock at 52 WU/s and a fighter
that hits at 80 WU/s deliver the identical impulse — 640 in both cases, which is exactly the
fighter's mass times the cap — and take the identical damage. The IMPACT lane measured the same
thing from the other side: 150 WU/s and 400 WU/s produce indistinguishable collisions.

The arithmetic is worth stating plainly, because it decides step 2. With the cap in place, the most
damage a rock can ever do to that fighter is about 66 points against a 150-point hull, before
shields. **Even with no shields at all, a light hostile can never die on a wall.** The speed that B6
is written in is erased before damage is computed.

## WHAT I CHANGED

Nothing in the game. One new bench file that measures it:
`scripts/lib/bench/scenarios/feel.terrain_slam.mjs`.

It boots the real game headless and **flies** a real hostile into a real rock. The ship is given a
throttle — half, or three-quarters — and its own engine governor brings it to that fraction of its
cruise speed down a 520-unit runway; the speed at the moment of impact is then read off the hull, not
written onto it. Commanded 52.5, flown 52.5. Commanded 79.8, flown 79.8.

That took two passes. The first version teleported the ship next to the rock and simply wrote a
velocity onto it with the flight model switched off. A reviewer that did not write it (Kimi K3) named
it exactly: *"the measurement of consequences is real; the flight is not."* A scripted velocity is a
made-up number even when everything downstream of it is honest, so it was replaced with a real
approach under the ship's own drive.

The same review found four other things now fixed: the run could measure a collision with the wrong
object and print it under the right name; it could report a plausible-looking closing speed after a
crash that never happened; it never checked that the physics engine's contact reporting was switched
on; and the headline "bar met" checked only the light ship's half of B6, so a run that vaporised the
heavy hauler could still have printed a pass.

## WHAT YOU WILL FEEL

Nothing yet — this step only tells the truth. What it says is: **flying an enemy into an asteroid at
speed does nothing to it except make it wobble.** The rock takes its shields down by half and leaves
the hull untouched, and it does that identically whether you shove them in gently or at full speed.
"Slam them into asteroids" is currently not a way to kill anything.

# Engineering appendix

**Hypothesis line.** *B6's "helm met, damage ≤ 25 %" comes from a closed-form arithmetic block in
`verbBench.mjs`, not from SpaceFace; measured on the real contact path the hull loss will differ,
because the damage input is the POST-BOUND contact impulse (capped at `mass × MAX_CONTACT_DV` in
`sg02DynamicBodyOwner._captureContactImpacts`) rather than the pre-solve closing speed.* **Confirmed,
and worse than the hypothesis: the hull loss is zero and the bar is unreachable at any speed.**

**File.** `scripts/lib/bench/scenarios/feel.terrain_slam.mjs` (new) — id `feel.terrain_slam`, which
REPLACES the inline arithmetic block of the same id by the drop-in module contract. No shared bench
file was edited. Bars are fed through the generic `metrics.bars` seam.

**The causal chain, quoted.**
- `src/core/sg02DynamicBodyOwner.js:90` — `const MAX_CONTACT_DV = 40;`
- `…:890–893` — `dynamicCaps.push(effectiveMass(rec) * MAX_CONTACT_DV)`, then
  `const boundedImpulse = Math.min(rawImpulse, Math.min(...dynamicCaps));` (the per-pair merge at
  `:929` re-clamps to the same ceiling).
- `src/systems/collisionConsequences.js:106` —
  `const exchangedMomentum = Math.max(0, finite(payload.impulse, payload.dp));`
- `src/combat/impulseKernel.js:138` — `const deltaV = exchangedMomentum / mass;` → **always ≤ 40**.
- `…:151–168` — `overDamageSpeed = max(0, deltaV − 8)`; `energyProxy = 0.5·mass·overDamageSpeed²`;
  `impactDamage = clamp(energyProxy · 0.007 · SURFACE_DAMAGE_MULTIPLIER.terrain (1.15), 0, cap)`.
  Ceiling for mass 16: `0.5·16·32²·0.007·1.15 = 65.9` against a 150 hull.
- `src/combat/damage.js:87–94` — kinetic shield multiplier `0.82` (`src/data/combatDefs.js:36`);
  Wasp shield 110, Atlas shield 300 (`src/data/ships.js`), so the whole packet is absorbed and
  nothing reaches armour or hull.

**A tuning comment in the codebase is falsified by this.** `impulseKernel.js:42` reads *"light
(mass ~16) at a committed concussion-stack deltaV (~50) is near-lethal on terrain."* A per-contact
ΔV of 50 is unreachable: every receipt is capped at `mass × 40`. The number that rule was tuned
against cannot occur on the real path.

**Adversarial review.** Kimi K3 xhigh (`cline-pass` route) reviewed the module without having
written it and returned five findings; all five are addressed in the committed version:
1. *"It teleports and shoves; the header's 'flies a real NPC' is generous."* — Replaced with a real
   flown approach through `data.intent` and the assisted governor; `flownSpeed` vs `commandedSpeed`
   is now published per case so the case labels are auditable.
2. *"It confirms the post-bound impulse is the damage input."* — Quoted above; now published as
   `contactDeltaV` per case, which reads 40 in every row.
3. *"Shields absorb it, and the metrics never show it."* — `consequenceImpactDamage`,
   `shieldBefore/After` and `shieldAbsorbed` are now top-level metrics per case. Before this, a run
   where the shield ate 54 points and a run where nothing happened printed the identical zero.
4. *"Guards are one-sided: it catches a bodiless hostile but not a missing rock, a missed approach,
   or contact capture switched off."* — The run now throws unless the first impact NAMES THE ROCK
   (the "any body it touched" fallback is removed), and asserts `contactCaptureEnabled` and
   `backend === 'rapier-dynamic'` alongside `sg02Ready`.
5. *"`barMet` under-checks its own bar; a run that vaporised the Atlas would print true."* — `barMet`
   now requires all five clauses including the heavy's hull ceiling and its helm.
   Also fixed from the same review: `heavy75ClosingRatio` divided the Atlas's closing speed by the
   *Wasp's* cruise, and `impactDamage` was a hull delta wearing the kernel's field name (now
   `hullLost`).

**Determinism.** Two consecutive runs of seed 4242 produce byte-identical metrics.

**Checks run.** The module is bench-only; no `src/` file was touched. `node --test
test/fun-bench.test.mjs` → 7 pass / 0 fail with the module present. Golden expected to move: **none.**

**Not done in this step, by design.** `test/weapon-impulse-consequence.test.mjs` gains the heavy
terrain case in step 2, alongside the damage law, so the assertion and the behaviour it pins land
together rather than red-then-green.
