# PQ-138.03 — Wrecks drift and tumble ("dead man's mass")

## WHAT I FOUND

When you killed a ship, the wreck it left behind stopped dead in the air. Not slowed — stopped, on
the frame it died, wherever it happened to be. It also weighed a million tonnes, which in this game
means "immovable": you could fly into it at full speed and it would not budge a centimetre. A ship
that was doing 120 units a second one frame ago became a wall the next.

That is why kills felt weightless. The moment of death, the one moment the whole fight was for,
threw away everything the fight had built up — all the speed, all the spin, all the momentum you
had just spent a minute working against.

## WHAT I CHANGED

A wreck now keeps what the ship had. When a ship dies, the game writes down how fast it was going,
which way it was spinning and what it weighed, and the wreck is created carrying all three. It
drifts on the course the ship was flying. It tumbles at the rate the ship was turning. It weighs
what the ship weighed, so you can shove it — with your hull, with a shot, with anything.

It also remembers. If you leave the sector and come back, the wreck is where it drifted to, not
teleported back to the spot where the ship died.

Nothing was added to the game to make this happen. The physics already knew how to fly a wreck; the
wreck was simply never told it had anywhere to go.

## WHAT YOU WILL FEEL

You kill a hauler at cruise and the corpse keeps coming at you. You have to move. The wreck is now
part of the fight for the next couple of seconds instead of scenery that appears where the ship was.
And you can push it — into someone, out of your way, off a station.

### Engineering appendix

- **Hypothesis line.** When a ship dies, `aftermathWrecks.makeMarker` records no velocity and
  `_specForMarker` hardcodes `mass: 1e6` with no `vel`/`angVel`, which breaks the vision sentence
  "wrecks are bodies, not decorations"; recording the victim's motion on the marker and replaying it
  onto the spec should move bar B10d from 0 % to ≥ 80 % of the victim's speed.
- **Files:** `src/systems/aftermathWrecks.js`, new `test/aftermath-wreck-drift.test.mjs`.
- **Mechanism (connection, not construction):** record on the marker (`makeMarker`), read back
  (`normalizeMarker`), put on the spec (`_specForMarker`), adopt on bind (`bindImmediateWreck`, only
  when the entity is not already moving). The live physics already integrates a wreck's `vel` and
  `angVel` — no physics file was touched, and no per-tick writer was added.
- **The bounds.** `boundedDriftVel` caps recorded speed at 400 WU/s by scaling the WHOLE vector
  (direction preserved to 1e-9, proven in the test) and `boundedTumble` clamps spin at 3.0 rad/s by
  magnitude with the sign kept. These fire once, at record/normalize/spec time; they never touch the
  live integrator, so a wreck in flight is never decelerated by this code. This is a persistence
  sanity bound, not drag — and not a live guarantee: within a session a collision can punt a wreck
  past 400 WU/s, and the value is re-bounded at the next save/unbind.
- **Persistence.** `_writeBackBoundWreck` / `_writeBackAllBound` refresh the marker from the live
  body at unbind and at `serialize()` — never per tick — so re-entering a sector continues the drift
  instead of restarting it at the kill point. Note for whoever touches this next: after the first
  write-back, `victimVel` holds the WRECK's last-known velocity, not the victim's.
- **Determinism:** no `Math.random`, no `Date.now`, no host branching. `_writeBackAllBound` iterates
  `Object.values(own.bySector)`, which was already the established pattern (`_markerById`), and
  `serialize()` re-sorts through `trimAndSort`, so iteration order cannot reach the output.
- **Save schema:** `STATE_VERSION` 2 → 3. Verified: nothing in the repo reads it (`deserialize`
  ignores it, `ensureAftermathState` force-stamps it, the v7→v8 migration hardcodes 1), and
  `grep -rl aftermathWrecks test/ --include=*.expected.json` is empty — no recorded golden hashes
  the aftermath bag. Kept because the marker schema genuinely gained three fields.
- **Golden expected to move: none recorded.** No `test/*.expected.json` contains the aftermath bag.
  A future re-record of any tape that kills a ship inside a named zone would need this causal
  record: (1) markers gain `victimVel`/`victimAngVel`/`victimMass`; (2) the wreck entity spawns with
  non-zero `vel`/`angVel` and the victim's real mass instead of `1e6`, so it integrates and its
  positions and contacts diverge from the first post-kill tick; (3) on save-while-bound,
  `marker.pos` is the drifted position rather than the kill point.
