DONE PQ-142.00 — the fit screen says what you can do, not what went up.

DONE WHEN (verbatim): Four before/after scenarios; the fitting screen states the capability in words.

WHAT I FOUND — The fit screen already had a verb bank (`capabilityDefinitions`), and every verb in it was a conditional chip that appeared when a module was fitted — "Swing off things further away", "See them first". None of them carried a physical quantity, none of them changed when the hold changed, and none of them answered the packet's question, which is not "what did I bolt on" but "what can I do now". The four things the packet names were all computable from laws already in the tree and none of them were surfaced: `TERRAIN_CRUMPLE_LAW` and its mass floor, the tether policy's own break rating, the coupled-mass arithmetic the kernel runs, and the countermeasure catalog.

WHAT I CHANGED — Four physical verbs, always present, always with a number behind them, each a prediction of a live law rather than a table written for the screen. They lead the capability rack on the Shipworks fit screen, ahead of every module chip, and each carries its own why. They are computed in one new read-only module; `ships` remains the sole authority for derived stats.

And then I booted the real game and opened the screen, which is the thing that caught the defect worth reporting. The verbs were rendering and were **effectively unreachable**: the hero that opens their band read "250 / hold", a cargo number, so nothing on the fit screen gave a player any reason to click it to find out what their ship can tow. The capability band now publishes a lead and the hero renders it — "Pelican / can tow", falling to "nothing / can tow" when the hold is full, with the whole sentence on the why. The cargo cap is not lost; it is already in the hull stat row two inches above.

WHAT YOU WILL FEEL — Open the fit screen and it tells you the heaviest hull you can tow, the speed you can meet rock at and walk away, the swing your line will carry, and whether you can put a field in the water — then all four move when you change the fit or fill the hold. What it still does not do is move the four handling BARS with the fit; those read the compatibility flight model and belong to `PQ-176.03`.

OPTION TAKEN — Compute each verb from the live law and prove it by driving that law at the crossing point. Rejected: authoring a capability table the screen reads (the packet's named failure — it drifts from the sim the day someone tunes the sim); expressing any of the four as a percentage; inventing a rope break rule so that "line load" would have a wall.

UNPROVEN — Two honest findings the orchestrator should see, because both are the live design speaking and neither is something this leaf should quietly paper over:

1. **The player's Massline cannot break in ordinary play, by design.** `combatDefs` gives `tether_standard` a 10.5 M tension rating with `automaticBreakPolicy: 'extreme_load_only'`, because "lines were snapping too readily in play". So the line-load verb reads as headroom on an unfitted hull ("Line holds any swing you can build") and as a real reachable number only on a fitted specialist head. The verb is truthful and it moves with the fit; it is not a wall the player will hit with the standard rope.
2. **The player never consumes collision damage.** `collisionConsequences` skips `state.playerId` before routing impact damage, so the slam-survival number is a property of the hull as the sim would treat it (and as it treats every NPC of that hull), not a death the player can currently suffer. It is priced against structure rather than structure-plus-shield deliberately: the working tree carries another lane's uncommitted 3× player shield buffer, and pricing the verb in shields would make the sentence swing on a difficulty buffer instead of on the ship.

No headed capture; the four bars are bench numbers printed from a named seed.
