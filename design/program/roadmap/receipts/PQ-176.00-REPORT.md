DONE PQ-176.00 — a loaded ship flies like a loaded ship.

DONE WHEN (verbatim): A full-gun fit and a full-cargo fit of the same hull differ in reversal time by ≥ 25 % on the flight bench; migration check green.

WHAT I FOUND — The nested budgets the leaf asks for already existed and were already enforced (`outfitBudgetForFittings`: master pool plus weapon and engine sub-pools, refused at the fit with a sentence). What did not exist was any consequence. The propulsion catalog authors ACCELERATIONS and `propulsionKernel.makeResult` turns them back into force with `acceleration * body.mass`, so mass cancelled out on the way through: a Drifter with an empty hold and a Drifter carrying 480 tonnes of iron ore published the same `mainAccel`, the same `reverseAccel`, and reversed in exactly the same 2.133 s. `buildDerivedPropulsion` was handed `totalMass` and passed it only to a legacy fallback that a hull with an authored `driveId` never reaches.

WHAT I CHANGED — Every hull now authors `designMass`, the operational mass its drive is rated for (the dry hull plus about seventy percent of its outfit space). The derived propulsion profile divides its accelerations — and only its accelerations — by the overload ratio whenever the ship is heavier than that rating. Nothing writes velocity, nothing adds a drag term, no cap is placed on momentum the ship was given: the hull's own motor simply pushes a heavier ship less hard. Being lighter than the rating buys nothing, so a bare hull, an unfitted NPC and the shipped starter fit are bit-identical to how they always flew.

WHAT YOU WILL FEEL — Fill the hold and the ship stops answering the way it did: it takes twice as long to turn a run around, and you feel every tonne of it in the stick. Empty it and the ship comes back. What is still missing is the four handling bars on the fit screen moving with the load — they read the compatibility flight model, and moving them onto the live profile is `.03`'s job (the fit screen does now say it in words).

The done-when compares two FITS, and two fits a player actually builds differ in guns, pods and drive tier at once — that comparison is the 126.1 % row. The **isolation control** is the honest half of it: the same full-gun fit, same guns, same drive, same bay, hold empty against hold full, is **99.2 %**. So of the gap a player will feel, essentially all of it is mass.

The law: `load = clamp((designMass / operationalMass) ^ 0.65, 0.30, 1)`. The exponent softens the raw force-over-mass curve so a scout with a part-full hold is legible rather than saturated, and the floor is the same safety net, and the same shape, as `TERRAIN_CRUMPLE_LAW.massFloor`. On a Hitch the floor is only reached past 204 t, so the whole usable load range stays readable in the hands.

THE FEEL — A hauler is a pig because it IS one, and a stripped hull is quick because there is nothing in it. It is the first time in this game that the answer to "why does my ship feel wrong today" is something the player did.
