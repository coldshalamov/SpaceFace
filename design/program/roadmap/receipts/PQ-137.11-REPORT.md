# PQ-137.11 step 1 — the knock budget, measured on the real path at last

**Bar B13** — *"The owner's own ship is never knocked around."* In ordinary flight, contact changes
the player's velocity at most twice a minute, never by more than 10 % of cruise in one event, never
changes the player's heading, and never produces visible jitter. A deliberate big event may, and
must be legible.

This step builds the instrument and prints the honest BEFORE. It changes no game code.

## WHAT I FOUND

**B13 had never been measured.** Both benches were making the number up.

The verb bench asked a file called `knockModel.mjs` how often the player gets hit. That file
*invented* the answer: it drew the number of bumps from its own random number generator (one every
18–32 seconds), drew the other object's weight, drew the closing speed, drew the angle, and then
did the collision arithmetic itself with a bounce factor typed into the file. The game was never
asked. The Crucible bench used the same invented model and then simply added the made-up velocity
change onto the player. So "2.3 knocks per minute, largest 17.7 % of cruise" was a description of a
random-number generator, not of SpaceFace. Turn one of its dials and the bar passes or fails with
nothing about the game having changed.

Then the first honest attempt at a real instrument **also lied, in the opposite direction** — and
that is the more useful finding, because it is the shape of mistake this whole campaign exists to
catch. It flew a real ship through a real rock field on the real physics and printed a clean pass:
zero knocks, bar met. It was wrong three times over:

1. The ship out-flew its own rock field in the first third of the run and spent the rest measuring
   empty space. Nothing was there to hit.
2. It was flying at three times cruise speed. B13 is a statement about ordinary flight.
3. Its contact detector was set so fine that it counted floating-point dust as contact — 3,312 of
   7,200 ticks "in contact" while the physics engine reported not one collision.

A pass printed by an instrument aimed at nothing looks exactly like a pass printed by a game that
behaves. The rebuilt instrument now refuses to print at all if the ship is alone in space, if the
arena did not build, or if the physics authority is not the real one.

## WHAT I CHANGED

Nothing in the game. Two new bench files that measure it:

- `scripts/lib/bench/scenarios/feel.knock_budget.mjs` — boots the real game headless (the same
  Rapier physics, the same flight code, the same collision consequences the player route runs),
  flies a ship at exactly its cruise speed down a corridor of rock and slower traffic, and asks the
  physics authority itself what it did to the hull.
- `scripts/lib/bench/scenarios/feel.knock_budget_10min.mjs` — the same measurement over the
  contract's ten minutes.

Three things make it hard to fool:

- **The corridor rolls with the ship.** Rock is seeded ahead of the hull along the direction it is
  actually travelling, so the field cannot be out-flown and a knock cannot fling the ship out of its
  own test.
- **Every planned contact is a graze, never a wall.** Rocks are placed so the shoulder of the hull
  brushes them. Hitting a wall is allowed to hurt, and B13 exempts it; scraping past a rock is what
  the bar is about. (For the record, taken head-on at cruise a rock removes **98 % of the ship's
  speed in a fifth of a second** and turns its course 63 degrees.)
- **Two independent measurements must agree.** The physics engine's own collision receipt and an
  independent reconstruction of the ship's velocity are both computed and compared. On the runs
  below they agree to within 0.08 % of cruise.

## WHAT YOU WILL FEEL

Nothing yet — this step only tells the truth about what happens now. What it says is:

**Scraping past a rock at cruise takes up to 11.4 % of your speed away in a fifth of a second, and
swings your course by nearly six degrees — and if the ship's rated speed is halved without touching
contact, the same scrape costs 19 % instead.** The ship does not spin and does not wobble afterwards —
those two clauses of the bar are clean — but a graze is currently a shove, and it is slightly over
the ceiling the contract sets. Step 2 makes an ordinary scrape cost the player less than a tenth of
their cruise, without ever taking away speed the player earned.

### The finding that matters most for the rest of the campaign

**Making the ship slower makes it feel MORE knocked around, not less.** A rock does not know how
fast the ship's engine is rated. The velocity a contact takes away barely moved between the two
regimes (22.2 WU/s at cruise 195, 18.1 WU/s at cruise 95) — but as a share of cruise it nearly
doubled, from 11.4 % to 19.1 %. Halving the speed table halves the denominator and leaves the
numerator alone. If the nimble-regime speed cut lands without the player-contact bound landing with
it, every scrape in the game becomes roughly twice as violent in the only units the player
experiences. This is filed to the FLIGHT lane and to the taste director; it is a sequencing fact,
not an objection to the speed cut.

Supporting numbers (seed 4242): course deflection 5.9°; peak spin after a knock 0.0000068 °/s
(i.e. none); receipt vs. independent measurement disagree by 0.08 % of cruise; instrument noise
floor 0.000015 % of cruise; 84 rocks and 3 overtaken ships laid; 7 grazes planned, 3 landed; fewest
physics bodies present at any moment 7.

**On the heading clause.** The ship is not spun by the contact — its rotation rate stays at zero,
so the old "contact yaw is stripped" rule is holding. What happens is that the contact swings the
ship's *course* by up to 5.9 degrees and the flight code then turns the nose to follow, by up to
1.85 degrees. So the heading failure is not a separate defect: it is the velocity shove, seen from
the outside. Fix the shove and the heading clause closes with it.

## THE FRAMES

None this step. This is an instrument, not a player-facing change; the frames belong to step 2,
where a critic who did not write the code grades a capture near rocks and traffic.

## NEXT

Step 2 (waiting on the FLIGHT lane's nimble-regime commit, per the campaign's ordering): make
ordinary bumps and scrapes on the **player** hull cost at most 10 % of cruise, with no heading
change, by an asymmetry in how contact is resolved for the player — mass ratio, restitution and
friction, or a contact-impulse cap that applies to scrapes and not to deliberate slams. Never drag,
never a clamp on speed the player earned, never a write to the ship's position. NPCs keep the full
physics: they are ammunition, the player is not.

# Engineering appendix

**Hypothesis line.** *B13 is fed by `scripts/lib/bench/knockModel.mjs`, which generates the contact
population and resolves the impulse itself; measured instead from `physics:impact` receipts on the
live `rapier-dynamic` authority in ordinary cruise flight, the rate and the magnitude will both be
different numbers, and the magnitude will be the one that fails.*

**Files.**
- `scripts/lib/bench/scenarios/feel.knock_budget.mjs` (new) — id `feel.knock_budget`, which
  REPLACES the inline stand-in of the same id in `verbBench.mjs` by the drop-in module contract
  (`scripts/lib/bench/scenarios/README.md`). No shared bench file was edited.
- `scripts/lib/bench/scenarios/feel.knock_budget_10min.mjs` (new) — id `feel.knock_budget_10min`,
  delegates to the same `runKnockBudget` with `simSeconds: 600`.
- `scripts/lib/bench/knockModel.mjs` — a five-line non-finite input guard only; the module is now
  dead for B13 and is retired when `crucibleBench.mjs` stops importing it (BENCH lane's file).

**Scenario ids and seeds.** `feel.knock_budget` (120 s) and `feel.knock_budget_10min` (600 s),
seeds 4242 and 8008. `feelBars.evaluateB13` already consumes `knockEventsPerMinute` and
`maxKnockDeltaVFractionOfCruise` from a `feel.knock_budget` run, so the bar seam needed no change.

**The knock definition, shared with the Crucible bench (master's ruling).** A knock is a
`physics:impact` receipt carrying `playerInvolved`; its magnitude is that receipt's own
`playerDeltaV` (`dp / player.mass`, `src/core/physics.js` `emitPhysicsImpact`). Consecutive receipt
ticks bridged over ≤ 6 quiet ticks are ONE event, because Rapier answers a single graze over a run
of ticks and B13 counts events. `knockSource` is published in the metrics so the two benches cannot
drift apart silently.

**Cross-check, not a second definition.** The velocity discontinuity
`(Δv_measured − Δv_commanded)`, with the commanded part read off `readPhysicsTelemetry(player)`
(`force / mass * dt`), is summed across each event and published as
`measuredMaxKnockDeltaVFractionOfCruise`; the disagreement is published as
`receiptVsMeasuredMaxGapFractionOfCruise` (0.0008 and 0.0007 of cruise on the two seeds) rather than
reconciled. A receipt that misstated what the hull did would be a finding, not something to hide.

**Real-path facts established on this boot (2026-09-04), none previously recorded.**
1. `rot = 0` + `writeRealPathInput({ moveZ: 1 })` accelerates along **+X**; `rot = π/2` along +Z.
2. The assisted governor is real and exact: straight-line full throttle settles at **195.0 WU/s**
   (`combatSpeed`) within 10 s and holds it for the next 50.
3. **The governor caps only LOCAL FORWARD velocity.** A gentle constant weave
   (`turnIntent = sin(i/600) · 0.06`) reaches 207 → 390 → 541 → 546 WU/s at 10/20/30/40 s —
   **2.8× cruise from ordinary weaving**, because velocity the hull yaws away from becomes lateral
   and is never governed. Filed to FLIGHT; it is not a CONTACT fix.
4. SG-02 body admission by distance from the player, NPC ship: 40/80/150/300/600 WU → body;
   **900 WU → `S4_AGGREGATE`, no body, dV = 0 forever**; 1500 WU → none.
5. `host.spawnObstacle` rocks are STATIC bodies, so `readPhysicsTelemetry(rock)` is `null` even when
   the rock is correctly in the world. `assertBodies` is therefore valid on ships only.
6. `state.physicsRuntime` does **not** carry `sg02Bodies`; only `host.proof()` does. Reading it from
   the state silently yields `NaN`, which is what let the "alone in space" guard fail to fire in the
   first version.

**Two defects found in this instrument's own first draft, both fixed, both worth remembering.**
- A contact-detection epsilon of `1e-9` against a measured reconstruction noise floor of `3e-5`:
  46 % of ticks read as "in contact" with zero real collisions. The floor is now measured inside
  every run from receipt-free ticks and published (`residualFloorP995`).
- The heading clause was first computed from the commanded-torque reconstruction residual and
  reported 1.6° of "heading change" on a hull whose measured angular velocity was 6.8e-6 °/s — i.e.
  it manufactured a B13 failure out of the instrument's own arithmetic. It is now measured from the
  hull's actual `rot` across the event, with the discarded residual still published beside it as
  `maxCommandedRotResidualDeg` so the two can be compared.

**Guards (any one of them throws rather than printing a number).** `sg02Ready !== true`;
`backend !== 'rapier-dynamic'`; `contactCaptureEnabled !== true`; fewer than two physics bodies at
any tick of the counted window; no traffic ever reaching overtaking range with a body
(`host.assertBodies`); fewer planned contacts than the declared density implies; counted tick count
not equal to the requested window.

**What the arena declares, so the RATE is auditable.** `plannedGrazes`, `plannedHeadOns`,
`knocksPerPlannedGraze`, `rockCount`, `trafficCount`, `rockSpacingWU`, `grazePeriodS`,
`trafficPeriodS`. The rate clause of B13 is a property of how much rock a route passes and of a
pilot who never steers; the magnitude clause is the density-independent one and is the real bar.
Both are reported, and the rate is never reported without its arena.
