# Engineering appendix

## 1. What each old instrument was actually counting

**The heading clause was counting the pilot.** The bench compared the player's raw per-tick rotation
against a 1e-9 epsilon. The bench pilot yaws whenever its aim error exceeds 0.12 rad — nearly every
tick — so the instrument reported 1.3–1.8 rad of "contact heading change" across grinds the pilot was
steering through, and 96 "heading changes" in one Cinder rope run. On the same runs today the
retired instrument would still fire **5** times (Lagrange starter), **14** (Helios rope) and **12**
(Cinder rope); the contact-sourced witness fires **0**.

**The magnitude clause was dividing by a number the rule had never heard of.** The bench used
`maxSpeed × CRUISE_FRAC`; the rule budgets a contact at 10 % of
`resolveGovernedCombatSpeed(entity, null, fallback)`. Measured today: 117.4 (Lagrange starter) and
141.1 (rope cells) legacy, against the rule's own **95.0** WU/s.

**The rate clause was counting slams the player chose.** B13's own sentence exempts them; the bench
did not have the class.

**The jitter clause was a promise.** The strip's `visibleJitter` block was trusted as stored;
`attachStripJitter` now re-measures it from the manifest's own frames with the measurer this build
ships, so an old reading cannot outlive the rule it was reading.

## 2. The new heading witness, and its floor

```
witness = wrapAngle(rot_after − rot_before) − angVel_after × SIM_DT
```

Independent of the give rule's claim. It cancels the pilot exactly on the live path because
`_applyPlayerStructuralGive` writes `rot_after = rot_before + e.wy·dt` and `angvel_after = e.wy` from
the same tick's kinematics; anything the SOLVER left in the pose that the spin does not account for
shows up here and nowhere else. The threshold is the run's **own measured residual floor** (p99.5
over receipt-free ticks), never a typed epsilon.

| cell | heading floor (rad) | course floor (rad) | largest heading witness on an ambient contact | witness vs the rule's own receipts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lagrange · starter | 1.54e-7 | 4.18e-5 | 7.17e-6 (0.00041°) | 7.17e-6 rad |
| Helios · rope kit | 1.66e-7 | 4.07e-5 | 3.68e-6 (0.00021°) | 3.68e-6 rad |
| Cinder · rope kit | 1.62e-7 | 5.02e-5 | 6.22e-7 (0.00004°) | 6.22e-7 rad |

The rule claims it retains **zero** contact heading and zero contact course on the player. An
instrument that owes the rule nothing agrees with that claim to four ten-thousandths of a degree.
That is the strongest single result in this leaf: the nose really is the player's.

## 3. The deliberate-event classifier, and its audit numbers

`playerInitiated` = **every** constituent receipt names the player as causal actor, AND either

- the normalised commanded thrust dotted with the player→other direction is **≥ 0.7** (within ~45°
  of straight at it) AND the contact is held **≥ 30 ticks / 0.5 s**, or
- the radial pre-solve closing speed is **≥ 0.3 × cruise**.

`ropeInitiated` = the other body is the live Massline target, tracked from `tether:latched` /
`tether:released` / `tether:broke` on the real bus.

The integrator's correction is the aim-and-hold pair. A shoulder graze past a rock with forward held
scores 0.3–0.6 and lasts a handful of ticks; a bare "positive component" test would have exempted
exactly the knocks this bar exists for. The audit rows below are published in the metrics
(`deliberateAudit`) for every non-hostile event, so the exemption can be checked rather than trusted.

**Why no event on any cell classified as a hostile ram, and what that costs.** Checked rather than
assumed, on the worst cell (Helios rope kit, 1338 player-knock receipts): the intent machinery is
alive — 14 receipts carry a live hostile phase (`interceptor_flyby`, `extend`/`intercept`) whose
`targetId` is the player, with a telegraph in force. Hostile-ram attribution is nevertheless zero
because `causalActorId` on these contacts is the **player's own id**: the player is the body moving
into the other, so `isLiveCohortHostile` correctly declines. Two consequences to state plainly:

- **301 of 1338 receipts (22 %) carry no `causalActorId` at all.** A coalesced event containing one
  of them fails closed to ambient — by design, so the bar can never be flattered by a guess. The
  ambient rate is therefore an **upper bound**, and part of the Helios rope kit's 10/min is contacts
  whose author the authority could not name.
- Some of those unattributed events open with a hostile intercepting the player (closing 0.6–1.34×
  cruise for 0.4–1.6 s at ticks 3739 / 4119 / 4378 / 4772). Whether they belong to the player or to
  the hostile is not decidable from the receipts as they stand.

Neither changes the verdict — the 7.12-second Lagrange grind is ambient by every test and spends the
full budget on its own — but it does change what the next cycle should look at first (§11).

**Does the flight bench apply the same classifier?** No, and it does not need to: its corridor has
no hostiles, no rope and a pilot that never steers (`turnIntent = 0`, forward held down a rolling
rock corridor), every planned contact is a designed shoulder graze, so every counted contact is
ambient by construction — and its only deliberate class, the head-on the arena itself plans, is
already reported separately (`headOnEvents`, 0 in all three runs).

## 4. The cruise denominator, and the one-ulp trap

Both benches now resolve cruise the way the rule does: `resolveGovernedCombatSpeed(player, null,
fallback)`, called with the same arguments the rule uses (it does not pass `state` either).

| where | cruise used | legacy number | the hull's own derived combatSpeed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crucible, Lagrange starter | 95.0 | 117.4 | — |
| Crucible, rope cells | 95.0 | 141.1 | — |
| flight bench, all runs | 95.0 | — | 95.0 (they agree) |

The rule caps a contact event's **cumulative** applied ΔV at exactly `0.10 × cruise`; the bench sums
the per-receipt applied values in a different order, so a run that spends the whole budget can land
at 0.10000000000000002 and read red by one ulp. Both comparisons (`feelBars.evaluateB13` and the
flight scenario's own `barMet`) now use a **stated tolerance of 1e-6**, printed in the receipt's
notes. At cruise 95 that is 0.0002 WU/s. It is float slack, never headroom — the rule's budget is
untouched. This is not hypothetical: three of the seven Lagrange-starter events land at exactly
0.100 of cruise.

The jitter block computes the **strip's exact definitions** (a heading reversal or a motion reversal
between consecutive samples inside the half second after a counted knock) on the sim's own samples,
once at the strip's frame cadence (every 8th tick) and once at 60 Hz, so a wobble too fast for a
strip to photograph cannot hide behind the frame rate. It is not a picture and never claims to be
one: `feelBars` accepts it for this bench **only** when the same evaluation also carries a
normal-speed Crucible strip that measured jitter from frames, and the note it prints says in plain
words that the pictures witness is the Crucible strip. Without that strip the clause stays open and
the bar is withheld — `test/fun-measurer.test.mjs` pins both halves of that behaviour.

One consequence worth naming: `measure-fun-loop.mjs` pools bars **per bench** when it writes a
per-seed receipt, so the standalone verbs receipt prints "no pictures behind it" even in a run that
captured the strip. The combined evaluation (all benches, the rollup) is where the clause is
answered. That is a receipt-layout fact, not a loosened gate, and I did not loosen the gate to hide
it.

## 7a. What the one wobble does and does not prove

It is **1 screen-position reversal, 0 heading reversals**, and it is not charged against B13 as a
failure, because three things about it are unknown and the strip cannot answer any of them:

1. **The opening contact's class is unknown.** `measureVisibleJitter` opens a window after *any*
   `playerInvolved` contact. B13 exempts deliberate ones, and the strip does not classify.
2. **It is not the same fight the headless cell measured.** `attachStripJitter` matches by arena,
   loadout and seed — but the strip is flown by the `swarm_piloted` input tape and the headless run
   by `drivePilot`. The headless `helios_core/physics_toolkit/s4242` cell counts **0.0** ambient
   knocks per minute while the strip photographs **6** player-involved contact windows; those are two
   different flights of the same arena, not two readings of one.
3. **Screen position is not hull position.** A reversal with zero heading reversal is as consistent
   with the chase camera settling as with the hull jogging. The strip records `playerScreenXY` and
   `playerRot`, and cannot separate them.

So the honest verdict on the Crucible jitter clause is **unresolved**, not failed. Closing it needs a
strip whose opening contacts are classified and whose motion channel is the hull's world position as
well as its place on the glass. That is named in §11.

## 11. What the next cycle should fix (the §A format)

- **Rule in the live code:** `src/core/sg02DynamicBodyOwner.js` `_applyPlayerStructuralGive` bounds
  each contact EVENT at 10 % of cruise, but nothing bounds how often an event may start. A hull
  ground along a rock or reeled in on a live line opens a new budgeted event every few ticks.
- **What it does:** in the Crucible's rope-kit and starter-grind cells, ambient contact fires 8–10
  times a minute, each one allowed the full tenth of cruise.
- **Effect on the fantasy:** "my ship keeps getting knocked around" — not one big shove any more, but
  a stream of small legal ones.
- **Vision sentence broken:** "A controllable mass, not a cursor."
- **Bar it should move:** B13 — ≤ 2 ambient contact events per minute on every Crucible cell, and
  zero visible wobbles on the glass.
- **Status:** OPEN → PQ-137.11, next cycle, in this order:
  1. **Close the attribution hole first** — 22 % of player-knock receipts name no causal actor, and
     several unattributed events open with a hostile intercepting the player. Until those are
     attributed, part of the "ambient 10/min" may be hostile rams, and fixing the rate before
     knowing that would be tuning against a number that is partly wrong.
  2. **Then the rate itself.** Candidate: a refractory window on the player's contact budget — a new
     event may not begin at full budget while the previous one is still inside its bridge window.
     Never drag, never a clamp on given momentum, never an NPC gyro.
  3. **Then the jitter clause, properly.** The strip must classify the contact that opens each
     window (so a deliberate slam is exempt, as B13 says) and must record the hull's world position
     beside its place on the glass, so a camera settle can be told from a hull jog. Only then is
     "1 wobble" a verdict rather than a question (§7a).
