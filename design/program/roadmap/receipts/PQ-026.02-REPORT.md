## Honesty gaps

### 1. The proof's "cruise" was the target's cruise (this is why the screen bar is NOT MET)

`src/core/flight/propulsionCatalog.js` is the route's speed authority, and its own docstring at
`:394-402` says so: "`entity.maxSpeed` is the LEGACY derived stat … It reads ~172 for the starter,
whose governed cruise is **95**."

| hull | drive | governed cruise | boost ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hitch (starter, mass 18) | `drive_reaction_m` | **95** | 95 × 1.55 = 147.25 |
| Hornet (mass 24) | `drive_gravimetric_s` | **84** | `boostMaxSpeed` 122 |
| Wasp (the light, mass 16) | `drive_reaction_s` | **105** | 105 × 1.6 = 168 |

The first pass ran both proof hulls at 105. That is the Wasp's number. The planner is linear in
closing speed — target Δv = `(m_shunter / m_target) × 0.95 × closing` — so the headline moves with it:

| ram | closing | light travel / 1 s | screens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hornet, as proven | 105 (constructed) | 149.625 | 1.188 |
| Hornet, at its own cruise | 84 | **119.700** | **0.950** |
| Hitch, as proven | 105 (constructed) | 112.219 | 0.891 |
| Hitch, at its own cruise | 95 | **101.531** | **0.806** |

For a Hitch to clear 126 WU the closing speed must be ≥ 117.9 WU/s — above its governed cruise. The
only pilot-held speed above the cap is boost (`propulsionKernel.js:319-320`,
`baseCap = commandFraction × combatSpeed × boostSpeedMult`), and a boosted Hitch at 147.25 does clear
it: **157.373 WU (1.249 screens), player 7.363 WU**. That is the single configuration on the route
that meets both bars, and it requires the light to be effectively stationary.

### 2. The engine's closing speed is RELATIVE, so a moving target reverses the player

`src/core/sg02DynamicBodyOwner.js:162-169` — `preSolveRadialClosingSpeed` returns
`(aV − bV) · n̂`. `inertialShunt.js:84-86` takes that number as `closing`, and `:106` spends
`closing × couple × dump` off the **shunter's own velocity**. The shunter's velocity never enters
the cap. So the faster the target is coming at you, the harder you are thrown backwards:

| ram | closing | player after | player travel / 1 s | light travel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hitch 95 → parked light | 95 | +4.75 | 4.750 | 101.531 (0.806 screens) |
| Hitch 95 → light at −105 | 200 | **−95.00** | **95.000** | 108.750 (0.863 screens) |
| **parked** Hitch ← light at −105 | 105 | **−99.75** | **99.750** | 7.219 (0.057 screens) |

Both bars fail head-on at once. The light misses because half of its 213.75 WU/s kick is spent
cancelling its own approach — displacement, not Δv, is what the leaf asks for. Momentum is
conserved throughout (18×95 + 16×(−105) = −50 before and after); it is kinetic energy that the
exchange invents.

The last row is not a ram at all. `inertialShunt.js:126-137` selects the shunter by **fitting**, not
by who caused the contact, so a hostile flying into a parked player wearing the plate launches the
player ~100 WU while barely moving itself. The player never touched a control.

### 3. The one clamp built to prevent this cannot see the shunt

`sg02DynamicBodyOwner.js:711-714` is explicit: *"The player is not ammunition … limits response to
25 % of solver dV, enforces cumulative 10 % cruise cap across contact episodes … and prevents
reversal."* That rule measures `actual − expected`, and `_captureExpectedKinematics` (`:624`) runs
**after** `_applyCommand` has already applied every queued impulse (`:614-617`, and the comment at
`:1393-1400`: the `maxSpeed` cap "never truncates momentum the body was GIVEN"). The shunt arrives
through `queuePhysicsImpulse`, so it lands inside the expected baseline and passes through
unclipped. `MAX_CONTACT_DV = 40` (`:107`) is likewise contact-sourced only.

This is deliberate and correct for a shove weapon. It means the −95 WU/s reversal in gap 2 is not
bounded by anything, and it is the same behaviour the owner ruled out on 2026-09-03 ("my ship never
knocked around") and that `PQ-137.11` exists to enforce.

Ordering, for the record: the impulses are queued from a `physics:impact` emitted post-solve, so
they land at the **start of the next tick**, on top of whatever the solver's own bounce left. The
solver's share of the player's velocity is capped at 10 % of cruise (≤ 9.5 WU/s on a Hitch), so it
does not change any verdict above — but the test's single-step model is one tick early.

### 4. The 1 s coast is an upper bound on the light and a lower bound on the reversal

Travel equals Δv by construction: no Rapier bounce, no drag, no drive authority, no AI. The Wasp's
`drive_reaction_s` carries `reverseAccel` 96 and `mainAccel` 124 WU/s², and `_clampSpeed`
(`:1416-1424`) explicitly permits a body over its cap to **brake** — so a light that fights the
shunt covers less than the number printed, never more. Every "MET" row above is therefore the
best case.

The reversal cuts the other way: with the starter's full 100 WU/s² main drive held against a
−95 WU/s throw for the whole second, the player is still ~45 WU from the contact point (arithmetic,
not a measured route number) — over twice the 20 WU bar.

### 5. "1 screen" is 126 WU from a bar that is itself OPEN

`INERTIAL_SHUNT_TUNING.screenDepthWu` is 126, sourced to `FEEL_CONTRACT` B3. B3 is **OPEN**
(`PQ-137.03`) and its measured route value is 0.6–1.0 s of crossing, i.e. a visible depth *below*
126 WU at cruise. 126 is the target depth, not the shipped one. Using it is the conservative
choice and I kept it, but no row here is a claim about what the camera actually frames.

### 6. Reachability, and the cost of the plate

`wpn_inertial_shunt_s` is gated on `tech_graviton_drives` (95,000 credits + 150 RP) and costs
24,000 credits at tier 2. The starter Hitch has exactly **one** weapon slot
(`ships.js` `ship_kestrel.slots.weapon: ['S']`), so wearing the plate on the default hull means
flying with no gun. Not a defect — a design fact the leaf's "the player" wording should own.

### 7. What would close it

Not editable from here — `src/combat/inertialShunt.js` and `src/systems/weapons.js` are outside this
job's write permission. Recorded as a shared-change request: `lostSpeed` at `inertialShunt.js:106` is
keyed to the **relative** closure. Capping it at the shunter's own along-normal speed
(`min(closing, v_shunter · n̂)`) would stop the reversal in gaps 2 and 3 while leaving the
parked-target behaviour identical, and decoupling the target's launch from the shunter's loss would
let the screen bar be met at route cruise without paying for it out of the player's hull. Both are
tuning-and-planner changes; neither is a physics-membrane change.
