# Ceres: the first industrial disposal job

Status: reviewed source candidate for draft integration. Not native-qualified or visually accepted. Publication is established only by the exact PR commit containing these files, not by this document.

## Why this follows Scale 4

Book 1 first has the player carry sealed alloys to Tethys, then compare a real cargo body's honest mass with a discrepant institutional reading. Ceres makes the distinction consequential: the refinery's ledger should count what physically reached its mouth, not every nearby explosion or a generic mission counter. The scene teaches a second use for movable bodies, disposal, without introducing another model-production lane.

The old contract advertised four mites and three pods but supplied a generic patrol quota of seven combat ships. The new versioned job must supply four references to existing refinery repair fauna and exactly three physical ore lots. These are not seven interchangeable kills. Existing accepted legacy contracts retain their version and must not be silently rewritten.

## The player's sequence

1. The Tethys board offers Furnace Detail after the recorded Scale 4 step. Its brief names the actual Ceres destination, calls the fauna repair mites rather than fighters, warns that blast damage also reaches nearby Casket worms, and explains paid delivery versus accounted loss.
2. The player can buy frag and then Load it through existing Shipworks at Tethys or Helios. Buying alone is not equipping. The implementation does not grant ammunition, promise that the initial load is sufficient, or guarantee that Ceres's conditional Sable workshop is available.
3. At the closed refinery, the existing world-placement owner supplies a permanent measured hauler landmark. It is static colliding dressing, not a floating quest-only decoration that disappears on completion. Existing ecology retains ownership of repair movement, behavior and permanent deaths.
4. A versioned active contract directs the player to its real repair-fauna clearance targets. The four stable ecology keys are the accounting boundary. An already-cleared site can be recognized as clear without inventing a fresh kill or combat reward.
5. Three finite dynamic ore lots sit outside the intake. The normal nav owner switches from clearance to a remaining lot, then from a held lot to the furnace mouth. The player uses existing tether/throw physics to place that actual body across the visible open ring.
6. The furnace can still burn ordinary eligible material, but unrelated rocks do not settle these three disposal lots. A receipt must identify the exact mission, slot and actual body consumed by the furnace owner.
7. Settlement requires all four site clearances and all three lots accounted for. Each genuinely received lot pays one third of the posted 480-credit reward. Verified destroyed lots remain losses. Three losses produce an explicitly named zero-pay clearance-only outcome that advances Book 1 without falsely reporting three deliveries.

## Consequence and return

The finite lot ledger belongs to the existing mission owner, not a new global cargo system. A bounded one-worksite aftermath record retains three slot identities/statuses and real poses across internal failure/cleanup recovery and Save/Continue. Terminal received/lost slots cannot be minted again. Internal retry recovery adopts surviving bodies rather than producing three fresh lots. Campaign 47-A's public mission API forbids abandonment and its UI hides that action; these cleanup/retry tests do not establish a player-accessible Abandon route. Residency removal alone is not destruction. Borrowed ecology actors are released from mission references, not deleted by generic target cleanup.

The player can leave a recognizably worked place: the hulk stays, ecology remembers clearance, received ore is gone, destroyed ore is recorded as loss, and surviving unaccounted lots remain material rather than a reset reward dispenser. This is the intended behavior; final source review must verify the complete persistence and transition call sites before claiming it is implemented end to end.

## Asset-to-experience decisions

- Reuse the existing dead-hauler place via worldOneOffs and its normal measured-skin/static-body owner. No new ship model or quest-only duplicate.
- Reuse actual refinery suture-mite ecology. Do not disguise Wasps as mites or import Swarm enemy behavior into Adventure fauna.
- Reuse physical payload and intake-ring geometry. Three distinct lots are material instances, not three newly counted asset designs.
- Reuse Shipworks, navigation, comms, mission completion and credit intents. Do not add an unrelated tutorial HUD or bypass the wallet owner.
- Retain Casket worms as genuine collateral risk. No scripted immunity, guaranteed-safe firing spot, or claim that the source preview proves combat readability.

The real completion path emitted exactly 0/160/320/480 credits for 0/1/2/3 actual receipts with complementary verified losses, once, and advanced Book 1 without a second story credit grant. Additional checks covered same-process Continue before lot adoption and a moved surviving lot restored in Tethys at its saved pose/velocity, with only the other two lots materialized on later Ceres return.
