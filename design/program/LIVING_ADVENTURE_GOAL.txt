THE ASSIGNMENT, in one breath

Make a stranger feel they were dropped into a busy little mechanical universe
that was already mid-shift — everything real enough to fuck with — and that an
hour of touching it produces decisions worth making and at least one story they
would retell starting with "so then." Authored, not populated. You are not here
to implement the campaign briefs. You are here to make the adventure half of
this game feel like the game VISION.md describes, and the campaigns only named
the holes somebody had already noticed.

The briefs are floor, not assignment. Every §23 campaign (CV-DAY, CV-SO,
CV-QUIET and all eight CR-*), every §22 world/economy row, and every queue leaf
in THE WORLD and THE LONG GAME lanes is the list of what we already know is
missing. Do them, fold them together, supersede them, or invent better — a
better foundation than the brief is the success, not a miss (build_map.md
§23). When a campaign's *feeling* is already ordinary on the default route,
say so and move on; do not build the letter of it.

WHERE THE UNEXPLORED TERRITORY IS (known blind spots, 2026-09)

  - Early play recycles ambush, patrol, convoy. Trade, yard, archive and
    escort encounters are almost unique — almost nobody has met them.
  - Six factions have a voice and no address. Nowhere remembers you standing
    in it.
  - Kill machines and weather exist in data and the opening neighbourhood
    never meets them.
  - Most "places" in the first sector are beacons. Busy means ships that
    should not be together; quiet means work that is hushed. Both are
    currently bare aprons.
  - The sim already runs more life than the session shows. The gap is usually
    a missing listener, a dead seam, or a chain that resets when you look
    away — not a missing feature.

THE TYPES OF THING THAT COUNT (invent inside these — not a checklist)

  1. Places that are bodies, not beacons. Somewhere a stranger can fly back
     to, that has a job, a cycle, and people doing something with their hands.
     A berth that is different because of what you did is worth six new
     sectors that are not.
  2. Chains already running. Work the player can watch or interrupt anywhere
     along it — mining → accumulation → haulage → attention → response —
     that continues whether or not anyone pressed accept.
  3. Consequences that outlive the scene. A failure leaves a body with a job:
     salvage someone wants, a pilot who comes back, a price that moved, a
     patrol that remembers. Within a cap the machine can hold.
  4. Toys made of the world. Machinery, wells, weather, geometry — objects
     with a cycle the existing verbs (rope, shove, well, fields, hitch, tow)
     already know how to use. Discovered by curiosity, not a hazard label.
  5. A way of life per place. Occupation a stranger reads in thirty seconds
     of unlabeled activity. Sectors differ by what the job *is*, never by a
     density slider.
  6. Specifics a stranger remembers. One-offs wired to a rumor or a skyline —
     the tug spinning by the refinery, the memorial that is a destination,
     the tanker that makes your ship feel small. They need no system. They
     need to be specific.
  7. Decisions an hour. Fits, routes, custody, forecasts — a choice a player
     can reason about and be right about. Six interesting ones per hour is
     the §17 bar; the fit, the market and the story are where they live.

WHAT THIS RUN REFUSES (inherited law — violations get reverted)

  - No dialogue trees, empire screens, loot-rarity ladders, character
    creators, second renderers, second physics/economy/heat/AI systems.
  - No decorative orbiters, briefing text, beacon-plus-flavor-text, density
    bumps sold as life, combo meters, tutorial modals, captioned feelings.
  - No drag, no clamping earned momentum, no NPC gyros, no hit-point scaling,
    no starter-tool limits, no overheat locks, no ammo starvation.
  - Failure mutates the situation; it never reloads it. Determinism:
    state.rng and state.simTime only. Single writers for credits, rep, cargo,
    derived stats, heat — other systems emit intents.
  - Nothing player-facing exists unless it is reachable on the default route
    with no flag, URL, or debug key.
  - Screen *styling* belongs to THE INSTRUMENT's agent. You own the content,
    model and decisions behind station/market/outfitting screens; do not
    restyle them. Coordinate by exact path, or ledger the seam.

WHAT "BETTER" LOOKS LIKE

  - The bars a stranger would feel: one "so then" story per hour of play;
    each sector recognisable from thirty seconds of unlabeled activity; every
    actor able to finish "I am here because ___" and "if something changes,
    I will ___"; six interesting decisions an hour; a kill, a spill or a
    screw-up still doing something when you come back.
  - Know the area before you change it, and show that your change moved it.
    The instruments already exist for every kind of claim — seeded headless
    sessions and the event bus for world behavior, fixed-seed fixtures for
    beats, scripted slices, ui-bench and a live look for surfaces. Which
    evidence a claim needs is your call; a cheap agent can take a look at
    anything you build.
  - Thin means: a label, a tint, a toast, a timer, a second system beside the
    one that already computes the thing. Connect the listener first. Then
    make it beautiful.

HOW TO RUN

  - Work where the stranger walks: opening neighbourhood → first dock and
    job loop → the sectors outward → the hour-scale decisions. The checklist
    is unordered; follow whatever the area needs next.
  - One slice is a commit the player can perceive. Land it by pathspec, drop
    a checkpoint row while mutating, take the next.
  - A defect outside this lane follows root AGENTS.md §7: small, fix it now;
    medium, subagent it; big or unknown, one row in the demo defect ledger —
    never a second bug list.
  - When a §23 campaign's feeling becomes ordinary because of your work,
    write one line under its heading in build_map.md: the feeling, and the
    commit that made it ordinary. Do not delete headings.
  - Report in the owner's words (build_map.md §1.4). No file paths in the
    summary. Say what a stranger now sees, what still falls short, and what
    you invented that no brief named.

You are trusted with the taste. The briefs were a weaker agent's map of the
territory; your map should be better than it by the time you stop.
