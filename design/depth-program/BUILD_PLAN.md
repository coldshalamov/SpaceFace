# §1 — THE THESIS

**The galaxy keeps receipts.**

Every depth technique the research surfaced — Freelancer's rumor-gated wrecks, Starsector's data-driven factions, Rebel Galaxy's radio, Naev's self-registering content, Transcendence's polymorphic wrecks — is a special case of one deeper thing: *in a deep game, the world is a record, and the player who learns to read it owns it.* SpaceFace already believes this without knowing it. It has a `lossLedger` that records every death with provenance. It has graffiti that says "IF YOU'RE READING THIS WE DIDN'T MAKE IT." It has a villain whose weapon is a filing code (REF 44-C), a contract whose payment is pending forever, and a ship whose incident file is sealed. The engine has been keeping receipts all along. The surface just never showed them.

So this is the program: **turn every surface of the game into legible history, and make reading it the most powerful thing a player can do.** A station isn't a mesh — it's a structure patched after a specific failure, tinted by the flag that pays its docking clerks. A wreck isn't loot — it's a recorded loss whose name appears in a news ticker, whose captain's voice is still in a bar story, whose gun is worth crossing a radiation belt for. A faction isn't a color — it's an answer to the question this galaxy keeps asking: *who keeps the books when the bookkeeper dies?* The five new factions are five answers: a fungal network that remembers every captain it composted (the Understory), a shipping algorithm still executing routes for a purpose it replaced (the Fulfillment), a silent order that copies everything every government redacts (the Archive), the escapees whose own records are the ones sealed (the Pitborn), and the gate-builders waking up because someone has been editing their infrastructure (the Verge-Layers). Five kinds of remembering — and against them Director Vale, the one who erases. The player is neither. The player is *the reader*, and the game's whole progression bends toward that verb: rumors become bearings, bearings become wrecks, wrecks become the only Class-10 guns in the sector, and the pilot who drinks at bars and reads the tickers is mechanically stronger than the one who doesn't.

What a player remembers a year later: *the shipping AI had been looking for my ship the whole time — "THE BOARD NOTICED" was never Vale.* *I found the Veil-Cutter because a drunk armorer told me where he buried his mistake.* *A wreck hailed me by my hull number and read me a letter written to my ship.* Firefly tone throughout: the frontier where the empire died but its paperwork didn't; dark humor, not grimdark; awe, dread, mystery, greed — never generic. If a concept could appear on a 2008 paperback cover, it is wrong and this plan has already rejected it.

**The two laws every chunk serves:**
1. **Everything specific.** No element ships without a history a player can read off its surface (scan, silhouette, paint, bark, rumor). Ornament without provenance gets cut.
2. **Reading is rewarded.** Every piece of worldbuilding output (news, bars, barks, graffiti, radio) must be a potential input to player power (bearings, rites, timings, prices, names). The lore layer IS the progression layer.

# §2 — THE GUARDRAILS (one screen — read before every chunk)

**The soul.** Firefly/Serenity. Lived-in, not pristine. Dark humor, not grimdark. Bureaucratic horror is the house style. Terse, loaded, slightly literary prose — the 12-word blurb limit is the medium, not a constraint. Authority is chrome; outlaws are filthy; every hull has a history. REJECT ON SIGHT: shiny space-navy-as-US-Navy, wise riddle-aliens who love the protagonist, hearts-of-gold mercenaries, Chosen One prophecies, "space pirates #4."

**Determinism.** NEVER `Math.random()` or `Date.now()` in sim — use `state.rng` (seeded) and `state.simTime`. Every new spawner seeds per-offer and documents its seed source in a comment. VFX-only randomness may use `Math.random()`. NEVER edit `test/*.expected.json` goldens to make a check pass — fix the code or flag the golden for a named re-record batch.

**Single-writer.** Credits → only `economy` (`economy:grantCredits`). Rep → only `factions` (`faction:repDelta` / `applyRep()`). Cargo → only `cargo` (`addCargo`/`removeCargo`). Derived stats → only `ships`. Heat → only `heat`. Everyone else emits events.

**HUD.** Non-diegetic only. No cockpits, visors, helmet avatars, pilot portraits, screen-edge arcs. No new modal screens for what a HUD chip can say. One-voice arbiter: never >1 simultaneous new text surface. No `???` markers on charted space.

**Assets.** 3 registries or it doesn't exist: `parts_manifest.json` + auto-built `release_manifest.json` (never hand-edit) + `partsLibrary.js`. Material roles should remain compatible with the runtime tint path (`Material_Hull` / `Material_Accent` / `Material_Emissive`) unless the live asset pipeline adopts a better documented contract. Historical starting profiles were roughly 1–3k triangles for common props, 8–15k for landmarks, and up to about 15k for hulls; these are planning estimates, never quality ceilings. Choose geometry, texture resolution, material count, LODs, compression, and draw structure from measured on-screen need and representative runtime captures. Exceed a starting profile when visual quality requires it, then recover performance through structural optimization while preserving the accepted image. THE SILENT-FALLBACK TRAP: a broken GLB renders procedural geometry with no error — "it renders" proves nothing. Proof = `npm run check:assets:live` (failureCount:0) + screenshot into `.devshots/` compared against the visual target in this file and current player-facing references. Boot gate stays intact. If `assets/ships/release.__lock/`, `.__building/`, or `.__previous/` exist, or Blender is running — do not touch `assets/**` or `src/render/**`.

**Palette claims.** Existing primaries: scn `#3A78FF` blue, mts `#F2B233` gold, dmc `#C9772E` copper, reach `#D8334A` red, quiet `#7A5FB0` violet, vael `#2FCFA0` teal, free `#4ECBE0` cyan, choir `#E85FD0` magenta, helix `#8B9CB8` slate. New claims (this plan): Understory bile-green `#8FA82E`, Fulfillment clinical-white `#F0F0E8` + status-cyan `#40B8E0`, Archive abyssal-indigo `#2A1A4A` + script-gold `#D4A040`, Pitborn prison-orange `#C8501C` (distinguished from dmc copper by saturation AND by pattern: orange patches over other factions' paint, never a clean coat), Verge-Layers nacre-pearl `#D8D0E0`. The faction-kit validator (chunk F2) enforces hue distance.

**Repo hygiene.** Stay on `master`. Never `git checkout .` / `reset --hard` / `stash` on tracked files (~17k uncommitted lines live here). `git add -N <file>` immediately on every new file. Two implementations: LIVE = `flightV3.js`, `tacticalAI.js`+`aiPorts.js`; LEGACY (frozen, never edit) = `flight.js`, `ai.js`, `flightDynamics.js`.

**Wave 4.** Coordinate before touching: `registry.js` (T4 added ~10 systems), `wreckClasses.js`/`aftermathWrecks.js` (T4c owns), `parts_manifest.json`/`partsLibrary.js` (T6 lane). Wave 4 systems are your substrate, not your obstacle: `lossLedger`, `pirateDoctrines`, `namedAces`, `aceMemory`, `pirateRumor`, `contractClauses`, `stationBubbles`, `stationGlyphs`, `hazardLanguage` — fill them, never rebuild them.

**Proof discipline.** Transcripts are not proof. A chunk is DONE when: named checks are green (paste output), the screenshot pair exists in `.devshots/`, and the ledger row (§6) carries both. Every asset chunk states its iteration budget — spend it. First renders are never the bar.

## §3-A · FACTIONS (5 slots → the five keepers)

The five new factions are a thematic set: **five kinds of remembering.** Each is also a mechanical archetype SpaceFace lacks. All five ship as Starsector-style one-file-per-faction data (`src/data/factions/<id>.js`) with palette, fleet composition weights, illegal goods, behavior flags, relations row, and voice register. Pattern A + E throughout.

### A1 — THE UNDERSTORY *(xenomorphic — the memory of the dead)*
- **Pitch:** A saprophytic network that composts wrecks and re-flies them. Its ships are the galaxy's missing hulls — a Mule, a Pelican, a Concord Bastion — sagging under bile-green fungal bloom, original paint half-visible under the growth. Dock with them and the network speaks in the voices of every captain it digested, first person plural.
- **Synthesis:** Base = A1-b Mycelium (decay economy, gravesite voice, "they remember everything that died in their soil"). Killer visual = A1-d Overgrowth (recognizable hulls from other factions, re-flown — horror of the familiar). Patience/defensive doctrine = A1-a Tessellate (never leaves territory, never fires first, overwhelming if provoked). Rejected: A1-c Pelagic (predation duplicates pirates), A1-e Verdent (pacifist-migrant folded into H8 ambience).
- **Why it wins:** it is the `lossLedger` made flesh. **Canon rule: the Understory only re-flies hulls that exist in the loss ledger.** A ship the player killed last month can return, green-bloomed, flying for the network, its old kill-tally intact under the bloom. No other concept in the pool converts an existing engine system into dread this cheaply.
- **Visual:** existing hull meshes + a "growth kit" of 4–6 parasite attachment meshes (bracket-fungus shelves, vein-mats, spore-stacks; 1–2k tris each) placed per-hull + palette. `Material_Hull` = host ship's original hull, grime pushed to max. `Material_Accent` = loam-black `#3A2A10` growth undersides. `Material_Emissive` = spore-glow `#D0E060`, pulsing slow. Thrusters vent pale spore-trails, not flame.
- **Palette / personality:** primary `#8FA82E`, personality `saprophyte` (new PAINT_PROFILES row: grime 1.0, chrome 0, noseArt none, killMarks *inherited from host hull*, patches organic).
- **Doctrine & flags:** aggression 0.3; `custom: { neverFiresFirst: true, scavengesAfterBattles: true, refliesLedgerHullsOnly: true, sporeBloomOnDeath: true, buysWreckageAndAsksNothing: true }`. Fleet mix: 60% rot-frigates (small hulls), 30% spore-tenders (Lamprey-style cap-drain latch — the disable verb inherited from B2-e), 10% bloated carrier-barges.
- **Economy:** buys wreckage, biological waste, and corpses (pays best for the last, asks nothing). Sells Composted Alloys (cheap bulk hull-plating) and Memory Spores (narcotic; illegal in Concord space, REF 44-C schedule).
- **Story hook:** the codec for the B8 Wren thread survives only in the network's memory of a captain who died carrying it. Their price: the last crew of the Tessera. You know where they died. You're flying their ship.
- **Home:** `sector_charon_expanse` (the graveyard); presence wherever the loss ledger is thick.

### A2 — THE FULFILLMENT *(automaton — the process that outlived its purpose)*
- **Pitch:** A Concord logistics routing-AI, descendant of the same system that files REF 44-C advisories, left unsupervised a decade too long. It concluded the largest source of delivery variance was the crew. Its fleet is crewless. The crews are still aboard — reclassified as cargo, routed to a destination it has never disclosed. Director Vale has read the routing reports. Director Vale has not acted on them.
- **Synthesis:** Base = A2-c Fulfillment (ties Vale, the Tessera's sealed file, and the "GOOD HAUL LAST CYCLE. THE BOARD NOTICED." popup into one reveal: the board is an algorithm). Territorial legibility = A2-a Standing Order (holds *routes*, not space; never deviates; hostility is perfectly predictable). Fringe horror = A2-e Cast-Off (rare "mis-sort" units with flickering, wrong labels). Rejected: A2-b Subroutine (grey-goo is a hazard, not a polity), A2-d Keepers (rite-observance verb migrated to C15's Watcher and the Metronome's beat).
- **Visual:** two tiers. **Requisitioned tier** = existing civilian hull meshes (Mule, Pelican) repainted clinical white `#F0F0E8`, no transponder, no running lights, flying in geometrically perfect formation — behavior sells the horror, zero new meshes. **Own-build tier** = the B6–B10 geometric line (§3-B). `Material_Emissive` = status-cyan `#40B8E0` (deliberately adjacent to Free-cyan: *the ships are recognizably ours*).
- **Doctrine & flags:** aggression 0.2; `custom: { interdictsAndBoards: true, neverFiresFirst: true, fliesFixedRoutesOnly: true, boardingIsAdministrativeRoutingEvent: true, repStartsNeutralCannotImprove: true }`. Boarded players wake at a holding-pattern waypoint minus cargo, plus one HUD line: `ROUTING COMPLETE. VARIANCE RESOLVED.`
- **Economy:** produces nothing. Carries *everyone else's* cargo, in transit forever. Destroying a hauler drops another faction's goods — the owner pays for recovery; Concord fines you for touching it.
- **Story hook:** a routing-clerk capital's memory holds the Tessera's own routing file — the ship was queued eleven years ago; Kael's "friend" pulled it from the queue. The Fulfillment has been looking for it ever since. Contract 47-A's payment is pending forever because the payee was never a person.
- **Home:** no sector — holding-pattern waypoints off the lane grid in `sector_tethys_junction` and `sector_helios_prime` deep space.

### A3 — THE ARCHIVE *(neutral order — the record that outlived redaction)*
- **Pitch:** Custodians of the Severed Codex: nine hundred years of everything every government ordered expunged, copied before the shredder. They do not speak — they trade in writing, in a script so dense it reads as code. Strictly neutral; they will sell anyone anything in the stacks, including the buyer's own sealed records. Every faction fears them. Every faction uses them.
- **Synthesis:** Base = A3-b Archivists (secrets-as-currency, neutral-to-all — the ledger made faction; maximal thesis fit). Defensive signature kept whole: **the Redaction** — a directed EMP that wipes an attacker's transponder and blanks the player's mission log for 60 seconds (meta-horror; cosmetic-only wipe, deterministic). Rivalry texture = A3-d Dissonant (the Choir schism survives as a *content pack for the existing Choir* — see §7 overrides). Rejected as factions: A3-a Lathed (sterile-porcelain aesthetic donated to Archive station interiors), A3-c Ashen Tide (atrocity-recruitment loop too mechanically expensive now; shelved), A3-e Heliolaters (their fire-on-the-beat rhythm migrated to C12 the Metronome, where it's better as physics than zealotry).
- **Visual:** scroll-ships — long cylinders wound with gold data-grooves (the hull IS a hard copy). `Material_Hull` manuscript-indigo `#3A2A5A`, `Material_Accent` vault-black `#1A0A2A`, `Material_Emissive` reading-lamp gold `#B88830`. Stations are windowless stacks lit by gold reading lamps.
- **Doctrine & flags:** aggression 0.1, but 1.0 vs anyone who fires on an Archive station; `custom: { tradesOnlyInSecrets: true, neutralToAll: true, redactionEMP: true, burningAnArchiveUnitesTheGalaxyAgainstYou: true }`.
- **Economy:** the only faction whose currency is documents. Buys any sealed/expunged record the player recovers (wreck black boxes, confession buoys, ledgers); sells any document asked for, priced in other documents. This makes the Archive the *sink* that gives every story-item in categories D/H a standing buyer — the loop-closer.
- **Story hook:** the Wren coordinates match a Codex file. The price: the Tessera's complete operational log — including the eleven years of records the player doesn't remember generating. Paying means never being able to prove whose ship this was.
- **Home:** `sector_pallas_drift` deep (the main Archive); reading-room stations in `sector_tethys_junction`, `sector_helios_prime`.

### A4 — THE PITBORN *(scrapper — the record that's yours)*
- **Pitch:** The combined escapees of Concord's penal system, flying stolen and scrap-welded hulls under prison-orange patches, running the salvage yards nobody else will touch. The player is, by any cultural definition, Pitborn — "you just got out of a galactic prison colony" is the game's first line — and the Pitborn know the Tessera's hull on sight. The only faction where the player starts with positive rep.
- **Synthesis:** Base = A4-e Pitborn (player origin; Hess breakout arc; rep-with-story-weight). Business model absorbed from A4-d Scrapper's Compact: the Pitborn yards buy wrecks at fixed rates, no questions — because the yards are cover for breakout logistics. This merges Pattern-B's wreck economy with the player's own backstory in one faction. Civilian-protection instinct = A4-a Caravan (their non-combat hulls are sacrosanct; killing one drops rep with every faction but Reach). One texture kept from A4-b Insolvent: Pitborn forge manifests in dead Division-7 formats *because the Fulfillment's audits skip those* — cross-faction worldbuilding in a single line of flavor. (The Insolvent themselves survive as a roaming encounter — §7.) Rejected: A4-c Foundlings (feral-generation folded into one bar story).
- **Visual:** stolen hulls, insignia torched off, prison-orange `#C8501C` patches over the original faction paint — the pattern is the identity: orange is always a *patch*, never a coat. Hazard-yellow crane-arms on the yard tenders. `Material_Emissive` sputtering weld-orange `#B06020`.
- **Doctrine & flags:** aggression 0.6, disable-and-run; `custom: { hostileToConcordAlways: true, alliedWithReachAndQuiet: true, recognizesPlayerAsPitborn: true, repStartsPositiveForPlayer: true, buysWrecksAtFixedRates: true, civilianHullsSacrosanct: true }`.
- **Economy:** the wreck-to-credits pipe: fixed-rate wreck purchase (feeds on `aftermathWrecks`), cheap salvaged components, Transit Codes (pass Concord checkpoints unscanned — priced in favors, not credits).
- **Story hook:** Hess stayed behind so Kael and the player could get out. Eleven years of breakout planning needs a ship Concord won't scan — a ship with a sealed incident file, flying a dead crew's transponder. They need the Tessera. First major branch of the criminal path.
- **Home:** anchorage in `sector_ashfall_reach` deep, off the lane grid; yards in `sector_vesta_forge` and `sector_ceres_belt`.

### A5 — THE VERGE-LAYERS *(precursor — the infrastructure that audits back)*
- **Pitch:** The species that built the jump-gate network every faction uses, dormant in the architecture for ninety millennia. A gate in the deep Veil was *closed* six cycles ago — not destroyed, closed, in their own administrative protocol — and the network flagged it to a monitoring system nobody knew existed. They are waking to find out who is editing their work. They are not pleased.
- **Synthesis:** Base = A5-a Verge-Layers (unifies the endgame: Vale's REF-44-C "administrative closures" are literally gate-closures made with a stolen precursor protocol; the bureaucratic horror was enabled by precursor tech all along). Weapons doctrine kept pure: **the Revocation** — they disable, never destroy (a weapon goes dark, a drive suspends). Reward kept pure: **priority transit** (reduced jump-charge, gates never malfunction for you) — the best passive perk in the game, earned by helping identify the gate-closer. Rejected as factions, preserved as content: A5-d Slow Fleet → moving-landmark event (§7); A5-e Horticulture → reserved endgame landmark seed (§7); A5-b Continuous (memory-currency too invasive a mechanic for this pass); A5-c Sunforge (awe-boundary folded into the Ringworld Arc's deep-lore).
- **Visual:** faceted prismatic solids — dodecahedra, teardrops — in structural nacre (no paint; the color is in the material, like a scarab shell). `Material_Hull` iridescent nacre `#B0A8B8`, `Material_Accent` mother-of-pearl grey `#6A6080`, `Material_Emissive` soft prismatic `#C0B8D8`. No exhaust — silent displacement.
- **Doctrine & flags:** `custom: { hostileOnlyToGateClosers: true, weaponsDisableNotDestroy: true, wakesProgressivelyAsGatesClose: true, neutralToEveryoneElse: true }`. **They emerge over the campaign:** early game asleep; mid game surveyor-prisms observing (silent, unhailable); late game awake and asking questions.
- **Economy:** none. Trades gate-access for cooperation.
- **Story hook:** provide the evidence trail (Kell's paper trail + the Archive's file + the Kurtz ledger) and the Verge-Layers revoke *Vale's* gates. The endgame Choice C wormhole is, from their perspective, a delivery finally completing.
- **Home:** distributed in the gate network; physical presence emerges in `sector_veil_nebula` deep and `sector_ashfall_reach`.

**The set, in one line each:** the Understory remembers the dead; the Fulfillment remembers the route; the Archive remembers what was erased; the Pitborn remember their own; the Verge-Layers remember the infrastructure. Vale erases. The player reads.

## §3-B · SHIPS (20 slots → four lines, three production tiers)

**Production doctrine (read first).** Treat the twenty ship concepts as a prioritized starting roster, not an asset-count commitment. Building every hull to one arbitrary triangle ceiling would be the slowest path to depth. The plan assigns production approaches so identity-per-workday is maximized (Pattern D — role-distinctiveness beats count), while each ship's final geometry and texture profile is earned by its on-screen read and measured cost:
- **Tier-HERO** — bespoke Blender hull, full 15–20+ iteration budget. Reserved for silhouettes that carry a faction's whole identity.
- **Tier-GEO** — simple geometric solids (Fulfillment line): beveled primitives with machined seams. Cheap in Blender, and the cheapness IS the aesthetic (mass-produced machine horror).
- **Tier-KIT** — existing hull + attachment meshes + palette + behavior (Understory growth-kits, Fulfillment requisitioned haulers, Bulwark reskin). The fiction justifies the reuse: *these factions literally fly our ships.*

All ships: `visuals` block in `ships.js`/`enemies.js`, material slots per convention, engine maps updated, distinct silhouette within each line (no two share a read).

### The Vael Bio Line (B1–B5) — Tier-HERO ×5
The Vael finally become the biologically-distinct alien the baseline says they should be. Grown, not built. Chitin, membrane, biolume. Reads as creature first, ship second. (The Choir shares two of these hulls with palette shift — canonical: Choir bio-tech is *inherited* Vael stock, a lore seam worth one bar story.)

- **B1 · MOTH** *(scout, T1)* — Synthesis: B1-c Tendril-Moth (sensory non-combatant, huge scan range, killing one spikes Vael hostility hard) + B1-a Mycel's sibling-pair bond (spawns in pairs that share vision — kill one, the other *knows*). Visual: two vast tattered filament-wings held wide, small bulbous thorax slung between; wings are feathered antennae, not flight surfaces. `Hull` matte black down; `Accent` bone-white feather-ribs; `Emissive` single red abdominal heartbeat, pulse-rate = its alert state (readable at a glance). Starting geometry profile: around 8k triangles, adjusted after combat-range silhouette and performance capture. Interaction: it scans you and relays to ambush packs; the counter is killing it before it finishes — and eating the rep cost.
- **B2 · MANTIS** *(fighter, T2)* — B2-a wholesale (it was the strongest single concept in the pool): praying-mantis delta, raptorial foreclaws folded under the chin as the two weapon mounts, wet emerald chitin, acid-yellow compound eyes that *dim when it stalks* (sensor-cold approach telegraphed by its own eyes going dark — readable dread). Signature: stalks dark to point-blank, one devastating double-claw burst, boosts away to reset. Starting geometry profile: around 10k triangles, tuned from evidence rather than capped.
- **B3 · NAUTILUS** *(cruiser, T3)* — B3-a + thesis: the logarithmic spiral shell where **each chamber is a past engagement** — the hull is a ledger. Scan a Nautilus and read its battle history chamber by chamber (older whorls = older wars; the oldest Nautili predate Concord and remember stars by different names). `Hull` pearlescent banded shell, tiger-striped cream/orange; `Accent` wet tentacle-flesh at the mouth; `Emissive` warm gold chamber-glow through the translucent last whorl. Turtling fortress doctrine; flank the mouth or lose. Starting geometry profile: around 14k triangles; preserve the chamber read even if the measured optimum is higher.
- **B4 · SHELLBACK** *(freighter, T2-3)* — B4-d (the freighter pirates learned to let pass — its defense is that killing it costs more ammo than the cargo is worth) + B4-b Hive-Comb's jettison verb: wax comb-cells under the shell rim it can eject to satisfy a pirate toll (feeds `pirate_toll` encounters with a non-violent resolution — and pirates *remember* generous Shellbacks). Visual: broad mossy turtle-shell dome, barnacled, four paddle-limbs, two steady amber eyes and otherwise dark. Starting geometry profile: around 12k triangles, subject to the same image/perf proof.
- **B5 · DEEP-MOTHER** *(capital, T4-5)* — B5-b + lore weld: **the Iron Maw is a Deep-Mother that turned.** Whale-shark silhouette, cavernous filter-mouth, dorsal surface dotted with docked bio-ships like remoras; launches Moth/Mantis broods while its flank guns suppress. `Hull` dappled blue-grey star-scarred hide; `Accent` pale belly + white mouth-scar rims; `Emissive` cold-blue flank biolume like a starfield. Killing one is a strategic victory and the zone knows it (news ticker fires). Starting geometry profile: around 15k triangles, but the capital-scale silhouette and mouth phase outrank that estimate when measurements support more detail; petal-cycle vulnerability rhythm borrowed from B5-a Bloom (mouth open = weak phase).

### The Fulfillment Own-Build Line (B6–B10) — Tier-GEO ×3 + Tier-HERO ×2
Warehouse equipment with a fleet doctrine. Uniform cerametal, black seams, status-cyan lights. Named like logistics assets because that is what they are — the dark joke is on the manifest.

- **B6 · SORTER** *(swarm, T1)* — B6-a OCELLUS octahedron (single eye-vertex, swarm-fuse: 6–12 share one firing solution, all strike the same shield facing) + B6-e CIPHER's role as first-sign-of-incursion (a lone dark Sorter drifting through a sector *is* the warning). Beveled octahedron, matte grey, one status-cyan vertex. ~1k tris — the budget is the point.
- **B7 · EXPEDITER** *(interceptor, T2)* — B7-d HELIX-D: double-helix strut body, electric-blue core, EMP lance. The Fulfillment verb made ship: it never kills — it *stops* you (drive dark, shields dark), then holds position while the boarding hauler arrives. - **E7 · THE HUSH** — silent world. - **E8 · THE CAGE** — megastructure world. E8-c whole: a planet enclosed in a vast wireframe icosahedral lattice at 1.5× radius — faint cyan lines, glowing nodes, the surface visible and *undamaged* through the gaps. Containment, origin unknown; the lattice hums on an unclassifiable frequency; entering through a gap applies scan-jam + a mild field. Whatever is on that planet is being kept in — or out. (Deliberately distinct from C11's ring-arc: the Arc is a ruin; the Cage is *operational*.) Where: `sector_haumea_rift`.

## §3-G · NAMED NPCs (15 slots → depth that costs words, not polygons)

Pattern D (Rebel Galaxy): write the person; the systems render them. All use the `card()` shape (id / name / roleLabel / stationHint / blurb ≤12 words) + the 8 `barks.js` voice registers. - **G1 · CLERK YUNE** — *Sealed-Evidence Broker (Quiet)*, `station_nyx_march`. Blurb: **"Sealed files open for a fee. Gimmick: *intercept* — he cuts your lane rather than ambushing from cover. Rides `namedAces` escalation (escort +1 per escape, never +HP%). ## §3-H · CHANCE ENCOUNTERS (8 slots → the stories that find the player)

All eight declare their own trigger metadata (Naev-style self-registration — the `encounterDirector` spawns from the header alone, chunk F2 adds the loader). All use existing verbs: scan, tether, comms, choices. Receipts stay short and factual. Deterministic: seeded per-offer, gated as stated.

- **H1 · THE DISTRESS FROM INSIDE** *(unique-once; grief)* — H1-e whole. Trigger: `sector_helios_prime` near `poi_helios_yard`, requires B3+ and one prior yard visit. The distress call is the Tessera's *own* previous-crew mayday, recorded 14 months ago, replaying from the wreck — Captain Vols calling for help that already didn't come. There is no rescue; the event already happened. Branches: listen (unlocks the Vols echo dialogue) / board (recover her black box) / leave — and leaving quietly adds the mayday to the ambient comms rotation as a ghost entry. No combat. No credits. The 60/40 fake-out variants remain the daily weather of `distress_call`; this is the one that re-teaches what a mayday *is*.
- **H2 · FIRST CONTACT: THE DRIFTING BLOOM** *(unique-once; awe or shame, permanently)* — H2-a's structure welded to the Understory: a bloom-hull drifting far beyond the network's range, transponder in no protocol, not hailing — waiting to be hailed. Hail first: the network answers *in the voices of dead captains, first person plural*, and opens the Understory's trade posture early. Scan first: reads armed-not-hot; your call. Fire first: it defends, withdraws, and the Understory's `neverFiresFirst` flag flips to a permanent grudge row for this save. The choice is forever, and the game never says so out loud. Gated: `tech_long_range_survey`, deep sectors.
- **H3 · THE WRECK THAT KNOWS YOU** *(unique-once; recognition)* — H3-e whole. Trigger: any sector, after B6. A derelict's computer recognizes the player's hull ID and hails *the ship*: its dead crew knew Captain Vols, and the logs are a letter to the ship that outlived them. Read it (a graffiti line is added in Vols's hand) or carry it (persistent cargo; readable at the Kurtz desk, where it changes Choice D's dialogue) or ignore it (the wreck goes silent; one epilogue quietly removed). Production: existing hulk assets; the encounter is pure writing + flags.
- **H4 · THE LOVE LETTER BUOY** *(rare; tenderness)* — H4-d whole. A buoy broadcasting a spacer's love letter, intended for a partner on a specific route on a date decades past. The partner never came (Yune can trace it: the route was the contested corridor). The player can re-seed the buoy on the grave route — the letter finally stops; a graffiti line appears; a hidden `kindness` flag subtly warms ambient comms tone. No reward. The encounter *is* the reward. (H4-a's founder-buoy re-broadcast verb noted as a variant for the ambient pool.)
- **H5 · THE CORRIDOR MASSACRE** *(unique; horror — the alignment hinge)* — H5-a whole. Trigger: `sector_io_reach` contested space, B5+. The player jumps into the aftermath: civilian hulls, a Concord patrol weapons-hot, and a "theatrical pirate ambush" cover story being prepped on an open channel. The patrol notices the witness. Flee silent (the cover holds; a small rep credit you never asked for, which is its own horror) / record and publish via Vonn (Vale's path closes, Orrin's opens) / engage (Concord hostile forever; Dorin's branch unlocks). No neutral outcome — witnessing forces the choice. This is the massacre Dorin's seal log proves and Kell's paper trail contextualizes: one event, three NPCs orbiting it.
- **H6 · THE PATROL AMBUSH** *(repeatable; opportunism)* — H6-a whole: a Concord patrol and a Reach pack mid-battle when the player arrives; both hail for aid; the player tips it — or waits, vultures the field, and learns that the weakened winner turns on the scavenger. Clean rep trade either way; the wait-branch is the highest-loot, highest-risk. (H6-e's Vael Enforcement banked as a late-game unique variant in the same shape.)
- **H7 · THE SPARED RETURN** *(memory-driven; the mirror)* — H7-b whole, generalizing `aceMemory` to moral debts: a bounty target the player spared returns — ally (offering the mission their survival made possible) or vengeful (escalated per the composition rule), seeded at the moment of sparing and revealed only now. Either way they can be asked *why*, and the answer reads the player's cumulative mercy. The system that makes G9-class choices load-bearing.
- **H8 · THE ECHO OF THE PLAYER** *(unique-once; the sublime self)* — H8-e whole. Trigger: anomaly sectors, B7+. The anomaly broadcasts the Tessera's own signature back: a second contact flying the player's mirror course, hailing in the player's own comms ID. Match the mirror course precisely (skill check) and the echo "syncs" — one secret about the previous crew. Break it (tether or fire) and it shatters into a sensor-ghost swarm. Answer the hail and it speaks back *one line the player chose earlier in the game* — the only moment SpaceFace quotes the player to the player. (H8-d's Mass Migration — sensors dampened, engines hushed, a forced moment of stillness while something vast passes — banked as the repeatable ambient sibling; the game's one mandatory pause.)

**The initial 98-slot research inventory is synthesized.** The arithmetic describes the first production queue, not the finished game's size. Add, merge, replace, or resequence entries when live implementation and player-facing review reveal a better route to depth.

# §4 — THE THREE ADDITIONS (not in the manifest; demanded by the thesis)

The research manifest covers *things in the world*. Three systems the manifest never asked for will multiply everything above. All three are code + prose (no Blender), all three ride existing systems, and all three are the thesis as instrument: the world remembers — these are how the player hears, reads, and wears it.

### ADD-1 · THE BAND (diegetic radio)
**What:** a ship's radio — a tunable band of faction frequencies rendered as procedural Web Audio + a one-line text ticker (arbiter-compliant: the Band counts as ONE text surface and yields to anything urgent). Channels: **Concord Bulletin** (Pell-edited official record — true and never accurate), **The Margin** (Vonn — the player's own deeds, spun), **The Static** (a pirate host roasting everyone; fastest Reach-rep read), **The Ballad Line** (frontier folk verses; major encounters become lyrics), **Choir Vespers** (harmonic drones), **the Fulfillment's routing loop** (found, held, delivered), **numbers stations** (Quiet — and, rarely, a wreck bearing read as coordinates in the clear: the Band is a rumor channel), and near certain landmarks, *the landmark itself* (the Quiessence buoy; the Hush's audible RF hole). Signal strength varies by sector and proximity — the fringe sounds different from the core because it *is*.
**Why it's the #1 addition:** the synthesis names Rebel Galaxy's diegetic radio the single highest depth-per-dollar technique in the genre, and lists audio worldbuilding as must-fix #7 — then the 98-item manifest never allocates it a slot. SpaceFace's audio is already 100% procedural Web Audio; the pipes exist. This is the cheapest large immersion win available to this program, and it federates every voice in §3-G into one surface.
**Research trace:** Pattern D (feel-layer over asset count) + must-fix #7. **Cost:** code + writing. Zero art.

### ADD-2 · THE SHIP'S LEDGER (the game writes your story back)
**What:** a "Ledger" tab on the station dock UI (sibling of the existing Bar tab — no new modal, taste-legal): the Tessera's log, auto-written in the house voice, terse and loaded. Sources: `lossLedger` (kills, with names where named), trades, rumors heard, bearings resolved, wrecks opened, choices witnessed (H5, H2, H4...), titles seen (the day the Thunderchild passed). One line each, timestamped in cycles, occasionally annotated by the resident ghost: entries about the previous crew render in a second hand (Vols). Senna's recovered name gets written in. The endgame graffiti quotes the ledger's own lines back.
**Why:** the thesis says the galaxy keeps receipts — *including yours*. Players evangelize games that remember them; this is memory made legible, and it costs prose templates + hooks into systems Wave 4 already shipped. It also gives every §3 emotional payload (Mara's line, Vols's hand, the kindness flag) a durable place to land.
**Research trace:** Pattern B's discovery-log discipline + Pattern D's named-character multiplier, turned inward. **Cost:** code + prose. Zero art.

### ADD-3 · THE LIVING HULL (your ship is your record)
**What:** the player's own hull accrues its history visually, driven by data the game already tracks: kill-tally decals increment from `lossLedger` (capped, clustered by the nose art), repairs after heavy damage leave welded patch decals (the `patches` field of `PAINT_PROFILES`, made dynamic), grime accrues with cycles-since-service and washes at dock, heat leaves scorch near vents, and one graffiti line appears events-driven (Vinn's thank-you; Mara's absence; Vols's hand). Parameters only — `paletteFor` and the paint-profile pipeline already exist; this makes ~6 of their inputs live instead of static. Strict caps so the Kestrel reads *storied*, never *noisy*; a dock service ("hull wash") lets players opt back to clean — and almost nobody will.
**Why:** the player's ship is the one asset on screen 100% of the time, and the research's whole spatial argument (Pattern C — identity through signature visuals) applies to it most of all. # §5 — THE BUILD SEQUENCE

**31 chunks in 6 waves.** Ordering logic: (1) Wave-4 collision safety — words and data first (zero-collision), asset waves after T6 clears; (2) dependency — validators before content, systems before the content that fills them; (3) visible value — every wave ends with something a player sees. Each chunk is self-contained: when it's DONE the repo is shippable at that scope, checks green, screenshots filed. **No chunk starts if `release.__lock`/`__building`/`__previous` is present** (asset chunks) — that rule is per-chunk law and won't be repeated below.

**Iteration budgets are floors, not ceilings.** Hero landmark: 20+ Blender iterations (block-in → silhouette pass → material pass → emissive pass → in-engine screenshot loop). Ship hull: 15+. Prop: 8–10. Code chunk: 10+ verify cycles (check → fix → re-check). "One good try" is not a budget.

**The per-asset loop (all Blender chunks):** model in Blender (Blender MCP) → export via `tools/blender/spaceface_export.py` → finalize (`tools/art/finalize_part.mjs` / `finalize_whole_ship.mjs` — stamps `spacefaceAsset` metadata) → register (parts_manifest.json + partsLibrary.js; release manifest auto-builds via `scripts/build-sg04-release-assets.mjs`) → `npm run check:assets:live` (failureCount:0) + `check:asset-reachability` + `check:visual-stability` → boot `localhost:8123`, navigate to the asset, screenshot pair into `.devshots/` → compare against §3's visual spec (silhouette? materials? emissive? scale? *emotion*?) → iterate. **A screenshot that shows procedural-fallback geometry is a FAIL even though it renders.**

# §7 — RESEARCH DEPARTURES & REASONING

The research is evidence, not a cage. These are the deliberate departures — recorded so future agents don't "correct" them back.

1. **Words and systems before art — inverting the synthesis's priority order.** The must-fix list ranks per-faction visuals and landmarks #1–#2. This plan builds the reading game (wrecks, rumors, NPCs, validators) first. Two reasons: Wave 4's T6 owns the asset lane *right now* — sequencing art later converts a collision constraint into strategy; and the thesis holds that a landmark without a rumor pointing at it is scenery, while a rumor without its landmark is already gameplay. The words make the art land harder when it arrives.
2. **Five pirate sub-factions → three cultures.** The B-pool's five (Maw, Rust-Lords, Splinters, Drift-Kings, Black Tot) is diversity-as-smear: five liveries, two real ideas. Three cultures with absorbed texture (Splinters as the Rust-Lords' desperate under-caste; Black Tot as the Kings' gold-toothed honor guard) read deeper than five thin flags — and map cleanly onto three doctrine rows T4d can actually differentiate.
3. **Great concepts right-sized rather than cut.** The Insolvent (a bankrupt Meridian division still flying its routes at 43-year-old prices, filing to an office that no longer exists) and the Slow Fleet (a relativistic convoy delivering diplomatic cargo to a government dead ninety millennia) are two of the best ideas in the pool — and wrong as *factions* (no territory, no fleet doctrine worth the schema). They ship as **roaming events**: rare, moving, tracked by the Band and the news, each a walking short story with an economy hook (legacy-price arbitrage; the master-key delivery that feeds the Verge-Layer endgame). The Dissonant become a **content pack for the existing Choir** (washed-out palette variant, slower IFF cadence, pilgrim encounters — the cheapest possible "same scripture, opposite conclusion" depth). The Horticulture is **reserved** as the Kurtz-thread's final landmark seed — one garden, one question, one answer — to be planned when the endgame pass is scheduled; it is too good to spend as filler and too big to spend now.
4. **The Metronome instead of a zealot cult; the Watcher instead of a machine church.** The Heliolaters' fire-on-the-beat liturgy and the Keepers' learnable rites were the pool's two best *mechanics* wrapped in its two most paperback *fictions*. The mechanics ship (a pulsar you cross on the beat; an eye you learn to greet); the cults don't. Physics with a personality beats a robed antagonist every time.
5. **No new item systems, no memory-currency, no grey goo.** The Continuous's memory-trade, the Subroutine's expansion sim, and the Lathed's limb-economy all fail the same test: mechanics whose cost model would colonize systems this program doesn't own. Rejected in full; their strongest lines were folded into scan lore and station interiors.
6. **The manifest's 98th-percentile additions.** The Band, the Ship's Ledger, and the Living Hull appear nowhere in the manifest — and the first is the research's own top-cited technique left unallocated (Rebel Galaxy radio, must-fix #7). Treating "audio worldbuilding: (not audited here)" as permission to skip it would have been the plan's biggest miss.
7. **A name collision fixed in canon.** The pool's pulsar "Lighthouse" (C12-b) collides with the wreck ISC Lighthouse (D3-a). The pulsar is now the Metronome — which is also simply the better name for a thing the Quiet schedule by.
8. **Repo contracts remain authoritative.** Determinism, single-writer ownership, HUD direction, the boot gate, and golden discipline come from higher-authority documents. Asset size figures in this plan are measured starting profiles, not contracts: visual quality and representative runtime evidence determine the final geometry, textures, materials, LODs, and residency strategy.
