### Class stat summary (general verified design pattern)
Source: https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Freelancer/Ships
- Each ship has a **level requirement** (the player's "pilot level" must reach it to purchase).
- Stats grid: **Gun/Turret capacity, Missile capacity, Mine, Countermeasure, Cargo hold, Hull strength, Max weapon class, Price.**
- **Weapon class** is the key gating system: weapons are rated Class 1 through **Class 10**. The very heavy fighters are the only hulls that can mount Class 9/10 weapons (most of which only drop from wrecks — see Wreckage section).
- Price scales steeply: starters ~10k credits; VHFs 500k+; Class-10 weapons from wrecks cost nothing but are unbuyable.

## Wreckage (deep)

**This is THE feature SpaceFace's wreckage system should learn from. Investigate thoroughly.**

### What wrecks are
Wrecks are **hidden derelict ships** placed at fixed but unmarked locations in each star system. They are not on the Nav Map until discovered. Each wreck, when tractored in (or shot open, depending on version), **yields a unique reward** that is otherwise **unobtainable or extremely rare**.

Sources: https://freelancer.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Wrecks ; https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/pc/913966-freelancer/faqs/59990

### The loot economy: weapon classes and "Class 10"
Freelancer gates weapons by **class 1–10**. Merchant-sold weapons top out around Class 7/8. **Class 9 and Class 10 weapons exist almost exclusively as wreck drops** — they are the "endgame" equipment you can only get by exploring. Examples from the GameFAQs FAQ (which lists every wreck's loot):

- **Tizona del Cid** (Class 10) — drops from a wreck in Omega-41 / Omicron Gamma area.
- **Skyblast B / Titan's Claw / Nattermodul / Kraken Type 1 & 2 / Salamanca / Vengeance / Wraith / Nomad Energy Blaster & Cannon** — all high-class weapons, each tied to a specific wreck in a specific system.
- **Advanced weapons** (Class 9/10 codenames) — each has a **unique name** and a unique wreck.
- **Special equipment** — Advanced Sentinel / Adv. Countermeasure, special shields, thrusters.
- **Commodities / cargo** — some wrecks instead hold commodities (Cardamine, alien artifacts, gold, diamonds) used for trade profit.

### How many wrecks
The GameFAQs FAQ and the wiki's Wrecks category together document roughly **70+ wrecks**, distributed roughly one-to-three per system, with the Border/Edge worlds holding the richest (Class 10) drops.

### Discovery loop — the core gameplay verb wrecks create
This is the design pattern, verified across the Fandom wiki and the GameFAQs FAQ:

1. **Rumor acquisition.** Players hear about a wreck via one of three channels:
   - **Bar NPCs** selling "rumors" in the rumor sub-menu.
   - **News ticker (LNN)** articles referencing a lost ship.
   - **Mission briefings** that hint at a derelict.
2. **The rumor gives a *direction* but not coordinates** — e.g. "a freighter was lost in the radiation zone west of the Leeds jump hole."
3. **The player flies to the system and searches.** Wrecks do not appear on radar until the player is close. The player must cruise the asteroid field / radiation belt looking for a faint contact.
4. **On discovery**, the wreck is tractored (or shot). The loot pops into the cargo hold. The wreck is then marked "salvaged" on the Nav Map for that save.
5. **The reward is a unique blueprint / weapon / commodity** — often the only way to acquire a Class 10 weapon, a piece of lore (the dead pilot's log), or a cargo of contraband the player can sell.

### Why this is a textbook open-world depth pattern
- **Asymmetric knowledge.** Rumors are the only way to learn wrecks exist. The player who reads/listens is rewarded; the player who skips bars and news is permanently poorer.
- **Exploration-as-progression.** The strongest gear in the game is not bought or crafted — it is *found*. The player who knows the wreck map outclasses the player who doesn't.
- **Lore delivery through environment.** Each wreck is a tiny story: who flew it, how they died, what they were carrying. The dead pilot's name and cargo tell a micro-fiction.
- **Risk-reward placement.** The best wrecks sit in radiation belts, minefields, or pirate-patrolled space — so reaching them is itself the challenge.
- **Persistent, shareable knowledge.** Because wrecks are at fixed positions, the community built maps and guides around them (the GameFAQs FAQ is exactly such a document) — wrecks became **social knowledge capital** that extended the game's lifespan.
- **Tied to the rumor/news/bar system** — wrecks are not a separate system, they are the *output* of the worldbuilding delivery layer. Without the LNN/news/bar layer, wrecks would be undiscoverable.

## Trade infrastructure

### Trade Lanes — the intra-system highway
Source: https://freelancer.fandom.com/wiki/Trade_Lane

Verified definition: **"Trade lanes are structures that permit superluminal travel within a system. Trade lane entry and termination points are usually located near key industrial, military, and civilian bases. The trade lane system is the backbone of transportation within any star system."**

Mechanics (verified from the page):
- A trade lane is a **chain of ring buoys** (Trade Lane Rings) forming a corridor through space.
- The player **flies into a ring** to enter the lane, then is **accelerated to superluminal speed** along the corridor to the termination ring.
- Lanes **connect bases, jump gates, jump holes, and planets** inside one system. Cross-system travel uses **jump gates** (legitimate) or **jump holes** (hidden pirate shortcuts).
- **Ageira Technologies** invented the trade-lane tech; **Deep Space Engineering** built the physical infrastructure. (This lore is reused as a campaign MacGuffin: the prototype trade-lane blueprints are what Trent is hired to transport at the start of the game.)
- Lanes can be **disrupted**. When a lane is disrupted (by weapons fire or by a "Trade Lane Disruption" zone), the player and all traffic are **dropped out of cruise back into normal space** — which is exactly where pirate ambushes set up.

### The pirate ambush pattern
This is the canonical Freelancer loop, verified from the Trade Lane and Factions pages:

1. Traders and the player enter a lane and accelerate.
2. **Pirate factions (Rogues, Hackers, Hessians, Corsairs, etc.) position themselves mid-lane**, often at a known disruption point.
3. The pirates fire on the lane rings or use a **Lane Hacker device** to drop traffic out of cruise.
4. The trader is yanked to a halt in deep space, surrounded. They either **jettison cargo** (payoff) or fight.
5. Police/Navy patrols spawn to defend if reputation is friendly; otherwise the player must fight or flee.

This creates the **emergent PvE combat** that fills the sandbox between story missions. The trade-lane network is both the *transport* layer and the *ambush* layer.

### Jump Gates and Jump Holes — the inter-system layer
Source: https://freelancer.fandom.com/wiki/Jump_Gate

Verified: **"A Jump Gate allows instantaneous travel between two distinct points in space. Jump Gates were pioneered by Ageira Technologies and are constructed in partnership with Deep Space Engineering."**

Two parallel inter-system networks exist:
- **Jump Gates** — legitimate, charted, faction-controlled, near major bases. Appear on the Nav Map from the start.
- **Jump Holes** — hidden, unstable, often in hazardous space. Used by pirates and smugglers to bypass House control. Must be **discovered** (often via rumors) and do not appear on the Nav Map until flown through. Some are unstable and damage ships.

This duality is the design pattern: **the official grid vs. the hidden grid.** Players who know jump holes can smuggle Cardamine, dodge patrols, and shorten routes; players who only know gates are slower and policed.

### Trade goods — verified commodity list
Source: https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/pc/913966-freelancer/faqs/59990

Raw materials: **H-Fuel, Oxygen, Water, Basic Alloy, Semiconductors, Superconductors, High-Temp Alloy, Polymers, Hydrocarbons, Petroleum, Beryllium, Cobalt, Copper, Silver, Gold, Niobium, Diamonds, Tritium, Helium-3, MOX, Toxic Waste, Scrap Metal.**
Consumer / industrial: **Food Rations, Fertilizer, Consumables, Optronic Arrays, Engine Components, Side Arms, Optical Chips, Construction Machinery, Cardamine (narcotic — banned in House space).**

Each commodity has a **House-specific buy/sell differential** (e.g. Cardamine sells high in House space but only Outcasts produce it; H-Fuel is cheap near gas giants). This is the trade-route design layer.

## Worldbuilding delivery

Freelancer delivers its worldbuilding through **three in-station channels** plus the spatial environment itself.

### 1. LNN — Liberty News Network (news ticker / "Headlines")
- In-station, a **news screen** plays LNN headlines.
- Headlines are **dynamic and stateful** — they reference the campaign's progression, the player's actions (e.g. a base the player helped attack will be reported), and world events.
- LNN is the **"show, don't tell"** layer — it makes the Sirius Sector feel alive without cutscenes.
- It also **hints at wrecks and rumors** — references to lost convoys point players toward hidden content.

### 2. The Bar — rumor and recruitment hub
- Every base has a **Bar**.
- **NPCs in the bar** sell **rumors** for credits. Rumors reveal: wreck locations (direction-only), jump hole locations, faction politics, commodity price tips, and lore.
- The bar is also where the player **meets mission-fixers** (story and side-mission givers) and the **mercenaries-for-hire** (in some versions).
- The rumor economy is the **currency-converts-to-knowledge** loop: credits buy hints, hints lead to loot, loot is sold for credits.

### 3. The Mission Board / Job Board
- Bases have a **Mission Board** listing procedurally-generated contracts: **bounty, patrol, escort, freight-escort, attack, defend.**
- Missions scale with the player's level and the host system's faction.
- Missions are the **primary income loop** for combat pilots; reputation with the issuing faction improves the quality and pay of missions offered.
- Some missions are **story-gating** — the campaign advances by accepting a specific Trent-centric mission at a specific base.

### 4. Spatial / environmental worldbuilding
- **System naming** (Sigma, Omega, Tau, Omicron prefix conventions) communicates region type.
- **Base flavor** (a Liberty corporate dock vs. a Junker scrap depot vs. an Outcast Cardamine ranch) communicates faction through architecture.
- **Wrecks** themselves are environmental storytelling (see Wreckage section).

### The unified pattern
All four channels feed the **same discovery loop**: worldbuilding (who hates whom, where the wrecks are, what's worth trading) is delivered ambiently through **news, bar, board, and space**, not through a single infodump. The player who engages with the world's texture is mechanically rewarded.

## Depth patterns

Five verified depth patterns Freelancer uses to create a world that feels larger than its content.

### 1. The 4-House political structure (asymmetric civilizations)
Each House has a **distinct cultural archetype** (Liberty = American capitalism, Bretonia = British industrial decline, Kusari = Japanese insularity, Rheinland = German militarism) expressed through **system names, ship silhouettes, base architecture, commodities, and faction names**. The player learns the politics (who's at war, who's infiltrated) through play, not text. The Hispania diaspora gives the world a **founding myth** that produces two endgame pirate-nations (Outcasts, Corsairs). Cite: https://freelancer.fandom.com/wiki/Factions

### 2. Hidden-wreckage loot economy (knowledge = power)
The strongest gear in the game is not sold; it is **discovered through rumors → exploration → salvage**. This converts the worldbuilding layer (bars, news, rumors) into the progression layer (best weapons). Players who engage deeply with the fiction are mechanically stronger. See Wreckage deep-dive. Cite: https://freelancer.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Wrecks

### 3. Trade-lane ambush emergent combat
The trade-lane network is simultaneously **transport infrastructure** and **ambush surface**. Lane disruptions yank players into normal space, where pirates (or the player as a pirate) fight over cargo. This converts the boring "fast-travel" mechanic into a **site of recurring emergent encounter**. Cite: https://freelancer.fandom.com/wiki/Trade_Lane

### 4. The Nomad alien mystery (the slow-burn endgame threat)
The Nomads are introduced as **rumors, then anomalies, then an existential threat**. The campaign drip-feeds their existence (LNN reports, dead ships, the Order's warnings) before the player ever meets one. This is the **mystery-box pattern**: the world hints at something bigger long before the player confronts it, giving the sandbox a sense of **depth beneath the surface**. Cite: https://freelancer.fandom.com/wiki/Factions

### 5. The reputation matrix across ~50 factions
Every faction has a **standing with every other faction**, and the player has a standing with each. Helping Liberty Navy angers the Rogues; helping the Outcasts angers everyone except the Corsairs and Golden Chrysanthemums. This creates a **web of consequences**: the player cannot befriend everyone, and each choice cascades through the matrix. The wiki documents this explicitly: "each have their own particular roles, territories, and allegiances." Cite: https://freelancer.fandom.com/wiki/Factions

## What SpaceFace could learn

Five transferable design lessons, each grounded in verified Freelancer design.

### 1. Make the best loot findable, not buyable (the wreckage pattern)
Freelancer's Class 10 weapons only drop from hidden wrecks. **SpaceFace's wreckage system should do the same**: the strongest / most characterful gear should be **discovered through exploration and rumor**, not bought at a shop. This creates a perpetual exploration incentive and converts worldbuilding (lore about where a ship went down) into progression. Implementation: each wreck = unique item + lore snippet + environment hazard. Cite: https://freelancer.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Wrecks

### 2. Tie wreckage discovery to the worldbuilding delivery layer (bars, news, rumors)
Freelancer wrecks are **undiscoverable without rumors from bars and LNN**. SpaceFace should make wreckage a **knowledge-gated** system: the player hears a rumor (NPC, news ticker, faction rep) that gives a directional hint, then must search the space. This makes the bar/news system load-bearing for progression, not decorative. Cite: https://freelancer.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Wrecks

### 3. Make fast-travel the ambush surface (the trade-lane pattern)
Freelancer's trade lanes are not safe fast-travel — they are **where pirates hunt**. SpaceFace's trade-lane equivalent should make fast-travel a **risk**: disruption zones drop the player into normal space where combat can happen. This converts a boring convenience into a generator of emergent encounters. Cite: https://freelancer.fandom.com/wiki/Trade_Lane

### 4. Dual route networks: official grid vs. hidden grid (jump gates vs. jump holes)
Freelancer pairs **legitimate, policed jump gates** with **hidden, unstable jump holes** known only to pirates and smugglers. SpaceFace should implement a **parallel hidden route network** that players must discover via reputation/rumors, enabling smuggling, shortcut-taking, and patrol-dodging. This rewards deep play and faction investment. Cite: https://freelancer.fandom.com/wiki/Jump_Gate

### 5. The reputation matrix as the core political verb
Freelancer's ~50-faction reputation matrix means **every choice cascades** — you cannot befriend everyone. SpaceFace should implement a **cross-faction standing system** where helping one faction measurably shifts others (allies, enemies, vassals). This gives the player's actions political weight and makes the world feel like a society, not a backdrop. Cite: https://freelancer.fandom.com/wiki/Factions
