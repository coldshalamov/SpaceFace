# 05 — Encounters, Missions, and Story

## Goal

Alien content should enter through the systems SpaceFace already does well: travel, scanning, salvage, contracts, faction incentives, physical manipulation, and environmental investigation.

The default alien mission should not be "go to waypoint, kill xeno."

## 1. Encounter grammar

Alien encounters can be generated from five questions:

1. What ecological role is present?
2. What is it doing before the player arrives?
3. What stimulus does the player introduce?
4. What other system or faction complicates the situation?
5. What persists after the encounter?

This gives more variety than enemy archetype plus squad size.

## 2. Encounter families

### Observation

Examples:
- a ray migration crosses a trade lane;
- a solitary organism repeats an old route;
- three unrelated species synchronize around a relay pulse;
- a precursor robot silently surveys an infected site.

Player verbs:
- scan;
- follow;
- remain dark;
- triangulate;
- record.

Failure is often disturbing the subject, not dying.

### Salvage conflict

Examples:
- valuable wreck partially colonized;
- hull leeches wake when power is restored;
- another salvage crew wants the same lot;
- precursor clamp marks the wreck as quarantined.

Player verbs:
- tether;
- cut;
- tow;
- sample;
- negotiate;
- choose what to leave.

### Containment

Examples:
- cyst carrier enters a shipping corridor;
- contaminated cargo has reached a station;
- active growth breaches a sealed module;
- pirate bait has lured fauna toward a settlement.

Player verbs:
- redirect;
- isolate;
- destroy;
- tow;
- seal;
- escort.

### Ecological rescue

Examples:
- research crew trapped inside a bloom;
- migratory animals boxed in by mining charges;
- precursor suppression field fails and fauna stampede;
- a station's heat plume is attracting predators.

Player verbs:
- rescue;
- reroute;
- restore machinery;
- manipulate heat/signals.

### Investigation

Examples:
- missing miner;
- impossible distress beacon;
- clean wreck with contaminated interior;
- animal carrying human material;
- field echo reproducing a ship maneuver.

Player verbs:
- scan;
- reconstruct;
- compare records;
- track;
- infer.

### Hunt

Hunting exists, but motive matters:
- remove a predator;
- collect a specimen;
- stop a carrier;
- bounty on pirate exploiting fauna;
- disable a dangerous relay.

The target is part of ecology, not a monster contract template.

### Passage

The objective is simply to get through:
- migration crossing;
- spore corridor;
- active relay zone;
- precursor quarantine;
- integrated wreck maze.

This turns navigation into content.

## 3. Mission families

### M1 — Biological survey

Board text:
Map an unknown biological signature.

Variants:
- non-hostile target;
- mobile target;
- target inside dangerous flock;
- signal actually from colonized machinery.

Rewards:
- credits;
- research standing;
- scanner taxonomy progress;
- sample.

### M2 — Quarantine tow

A contaminated wreck must be moved to a quarantine orbit without destroying it.

Core verbs:
- Massline;
- careful flight;
- route planning.

Complications:
- leeches;
- pirates;
- spore rupture if impact threshold exceeded.

### M3 — Filter run

Deliver replacement filters or scrubber parts to a station inside contaminated space.

Core:
- logistics under environmental pressure.

Complication:
Cargo is mundane; route is not.

### M4 — Missing crew

A ship exists, transponder active, crew absent.

Possible answers:
- evacuated;
- killed;
- hiding in sealed compartment;
- organism carried them away;
- deliberate fraud exploiting quarantine rules.

Do not make alien explanation guaranteed.

### M5 — Live capture

Recover a small organism intact.

Core:
- disable/contain, not kill.

Tools:
- low-energy pulse;
- tether cage;
- environmental lure.

### M6 — Relay mapping

Triangulate a network relay by observing synchronized fauna.

Core:
- inference.

The relay is not marked as objective until player earns the conclusion.

### M7 — Bloom burn

A faction wants a colony sterilized.

Choices:
- comply;
- discover valuable/rare ecology and refuse;
- move it;
- falsify completion;
- sell coordinates.

Consequences depend on faction.

### M8 — Cyst convoy

Escort a sealed research shipment.

Twist:
The threat may be other humans, not the specimen.

### M9 — False contamination

A station is using an alien scare to suppress claims or prices.

Purpose:
Prevent every contamination report from being true.

### M10 — Lost route

Follow repeated animal migration through sensor-obscured space.

Reward:
- hidden route;
- wreck;
- precursor site.

### M11 — The warm wreck

Restore power long enough to pull a black box.

Restoring power also wakes biological occupation.

### M12 — Dead relay

A networked region has become strangely chaotic.

Cause options:
- relay dead;
- precursor suppression activated;
- different strain moved in;
- humans removed an organism they did not understand.

### M13 — Contaminated claim

Two human parties dispute mining rights over a biologically active asteroid.

Questions:
- is the organism legally life;
- who owns biological material grown on a claim;
- can a claim be mined without destroying the habitat.

This is pure SpaceFace: bureaucracy meets ontology.

### M14 — Carrier diversion

A giant reproductive organism is headed toward inhabited space.

Methods:
- heat lure;
- Massline anchor;
- precursor beacon;
- engine decoy.

Killing it is possible but causes a regional bloom.

### M15 — Deep trace

Recover a rare network memory trace from a relay without destroying it.

Outcome:
One short, contextless sensory fragment from long ago.

Never exposition dump.

### M16 — Quarantine audit

A Verge-Layer machine demands a protocol the player does not understand.

The mission is to discover what action satisfies it before the gate closes.

### M17 — Machine witness

A precursor robot has been observing a contaminated site for centuries.

The player can:
- leave it;
- interfere;
- follow its patrol;
- present an artifact;
- provoke a procedural response.

### M18 — Revoked gate

Restore a route humans want reopened.

Deeper question:
Why was it revoked?

Possible result:
The economically rational mission and biologically safe action conflict.

### M19 — The wrong corpse

A fauna carcass contains tissue from a species not known in the current region.

Implies migration or memory-linked host transfer.

### M20 — Infected smuggler route

The Quiet has a safe path through a high-C region.

Their secret:
They learned to mimic a low-interest signal rather than defeat the ecology.

Unlock opportunity:
signal masker.

## 4. Ambient encounter seeds

- Fauna feeding on debris after NPC battle.
- Cyst cluster riding a freight container.
- Patrol scans an organism because the law has no category for it.
- Miners firing at harmless rays blocking a lane.
- Quiet ship traveling dark through a bloom.
- Precursor prism crossing combat and ignoring both sides.
- Pirate uses a spore carrier as cover.
- Colony barge vents heat and attracts Furnace Maw.
- Research ship asks player not to fire.
- Animal follows player for multiple zones without attacking.
- Dead ship continues tiny course corrections due to biological takeover.
- Multiple animals stop moving when a gate activates.
- Relay pulse causes every infected contact to rotate toward a distant bearing.
- A normal rescue mission passes through contamination and becomes stranger without changing objective.
- An Understory-colonized hull scavenges fresh wreckage after a battle.

## 5. Narrative reveal ladder

### Reveal 0 — category error

Language:
- corrosion;
- residue;
- contamination;
- non-standard biological.

### Reveal 1 — living

Language:
- active metabolism;
- growth;
- host.

### Reveal 2 — connected

Language:
- correlated response;
- shared signal;
- field coherence.

### Reveal 3 — historical

Language:
- old samples match current sites;
- route records align;
- REF 44-C crosses multiple incidents.

### Reveal 4 — Vethari-linked

Language:
- matching filament morphology;
- matching field harmonic;
- Wren recognizes physical material.

### Reveal 5 — precursor response

Language:
- machine quarantine predates human settlement;
- gates were revoked around similar signatures.

### Reveal 6 — scale

Language:
- the known outbreaks are edge effects;
- route geometry points toward a larger domain.

## 6. Wren integration

Wren should not narrate the mystery.

Use body and behavior:
- he recognizes a frequency before scanner classification;
- he stops talking;
- he knows not to power something;
- he touches hull material and pulls his hand away;
- he predicts a synchronized movement once;
- he refuses a seemingly obvious route without explaining;
- he recognizes grief-static behavior in the field.

These should be sparse.

His knowledge can be mechanically useful without turning him into a lore audiobook.

## 7. Human conflict around alien ecology

The ecology is a pressure applied to existing politics.

Good conflicts:
- quarantine vs commerce;
- research vs preservation;
- salvage rights vs biological risk;
- public warning vs institutional reputation;
- access vs containment;
- patenting vs open science;
- military exploitation vs ecological safety;
- local survival vs galactic caution.

The aliens do not need human motives for humans to fight over them.

## 8. Procedural encounter composition

A data-driven alien encounter can combine:

ecology role + zone + complication + objective + aftermath.

Example:
carrier + trade lane + Meridian research crew + divert + future route contamination reduced.

Example:
relay + derelict field + pirate salvage + scan + pirate later uses recovered sample.

This can create many authored-feeling situations from a small set of behaviors.

## 9. Setpiece encounters

Reserve bespoke scripting for moments that permanently change the player's mental model.

Candidates:
- first synchronized multi-species turn;
- first integrated station;
- first precursor machine intervention;
- first giant carrier;
- first revoked gate;
- first field echo recognized by Wren;
- first Vethari-scale shadow.

These deserve unique audio/visual craft.

## 10. Failure design

Alien missions should have nonbinary outcomes.

Possible outcomes:
- specimen killed;
- route reopened but ecology harmed;
- cargo recovered but contaminated;
- crew rescued but evidence lost;
- relay destroyed and region becomes unpredictable;
- carrier diverted into pirate territory;
- machine protocol satisfied but gate permanently closes;
- sample sold and later commercialized.

Failure creates world state, not just reload pressure.

## 11. Acceptance

The mission layer succeeds when:
- at least half of alien missions can be completed without killing the main organism;
- physics verbs matter;
- factions respond differently to the same ecology;
- mysteries sometimes have human answers;
- ecological consequences persist;
- narrative revelation is carried by play, not only text.
