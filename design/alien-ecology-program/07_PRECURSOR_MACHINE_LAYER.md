# 07 — Precursor Machine Layer

## Goal

Create the legendary "godlike race" indirectly through surviving machines and structures.

The player should meet the civilization's competence before meeting any claim about its people.

The existing Verge-Layers are the ideal bridge. Their runtime identity can remain while their ontology becomes clearer: they are surviving machine institutions, not ordinary biological aliens.

## 1. Core fantasy

Somebody built the gates.

Somebody understood space, containment, and systems engineering on a level that makes human infrastructure look provisional.

They are gone, withdrawn, unreachable, or hiding.

Their machines are not.

## 2. Asimovian design principle

Robots are interesting when rules create behavior.

Do not make precursor robots mysterious because they speak in riddles. Make them mysterious because:
- their directives are exact;
- their definitions are old;
- their categories do not map cleanly to human law;
- they possess enormous capability but limited authority;
- they will execute a procedure for ten thousand years rather than improvise outside it.

A machine can be terrifying while saying something as plain as:

TRANSIT AUTHORITY REVOKED.
BIOLOGICAL CONDITION UNRESOLVED.
APPEAL WINDOW CLOSED.

## 3. The creators

Keep the living creators undefined.

What is safe to establish:
- technologically far beyond current humanity;
- built or radically modified parts of the gate network;
- left machine institutions with narrow but durable authority;
- encountered biological threats unknown to present humans;
- had enough time depth that human myths contradict each other.

What should remain uncertain:
- morphology;
- population;
- extinction vs departure;
- exact relationship to Vethari;
- whether the machines still receive remote instructions;
- whether "one civilization" is even the correct category.

## 4. Verge-Layer reinterpretation

Existing roles map cleanly.

### Surveyor Prism

Function:
- inspect geometry;
- map routes;
- sample fields;
- compare current conditions to ancient baseline.

Behavior:
- non-hostile;
- ignores ordinary combat;
- may position itself between player and anomaly to scan both.

### Revocation Lattice

Function:
- enforce closed routes;
- establish exclusion geometry;
- disable/redirect rather than destroy.

Behavior:
- treats passage as a protocol problem.

### Gate Auditor

Function:
- evaluate gate state;
- compare contamination;
- grant/revoke transit.

Behavior:
- may awaken only after route changes or contamination thresholds.

Existing custom flags such as hostileOnlyToGateClosers and weaponsDisableNotDestroy become stronger under this interpretation.

## 5. Machine families

### 5.1 Custodians

Maintain structures.

Traits:
- ignore player unless maintenance is obstructed;
- repair ancient surfaces;
- disassemble foreign additions;
- can accidentally destroy human modifications with no hostility.

Gameplay:
- follow one to hidden service route;
- protect it;
- stop it from "repairing" something humans need.

### 5.2 Witnesses

Record conditions.

Traits:
- observe;
- tag;
- rarely intervene;
- preserve extremely old local records.

Gameplay:
- earn access by recreating a condition or presenting evidence.

### 5.3 Auditors

Evaluate rules.

Traits:
- interrogate transponders;
- compare biological signatures;
- issue status.

Gameplay:
- protocol puzzle;
- lawful terror.

### 5.4 Wardens

Contain.

Traits:
- activate fields;
- close routes;
- disable ships;
- sterilize small volumes if threshold crossed.

Gameplay:
- avoid, satisfy, or deliberately invoke.

### 5.5 Shepherds

Manipulate ecology.

Possibility:
Machines once redirected infected fauna rather than exterminating it.

Behavior:
- suppression tones;
- lure fields;
- corridor shaping.

This gives the player clues for later tools.

### 5.6 Masons

Build/repair large infrastructure.

Rare.

A Mason waking should be a world event:
- gate changes shape;
- destroyed structure rebuilds;
- asteroid is moved;
- human station must evacuate its berth.

### 5.7 Executors

Reserved high-threat machines.

Their purpose is not combat dominance but final enforcement when lesser protocol fails.

Use sparingly.

## 6. Structure families

### Gateworks

Ancient layers beneath human retrofits.

Visual reveal:
human gantries attached to impossible original geometry.

### Quarantine pylons

Form exclusion volumes.

Can be:
- dormant;
- broken;
- partially active.

### Survey monoliths

Map stellar/biological state.

### Null corridors

Volumes where shared-field coherence collapses.

Could explain safe pathways through infested regions.

### Black vaults

Contain unknown biological or machine material.

Do not make all vaults loot rooms.

### Machine ossuaries

Fields of inactive robots arranged for recycling, storage, or decommission.

Humans interpret as graves.

### Star-scale markers

Structures visible from far away whose purpose is route geometry, not monument.

### Containment rings

Huge structures around a moon, wreck field, or planet.

A late reveal could show that a visually impressive "ancient ruin" is actually one component of a much larger quarantine system.

## 7. Visual language

Oppose the fungus.

Geometry:
- exact;
- spare;
- repeated ratios;
- surfaces with no decorative noise unless function requires it;
- impossible tolerances;
- structures that align across enormous distances.

Materials:
- pale metal;
- obsidian;
- ceramic;
- nacre;
- controlled emissive lines.

Motion:
- precise;
- minimal;
- no idle bobbing;
- long stillness followed by exact movement.

Damage:
- old machines can be scarred without looking derelict;
- self-maintained systems should feel maintained.

## 8. Audio language

- dry transients;
- narrow harmonic tones;
- phase-precise pulses;
- long silence;
- no monster bass growls;
- speech, if used, is clean, literal, and unhurried.

A machine waking should be recognizable before it is visible.

## 9. Protocol interaction

The precursor layer should have a small grammar the player gradually learns.

Possible concepts:
- PRESENT — provide object/signature.
- VACATE — leave volume.
- HOLD — remain still.
- WITNESS — observe event without interference.
- RETURN — restore object to location.
- ISOLATE — separate contaminated material.
- CLOSE — revoke route.
- OPEN — authorize route.
- APPEAL — provide evidence contradicting machine classification.

The UI can translate observed behavior gradually, not instantly.

## 10. Machine "reputation"

Do not use ordinary faction reputation if avoidable.

Use protocol state:
- unknown;
- observed;
- compliant;
- witnessed;
- exception granted;
- violation;
- revoked.

This feels less human.

Technical implementation may still map through faction metadata for IFF, but player-facing language should differ.

## 11. Relationship to infection

Possible ancient strategy:
- classify;
- route around;
- isolate;
- suppress field coherence;
- close gates;
- preserve samples;
- observe over geological time.

Notice what is absent:
"kill all infected life."

That restraint implies the creators understood the problem better than humans.

## 12. Key mystery beats

1. Robot ignores humans but reacts instantly to fungal sample.
2. Ancient pylon makes infected fauna scatter.
3. Gate closes when contaminated cargo approaches.
4. Machine labels a region with an age older than human settlement.
5. Player discovers several "unrelated ruins" align into a quarantine boundary.
6. Machine recognizes Vethari-linked harmonic.
7. Deep gate is not broken; it is administratively revoked.
8. A human faction has been overriding a machine warning for decades.
9. Machine grants player an exception for reasons not explained.
10. An active robot receives a command from somewhere outside known map — or appears to. Keep ambiguous.

## 13. Rare robot personalities without humanization

Personality can emerge from directive history.

Examples:
- a Witness that has observed one site for 18,000 years and refuses relocation;
- a Custodian that considers a human station temporary debris;
- an Auditor with a damaged category table that repeatedly requests a missing authority;
- a Shepherd still maintaining a safe corridor nobody uses;
- a Gate Auditor that recognizes Wren's contaminated artifact as old evidence.

They need no jokes or emotional speeches.

## 14. Combat

Most machine encounters should not start hostile.

When they fight:
- disabling weapons;
- vector control;
- system shutdown;
- geometric fields;
- perfect interception;
- route denial.

They should feel technologically superior without simply having huge HP.

Ways to survive:
- obey;
- exit;
- break line;
- present correct token;
- exploit directive boundaries;
- use environment.

A player killing one should be rare, consequential, and not automatically rewarded with its best technology.

## 15. The creator reveal ceiling

Even by the end of the base arc, the player may know only:
- these machines had makers;
- the makers were ancient and extraordinarily capable;
- they encountered or anticipated Vethari-linked biology;
- they changed the route network around it.

That is enough.

The universe stays larger if the creator species is not sitting behind the next gate waiting to explain the lore.

## 16. Acceptance

The precursor layer succeeds when:
- robots feel rule-bound rather than human-faction AI in fancy hulls;
- a functioning ancient machine is exciting even when no combat occurs;
- their technology changes route/access decisions;
- the player can infer ancient containment history from infrastructure;
- the creator civilization remains mythic after its machines become mechanically familiar.
