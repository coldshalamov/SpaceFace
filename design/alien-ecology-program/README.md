# Alien Ecology Program

Status: durable design source; not admitted implementation work by itself.

This folder turns the owner's alien-ecology direction into a buildable program. It is intentionally broader than a faction brief. The goal is to make the late game feel as though the known human world is gradually losing its monopoly on reality.

The program has two deep-time poles:

1. Vethari-linked fungal ecology: one biological phenomenon, many hosts and environmental expressions. The Vethari remain the off-frame apex catastrophe. What the player physically meets are consequences: infected fauna, colonized wrecks, relay organisms, contaminated infrastructure, failed quarantines, and regions where the local ecology has become partially networked.
2. The precursor machine layer: surviving robots, gates, containment structures, wreckage, and automated protocols of a legendary civilization whose living members are presumed mythical or gone. The existing Verge-Layers are retained and reinterpreted as machines or machine institutions left by that civilization, not as a second ordinary faction of humanoid aliens.

The intended escalation is not "now enemies have more hit points." It is epistemic and environmental:

human space -> traces -> impossible biology -> active infected ecology -> colonized infrastructure -> networked regions -> precursor containment evidence -> domain threshold -> the shadow of the Vethari.

The program obeys current canon unless the owner later explicitly revises it. In particular, the protected Vethari rule remains: the Vethari themselves stay off-frame. Their ship, signal, field effects, artifacts, architecture, and ecological wake may become increasingly present. Routine renderable Vethari combatants do not.

## North-star rules

- One fungus, not two competing fungus civilizations.
- The fungus links nervous systems; it does not magically add brain mass or wisdom.
- Shared memory is lossy. Coordination can increase while cognition degrades.
- Mutations are biological and historical, not instant tactical shapeshifting.
- The player reads ecology from form and behavior before reading exposition.
- The Vethari are the apex source of dread, not farmable content.
- The precursor machines are not chatty quest dispensers. They are old procedures still executing.
- Weirdness increases primarily by geography and history. Story progress reveals and grants access; it must not make contamination appear by magic.
- New content should reuse existing world, scanner, encounter, salvage, AI, VFX, and asset systems wherever possible.
- Dense ecology must be designed around SpaceFace's measured performance constraints from the first prototype.
- Every new family needs a distinctive gameplay verb or information problem. A new silhouette with the same combat behavior is not enough.

## Program outcome

If this program succeeds, a player can start in familiar human commercial space and end up somewhere that feels biologically and historically older than humanity without the game changing genres or abandoning its existing economy, physics, contracts, bureaucracy, and exploration loops.

The alien layer should make those existing systems stranger. Concord still files forms. Miners still want claims. Salvagers still want wrecks. The problem is that the claim is growing nerves, the wreck is remembering things, and an ancient machine has just informed Customs that the gate has been under quarantine for forty-one thousand years.
