# 06 — Progression and economy: one tree, one rope, one wallet per door

**Director, 2026-11-18.** Ruling R1 gives three ladders a player can name: **the Pilot Tree** (how I
fight), **Line Class** (what my rope can take), **ship and gear** (what I fly and carry). This page
specifies the tree, restates the rope, and sets the money targets both doors are tuned to.

## 1. The one-breath sentence

> *"I level my pilot to specialise, my rope grows by hauling weight, and I buy or steal gear."*

If a new player cannot say that after twenty minutes in either door, something on this page has
failed (doc 10 §5.5, doc 17 sentence test).

## 2. The Pilot Tree

### 2.1 Shape

- **Six combat branches** shared by both doors — Gunner, Bruiser, Rigger, Conjurer, Juggernaut, Ghost
  (`01` §4) — plus **Industry**, which exists only in Adventure.
- Each branch: three tiers of three nodes and one **capstone** (ten nodes). Tier 2 opens at 2 points in
  the branch, tier 3 at 4, the capstone at 6.
- **Evolutions** — named cross-branch nodes — open when two branches each hold 5 points.
- **Power is concentrated at depth.** Tier-1 nodes are modest and useful to anyone; tier-3 nodes and
  capstones change rules. Spreading points makes a capable generalist; depth makes a monster. That is
  the owner's "you could spread all your points… but you'd be kind of weak."
- **Every node is felt within thirty seconds** of taking it, in the player's hands or on screen. No
  node is a percentage unless the percentage moves a threshold the player can see (a radius, a window,
  a count).
- **Mutually exclusive bends:** each power's three bends (doc 11 §3) are tier-2/3 Conjurer nodes;
  taking one bend of a power locks its other two for that run (Swarm) or until respec (Adventure).

**Evolutions** (two branches at 5): *Orbital Saw* (Gunner + Rigger: orbiting an anchor, your bullets
ricochet between it and its friends) · *Molten Avalanche* (Conjurer + Gunner: plough-captured rocks
glow white-hot and ignite what they hit) · *Wrecking Ball* (Bruiser + Rigger: a thrown body does not
stop at the first kill) · *Black Hole* (Conjurer + Ghost: Pull and Bomb on one point make a well that
eats everything for three seconds) · *Meteor* (Juggernaut + Conjurer: boost through the plough and
the whole pack rides your bow).

### 2.3 How each door fills it

| | Swarm | Adventure |
|---|---|---|
| Earned by | XP from kills (room kills 2.5×), chains, rounds, bosses (`04` §3) | XP from everything: kills, jobs, tonnes hauled, ore mined, modules built, trade profit, discoveries |
| Pace | ~14 points by round 10, ~32 by round 30 | ~10 by hour 3, ~25 by hour 15, ~40 by hour 40 |
| Choice | three cards per level, in the breath | the full tree, any open node |
| Keeps | nothing — every run starts empty | everything; respec at a trainer for a fee |
| Branches | six (Ghost partial), no Industry | all seven |

### 2.4 The screen

The existing radial **Research** screen (`02` C9, `src/ui/screens/techTree.js`) is the right shape: rebuild it
as the Pilot Tree — seven sectors, rings as tiers, the capstone at the rim. Research points and the
credits column go. In Swarm the same nodes appear as cards; the full tree is shown on Run Over (your
run's path lit) and in the Hangar.

## 3. Line Class (unchanged ladder, new meaning)

Earned by tonnes moved in either door (`src/data/lineProgression.js`, thresholds kept). Under R12 the
class no longer limits what you can latch; it sets the rope's **budget** — the tension it holds, the
yank it survives, the reel power under load and the reach (`03` §2.2). Doc 17 §1's names and moments
stand; read "you can now hold" as "you can now throw". Each class-up is still an event: the cable
thickens and changes colour, the screen names it, Static mentions it.

## 4. Ship and gear

- **Swarm:** gear is lent by the floor — wall guns, the Crate, Taps, the Forge — and returned at the
  end of the run (doc 12). Hulls are chosen at the start from those you have flown or unlocked.
- **Adventure:** gear is bought, found, salvaged, stolen or forged (doc 11 §7), and it shows on the hull.
  **Nothing basic is researched** (R7): the Wasp, Pulse Laser S and Autocannon S stop being tech-tree
  unlocks.
- **Slots limit loadouts** — "you can only have so much equipment at a time". A hull's slots are the
  real constraint on a build; the tree changes how what you carry behaves.

## 5. Money

### 5.1 Swarm — Scrap only

`04` §5. Every run starts at zero; doc 17 §3's scale; nothing persists.

### 5.2 Adventure — credits, tuned to the arc

Starting targets (`05` §0 stages; doc 17 §2's table, with the yard moved earlier):

| When | Cumulative earned (approx.) | The purchase that should be in reach |
|---|---|---|
| 20 min | 8 k | the first Line head (≤ 5 k) — it must change what your hands do |
| hour 1 | 15 k | a second gun family, or the Pelican |
| hour 2 | 35 k | a second hull that moves differently |
| hour 3–4 | 60 k | **the first claim** and its first machines |
| hour 6 | 150 k | a Forged gun at Vesta (job + fee + yard materials), or a heavy hull |
| hour 12 | 500 k | drones and defences across two or three claims |
| hour 20–30 | 2 M+ | the god-tier pieces (`05` §6) |

Hull prices today run 0 → 15 k → … → 720 k (Leviathan) → 4.2 M (Saucer) (`src/data/ships.js`).
Re-price every hull to the moment it should arrive in this table.

### 5.3 How prices are tuned

1. A deterministic economy bench runs a scripted player doing each activity (jobs, bounties, a trade
   loop, piracy, laser mining, rover mining, one yard at three sizes) for one simulated hour each and
   prints credits per hour. Reuse the existing economy derivations (`src/data/economyDerived.js`) where
   they fit.
2. Tune until **every activity at a stage pays within a third of every other**, and the purchase table
   above holds for a player doing what they like.
3. Then the owner plays the first three hours; the card decides.

## 6. Ledgers — fewer things to track

A player of Adventure should see exactly these: **credits**, **pilot level and points**, **Line Class
and tonnes**, **standing with four or five factions**, **Pit record**, and **marks** (cosmetic history
on the ship). Everything else in the code either becomes one of these or stops being shown:

- research points — **go** (R1);
- the fourteen faction standings — **fold** into four or five (`05` §7);
- career ladders (`src/careers/`) — **fold** into jobs and standing;
- provenance tiers on gear — **become marks** (how a piece looks and the story it carries), never power;
- the second "Ledger" screen, achievements that reward nothing, the fake scan-risk figure — **go**
  (doc 10 D10, already ruled; INV-04 found several still live).

## 7. Tests and saves

- Saves: old Adventure saves keep credits, Line tonnage, gear and standing (folded); research points
  become a one-time refund of Pilot Tree points by a fixed rate; nothing earned is lost.
- Tests that pin research costs, tech gates on basic gear, the run-XP announcer-only behaviour or the
  removed ledgers are rewritten or retired with "superseded by `design/overhaul/06`".
