# 10 — Recover playable flight

**Decision, 2026-11-24.** Replace the live flight path in one representative Swarm fight, then use that same path in Adventure. Preserve the fight, its density, the art that makes it readable, and existing saves. Treat the current engine boundaries as choices that can change. Agents working on flight stability or performance start here. The older plans and receipts supply history and measurements.

The outcome is a player who can see what happened, decide, act, and see the effect in roughly half a second or less during a crowded fight. Aim for an engine contribution of **100 ms or less at p95** from an immediate input to a correct visible response. A turn starts turning, a brake starts slowing the ship, a latch visibly takes hold, a throw changes the motion of the held body. Projectile travel and other deliberate delays retain their gameplay timing. Every live hold, including one covered by a recovery screen, counts as lost control.

**Order of value.** Fix lost or late control and multi-second world holds first. Then remove frame-time spikes, sustain real-time simulation, reach a steady 30 fps on the owner's normal machine, and work toward the steady 60 fps finish bar. Initial loading time ranks below all of these. Spend loading time on preparation where that protects a fight. A loading hang that prevents entry is a correctness fault.

### Why a broad rewrite is a poor first experiment

Rapier's solver already runs in WebAssembly. I re-tallied the saved Swarm round-six worker CPU profile by sample time and stack ancestry. Within the recorded authoritative step, **42.5%** was under the physics system, **23.2%** under its fixed-step body owner, and **6.7%** inside Rapier's step call. Those are nested costs. Across that same authoritative step, about **8%** of sampled time had a Wasm leaf. Tactical AI and fields accounted for about **17%** and **15%** respectively. Even making Rapier's step call free would improve that sampled step by only about **7%**, holding all other work constant. This is one loaded capture and a bound on a solver-only replacement in that workload. A compiled core could still be valuable if it also eliminates the object walks and per-body transitions around the solver. [Raw worker CPU profile](../../.devshots/no-freeze/perf2-r6/worker.cpuprofile) · [Current physics pipeline](../../src/core/physics.js#L289) · [Pinned dependency](../../package.json#L873)

Three prior changes show how smaller fixes can lead to a real replacement. Removing transforms from the journal cut applied records from about **117,000 to 3,200** in its measured run (5d1859f7de). Batching a sector's spawn notifications made consumers reconcile once instead of on roughly **75** spawns (36ac3f5531). Spacing read-model updates improved world speed in three of four pairs (6eb725ef34), while the worker still missed real-time under heavy input. Keep the separation that worked. Replace repeated read-model reconstruction with compact projections of committed state. Handle rare reliable outcomes separately from high-frequency poses.

The September Table Authority plan already recognized that dormant world records, decorative props, and active bodies have different needs. Today there are real activity tiers, sleeping bodies, reuse, indexed queries, and a shared pose table. Yet the crowded path still crosses activity classification, body reconciliation, physics, a gameplay spatial index, multiple consequence listeners, and several display representations. The opportunity is to **finish the change of ownership and remove the displaced work**. Adding another index or timed queue beside those paths would preserve the cost we need to remove. ## Target architecture

**Input → one authoritative combat step → complete, time-stamped combat state → visible response.**

### The fight owns its clock and its state

- While actively playing, the worker owns the fixed step and its deadlines. Pause, suspension, and deliberate time effects are explicit lifecycle transitions. Ordinary display congestion carries no authority to slow or stop the fight. The worker now uses these transitions in place of the one-second presentation heartbeat hold. Preserve the safe behavior of focus loss, held-key release, and suspend. Limit catch-up work per turn so recovery leaves room for fresh input. Sustained deadline misses mean the workload still needs to shrink. [Worker clock](../../src/core/wholeSimBrowserWorker.js)
- Keep held aim, steering, and throttle as current state. Preserve ordered button edges, especially fire, release, and Line changes. Timestamp input at capture and report the first physics step that actually acted on it. Poll input at a cadence that does not depend on the expensive part of a render frame. Keep picking and aiming tied to what the player was shown. Give the fight the keypress before camera and UI read-model work. [Current command flush](../../src/core/simLaneMain.js#L3005)
- Maintain one active body roster with stable ID and generation, contiguous hot fields, and reusable scratch. Add or remove membership on spawn, wake, sleep, or retirement. Let the solver own rigid-body state. Publish one compact committed motion view for gameplay queries and rendering. Share spatial candidates where the query semantics fit. A broad-phase candidate list, an exact contact, and an off-screen job query have different responsibilities. Keep continuous collision and the approach margin for fast bodies, incoming threats, ropes and thrown payloads. Keep cold records compact and schedule them by game time. Preserve world identity when a body re-enters or a save restores it. [Body ownership](../../src/core/sg02DynamicBodyOwner.js#L1892) · [Current activity lanes](../../src/world/activityRuntime.js#L2154)
- Apply force intents in batches. Resolve contacts, damage, provenance, effects on bodies, and destruction once in deterministic order. Send resolved outcomes to presentation and durable game state. Include player-caused contacts between two other bodies in the prompt consequence path. Fold resting contacts only where the rule already permits it. Audio, sparks, analytics, and mission notifications can consume the result on their own schedules. The current contact cap exempts contacts directly touching the player. That exemption alone does not protect the timing of a player-thrown rock hitting another ship. [Current impact carry](../../src/core/physics.js#L1033) · [Existing consequence owner](../../src/systems/collisionConsequences.js#L174)
- Share squad sensing and plans. Keep steering, attacks, and reactions that matter to the player responsive. Wake planning on meaningful changes and give lower-priority decisions deterministic deadlines. Retain the threat count, weapon pressure, and readable windups. Reuse an existing spatial index or body query where it meets the same correctness and cost requirements. A quieter battle is a different workload. For Adventure, distant records and scheduled changes become exact before they can affect the fight.

### The frame consumes the fight

- Publish a bounded latest-state stream. Pair the pose with body identity and the minimal metadata needed to draw the correct object. A frame may skip old motion. Required spawn, hit, and destruction outcomes retain identity and ordering. Save and purchase truth stays with the authoritative transaction owner. If a display falls beyond the bounded history, publish a coherent state with an event watermark so the reader can rejoin without replaying expired effects or resurrecting a retired body. Domain mirrors and rebuilds cannot strand active poses behind a queue. Move consumers onto the existing pose table or its direct successor, then remove the duplicate pose copy and application paths. Budget a browser fallback with the same freshness behavior.
- Interpolate over a short window of actual motion. Limit prediction to what can safely be shown for brief gaps and respond promptly to valid local input. A hit, latch, death, or physical consequence comes from the authoritative step. A long silence is a failed flight interval even if effects animate, a hold plate appears, or smoothing hides the stop. [Current bounds and reseat behavior](../../src/render/snapshotFence.js#L13)
- Keep the normal frame small. Acquire input, accept the current complete state, update camera and visible instances, render, then spend remaining time on optional work. Update HUD numbers and labels on meaningful change. Batch DOM writes and handle layout at resize or invalidation. Keep reticle, selected target, Line, imminent hazards, and hit feedback fresh. Keep static art out of dynamic pose and culling passes where possible. Group stable geometry and material variants, reuse buffers and pass resources, and prepare arrivals before interaction range. Keep asynchronous decode, file and save work outside the fight's deadline. Route asset churn away from the presentation interval the player is controlling.

Other engines illustrate how to organize this work. Box2D exposes contiguous moved-body and contact event batches after a solve. Unreal Mass groups like data into chunks and defers structural changes to boundaries. Wube rewrote how Factorio represented moving belt items and later parallelized only groups with independent ownership, resolving shared wakeups in an ordered batch. The common lesson is to remove whole passes and make ownership cheap before adding threads. [Box2D simulation](https://box2d.org/documentation/md_simulation.html) · [Unreal Mass](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/overview-of-mass-entity-in-unreal-engine?lang=en-US) · [Factorio belt representation](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-176) · [Factorio multithreading](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-364)

## How to hill climb

Judge in this order. A change can be a useful local result while the flight verdict stays red. Compare the visible result first. Use intermediate timings to pick the next cut. When making a stage free could barely move the whole frame or the action tail, stop tuning that stage.

| Order | What is measured | Working bar |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Honest control and combat continuity. Observe action outcomes, current moving-body poses, and coherent motion under normal inputs. Count all uncommanded world holds. | No multi-second freezes or lost input. Immediate action-to-correct-visible-response about **≤100 ms p95**, with individual long failures investigated at their actual owner. No hide-and-catch-up as ordinary play. |
| 2 | World time versus real time, with pause and deliberate slow motion identified. Measure physics completion as well as the outer tick. | **≥0.97×** in the worst 10-second fight window, with no recurring slow-down or bursts. |
| 3 | Presented frame intervals, fresh motion, and readable actual frames. Check frame tails as well as median. | Steady **30 fps** at the floor, **60** at the finish. **Zero >100 ms frames** in representative flight on the target under ordinary conditions. Count missing presents as gaps. |
| 4 | Headroom of the actual critical path. Split input, worker step, publication, main frame and GPU. | Aim for **≤8 ms p95** on the crowded authoritative step, including its required work. A 60 Hz step or frame has **16.7 ms** of wall time. Leave slack for rare admissions and other app work. |
| 5 | Secondary efficiency once play is protected. | Retain capacity as more content reaches the same fight. Shorten loading where the live loop stays as good or improves. |

Alternate baseline and candidate on the same build family, hardware, settings, scene, and meaningful work. Compare exact input traces where the change preserves outcomes. For legitimate behavior changes, match the fight's density and compare player-facing results, accounting for any differences. One real paired result with a visible response beats a pile of unit checks. Use focused tests for rules the refactor touches, and update the determinism recording when a change intentionally alters simulation behavior. Inspect the result in normal play before declaring the engine recovered. For live comparisons use the repository game lock. The existing smooth and no-freeze scripts already acquire it internally.
