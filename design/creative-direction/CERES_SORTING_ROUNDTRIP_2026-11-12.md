# Ceres: two loads make a shift

## Decision

Keep the first LARK sorting shift's existing geometry and masses. A native two-original journey succeeds without staging either original at the scale. No further lightening, teleports, automatic delivery or extra menus are justified by this probe. The useful next expansion is outside this already connected small job, not another layer of LARK advice.

This follows [first-shift assembly](CERES_FIRST_SHIFT_2026-11-12.md) and [receipt closeout](CERES_SORTING_SHIFT_CLOSEOUT_2026-11-12.md). Baseline is commit `42a7101ee0b9a653e5f4f2a065c806c2cc85c0f1`, open draft PR #982, branch `experience-commissions/EC-G15-work-that-is-enjoyable`; actual origin is `coldshalamov/SpaceFace`. No production source change is proposed by this evidence packet.

## Physical route observed

An explicitly composed native simulation uses the Kestrel starter hull, existing flight/tether/yard/credits owners and production feature flags. Initial pilot, yard anchor and funds are fixtures. After native admission there are no entity position/velocity writes: movement is throttle, strafe, yaw and brake; connections use the actual tether fire and service releases.

1. The original 18-mass sorting motor is released, hauled over 100 WU and handed over at 15.0 simulation seconds.
2. The unloaded pilot returns to the original cassette's approach side by 33.02 seconds. The cassette remains at its native berth, not moved for the test.
3. The actual tether attaches to the original 16-mass cassette, its clamp releases, and its native delivery gate admits handover by 50.92 seconds.

Both the exploratory run and the final focused test passed this complete physical circuit (1/1 final tests). The final run also used actual handover commands to display the receipt-derived closeout, then parked the paid claim. It paid 133 cr for the motor and 103 cr for the cassette: 236 cr against the 80 cr claim, a 156 cr margin. Both originals retained their initial condition throughout the flight (83.5% and 94.5%, not pristine new machinery). The two heavy remnants stayed attached and the whole job was not falsely completed. This is stronger than the earlier two-receipt closeout fixture, which explicitly staged receiving poses. It does not replace the older cold-owner restoration evidence and is not visual review.

No new asset is needed for this loop. Existing model ownership remains with local production agents. There is no new wishlist item merely to decorate an already functioning route.
