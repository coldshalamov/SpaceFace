## What this correction established

A prior revamp completion claim was revoked because loader success, mesh presence, clay renders, render
counts, and self-authored tables did not demonstrate finished professional assets. Many surfaces still lacked
convincing material response, role/faction identity, live-route proof, and independent visual judgment.

That episode established durable lessons:

- geometry and a passing loader are necessary but not sufficient;
- a lit beauty render cannot replace the real game camera and player-route scene;
- flat or generic materials, copied wear recipes, and metadata marked `PRO` do not prove visual quality;
- screenshots must be current, representative, and tied to the asset actually routed in default play;
- asset tables and evidence counts drift and must never become global status authority;
- modeling, surfacing, and life/polish should be applied where they solve visible gaps, not repeated to satisfy
  a quota;
- techniques such as bakes, trim sheets, decals, texture paint, procedural nodes, image-assisted masks, and
  hand-authored geometry are tools to choose from, not a universal checklist;
- exporter/reachability checks, player-route evidence, performance evidence, provenance, and independent
  visual review are complementary requirements.

## Historical evidence

The original session produced manifests, authored-source files, render collections, deficiency notes, and
tracking tables under the asset evidence and `.devshots` paths. Those artifacts may help diagnose provenance
or recover unfinished source work, but their dated counts and `PRO` labels are not current truth. Verify every
claim against the live manifest, runtime maps, checks, and current captures before relying on it.

Do not recreate the original mandatory technique counts, render quotas, fixed self-score thresholds, or
universal skin recipe. Those mechanisms produced process theater and samey surfaces without guaranteeing a
better game.
