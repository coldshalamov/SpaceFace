# Mode, progression and campaign composition

## Shared verbs, different pressure

Crucible is the rapid learning and replay surface. It should make one physical rule easy to notice, offer a distinct counter, then give earned breathing room to select a useful change. Adventure attaches the same rules to valuable objects, work, law and memory. Do not create two incompatible shove, mass, status or physics laws merely because their reward pacing differs.

### Crucible arc

Opening: clear own-hull and threat visibility, useful starting gun, one nearby object worth manipulating, obvious exit geometry. Let the first physical opportunity emerge from ordinary play rather than a compulsory combo tutorial.

Early variation: add one role that answers the player's current habit. Keep light enemies vulnerable to earned physical abuse. Introduce a specialist with a visible tool and recovery interval rather than inflate everyone's health.

Midrun: draft alternatives change handling, resource rhythm or geometry use. The player sees the cost as well as the benefit. Preserve a viable gun-first line; do not make specialized rig mastery the only way to afford survival.

Late: combine previously learned roles with topology and finite competing duties. A larger encounter should become more interesting, not less readable. Keep an escape/recovery opportunity after the player wins meaningful space.

End: cause-readable death or victory, seed/build identity, responsive retry and optional sharing via existing systems. Do not put a speculative online leaderboard on the finish path.

### Adventure arc

First contact with the game: ordinary work or a fight already in progress. The player can engage or decline. Navigation and control are taught at the point of need. One useful upgrade is earned and felt through a real service/fitting route.

Competence: small industrial, rescue, delivery and conflict jobs create different physical decisions. The player sees why cargo mass, route choice and witnesses matter. A failure produces a recoverable obligation or opportunity rather than erasing the session.

Ownership: the hull becomes recognizable through selected scars, fittings and relationships. Industry can provide supporting income but remains tied to finite inputs, output and physical intervention. The player has a reason to revisit a district.

Strangeness: a sensor mismatch, unusual residue or repeated animal route appears inside normal work. Knowledge adds distinctions; it does not create the physical phenomenon. A first ecology site allows observation and intervention before requiring combat expertise.

Ancient competence: a machine performs a real action on a real object, revealing access and constraint rather than a dialogue tree. Learn one specific procedure; don't unlock all ancient machinery with a generic reputation number.

Convergence: selected human obligations, biological discovery and machine procedures intersect in existing story geography. The campaign closes its actual character and world arc without requiring the player to farm the Vethari or finish every optional research branch.

## Build identities worth proving

A nimble interceptor uses precision and fast recovery; a tug turns heavy workpieces into positional power; a gun-heavy workhorse protects a lane with reliable damage; a salvage/research hull trades combat authority for cargo, sensing and controlled intervention. These are **demonstration goals**, not four new ships or classes. Use current hulls/modules first.

For each retained identity, demonstrate one thing it does well and one meaningful disadvantage. A tradeoff must be physical, economic or procedural enough to influence decisions. A comparison tooltip is not proof of gameplay distinction.

## Unlock philosophy

Information → access/safety → manipulation → rare transformative technology. Sterile human derivatives should let useful alien principles enter ordinary work without turning every pilot into an active infection. Originals remain limited by capability, containment, acquisition and context—not random arbitrary malfunction.

Avoid a new universal “Alien Tech” tree. Place analysis in existing scanner/research, containment in ship/cargo, coupling in physics rigs, and permissions in discovery/protocol. Each new unlock must have a demonstration mission or situation that makes its value tangible.

## Time and scope

Do not promise a campaign-hour count from paper. Measure current routes, including overhead, repairs and learning. Where the game needs more length, prefer better decisions and consequences over repeat quotas. Where the player gets stuck, remove unclear prerequisites before lowering all prices or giving free late-game tools.
