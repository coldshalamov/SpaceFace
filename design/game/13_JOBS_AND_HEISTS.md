# 13 — Jobs and heists: the five towns, written out

**From the lead designer, 2026-11-10.** These are written so that nobody has to invent a mission. Each is a physical problem with at least two ways through, a thing that goes wrong, and something left behind. People and places are from the sector cards. Where a job name matches an existing one in the game (`lw_…`), extend that one; do not make a second.

**The shape of every job:** *see it → the problem → two ways → what goes wrong → what is left.* Lines are eight words or fewer. Nobody briefs you in a menu if they can hail you from a window.

---

## Helios Prime — home

### Job: The Empty Plinth *(the first job in the game)*
**See it:** one dark gap in the Candle Fleet's crescent. Orla Venn hails: *"You've got a rope. We've got a lamp on the wrong side of the harbor."*
**The problem:** the lamp is the size of your ship, fragile, and across a harbor full of traffic at shift change.
**Two ways:** tow it slowly along the service lane behind a tug — or take it through the Slipway and sling it across in one go.
**What goes wrong:** Late Benny's bus pulls out. Brake and the lamp swings past you into the lane; or thread the gap.
**What is left:** the lamp lit, yours hung beside it. Scratch the lamp and it hangs there scratched, forever. Static: *"Somebody put the Venn lamp back up."*

### Job: Five Minutes Late
**See it:** Benny's bus dead in the lane, forty passengers, the horn about to go.
**The problem:** a bus is heavier than you can pull. It is not heavier than you can *push*, or than the Slipway can throw.
**Two ways:** nose it to the dock an inch at a time — or hook it to a passing tug and let the tug do it for a cut.
**What goes wrong:** the shift horn sounds halfway. The lane fills.
**What is left:** Benny owes you. From now on the bus will carry your cargo one stop, free, if you can catch it.

### Job: Borrowed, According to You
**See it:** Mr. Tolliver's tug *Kind Regards* is towing a family's cradle away from the crescent. *"Nothing personal. It's itemised."*
**The problem:** the cradle is on his line, not yours.
**Two ways:** pay what is owed and take the tow yourself — or wait for the Slipway, cut his line with a passing load, and catch the cradle before it drifts into the Tower's lane.
**What goes wrong:** the Tower's patrol watches the lane. A cut line is theft.
**What is left:** the cradle back on the crescent, or in the impound where you can see it. Tolliver remembers either way, pleasantly.

### Heist: The Counterweight *(Line Class II)*
The Slipway works because of a counterweight the size of a house, visible from your first minute. Pell at the front desk will tell you nobody moves it. At Class II you can. Move it and the Slipway throws twice as far — across the whole harbor, onto the Tower's side. The Tower would prefer it did not. The Fleet would like it very much.

### On the street *(no job needed)*
- A tug loses a crate at the Slipway. Catch it and return it, or do not.
- At Lamps-Up, one lamp flickers. Bump its housing and it catches. The family waves.
- Benny's bus is always somewhere on its route. You can Line it and ride.

## Tethys Junction — the crossroads

### Job: Wrong Address
**See it:** through the gaps in the Noon Train, a blue-banded crate on the wrong dock. Nurse Ilse Park: *"Blue band. Cold to the touch. That's mine."*
**The problem:** the crate is on the far side of the yard and the trains do not stop.
**Two ways:** bump the right switch paddle so the next string carries it to her — or go and get it, crossing three live tracks with a fragile box on a rope.
**What goes wrong:** Points Dunmore has routed a second string through. *"The instruction was valid."*
**What is left:** the clinic light green. Or a clinic light amber, and a crate somebody else is now selling.

### Job: Free Lunch
**See it:** Big Ansel's shutter is down at noon. That never happens.
**The problem:** his supply crate is third in a string of forty industrial containers, moving.
**Two ways:** switch the whole string into the siding and pick it out — which stops Customs for ten minutes — or Line the one container off a moving train.
**What goes wrong:** the Pitborn half of the station charges for the siding.
**What is left:** the shutter up, a queue of ships at it, a dish chalked with your name. Miss, and it stays shut until tomorrow and everybody in the lane is short with you.

### Job: Switching Fee
**See it:** a yard shunter — a stubby engine built to shove container strings — idle at a berth with a price on it.
**The problem:** you could rent it. You could earn it. You could just take it.
**Two ways:** do one honest hour moving strings for Points and it is yours to borrow — or Line it out of the berth at night.
**What goes wrong:** the shunter is stronger than your ship. On a rope, it tows *you*.
**What is left:** a shunter you can call on in Tethys, with the paperwork or without.

### Heist: The Whole String *(Line Class III)*
Forty containers on one coupling. At Class III you can take the lot — off the track, out of the yard, between the two halves of the station, with Customs on one side and the Pitborn on the other, both of whom believe some of it is theirs. Kessler will have the weights by morning. *"Twelve point four. Again. Interesting."*

### On the street
- The Noon Train. Every day. You can ride it out of the sector.
- A switch paddle left half-thrown. Bump it either way and see who complains.
- Customs holds a ship at the gate. Shove the queue and it is your fault.

## Ceres Belt — the mine

### Job: Winching
**See it:** a load twice your size hanging in a mouth of the Rock, and Old Hob looking at it. *"Easy, girl."*
**The problem:** it cannot be flown out. It can be *swung* out, on the fixed anchors.
**Two ways:** three careful anchor-to-anchor swings — or one long swing that clears the mouth by a ship's width.
**What goes wrong:** the Quarry Bell. Shift change. Everything in the mouth starts moving at once.
**What is left:** your swing chalked on the anchor board. This job is the lesson every later Ceres job relies on.

### Job: The Ninth Mouth
**See it:** the headcount board reads *9 IN / 8 OUT*. Dessa Kroll has stopped the shift.
**The problem:** one barge is still inside, in the mouth nobody uses, and its lamp line is dead.
**Two ways:** go in dark and feel your way — or tow a string of lamps in ahead of you and relight the line as you go.
**What goes wrong:** the barge is wedged. Pull the wrong way and the rock comes with it.
**What is left:** *9 OUT*. The Ninth Mouth lit, permanently, with your tag at the lip. Kroll: *"Nine went in. Nine come out."*

### Job: Bell Load
**See it:** a hauler's load has broken loose and is drifting toward the Bell.
**The problem:** it is going to ring it — hard enough to crack it.
**Two ways:** catch the load — or let it go and *ring the Bell on purpose*, softly, with something small first, to clear the lane under it.
**What goes wrong:** Tally Marsh wants the load, not the lane.
**What is left:** a Bell that rings true, or one that rings cracked from now on. Everyone in the belt can hear which.

### Heist: The Door *(Line Class IV)*
The Ninth Mouth has a door at the back, and what is behind it is the reason the mouth was closed. At Class IV the door moves. This is a Weight-thread beat: whatever is in there reads heavier than it should.

### On the street
- Ring the Bell. Everyone swears at you on open radio. It never gets old.
- A barge backs out of a mouth without looking.
- Loose ore at every mouth. It is somebody's. It is also right there.

## Vesta Forge — the forge

### Job: A Plate with Your Name on It
**See it:** the Pour. Bridge Achter: *"Show me you can hold it."*
**The problem:** carry a white-hot plate from the furnace to the fitting berth before it cools past working heat — without touching anything, because it burns what it touches.
**Two ways:** the long clear route at a crawl — or the short route under the coolant fall, timing the gap.
**What goes wrong:** clip the fall and the plate quenches in a burst of steam: ruined, and now you owe Drip a favour.
**What is left:** the plate, stamped, on your hull. The die on the foundry wall. This is how every Forged gun in Adventure is made.

### Job: The Cold Side of the Furnace
**See it:** the coolant trench is cracked and Drip Moreau's crew cannot reach the far end.
**The problem:** the patch has to be held in place while it sets, in the spray, beside the hot side.
**Two ways:** hold it yourself — or Pin it there and go and deal with the hauler that is about to come through.
**What goes wrong:** the Pour does not wait.
**What is left:** a trench that works, and a foundry that pours faster for everyone. Or one that does not, and prices that say so.

### Heist: The Plate Straight from the Pour *(Line Class V)*
Not the plate they give you. The big one, the one meant for a warship, as it comes out. It is too heavy and too hot for anybody. At Class V it is too heavy and too hot for anybody *else*. Yusuf Tane will fit it, no questions, and say only: *"Don't read it. Fly it."*

### On the street
- A cooled plate left on the apron. A fine thing to throw.
- The coolant fall. Fly a hot thing through it.
- The furnace door opens on a rhythm. Things can go *in*.

## Sker Haven — the den

### Job: Second Owner, Same Hull
**See it:** a hull hanging on the Chop Line that you last saw flying. Its pilot is arguing at the gate.
**The problem:** it is half apart. The parts are in bins along the Line, and the claws are still working.
**Two ways:** buy it back piece by piece from Tinker Zell — or take the pieces off the Line while the claws are busy, and tow the hull out through the gate with the Door watching.
**What goes wrong:** Boss Vane notices. *"My gate. My weather."*
**What is left:** a pilot who has their ship and owes you, or a favour owed to Vane that you will be asked for.

### Job: A Favor That Does Something
**See it:** Vane wants a thing brought to the Chop Line. He does not say what it is. It is a patrol cutter.
**The problem:** it is crewed, armed and inside Dione's lane.
**Two ways:** lead it here — it will follow a ship carrying Vane's mark — or disable it and tow it the whole way.
**What goes wrong:** it calls for help, and the help is real.
**What is left:** Vane's mark on your hull. The gate opens for it. Dione remembers it.

### Heist: Off the Line *(Line Class VI)*
The big hull on the last hook has been there since before you arrived. It is Vane's own. At Class VI you can lift it off — with Vane watching, because he is always watching. Whether that makes you his enemy or his successor depends on what you do with it in the next sixty seconds.

### On the street
- Feed the claws anything. Parts come out the other side.
- Market Night: the gate closes with you on whichever side you are on.
- A hull drifts in with nobody aboard. Finders keepers is, here, the law.

## The long game: your yard

Later, and built on what exists (the Asteroid Works and the automation the game already has): **you drill into a rock and it becomes yours.** A drill head, then a hopper, then a little factory that mines itself and makes parts. You can see it working from flight. Haulers you did not hire start calling at it.

And then somebody notices. **A raid on your yard is a Swarm round on your own ground** — your walls, your traps, your turrets, your freight lane, and a wave with a name. Everything you learned in the Pit, defending something you built. That is the two doors becoming one house.

Build order when the time comes: one drill → one hopper you can watch fill → one turret you place by towing it → one raid. Not an empire screen. A place you can fly up to. *(EC-G05.)*
