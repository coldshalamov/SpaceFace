# 16 — The story, shown

**From the lead designer, 2026-11-10.** The story is already written and already wired: `docs/worldbuilding/story/THE-STORYLINE.md` is the canon, `src/story/campaign47a/` plays it, and `design/program/finish/campaigns/W1/JOURNEY.md` arranges it into four acts. **Nothing here changes the plot.** This page is about the one thing the audit found wrong with it: the player meets a good story as text boxes. Every beat below says what physically happens in front of the ship.

## The rule

**A story beat is something that happens in front of you, with weight in it.** If a beat can be delivered by a popup, it has not been staged yet. The mystery is about a mass that does not add up — so every beat should put something on the player's rope that is heavier, or lighter, than it ought to be. The player *feels* the clue before anyone explains it.

Three staging tools, all existing: **a thing on the Line**, **a ship doing something on screen**, **a person at a window**. One line of speech at a time, twelve words at most, from a face.

## The premise, as the player meets it

You are **Wren**, flying the **Tessera**. You took a courier contract — **47-A** — to carry a sample. On paper it weighs one thing. On your rope it weighs another. Somebody already knew the difference, to the kilogram: **12.4 tonnes**. The rest of the game is finding out who counts the weight, and deciding what to do when you can count it too.

*On screen, Act I is titled "Cold Start", not "The Pit" — the Pit is Swarm's name now.*

**The "Rope" column is the pacing.** The story never requires a Line Class the player cannot have reached by playing to that point — but every act's set piece is noticeably easier one class up, which is the reason to go and move weight in the towns and the Pit between books.

## The six interludes

They are wired already as authored encounters. Stage each as **one thing happening on the route between towns** — a ship you pass, a wreck you recognise, a broadcast you fly through — never as a pause. The player may fly straight past. That is allowed. It should cost them something they would have liked.

## The five doors

The endings exist and are chosen by what the player actually did. Each gets **one last physical act**, performed by the player, not watched.

| Door | Canon name | The last thing you do with your hands |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | A Clean Uniform | you tow the evidence to the Concord and put it on *their* scale. It reads right, for once |
| **B** | Same Silence | you carry it to the Fence at Nyx and put it in a hatch. The light goes green. Nobody saw you |
| **C** | Only Honest | you take it through the wormhole and let go of the line |
| **D** | The Ledger Continues | you hang it at your own yard, lit, where anyone can weigh it |
| **E** | The Next Run | you cut it loose in a lane, and the board offers you another contract |

After any door the world keeps going, and the towns remember which one you walked through.

## How to build this and check your own work

- **One book at a time, in order.** B0 first: it is also the opening ten minutes (EC-G10).
- **The staging test:** play the beat with the text switched off. If you cannot tell what happened, it is not staged.
- **The weight test:** in the beat's capture, point at the moment the player *feels* something is the wrong weight.
- **Do not rewrite a line of the novel.** Shorten for the screen; keep the meaning; keep the names.
- **Checks:** the story has deterministic references. Staging changes what is on screen, not what is true. If a reference moves, find out why before anything else.

**Ways this gets faked.** A portrait added to the same popup. A cutscene. A new character who explains the plot. The Skim as a longer text box with a sound effect.
