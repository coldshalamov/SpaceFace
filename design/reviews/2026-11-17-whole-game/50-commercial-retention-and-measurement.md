# Selling the throw and earning the second evening

**Product hypothesis.** The audience to test first is the action player who enjoys pulling off an improbable stunt, seeing exactly why it worked, and trying a harder version on the same seed. Adventure gives that same skill a second use, saving a shift at the dock as readily as wrecking a fighter in the Pit. That makes the two modes a credible pair. It also gives a prospective buyer an immediate answer to “what do I actually do?”

The repo's “Light ships are ammunition” is already a sharper pitch than its package description. The store-copy table and the onboarding module carry related lines. The five listed store screenshots date to September 6–11. Four hide the HUD. None of their captions identifies Swarm, an enemy pileup or a round ending. The public beta and the reviewed source also present different entrances. If the store sends someone to the old entrance, improvements on master cannot rescue that impression. ## A saleable demo from what already exists

**Proposed first trailer, 32 seconds.** Open in the player's camera on a Wasp approaching the reticle. At seconds 1–4 the pilot latches, slews around a heavy load and cuts. Hold the camera through the impact and let the wreck travel out the other side. At seconds 5–10 a pilot slows a swinging load into a working berth, giving the same skill another use. A person is waiting for it. At 11–18 show a materially different failed approach to the Foundry followed by an intelligible purchase and a successful second attempt. At 19–26 show the changed harbour on return. End on eight words of actual player intent or the game's existing hook, then a playable demo action. Keep some HUD, use actual recorded frames, and cut scenes the release candidate cannot reproduce. The Steam trailer guidance also favors gameplay from the player's perspective. [Valve’s trailer documentation](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/trailer) · [Recorded-frame export](https://github.com/coldshalamov/SpaceFace/blob/4bbe545d550a4c43e720df18c254b1c8d190d8a1/src/ui/screens/clipExport.js#L390)

**Three screenshot commissions.** One readable flight frame has the player, line, two opposing ships and a visible destination on screen. A second shows the Armory in the act of an understandable choice, with each card still legible at store size. A third is the same harbour on return, a previously dark lamp burning and work taking place beneath it. Retain one factual caption per image. Keep the original captures, build receipt and route. Test at thumbnail size without the caption first. If the viewer loses the action behind a background planet twice, change composition before buying a new model.

**A complete invitation to play.** Deliver one public path from title → first intentional hit → next wave purchase → loss with an attributable cause → new attempt under control. Let Adventure deliver an independent short story with money, a named witness and a persistent visual result. A player who quits after those two episodes got a finished experience. Require earned traversal before showing later bosses or towns as promises in the demo. Keep challenge seeds and optional side modes accessible to interested players after first contact. No price or expected sales volume can be inferred from this repository.

## Reasons to return

| Time horizon | Concrete reason to play again | Failure that would reverse the benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Within a minute | “I cut too early. Put me back on the same layout.” Leave the death bearing visible and put Retry under the same input. | Loading and reconfiguration erase the player's motor memory. A generic grade hides the corrective action. |
| Next 20 minutes | A draft changes the answer to one familiar threat. Let the player compare the last attempt with the new one. | Spending is a pure stat tax or dying immediately buys the next permanent item faster than playing. |
| Next evening | Return to a named berth altered by a decision, or try one equitable seed and compare a ghost with a friend. | Missing a day removes progress, accumulated hangar ranks decide the challenge, or Continue loses the entrusted cargo. |
| After a clear | A thirty-round victory is recognized and banked. Endless continuation expresses mastery. Adventure offers another outcome with a recognizably different place or service. | The credit roll or reward masks a state that never persists. Extra rounds differ only in health totals. |
| Word of mouth | Let a player share a short, real clip and optionally its versioned setup code. Receiving players can try the situation without inheriting a stranger's campaign. | A generated title card stands in for a missing clip, or a matching seed silently uses different rules or permanent stats. |

The game already supports local ghost tapes, content hashes, share envelopes, achievements and a death recap. A share-code format and a local ghost are credible substitutes for a first-season online service. Compare a fresh profile and a heavily upgraded profile on the same stock Daily through the normal UI. Record effective hull, shields, funding, perks and offered equipment before first input. Separately use two clean profiles to verify setup-code and ghost import, including a readable failure on incompatible versions. Resolve those comparisons before funding ranked infrastructure or multiplayer. ## How to learn whether it sells or retains

The repository includes a session observer and a report that defines early milestones. Its firstSwing target allows 180 s. The design's execution targets are much tighter and use several different start points. Record *four clocks* from launch to interactive title, mode selection to control, control to first deliberate release with consequence, and death to control regained. Export a small, consented playtest summary with build, route, device, failure reason and active/wait time. Existing code already supports local recording. Individual raw input logs have no place in the commercial funnel. [Session report and targets](https://github.com/coldshalamov/SpaceFace/blob/4bbe545d550a4c43e720df18c254b1c8d190d8a1/src/observability/sessionReport.js) · [Observer](https://github.com/coldshalamov/SpaceFace/blob/4bbe545d550a4c43e720df18c254b1c8d190d8a1/src/observability/sessionObserver.js) · [Current acceptance baseline](https://github.com/coldshalamov/SpaceFace/blob/4bbe545d550a4c43e720df18c254b1c8d190d8a1/design/program/fleet/FINISH-LINE.md)

| Question | Exact denominator and event | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Did the store explain the game? | Among unfamiliar viewers shown one muted cut for ten seconds, count those who can name what the player grabbed, released and hit. Log guessed genres separately. | Start with 6–8 people per audience for qualitative diagnosis. A recurring wrong explanation changes the first shot. These numbers are too small to forecast conversion. |
| Did interest survive the click? | Unique demo starters reaching first real control / all eligible demo starters on the same build. Record boot errors and voluntary abandonment, and split by GPU/device and cold vs warm load. | If a group disappears before control, improve delivery and device readiness before tuning round difficulty. Include failures in the denominator. |
| Does the action teach itself? | First-time players producing an intentional contact after a release / players with first control. Verify intent in observed sessions, log the physical outcome in others. Track time and extra prompts. | Coach one verb or improve target readability. Compare matched builds and keep assisted vs unassisted runs separate. |
| Does loss create another attempt? | Players regaining control in a voluntary second run / players who saw their first genuine loss. Report Retry clicks and abandonment during reload separately. | A click-to-control drop funds fast retry. A low click rate with quick reloads sends the team to death explanation, fairness and build variety. |
| Does Adventure persist in memory? | Reached return visit / players who completed the first authored job. Ask what changed without prompting. Among players invited back, record voluntary D1 and D7 return in fixed windows. | Investigate loading/persistence if return attempts fail. If players return but cannot name a change, invest in making the change visible. |
| Will someone buy or recommend it? | Within a defined traffic source compare wishlists / tracked visits. After purchase, track paid returns/refunds and review counts and sentiment by build when available. Ask every tester what would stop a recommendation and preserve negative answers. | Hold traffic mix, launch build and asset placement stable when testing creative. Code feedback into comprehensibility, wait, control, fairness, value and other. Avoid attributing a sales lift to a simultaneous build and trailer change. |

For a small formative release assessment, observe eight unfamiliar pilots drawn to action and exploration. Seek six uncoached intentional throws, five voluntary retries among eight eligible first losses, and six correct accounts of visible consequences among eight eligible return visits. Recruit to the denominator. Never count a person who never reached a loss as a retry failure in that metric, and still report where that person left. One reproducible save loss, freeze or softlock takes priority over the cohort counts. These thresholds are design choices. Collect enough comparable traffic for confidence intervals before drawing market conclusions. Longer playtime caused by idle loading, forced repetition or slow travel is a regression even when total minutes rise.

Steam Playtest can provide a separate test application, and Steamworks UTM reports count tracked visits and attribute eligible conversions within a 72-hour window. Use tracked visitors as that conversion denominator. The repo’s App and Windows depot IDs are blank templates. Nothing in that fact establishes private partner status. Price testing, wishlist counts, refunds and actual review sentiment require evidence outside the repository.
