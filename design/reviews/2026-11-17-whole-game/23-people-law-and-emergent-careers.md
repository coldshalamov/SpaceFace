# The people aboard, the name on the hull and who gets to tell the story

A telling detail in SpaceFace is that a pilot can return a stolen ship and still get in trouble for its engine. The game remembers the individual part. This is the foundation of a distinctive salvage career. A well-timed rescue might earn no cash, a beautifully recovered hull might be somebody else's, and an apparently profitable job might cost a crew member more than it pays. Teach these distinctions in an ordinary shift. A new criminal reputation meter would communicate less.

## One proposed Sker shift

Extend the commissioned *Second Owner, Same Hull* at Sker. A damaged civilian ship reaches the Chop Line under another crew's tow. The pilot sees one living person signal from a window, then sees the tug start hauling. Both clues concern the same hull. The registry has an unresolved claim. The nearby claimant wants the ship back. A fence wants its expensive fitted part. Use the existing ship, its component instance and the two actual buyers. The proposed encounter connects these owners. The complete shift still needs to be built and flown. [Original job](https://github.com/coldshalamov/SpaceFace/blob/4bbe545d550a4c43e720df18c254b1c8d190d8a1/design/game/13_JOBS_AND_HEISTS.md#L134)

**Return it intact.** The pilot can steady the hull and give the crew safe passage, then work the tow into the appropriate berth. Recognized title still requires the owner’s evidence. Once the transaction settles, keep the disposition available from the Fleet record. A later shipyard visit could show the same patched hull at work. That last appearance is proposed staging and must follow the vessel’s saved disposition.

**Strip it and leave.** The pilot may remove one fitted component and return the shell. At the lawful counter the attendant can point to the serial and the source hull. The player can pursue legitimate adjudication or take a discounted offer from a named fence. The amount comes from the real quotation. If someone was rescued on the way out, preserve that fact too. A person can do two things on the same shift that the game judges differently.

**Leave the dispute alone.** The player may simply tow the survivor clear and depart. The radio can acknowledge the rescue if the rescue owner confirms it. Avoid a compulsory villain, a forced capture or a morality score. Reuse one existing rival only when the encounter director has already admitted that captain into the scene.

## Acceptance for the owning agents

1. The world author and shipjacking owner produce a public Adventure route from approach to dock. An unfamiliar pilot should be able to point to who is aboard, who laid claim and what part is disputed before committing a permanent action. Keep this side story outside the first compulsory shift.
2. The economy and save owners take a save before extraction, after the shell is returned and before accepting a quote. Change the part's condition or berth, resume, and verify that the obsolete quote cannot pay. A second submission cannot sell a missing part. Verify an old save with ordinary untracked parts still trades normally.
3. The narrative owner replays three histories from the same starting scene. Inspect what the claimant says, what the shop will do and what the chronicler can legitimately publish. Preserve useful ambiguity in the dialogue while keeping authorship, payment and loss exact. Promote the shift when an observer can distinguish the outcomes at the later berth with the quest log closed.
