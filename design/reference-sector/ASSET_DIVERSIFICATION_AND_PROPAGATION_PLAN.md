# Long-Term Asset Diversification and Lived-World Propagation Plan

## Purpose

This plan explains how SpaceFace should expand asset breadth and world depth **after** the Ceres production benchmark establishes a proven art, composition, systemic and performance method.

It is not permission to bulk-generate or bulk-promote assets. It is a production roadmap for turning a small number of coherent kits and families into many distinct, believable sectors.

---

## 1. Asset strategy: families, states and compositions

Professional breadth does not come from a raw object count. It comes from a matrix:

```text
functional family
× faction/ownership language
× condition/state
× scale class
× composition
× activity
```

A single cargo-pod manufacturing standard can appear:

- clean at a lawful refinery;
- battered on a frontier hauler;
- breached in a wreck field;
- hidden inside a smuggler transfer;
- stacked in a customs yard;
- incorporated into player construction.

That creates coherence and variation simultaneously.

### 1.1 Core functional families

Build and maintain production-quality families in this order:

1. **Occupational ships** — miner, hauler, tanker, tug/tender, salvor, surveyor, rescue, customs, construction, express.
2. **Cargo/logistics** — pods, bulk containers, racks, clamps, transfer arms, platforms, catchers.
3. **Mining/processing** — drills, extraction masts, crushers, sorters, tanks, radiators, conveyors.
4. **Service/construction** — scaffolds, work lights, repair rigs, parts racks, power units, frames.
5. **Law/navigation** — scanner gates, inspection platforms, interdiction devices, transponder infrastructure.
6. **Salvage/criminal** — clamps, cages, hull racks, illicit docks, patched power and concealment hardware.
7. **Civilian/habitation** — shuttle docks, utility modules, comms, small habitat and observation elements.
8. **Wreck/aftermath** — functional ship sections, cargo remains, engine sections, cooling fires, stripped states.
9. **Hero landmarks** — admitted only after sector identity requires one.

### 1.2 State vocabulary

Every family should define only states that change story or function:

- new/maintained;
- working;
- overloaded/high-output;
- damaged;
- abandoned/cold;
- repaired/player-restored;
- faction-modified;
- under construction;
- stripped/salvaged.

Avoid state variants that are merely color swaps.

### 1.3 Faction/ownership language

Use layered identity:

1. shared industrial standards;
2. faction paint/markings;
3. faction modifications and maintenance philosophy;
4. local wear/hazard response;
5. role-specific signal behavior.

A faction should not require a completely separate asset for every prop. But a major faction's ships and signature structures must differ in silhouette, construction and operation—not paint only.

## 2. Production tiers

### Tier A — hero identity

Examples:

- player ships;
- faction capitals;
- major stations;
- Cathedral-scale wrecks;
- planets and signature infrastructure.

Requirements:

- unique silhouette;
- complete source/release/provenance;
- authored materials and LODs;
- normal-route G0–G7 acceptance;
- independent art review;
- performance evidence in intended composition.

### Tier B — occupational and meso-scale assets

Examples:

- miner/tug/salvor/tanker families;
- freight platforms;
- large worksite machinery;
- customs gates;
- construction frames;
- secondary wrecks.

Requirements:

- function-readable silhouette;
- reusable material roles;
- sockets/collision/LOD;
- camera-scale review;
- one live activity/use proof;
- family-level performance budget.

### Tier C — modular production kits

Examples:

- cargo pods;
- racks;
- tanks;
- lights;
- scaffolds;
- small utility modules;
- debris pieces.

Requirements:

- controlled manufacturing language;
- shared atlases/trim strategy;
- instancing/batching compatibility;
- state variants;
- composition rules;
- normal-camera survival for any identity-critical feature.

### Tier D — dressing

Examples:

- tiny debris;
- cable runs;
- light fixtures;
- decals;
- small markers;
- ambient particles.

Requirements:

- pooled/instanced;
- no gameplay dependence;
- aggressive LOD/culling;
- used to reinforce existing action, never substitute for it.

## 3. Asset promotion pipeline

For every candidate family:

1. **Need statement** — what player-facing gap does it close?
2. **Coverage audit** — what current live, candidate or donor assets already cover it?
3. **Role brief** — construction, function, scale, sockets, state and faction hooks.
4. **Gameplay-camera silhouette test** — before detail.
5. **Source re-authoring** — macro/meso form, material zones, load paths.
6. **Production surfacing** — PBR roles, textures, markings, emissives, wear.
7. **Collision/socket/LOD** — exact and functional.
8. **Release pipeline** — KTX2/Meshopt/manifests/render package as required.
9. **Technical acceptance** — G0–G6.
10. **Independent art verdict** — G7 or current equivalent.
11. **Live-use proof** — work/traffic/site integration at normal route.
12. **Composition verdict** — does it improve the sector enough to justify its cost?

An asset may be technically accepted but rejected from a composition if it adds noise, duplicates another read or weakens visual hierarchy.

## 4. Sector production recipe after Ceres

Do not deepen all sectors at once. Select contrasting cases that test the method.

### Candidate second sector — Helios

Identity:

- commerce, customs, repair, passenger and courier traffic;
- safer and brighter than Ceres;
- stronger civic order;
- high route convergence;
- opportunities for smuggling and inspection evasion rather than open ambush.

Asset emphasis:

- courier/express/civilian craft;
- customs and inspection infrastructure;
- repair yards;
- cargo and passenger transfer;
- billboards/memorials/utility infrastructure;
- clean maintained material states.

Microevent emphasis:

- inspections;
- delayed departures;
- repair/service;
- courier overtake;
- smuggler breakaway;
- civilian emergency;
- construction or station maintenance.

### Candidate third sector — Tethys

Identity:

- planetary gravity/atmosphere infrastructure;
- sling and skimming opportunities;
- mass-driver cargo;
- volatile-resource logistics;
- dangerous route geometry;
- piracy and enforcement around predictable orbital traffic.

Asset emphasis:

- tanker/harvester/catcher families;
- satellite and orbital service craft;
- mass-driver/coupling hardware;
- field and atmospheric presentation;
- high-heat/hazard material states.

Microevent emphasis:

- cargo launch/capture;
- tanker loading;
- orbital repair;
- stolen capsule;
- gravity/atmosphere emergency;
- patrol response;
- environmental kill aftermath.

### Candidate fourth sector — a lawless or research/anomaly region

Identity:

- minimal civic structure;
- improvised criminal/logistics infrastructure or scientific operations;
- more unpredictable events;
- unusual field/traversal rule;
- sparse but highly distinctive landmarks.

This tests whether the Ceres recipe generalizes beyond industrial density.

## 5. Sector identity sheet

Before producing assets for any sector, write one page containing:

- economic activity;
- dominant occupations;
- civilian behavior;
- criminal opportunity;
- law response;
- physical/traversal rule;
- landmark hierarchy;
- pocket topology;
- visual/material/light identity;
- sound identity;
- aftermath/memory layer;
- deliberate absences;
- reused families;
- genuinely new asset needs.

No new hero asset is admitted without this sheet.

---

## 6. Lived-world activity rollout

### Stage 1 — visible jobs

Each sector gets 1–2 job chains using existing owners.

### Stage 2 — response roles

Repair, rescue, patrol, salvage or construction actors react to current world state.

### Stage 3 — interruptible opportunities

At least one legal and one criminal opportunity emerges without a mission.

### Stage 4 — event chains

Choose 4–8 sector-appropriate microevents. Use participant reuse, cooldowns and causal continuation.

### Stage 5 — memory and aftermath

Use current world records, wrecks, cargo loss, repair, construction or route changes to leave visible consequence.

Do not globalize every event immediately. Promote event patterns after two sectors prove them.

---

## 7. Diversity metrics that matter

Do not track only counts. Track:

- role recognition without labels;
- unique silhouettes at normal scale;
- percentage of activity visible in camera, not merely sensor range;
- number of distinct job chains;
- number of player-interruptible opportunities;
- event repetition within a ten-minute watch;
- proportion of reused versus newly authored assets;
- state-variant usage;
- average visual-empty interval inside active pockets;
- performance cost per populated pocket;
- independent art KEEP rate;
- donor rejection/re-author rate;
- sector identity recall in playtest.

A useful player test is:

> After visiting three sectors, can the player describe each using what happens there and how it looks, rather than only its name or faction color?

## 8. Long-term asset waves

### Wave 1 — Ceres production benchmark

- 3–4 occupational ship identities maximum;
- 10–16 selected industrial props;
- existing hero assets;
- 4–6 microevents;
- current physics/VFX program;
- accepted propagation recipe.

### Wave 2 — shared production foundations

- cargo/logistics family;
- repair/service family;
- law/inspection family;
- salvage family;
- shared material/marking/LOD standards;
- performance and batching templates.

### Wave 3 — Helios identity

- commerce/civilian/customs composition;
- express/courier/passenger identities;
- 4–8 relevant events.

### Wave 4 — Tethys identity

- orbital/planetary industry;
- tanker/harvester/catcher identities;
- gravity/atmosphere opportunities.

### Wave 5 — aftermath and wreck ecology

Only after normal-route evidence identifies the right scale and density:

- 3–4 ordinary wreck source families;
- fresh/cooling/old/stripped states;
- aftermath integration;
- salvage and law/crime response;
- performance-bounded debris.

### Wave 6 — faction capitals and territorial infrastructure

- faction-specific hero structures;
- recognizable fleet/maintenance culture;
- route and law differences;
- high-cost assets admitted one sector at a time.

### Wave 7 — player-built exterior world

- exterior manifestations of asteroid operations;
- depots, transfer systems, frames, defenses;
- construction traffic;
- station assembly;
- visible transformation over one save.

---

## 9. Things to avoid

- More source-only mega-packs without an admitted integration route.
- Recoloring one hull into every occupation.
- Unique hero assets in every pocket.
- Prop scatter that obscures play.
- Events that need constant dedicated spawns.
- Sector identity based only on palette.
- Art passes disconnected from actual route composition.
- Acceptance based on contact sheets instead of gameplay.
- Frameworks that promise future breadth but add no visible present behavior.
- Galaxy-wide rollout before the Ceres method is accepted.
