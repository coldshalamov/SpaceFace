## Discover and commit

A distress cue and identifiable pod appear from a real incident or authored site—not a detached rescue marker.

Expose actual equipment, cargo, permission and capacity prerequisites before charging money or moving cargo. Required tools need a legitimate acquisition/service route. Preserve a decline/abort and a recovery path.

## Physical layout

A survivor pod lies between two pieces of cover. At least one lateral approach and one sheltered pull line are visible. The pod is sturdy enough for a recovery mistake but not a tank.

Measure actual reference hull/workpiece sizes, mass, cruise/earned speed, turn radius and camera depth before choosing final coordinates. Use [play-space guidance](../engineering/04_PLAYSPACE_AND_BALANCE.md); no coordinates in this brief silently override the current level.

## Playable phases

Locate survivor; choose corridor or suppressor target; attach/reel; transfer custody at a safe berth; meet the saved person later if selected.

## At least two approaches

Clear the firing lane then pull; move cover or disable the key attacker and extract while other threats remain.

## Failure and aftermath

A damaged pod changes medical/recovery needs. Loss records a consequence; taking no action leaves a comprehensible outcome.

## Reward and progression

Fair current rescue payment and one future recognition payoff rather than a large universal reputation bonus.
