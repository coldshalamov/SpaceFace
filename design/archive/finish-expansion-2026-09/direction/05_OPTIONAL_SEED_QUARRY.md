# Optional seeds beyond the selected finish scope

These are **creative candidates, not ready INFERENCE tasks**. A strong agent must compare each with existing AE/XA/PQ content, select a concrete scene and write a bounded native implementation packet before a weak worker builds it. They do not extend the critical path automatically.

## Human physics and work

**Tow-train rescue:** a tug loses one coupling in a loaded train. The player must redistribute tension before safely freeing the trapped last segment. Requires the current multi-body capability; do not add it solely for this scene.

**Cargo umbrella:** a freighter deliberately moves a sacrificial freight rack between itself and an attacker. The player can preserve its contents or use the rack as cover/ammunition. Use actual custody and collateral.

**Counterweight ferry:** a small work shuttle relies on a rotating counterweight rather than an invisible path follower. Helping or stealing the counterweight changes local work. Start with one authored site, not every transport.

**Powerful, awkward salvage:** a valuable heavy reactor module is easy to grab but difficult to brake near the buyer. The last kilometer is the challenge, not another ambush. Keep a safe receiving method.

**Billiards demolition:** a contract bans direct explosive fire near fragile stock but allows a controlled structural impact. Provide a conventional slower isolation method so it is not a trick-shot-only puzzle.

**Maintenance traffic conflict:** an unscheduled hauler crosses an active workpiece path. Reposition the traffic, pause the machine or accept a declared delay. Every participant has a real job and stopping condition.

## Combat and progression

**A specialist worth capturing:** disabling rather than killing one enemy recovers an unusual module, but towing it makes escape harder. Don't invent a boarding game; reuse current disable/custody.

**The raider that cuts the wrong line:** an imperfect line-cutting specialist can be baited into freeing your cargo or removing a hostile bridge. Requires shared admission rules rather than encounter-only exceptions.

**A heavy as a moving wall:** one capital tries to protect its working side instead of orbiting the player. Flank, shift its cover or disable its support. Its vulnerability is geometry, not repeated invulnerability phases.

**The expensive miss:** a prototype round lodges intact in a wreck. Recovering it is possible but risky. Treat it as a finite physical item; no free-ammo loop.

**Loadout rescue:** a disabled build can exchange one fitted item for a temporary useful service, then recover it through a job. Use existing economy and never force an irreversible trap.

**A fair rival feint:** a returning opponent visibly prepares a familiar attack, then aborts once to expose a different choice. Give the feint a real cost and avoid unreadable incessant deception.

## Ecological relationships

**Cleaner rendezvous:** a harmless organism eats one specific residue class from hulls. The player deliberately brings a coated workpiece into its feeding space. Its reward is an interaction, not a new health potion.

**Cargo-nesting scavenger:** a species carries loose containers to a nest. Following stolen freight reveals a location; returning the freight changes the nest. Do not count removed cargo twice.

**Brood edge:** a normally passive host defends only a visible brood volume. A thrown wreck—not the player's ship—can cross it and trigger the response. Attribute cause without making the entire sector hostile.

**Symbiont separation:** a dangerous host becomes less coordinated when a tiny relay partner detaches. The player can separate them rather than kill both. No implication that severance cures infection.

**A molt as temporary shelter:** an empty giant shell provides cover, then slowly cracks under use. It is not an enemy with a hidden health bar. Its finite structural role must be clear.

**The heat-source migration:** miners shut a refinery down, and the grazers that lived on waste heat follow departing ships. Turn an economic event into a physical ecological opportunity.

## Behavioral fossils and discovery

**Obsolete transponder recognition:** two strains respond differently to an old identification pulse. Compare behavior to a historical traffic record; don't let a scanner simply announce the answer.

**Inherited docking mistake:** a flock repeats a maneuver that only made sense before part of a station collapsed. The missing structure becomes evidence rather than a new lore paragraph.

**Fear with a delay:** a precursor tone causes one animal to flee, followed seconds later by unrelated hosts through local shared coordination. The delay lets the player map the relay path.

**The wrong ship shape:** an old host-shadow reacts to a silhouette, not faction or heat. Testing it requires a harmless shaped workpiece or existing hull comparison, not a disguise system rewrite.

**Conflicting instruments:** a biological sensor sees activity while a machine measures inert mineral geometry. A controlled intervention resolves one question but leaves the larger origin uncertain.

**A negative discovery:** a supposedly haunted site remains clean through every valid test. The finding changes a fraudulent quarantine or rumor, proving that uncertainty is not always a monster reveal.

## Ancient work and procedures

**Self-amputating Custodian:** a unit isolates and ejects its contaminated arm, then attempts a reduced repair operation. Keep biology's action physical; don't imply a nervous-system fungus can magically reprogram any robot.

**Stale work order:** a machine still services an absent structure. Recovering the correct historical reference lets it stop or redirect once. Its sadness is in behavior, not sentimental dialogue.

**Maintenance inheritance:** a failing machine hands a finite token and unfinished job to a smaller unit. Interruption changes who can legally complete the work.

**A preserved human mistake:** a Conservator keeps a dangerous modern workpiece intact because it satisfies an old class. The player must demonstrate the relevant change, not persuade a humanlike NPC.

**A boundary that moves for a reason:** a machine relocates a quarantine marker after a measured contamination event. The new safe path is visible before the old one closes.

**Deliberation through arrangement:** several Jury frames align around different measurements. A missing reference produces a visibly incomplete formation and no verdict, not a random response.

## Matter and machine crossover

**Resin inside a sorting field:** an admitted field separates loose debris by coupling while adhesive links oppose the sorting. The tension geometry becomes the puzzle. Needs proven bridges before production.

**Suppression refuge ecology:** prey gather inside a coherence-suppressed zone because a particular coordinated predator struggles there. Do not make the zone universally lethal or safe.

**Machine-warmed garden:** an ancient radiator sustains a colony. Repairing the radiator helps local machines and expands the habitat; turning it off is not automatically the moral solution.

**Sensor occlusion:** biological tissue blocks a machine's inspection path. Physical cleaning restores its correct classification without any software “hacking.”

**Growth around patrol timing:** organisms occupy safe gaps between a known sweep. Observe and exploit the pattern; avoid full online evolutionary simulation.

**Contaminated reclaimed material:** a Sorter carries an infected workpiece toward a clean foundry. Intercepting it creates a physical quarantine job and demonstrates why custody metadata matters.

## Regional change and human adaptation

**A refuge becomes a market:** reliable machine protection draws a modest human service outpost. First represent the changed service and traffic, not a sprawling autonomous city simulation.

**Insurance fraud as a playable scene:** someone arranges a collision near hazardous material to blame an outbreak. Evidence must come from actual motion/provenance, not an all-knowing accusation.

**Industrial derivative competition:** two buyers offer different uses for the same sterile material—better tow gear or a research instrument. The choice changes a useful capability, not just reputation.

**Contaminated used hull:** an existing secondhand ship comes with a declared removable residue problem and a visible price tradeoff. No hidden irreversible curse that punishes buying a ship.

**Quarantine as a logistical challenge:** a station remains alive because outside workers deliver sealed essentials. Help with a physical chain; don't build a whole public-health management game.

**A local trick the core forgot:** frontier pilots know a quiet passage technique that formal instruments classify poorly. Learn it through observation and a bounded test rather than cultural exposition alone.

## Texture with a mechanical anchor

**Working graffiti:** a warning is painted exactly beside the cable players are tempted to cut. Cutting it proves why the warning exists; no text-only collectible.

**An ordinary name:** a worker uses a practical nickname for a known species before the formal scanner label is earned. It helps identify behavior without leaking cosmology.

**A robot crosses the fight:** neutral machine traffic passes through a distant safe lane, continuing its job. Never use a fake invulnerable near obstacle purely for spectacle.

**A cleared scar:** returning after sterilization shows an absence shaped by the actual operation. Suppression alone cannot produce that scar.

**A residue in Shipworks:** one meaningful attachment scar remains on the correct hull after repair. It is a selected personal history, not an unbounded surface log.

**A missing bearing:** several trusted records point toward a coordinate removed from human charts. The late-game clue opens an optional route without forcing an apex reveal or unfinished expansion cliffhanger.
