# SpaceFace: the places between the contracts

**Authored experience treatment, 23 October 2026.** This is a direction for composing the game that exists, not a replacement campaign, a second simulation, or a claim that the scenes below have shipped. The implementation evidence and build requests distinguish existing behavior from proposed staging. The established B0–B7 story, endings, character restrictions and original expansion concepts remain intact.

The immediate assignment is creative direction: make people, work, architecture, routes and consequences form a world worth inhabiting. A broken mechanic can be recorded and assigned without consuming the entire direction pass. Rope work is parked.

## 1. The missing connective tissue

SpaceFace does not primarily need another reason for a ship to appear. It needs the player to understand why that ship is here, what its crew is trying to get through before the end of the shift, and what its absence would change.

A refinery can have a good model, a market, a job board, a dozen encounters and still feel like a vending machine in an asteroid field. The missing ingredient is composition. The inbound route should make its work visible. The warm side and the dangerous side should not be interchangeable. A familiar hull should return for a reason. A player who has visited three times should know something a first-time visitor does not, even if neither has opened a lore page.

The world’s strongest existing idea is that the same economy produces very different rooms. Helios is maintained. The outer stations improvise. The difference is not a color filter attached to increasing difficulty: it is access to labor, parts, breathable air and the authority to make a problem somebody else’s responsibility. That should organize the physical game. A clean lane, a waiting service vessel and a pile of retired machinery can tell the player more than a paragraph about inequality.

But a world made only of evidence of suffering becomes as repetitive as a world made only of loot. People still have favorite routes. They compete over small things. Someone takes care of a place nobody pays them to maintain. Someone has found a good view from a bad berth. A bar can be crooked and still be the place its customers are glad to see. These are not distractions from the story. They give the player something specific to care about when a route closes or a familiar voice stops answering.

The aim is to turn existing systems into a sequence of recognizable human and physical situations. The player should be able to say “the sheltered side of that refinery,” “the market that assembles after the freight clears,” or “the ship that always waits for its partner,” rather than remembering only sector IDs.

## 2. A world that works while you are not its protagonist

The recurring cast already has private plans. Keep those plans private. Kessler’s daughter, Mira’s unclaimed ticket, Quinn’s impossible purchase and Slate’s failure-point inventory should shape behavior without becoming confessions performed for the player. A character is not deep because a menu contains a long explanation of their motive. A character is deep when they make a locally sensible decision that becomes disturbing, funny or moving after the player sees its wider consequences.

The encounter should begin with someone already doing something. A request is an interruption in work, not an NPC entering a waiting state because the player came within range. The request should usually be smaller than the character’s real problem. Move this shipment. Check this number. Keep this approach clear. Bring back this sample. The player can do the immediate job without understanding the whole private plan; understanding grows through returns and comparisons.

Nor should every character be an interchangeable emissary of corruption. The existing distinction between Hale and Dree matters. Hale is not waiting for a revelation scene that repairs his worldview. Dree’s later mercy has a specific institutional price. Spence’s silence is friendship, not a clue dispenser. Sump’s wave does not require an achievement popup. The Vael’s maintained air is real even where their commercial interests are ugly. Local competence and local kindness must survive the larger indictment.

Give each authored place three simultaneous stories:

1. **The work:** what moves through here and what keeps the place running.
2. **The private life:** what the inhabitants want beyond completing that work.
3. **The consequence:** who benefits, waits or pays when the work goes right or wrong.

The player encounters these at different distances. The first should be readable on arrival. The second rewards attention over repeated visits. The third connects this place to another one.

## 3. The experience is outside the menu, too

This remains the actual top-down space game. A docked bar scene is not permission to pretend there is an explorable first-person interior. A prose reference to a worker is not proof that a humanoid character is present in flight. When a small gesture belongs on an existing docked surface, put it there. When a gesture needs to be understood from the flight camera, translate it into ship movement, a service operation, a light changing purpose, an object returning to a recognizable berth, or a short radio exchange anchored to a visible action.

Compose each location in three scales:

- **Approach:** a silhouette, an interruption in the traffic pattern, a safe way in and a reason to slow down. This is where the player learns what kind of place it is.
- **Working distance:** distinct activity areas that share a practical geography. Intake should relate to processing, outbound storage to departure lanes, maintenance to the machinery being maintained. A prop’s position should make sense before its narrative meaning is considered.
- **Attention distance:** an optional detail whose meaning changes after another visit or another sector. A repeated cargo identity, a patched hull, a line painted over, a memorial that remains maintained. These are discoveries, not compulsory tooltip chains.

A landmark must have at least two jobs. It orients the pilot and tells the place’s story; ideally it also shapes play. A retired hulk can shelter a market from traffic, frame a return approach and carry a visible history. A second generic hulk scattered nearby merely fills space. Prefer one useful relationship between pieces over ten unrelated decorations.

Preserve navigable negative space. Congestion is not authenticity if every visit becomes a collision test. Keep a legible public approach, put optional activity off that approach, and make hazardous shortcuts intentional and recognizable. The player should learn the place, not fight an accidental arrangement.

## 4. A return should mean something

Repeated visits are the world’s primary storytelling instrument. The first visit establishes a pattern. The second makes the pattern familiar. A later visit changes one part of it. The change should be intelligible because the player knew what was normal.

This does not require every prop to simulate an individual life. Use the existing world, traffic, market, mission and save owners as the sources of truth. Authored staging should observe their actual conditions. Do not invent a second “story shortage” beside a stocked market, a second delivery receipt beside the cargo owner, or a timer that advances an event while the game is paused. If a place is only presented as busy, label the presentation honestly in its implementation contract. If its goods can be bought or delivered, the goods and consequences must be real.

The persistent unit should be the meaningful change, not every background animation. A completed repair, a delivered quantity, a lost named vessel, an earned discovery or an existing mission outcome can support a different return. Ambient routines can be reconstructed from the established world time and sector state, so Save/Continue does not multiply crews or restart a scarce event for profit.

Three kinds of change should coexist:

- **Routine change:** the place moves through its working rhythm whether the player participates or not. This makes it feel occupied, but should not punish a player for looking away.
- **Player consequence:** an action changes a concrete local condition. A berth is usable again, a shipment actually arrives, a crew can resume its work. The response should fit the scale of the action.
- **Story change:** a campaign fact changes what an already familiar sight means. Do not destroy the old meaning. Let the same object support both readings.

Not every return needs a new quest. A familiar place doing its familiar work after a difficult journey is itself a payoff. Leave room for relief.

## 5. A journey with a changing emotional temperature

The established sector gradient is a route through different distributions of maintenance, not a tour of interchangeable misery. The campaign can retain its structure while ordinary exploration gives the player a broader relationship with the world.

**The core teaches confidence.** Clean approaches, legible services and reliable routines make the player feel that someone has thought about their arrival. The unsettling detail is what receives that care. Licensed mourning stays lit. An inconvenient surface is cleaned. A depot’s unnecessary surplus can be meticulously catalogued. Do not make the core look secretly decrepit to signal that it is bad; that would remove the contradiction.

**The working middle teaches recognition.** Here the player begins to recognize ships, shift rhythms, the difference between a delivery that has arrived and a delivery that has cleared, and the places crews make for themselves around official work. This is the strongest home for comedy, practical competence, small competitions, and jobs that become interesting because two reasonable needs cannot both be met immediately.

**The contested reaches teach comparison.** The same service has another price. The same kind of ship has another role. A wreck is not merely a danger marker but a boundary people use, argue over and work around. The player can make useful local choices without being able to settle every jurisdictional claim or fix the whole economy.

**The maintained stranger teaches doubt about labels.** Vael space should feel hospitable through actual practical differences, not a speech insisting it is misunderstood. A workable approach, an unfamiliar but coherent service ritual, equipment that is cared for and a real invitation to understand it can contradict the HUD’s outsider label. Hospitality and a hard commercial bargain can coexist.

**The far edge teaches recognition again.** Ashfall is not the prize for visiting enough exotic biomes. Its familiarity is the point. Preserve the silence and the off-frame Vethari. Do not fill every empty approach with chatter, explain the mystery with a creature showcase, or turn every strange signal into a collectible reward. The player must have experienced living, noisy places for this quiet to have weight.

The excursion developed below should be one cross-section through that larger world, not a replacement for it. Its job is to make travel itself connect work, people and discovery.

## 6. The excursion: a surveyor’s eye

Ceres → Tethys → Io → Veil → Triton follows real neighboring sectors. The proposed excursion connects existing work and places without becoming another campaign spine. **Two separate contracts anchor it:** Eno’s assay remains in Ceres; Sori’s eight-unit helium-3 envelope begins later at Veil and settles at Triton Wake Lab. What travels between them is the pilot’s growing ability to read a place, not one miraculous crate serving every story.

### Ceres: a good day’s work, and a reason not to leave yet

Coming from Helios, the pilot first encounters the Flare Rack, then the Inbound Weigh Pin, then the refinery. The sequence matters. The small light says somebody works out here. The tally says the work is counted. The refinery finally shows the size of the business. Those existing anchors can make arrival a sentence rather than three unrelated props.

The refinery is not the whole sector. Its working throat faces the traffic that needs it. Farther away, the Cathedral keeps its own quiet. Between them are places where crews have found practical uses for broken material: a section cradle beside a shipbreak, a rack beside a job that needs parts, a tender attending a disabled hull rather than orbiting a random point.

Eno wants three reliable readings before cutters are committed to the wrong field. That is a good reason to put a calibrated case aboard a small ship. The pilot carries the assayer to Belt Outpost, learns the deposits and mines ten units of titanium. The job has the satisfaction of being useful. Someone knows what they need, asks clearly and can use the result.

Compose the survey leg so the pilot passes another crew’s working face before reaching the control patch. A cut surface and an untouched surface should be distinguishable in the arrangement, not only in their labels. The crew’s patience or impatience can be visible through its actual route. Eno’s caution is not a personality paragraph: it is the reason the player is looking at a second and third rock when the first looked good enough.

After the paid work, Red Kite offers an entirely different reason to stay. A public timing buoy, named corners, three ordered handoffs and a courier who remembers hull-class times turn the field into a place somebody enjoys knowing well. The bright version of Ceres belongs here. A pilot can shave a corner because they learned the shape of a worksite, then hear Kite concede, “Fine. I am stealing that corner.” The competition has a person in it. It need not reveal a villain or a secret ledger.

The proposed composition should make that diversion easy to notice on the way out, not mandatory on the way in. Let the player earn enough to leave, then decide to spend a little of the afternoon flying for its own sake. Keep the real course clear. A decorative cargo rack placed in a racing line would be the director failing to understand the place.

Then offer the other Ceres: a longer, quieter trip to the Cathedral. Do not carry Kite’s jokes into that approach. The wreck’s separation from the working throat lets the player feel the change before a line of text announces it. Last Shift’s partial clues can remain partial; a destroyed unread source stays lost. A good racing afternoon and a graveyard can exist in the same sector without either explaining the other.

On return, the modest things should persist at their proper scale. Kite remembers a time. A separated shipbreak section remains a finite piece. A recovered clue remains recovered. The assay has advanced accepted work. These are enough to make Ceres more than its market. No ten-unit job needs to revive an entire colony.

### Tethys: the long way around

Tethys is two unequal banks with a channel between them, not one central station surrounded by convenient content. Customs lies south of the log-yard. The Quiet Cache is farther up the other side. The trade hub sits east. A pilot cannot see the entire relationship from one camera position, and should not be forced to.

The first authored scene is a place to stop near the seized hull without becoming an obstacle. A freight platform belongs beside the work, a light faces the cut surface, an observation blister opens toward the approach, and a rack sits where a service crew could reach it. These four pieces should make a small working margin, not a new station. Existing berths, the counterweight cradle, freight corridor and public route stay clear.

From the first stopping position, the pilot can read waiting: a hauler holding, a patrol completing its usual beat, a surveyor still occupied with the seized hull. From the outside pass, the departure becomes legible as an opening. The runner and keeper’s movement gives the pilot a reason to follow their eyes toward the cache.

The current causal chain already moves real pickups. That is its strength. Three pieces of contraband cross a gap; pursuit can make the runner jettison a load. The player’s decision is physical and ordinary: continue to the hub, watch, follow or collect something that now exists in the lane. The cargo and law owners decide the consequence. The scene does not need a moral-choice screen explaining what sort of person each action would make them.

The first visit is about curiosity. The second is about recognition. The player sees the watch changing and knows where to look before the runner moves. That is a small but valuable form of mastery: understanding a place rather than upgrading a stat.

Mira’s practical competence currently lives in the Tethys contact table. A pilot can be glad of somebody who makes freight understandable. Her bought but unclaimed passage could later supply a quiet return detail: departures change, the document does not. However, the canonical Bourse/Quiet binding differs from the current Tethys/MTS one. Resolve that binding before staging the detail; do not explain the discrepancy away with invented travel. Nor should the player be asked to raise money for a ticket she already owns.

The larger visiting-market and switchyard concepts can eventually enrich this junction. They are not prerequisites for this scene. Tethys already has a working event with an object path. The first job is to give the player a place from which to understand it.

### Io: mail under a different flag

Reach Station has an open lee. Southwest of it, the cruiser wreck gives several groups a different reason to remain: a picket watches, a salvor works cut plate, a claim hauler continues its route, a tender heads toward Shepherd’s Ring. The same hull can be a boundary, a job, a memory and useful shelter. It does not need four different explanatory monuments.

The proposed human center is Maro’s mail rhythm. Maro keeps packets moving regardless of the dock’s flag. A returning pilot should recognize the carrier and the receiving position before they notice which authority now claims the port. Political control can change the inspection and the price; it should not erase the fact that somebody has been waiting for a letter.

A brief arrival can contain several different desires. Maro wants to finish the route. Coldburn wants the pilot to understand that he ran this lane first and lost the stamp. The player wants a berth and a profitable next job. None needs to become a villain to get in another’s way. Coldburn’s grievance can be funny because it matters to him out of proportion to its importance to everyone else.

Out along the patrol corridor, the Love Letter Buoy asks something smaller still. The pilot can let it finish or re-seed it on its grave route. Preserve that actual choice. It pays no credits and does not lead to combat. The signal belongs to a relationship, not to the reward economy. Re-seeding it stops the old broadcast; the changed silence can be the return consequence.

Io also contains machines listening toward a direction absent from human charts. Place the human mail route and the alien listening geometry so a curious pilot can notice the rhyme: one message has a recipient, the other an unknown destination. Do not make the rhyme a secret origin story. It is enough to let two kinds of attention inhabit one place.

At Shepherd’s Ring, six anchor organisms hold a dead relay shut. A player who treats everything unfamiliar as a target can release the bloom. This existing consequence gives the exploration a sharper edge than another collectible scan. The station’s ordinary work should remain the baseline against which the altered ecology is noticed.

On the inward trip, Io can be familiar without being safe in every circumstance. The player knows the lee, the working side of the wreck and the mail hull. That knowledge should remain useful when the flag changes. Belonging to a route is not the same as owning it.

### Veil: a place that takes looking seriously

There is an important binding debt here. Canon’s **Veil Expanse in Vael space** has warm air, genuinely good food and independent recyclers. Current **Research Station Veil** is a Free Frontier station whose chart says, “Instruments first, hospitality never. Sells readings it won’t explain.” Sori is DMC. A Vael patrol near the Obelisk does not make the station Vael. Preserve both facts until the place/faction binding is deliberately resolved.

The immediate excursion therefore visits the existing brusque research station, not an invented Vael resort. Its pleasure is competence and the view: a place where people know how to look, even if they do not make a performance of welcoming you.

The station at `(420, -1120)` has a different neighbor in the Surge Shrine: memorial, comms mast, observation blister. An instrument tender runs an anomaly leg while a dish listener follows a separate chord. The landscape’s missing center should remain missing. Do not fill it with a large decorative circle just to announce that the sector is strange.

At Quiet Ice, there is work before there is revelation: a prospector, a hauler serving a core rack, cysts that answer heat, rays and a mourning kite. A pilot can coast and notice a recurring path. They can approach the Black Orchard and discover that distance changes what their scanner can resolve. These are reasons to linger even when nobody has posted a job.

Sori’s work sharpens that attention. Four pockets, one contaminated by battle-afterglow, eight units of helium-3 for an independent comparison. The question is about natural Veil flow, not proof that the anomaly is alive. The most spectacular patch in view may be the least useful sample. The pilot’s new expertise is knowing when an exciting signal is a bad measurement.

The proposed apron should make the station’s priorities visible. Visiting craft have a clear place to wait. Cold storage relates to the sample route. A utility module serves the work rather than decorating a blank edge. The observation corner is away from the tender’s crossing. The small courtesy can be practical: somebody leaves room for the instrument run, or a local points the visitor toward the quiet side without offering a grand explanation.

If the player later witnesses a fight, a real recorded aftermath could make the sampling problem newly intelligible: the spectacular encounter was somebody else’s unusable afternoon. That dynamic connection remains a bounded request, not a claim made from the contract’s wording alone. The current authored exclusion can still teach the distinction honestly.

The Obelisk gives another kind of competence: knowing when to stop. The return survey pays no credits; more pulses shorten the response and advance patrols. Do not turn restraint into a hidden loot optimization. Let the player finish an observation because they understand enough.

The canonical Vael hospitality deserves its own properly bound destination or explicit adaptation. When commissioned, it should be genuinely pleasant, not a disguise for poison or a mandatory betrayal. Its warmth can coexist with hard contracts and inferior slurry sold elsewhere. That richer faction treatment must not be achieved by silently relabeling this Free Frontier station.

### Triton: a second opinion and an optional longer look

Sori’s physical envelope goes to **Triton Wake Lab, `station_triton`**, the Free Frontier research station at `(480, -980)`. It already has `place_station_research`. That is the ordinary destination: a place prepared to receive a useful sample and complete the existing comparison. It is not the Archive’s mast-like Field Lab farther away.

Make arrival at Wake Lab feel like reaching somebody who can use what was carried. A sample courier, tender and working receiving side give the shipment a social destination. The answer should remain the bounded answer the mission supports. No ancient species has to appear, no cosmic mechanism has to explain itself, and no new ledger is needed to make the work worthwhile.

After settlement, the pilot can leave satisfied. Or they can take the longer look. That optional exploration leads to two distinct places: the Watcher at `(-1700, 1500)` and the Archive Field Lab POI at `(1600, -700)`. The latter is not where Sori’s eight-unit sale occurs. Keeping the institutions separate makes the region richer rather than tidier.

The Watcher’s Tithe already places human-scale offerings near the anomaly: a memorial, tally post, pods and whistle. The composition should reveal these before the full scale of the object. Someone has come here before the player, stood at the edge and left something small. The next view should make the object’s fixed gaze apparent. What it points toward is empty.

Here the art does not yet meet the prose. The Watcher is a seamed-rock stand-in where the discovery describes a carved aperture. The Archive Field Lab is a sensor mast where a three-bearing research operation needs a readable working surround. Those are precise commissions: build the missing silhouette and compose the instruments, while preserving the existing identities, research behavior and approaches. They are not a request for another anomaly engine.

The Archive Field Lab’s own three-bearing research positions can make exploration feel like using a place; do not silently transfer that interaction to the Watcher. The stabilized echo can remain a result without becoming an explanation. A return to the ordinary Wake Lab can then feel different because the pilot has seen what its crews work beside. The station did not become more important; the player’s understanding of its work became larger.

On the trip back through Veil, Io and Tethys, the same traffic now has destinations. The player recognizes work that previously looked like background movement. That is the excursion’s payoff: a better mental map of a world that still has other people’s afternoons inside it.


## 7. Ordinary life should have texture, not a content counter

The world needs a repertoire of small situations, not a requirement that every sector contain one of each. Choose details that grow from the place’s work.

A proposed Ceres public screen has one program, **Kite’s Corner**: a named course corner, an actual wide recorded pass, then a cleaner recorded pass and its true time. Crews waiting for service have a reason to watch it. The pilot can stay for the result or leave halfway through. This is the useful lesson from an in-world television: the place contains culture and time beyond the current mission. It is not another reward track or a looping clip pasted onto every station. The companion commission defines its audience, truth boundary and resource scope.

A regular competition can be small enough to belong to the crews. A clean arrival, an efficient cut, a familiar racing circuit or an existing rival’s challenge can give expertise social value. Use existing racing and rival owners where they are already present. Do not make every station invent a new minigame. The player should recognize that people enjoy being good at their work even when the work is badly paid.

Food and leisure should not exist only as inventory transactions. A proposed shared stopping place can be readable through when service craft gather, where visiting vessels leave room, and what a docked conversation notices. If food actually changes hands, its stock and delivery must be real. If the scene is an observation of a meal, do not imply an item was consumed or sold when no owner recorded it.

Repairs, graffiti and memorials are ways a place remembers. Keep their meanings distinct. A repair is evidence that somebody cared enough to do the work. Graffiti is testimony, not a tutorial written by the designer. A memorial is a claim about whose loss is allowed to remain visible. The three need not occupy the same corner or tell the same story.

Some small interactions should remain free of accounting. Sump’s wave does not become a reputation farm. Spence’s drink does not unlock an informer dialogue. A love letter need not pay credits or become a combat ambush. These are important not because they secretly manipulate progression, but because they establish that people in this world have relationships that are not reducible to the player’s rewards.

## 8. The relationships between pieces

The reusable asset bank is a vocabulary. A vocabulary becomes a place through relationships.

**A container beside a service hull** can mean a delivery, a stalled job or a private arrangement depending on its owner, motion, label and surrounding traffic. Do not create three new container models before deciding which relationship is being shown.

**A refinery and a derelict** can tell a story about succession if their routes and silhouettes relate: the old approach ends at a dead machine; the new approach passes it without stopping. A random derelict beyond draw distance tells no such story.

**A billboard and a waiting area** make a broadcast plausible. A billboard floating beside a high-speed transit lane is probably an advertisement, not a public gathering place. Give the asset the right audience.

**A light and a working body** can reveal an operation. A lamp illuminating an empty patch of space is decoration unless that emptiness has a reason. The lighting owner should supply consistent materials, illumination and performance limits; the world director decides what deserves light and what the player should infer from it.

**A repeated hull and a changed context** create continuity more efficiently than introducing another named contact. Seeing a familiar courier at a different port can connect places without a scene explaining the route. Preserve identity through the existing actor/record owner when identity matters; do not attach a name to whichever generic ship happened to spawn first.

**An absence and an established routine** create a story. Establish the routine first. A missing vessel on a first visit is not an absence the player can read. Later, do not force tragedy as the only explanation: it may have departed on time, changed shifts, found work or taken a better offer. Let ordinary successes exist too.

## 9. How the excursion changes over a working day

The first pass establishes ordinary patterns. A refinery receives work. A patrol changes watch. Mail arrives. An instrument tender crosses. A laboratory receives a sample. The player may notice only one of these and still have a good trip.

The second pass rewards recognition. At Tethys, the gap is visible before the runner uses it. At Io, the mail hull is familiar before the current flag is. At Veil, the pilot can distinguish a service chord from an observation position. At Ceres, a corner of the racing course is no longer just a gap between rocks. This is knowledge earned by being somewhere, not a new skill-tree purchase.

A changed pass should alter one intelligible thing rather than replace the whole sector with an alarm. A real delivery has cleared its receiving point. A player’s race time has become the number to beat. A buoy no longer repeats from its old position. A recovered clue remains in the record; an unread destroyed one does not. Where a proposed consequence needs a new connection, build that connection to the actual event owner before writing the acknowledgment.

The return after the outward comparison can be quiet. The player has done a useful job and now knows where to stop on the way home. A familiar place doing its familiar work is an earned form of comfort. Not every completed task needs a new threat to justify continued play.

Different players should carry different memories of the same route. One remembers the race. One remembers the dropped cargo they decided not to take. One remembers the letter. One remembers the empty direction the machines were watching. The excursion succeeds when these are compatible stories about the same composed world, not mutually exclusive scripted tours.

## 10. Canon binding is a production task, not a retcon opportunity

The current corpus contains a strong story and a weaker binding between that story and some playable locations. Before connecting scenes around the recurring cast, settle the mapping explicitly.

At the inspected source baseline, `src/ui/station/barContacts.js` places Rook at `station_coalition`, Voss at `station_beltout`, Mira at `station_tethys` with MTS affiliation, Drift at `station_drift`, and Quinn across `station_smuggler` and `station_sker`. Their canonical sheets point respectively to Cinder, Hollow, Bourse/Quiet, Meridian and one Outpost 9. These are not harmless aliases to silently reconcile in new prose. A contact’s place determines the route the player learns and the people they associate with it.

The request is an explicit editorial and integration decision for each identity: canonical location, current runtime location, intended game ID, any deliberate adaptation, and the migration required for existing missions and saves. Preserve stable IDs where possible. Do not move a contact by changing a label while leaving its mission offers, references and arrival context elsewhere. Do not duplicate a named person to make every desired scene available at once.

A further binding debt separates canonical Vael hospitality in the Veil Expanse from the current Free Frontier Research Station Veil. Sori’s DMC affiliation and Vael patrol presence do not settle that question. Decide where the canonical hospitality belongs before scripting it; do not silently overwrite the existing station’s brusque research identity.

Likewise, the embodied dialogue card calling Rook a rescue broker and the Witness “Kurtz” should be reconciled with the canonical role and naming law. A new longform treatment must not amplify these accidental differences.

Chronology also needs a firm boundary. The grid’s legal year-3 transfer and the present week of 47-A are not interchangeable dates. Creative Direction §12’s rack tag is `RECEIVED YEAR 3 / INSTALL PENDING / ISSUED YEAR 17 — WEEK OF 47-A`. Use the higher-authority ruling when an older ecology paragraph is ambiguous. Do not restage Quinn’s six-year-old currency pullout as a new player-triggered crisis. Let present conditions and old records make the history legible.

Protected unresolved lives stay unresolved: Mira’s passage remains unclaimed unless a future authorized canon change says otherwise; Quinn does not buy the bar that is not for sale; Spence is not converted into an informer; Ivo is not identified as the writer; Hale is not given an awakening; Lida and the Vethari remain off-frame. These are creative constraints that make the world more particular, not missing quests to complete.

## 11. What to commission, and what not to commission

Commission the smallest missing piece that lets an authored experience become ordinary play.

A new set piece should arrive with its purpose, readable approach, activity zones, interaction boundaries, collision/clearance requirements, material language, expected camera scale, attachment points for existing systems and evidence needed for acceptance. A beautiful isolated model with no intended activity is not a finished world increment.

Conversely, do not send the model team a request for “more space props” when the bank already contains the needed vocabulary. First make a composition using existing pieces. Its weak points will reveal the actual missing silhouette, service connection or working surface.

Requests for larger systems should describe the experience they enable and the current owner they extend. A public program needs an authored program, a plausible screen and an audience; it does not automatically need a universal entertainment simulation. A remembered local routine needs identity and persistence through the existing world owners; it does not require a second population simulator. A receiving bay needs a truthful relationship to cargo and work; it does not require a new economy.

The companion build requests translate this treatment into bounded work routed through the existing build map. They are requests, not completed assets, not silently claimed owners and not a competing queue. The current world should keep improving while larger pieces are built.

## 12. How to tell whether this direction is working

Ask questions in player language before counting features:

- Can a new pilot tell what the place does before opening the board?
- After two visits, can they name a landmark and a local routine without reading the sector title?
- Does a character’s practical request make sense in this place?
- Can the player find something worth doing or noticing without accepting the highlighted mission?
- Does the return after a completed job show the right-sized consequence?
- Are ordinary kindness, humor, skill and beauty present without all becoming reward machines?
- Can the player explain why the next place needs the thing they are carrying?
- Are the same cargo, identity and outcome still true after leaving, returning and Continue?

Technical checks support those questions; they do not answer all of them. A green fixture does not establish that a place is memorable. A source preview does not establish that the normal camera communicates its landmarks. A written treatment does not establish that the experience is implemented. Keep the world-direction and sector-coherence goals open until the composed experience is in the game, reviewed in ordinary play and accepted.

The first directorial choice is Tethys’s change-of-watch overlook: four existing kit pieces, two voluntary stopping positions and an existing handoff made readable through movement. Compose that place now, preserve its actual traffic and law, then use what the build teaches to shape the next section of the excursion. Do not postpone the world behind another unrelated system.
