# S-12 drafts — Landmarks, three personas

## The Drifter (working-class regular)
The Drifter has docked at Sker, cut the Reach, watched the candles from the Helios lane.
What a monument means to him is who still shows up: the family that pays for a flame, the
pilgrim raft moored a watch off the plinth, the flowers that are actually ribbons because
nobody grows flowers out here. Draft: physical pilgrim presence — a mooring buoy with
ribbons beside the Candle Fleet, a vigil-light array beside the cathedral stern, a penance
moor under the Throne, a keykeeper's watch outside the Vault Maw. Each plate in the
pilgrim voice: what they leave, who comes back, what they won't say out loud.

## The Archivist (continuity and memory)
The landmark lore pack is the codex of monuments, and twelve of its nineteen entries stop
one beat early — the fifth scan line is the difference between a record and a dossier. The
Archivist wants each entry completed to its own cap: the missing lines should answer the
question the other four circle (who built it, who keeps it, what it does to the people who
stay). Draft: +1 line per four-line entry — c2_05 through c15_05 — in each entry's own
idiosyncratic scan register (sensor data, field note, overheard prayer), never a summary.

## The Set Dresser (visual storyteller)
A monument should cast a shadow of small things. The Dresser's eye: the memorial-array prop
(place_memorial_array) is shipped and used exactly once (the strut shrine) — the world's
saddest under-deployment. Draft: memorial arrays and mooring buoys staged just off each
ledger-named landmark's silhouette — close enough to read as *attending* the monument,
far enough to not clip the hero GLB. She also argues the Vault Maw (Ashfall) belongs in
scope: four ledger-adjacent monuments, not three — the Maw is the fourth thing a pilot
remembers.
