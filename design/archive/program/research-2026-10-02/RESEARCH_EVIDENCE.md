# SpaceFace research evidence atlas

## Reading the evidence

This targeted review informs design decisions, rather than claiming to cover every game-design study. Sources were selected for a plausible mechanism, a usable intervention or a method relevant to SpaceFace. Evidence classes are experimental, observational, synthesis, formal, practitioner and technical. Full text was inspected where available; access limits are stated. Recommendations are our applications, not features tested by the cited studies. Review date: 2 October 2026.

## Motivation and experience

### M1 Selectable growth and competence support

Peng, Lin, Pfeiffer and Winn (2012), Need Satisfaction Supportive Game Features as Motivational Determinants, Media Psychology. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/15213269.2012.673850), [original full text](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/2012_PengLinPfeifferWinn_MP3.pdf).

Experimental: 160 participants, 2×2 exergame design. Choice/customization and challenge/feedback bundles improved corresponding need satisfaction and experience measures. Bundles prevent isolating individual ingredients; mediation paths were not separately randomized. Short university-sample exposure limits transfer. Decision: let earned growth offer understandable alternatives and truthful mastery feedback; do not claim achievements or adaptive difficulty alone produced the effect.

### M2 Avoid arbitrary control impediments

Przybylski, Deci, Rigby and Ryan (2014), Competence-Impeding Electronic Games and Players' Aggressive Feelings, Thoughts, and Behaviors, JPSP. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0034820), [corrected original](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/2014_PrzyDeciRigbyRyan_JPSP.pdf).

Experimental multi-study paper. Study 4 randomized 141 students to simpler/complex puzzle controls; complex controls reduced reported mastery and competence. Short-term laboratory outcomes do not establish real-world violence or prohibit sophisticated inputs. Decision: spend difficulty on physical judgment, preserve stable mappings and explicit modes, and separate acquisition errors from execution errors.

### M3 Choice has evidence but no magic menu size

Patall, Cooper and Robinson (2008), The Effects of Choice on Intrinsic Motivation and Related Outcomes, Psychological Bulletin. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.134.2.270), [full text](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/2008_PatallCooperRobinson_PsychBulletin.pdf).

Meta-analysis: 41 studies/46 samples; positive average motivation effect, moderated by study conditions. The often-cited two-to-four choices concern successive choices, not an optimal menu option count. Historical heterogeneous tasks limit precise translation. Decision: retain meaningful player choice and usable previews; select a menu size from the actual task, not a misquoted finding.

### M4 Challenge depends on goals and motivation

Abuhamdeh and Csikszentmihalyi (2012), The Importance of Challenge for the Enjoyment of Intrinsically Motivated, Goal-Directed Activities. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167211427147), [author-uploaded paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51783477_The_Importance_of_Challenge_for_the_Enjoyment_of_Intrinsically_Motivated_Goal-Directed_Activities).

Observational chess and experience-sampling studies. The chess sample was 87 experienced men across 1,430 games; challenge–enjoyment relationships depended on motivational context. Its fitted winning-probability peak is not a tuning target. Decision: support a range from safe exploration to demanding setups; test mastery leverage without a universal win-rate goal.

### M5 Setup uncertainty and payoff

Abuhamdeh, Csikszentmihalyi and Jalal (2015), Enjoying the Possibility of Defeat. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11031-014-9425-2), [author paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/266145593_Enjoying_the_possibility_of_defeat_Outcome_uncertainty_suspense_and_intrinsic_motivation).

Experiments manipulated contest closeness in short competitive sessions. Close wins increased enjoyment/suspense under studied conditions, despite lower competence ratings;33 of 48 participants selected the close-win game in a subsequent choice. Concealed opponent manipulation and victory-focused tasks constrain transfer. Decision: use uncertain setup around earned spectacular success, without rubberbanding away the payoff or forcing every fight close.

### M6 Curiosity and a resolvable information gap

Kang and colleagues (2009), The Wick in the Candle of Learning. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02402.x), [full text](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/WickCandleLearning.pdf).

Three small trivia studies link measured curiosity with uncertainty, willingness to obtain information and memory. Curiosity was mainly measured rather than randomized; neural activation is not a dopamine recipe. Decision: expose learnable physical possibilities and let the player resolve them. Do not equate opaque randomness with rewarding discovery.

### M7 Predictions can stimulate curiosity

Brod and Breitwieser (2019), Lighting the Wick in the Candle of Learning, npj Science of Learning. [DOI and full text](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0056-y), [preregistration](https://osf.io/hjr5k).

Preregistered within-person study, 33 recruited/29 analyzed. Generating numerical predictions increased curiosity compared with examples; no statistically significant between-condition memory difference was detected. A non-preregistered exclusion and small task-specific sample merit caution. Decision: enable optional quick intervention-and-result experiments. An aimed shove already embodies a prediction; compulsory quizzes would be an unjustified translation.

### M8 Boredom has attention and meaning routes

Westgate and Wilson (2018), Boring Thoughts and Bored Minds, Psychological Review. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000097), [full text](https://www.erinwestgate.com/uploads/7/6/4/1/7641726/westgatewilson.psychreview.2018.pdf).

Theory plus experiments/reanalyses. An air-traffic task crossed attentional demand with a bundled meaning manipulation; understimulation and low meaning increased boredom. It does not imply more stimulation always helps or a mission label creates meaning. Decision: remove forced low-decision intervals and clarify useful goals while preserving purposeful rest and optional calm play.

### M9 Rewards depend on their function

Deci, Koestner and Ryan (1999), [meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627); Cameron, Banko and Pierce (2001), [reanalysis](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF03392017), [full text](https://behavior.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/Pervasive-Negative-Effects.pdf).

Competing syntheses disagree about pervasive reward harm; task interest, contingency and outcome definition matter. They share underlying evidence and often involve later reward withdrawal. Deci evidence was abstract-verified here; Cameron full text inspected. Decision: retain progression that opens possibilities and communicates achievement. Do not infer that every badge is harmful or every unlock intrinsically motivating.

### M10 More output does not establish more enjoyment

Mekler, Brühlmann, Tuch and Opwis (2017), Towards Understanding the Effects of Individual Gamification Elements on Intrinsic Motivation and Performance. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2015.08.048), [institutional abstract](https://research.aalto.fi/en/publications/towards-understanding-the-effects-of-individual-gamification-elem/).

Online experiment on image annotation: points/levels/leaderboards increased output without significant improvements in measured intrinsic motivation or competence. Abstract and method overview inspected; no full-methods claim. Absence of significance is not proof of universal zero effect. Decision: do not optimize clicks, XP or session duration as the experience itself.

### M11 Needs are constructs not counters

Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski (2006), The Motivational Pull of Video Games. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8), [full text](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf).

Four studies relate perceived competence/autonomy to experience; multiplayer survey work also examines relatedness. These were not isolated randomizations of every psychological need. Decision: use needs to form hypotheses, then identify concrete interventions. Option count, wins and NPC count are not direct measurements of autonomy, competence and relatedness.

### M12 Identity and meaningful experience

Przybylski and colleagues (2012), [The Ideal Self at Play](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611418676), [full text](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2012_PrzybylskiEtAl_PsychScience.pdf); Oliver and colleagues, [Video Games as Meaningful Entertainment Experiences](https://doi.org/10.1037/ppm0000066), [manuscript](https://butler.elsevierpure.com/ws/portalfiles/portal/40130846/Video%20games%20as%20meaningful%20entertainment%20experiences.pdf).

Measured identity congruence and recalled meaningful experiences relate to different experience dimensions. Actual identity congruence and game features were not randomly assigned. Decision: let a build and its consequences express a recognizable role; distinguish appreciation from immediate excitement. These studies do not prove adding dialogue or NPCs will cause attachment.

### M13 Voluntary play and social context

Reer and Krämer (2020), [social FPS experiment](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.entcom.2020.100353), [full text](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/2020_ReerKramer_SDTbasedLaboratoryExperiment.pdf); Vuorre and colleagues (2022), [objective-play telemetry study](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.220411), [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9326284/).

The former compares social settings in 139 players; co-location improved enjoyment, while adding remote voice did not establish the same effect. The latter observationally links six weeks of telemetry from 38,935 players to surveys, finding small average time–well-being effects. Neither mandates multiplayer or establishes universal safety. Decision: preserve solo viability and easy stopping; treat ethical design commitments separately from medical claims.

### M14 Avoid superficial theory use

Tyack and Mekler (2024), Self-Determination Theory and HCI Games Research. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3673230), [full accepted manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.12639v1).

Research-practice review of 259 papers and practitioner material critiques shallow theoretical application. It does not disprove all need-support findings. Decision: name the intervention, outcome and inference instead of decorating a feature with an autonomy or competence label.

## Control perception and learning

### H1 Latency changes accuracy as well as speed

MacKenzie and Ware (1993), Lag as a Determinant of Human Performance in Interactive Systems. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/169059.169431), [full text](https://www.yorku.ca/mack/p488-mackenzie.pdf).

Eight experienced mouse users performed acquisitions with manipulated lag/distance/width. Larger delay impaired speed and accuracy, interacting with difficulty. Small sample and desktop pointing limit transfer. Decision: instrument input, simulation and presentation separately; keep cosmetic easing outside the gameplay input law. Published delay conditions are not universal acceptance thresholds.

### H2 Assistance and agency

Wen, Yamashita and Asama (2015), PLOS ONE 10, e0125226. [Original full paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0125226).

Seventeen students steered a dot with varied delay and goal-helping command filtering. Assistance improved performance and reported control, particularly at longer tested delays. This does not prove hidden assistance is always beneficial. Decision: preserve explicit, predictable assistance modes and test whether they help the intended goal without changing unrelated control semantics.

### H3 Acquisition tolerance

Fitts (1954), The Information Capacity of the Human Motor System in Controlling the Amplitude of Movement. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0055392), [original scan](https://www2.psychology.uiowa.edu/faculty/mordkoff/infoproc/pdfs/Fitts%201954.pdf).

Movement amplitude and endpoint tolerance were manipulated in manual tasks, establishing a speed–accuracy relation. It is not a direct model of continuous inertial flight. Decision: distinguish selecting the right target from executing a difficult physical maneuver; do not use tiny selection areas as accidental combat difficulty.

### H4 Route clearance

Accot and Zhai (1997), Beyond Fitts' Law. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/258549.258760), [original paper](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Shumin-Zhai/publication/2608427_Beyond_Fitts%27_Law_Models_for_Trajectory-Based_HCI_Tasks/links/544733900cf2d62c30505969/Beyond-Fitts-Law-Models-for-Trajectory-Based-HCI-Tasks.pdf).

Four experiments varied continuously constrained paths. Steering demand depends on clearance along a route, beyond endpoint difficulty. Stylus-task coefficients do not transfer directly to ships. Decision: author preparation space, bottlenecks, curvature and useful exits; evaluate swept-body clearance and recovery rather than center-point arrival alone.

### H5 Learnable dynamics

Shadmehr and Mussa-Ivaldi (1994), Adaptive Representation of Dynamics During Learning of a Motor Task. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.14-05-03208.1994), [full original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6577492/).

Robot-imposed force fields distorted reaching; practice restored trajectories and removal produced aftereffects, supporting adaptation to dynamics. This does not select a SpaceFace flight model. Decision: retain stable laws, make changed regimes explicit, and teach anticipation rather than hide continuous retuning.

### H6 Feature distinction

Treisman and Gelade (1980), A Feature-Integration Theory of Attention. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(80)90005-5), [full text](https://facultypsy.hope.edu/psychlabs/exp/visualsearch/readings/TreismanGelade_1980.pdf).

Visual-search manipulations distinguish some single-feature targets from conjunction searches as distractors increase. The result is task/feature dependent. Decision: make selected targets and critical states distinct without requiring tiny color-shape conjunctions; use redundant non-color encodings for essential information.

### H7 Clutter depends on scene structure

Rosenholtz, Li and Nakano (2007), Measuring Visual Clutter. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1167/7.2.17).

Map-search experiments compared clutter measures and manipulated scene properties. Metrics correlated with search outcomes under those conditions, but omitted important top-down knowledge. Full original inspected; mirror filename was misleading and is omitted. Decision: protect cue regions in actual crowded frames; particle counts and telemetry-only recognition are insufficient evidence.

### H8 Transient attention

Yantis and Jonides (1984), Abrupt Visual Onsets and Selective Attention. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.10.5.601), [original text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/16958392_Abrupt_visual_onsets_and_selective_attention_Evidence_from_visual_search).

Experiments manipulated abrupt search-item appearance and attention allocation. Abrupt onset could gain processing priority, with important boundary conditions. Decision: reserve strong transient emphasis for consequential events and keep persistent state cues afterward; do not assume every flash always captures attention.

### H9 Faded coaching

Winstein and Schmidt (1990), Reduced Frequency of Knowledge of Results Enhances Motor Skill Learning. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.16.4.677), [original scan inspected](https://www.scribd.com/document/1064584227/Winstein-1990).

Feedback schedules affected delayed motor-task retention; simple reduced frequency did not consistently win, while faded schedules helped in later experiments. These are post-response coaching results. Decision: fade prescribed routes and prompts, not essential real-time velocity, contact or danger feedback.

### H10 Mixed practice and transfer

Shea and Morgan (1979), Contextual Interference Effects on Acquisition Retention and Transfer. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.5.2.179), [full text](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/1979-shea.pdf).

Seventy-two students practiced physical sequences in blocked/random orders. Random practice harmed acquisition but improved later retention/transfer in studied conditions. Decision: establish component mappings before mixing maneuvers and testing new geometry. Do not infer maximal randomness is best for every beginner.

### H11 Specific input access

Trewin, Keates and Moffatt (2008), Individual Responses to a Method of Cursor Assistance. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/17483100701273128), [original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18416515/).

Repeated-measures clicking study with 11 people with motor impairments found heterogeneous benefits from targeted assistance. Abstract-level evidence only. Decision: independently configurable input accommodations and cancellation paths; simulation perturbations do not establish population accessibility.

### H12 Contextual tutorials

Andersen and colleagues (2012), The Impact of Tutorials on Games of Varying Complexity. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/2207676.2207687), [original paper](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/andersen2012tio.pdf).

Over 45,000 players encountered tutorial variants across three games. Benefits depended strongly on complexity; contextual guidance helped the most complex game, while simpler games did not show the same gains. Restricted freedom did not establish a general learning benefit. Decision: contextualize help around unfamiliar physical relationships, preserve skipping and experimentation, and do not maximize instruction volume.

### H13 Interruptions have resumption costs

Monk, Trafton and Boehm-Davis (2008), The Effect of Interruption Duration and Demand on Resuming Suspended Goals. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0014402), [original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19102614/).

Three experiments varied interruption duration/demand in hierarchical interactive tasks; longer and more demanding interruptions increased resumption time. Abstract and original indexed methods overview inspected. Decision: preserve useful context when closing a map, returning from menus or resuming a save; avoid unnecessary interruption. Specific game-resume UI remains a proposed application.

## Feedback and commercial evidence

### J1 Feedback tied to success

Kao, Ballou, Gerling, Breitsohl and Deterding (2024), How Does Juicy Game Feedback Motivate. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642656), [full text](https://people.csail.mit.edu/dkao/pdf/3613904.3642656.pdf).

Preregistered five-condition action-RPG experiment, n=1,699: four amplified-feedback conditions plus standard-feedback control. Success-dependent amplification helped; the tested non-success-dependent amplified condition reduced some experiences. Agency disruption is a proposed explanation. Psychological paths/playtime predictors came from fitted models, including exploratory additions. Decision: distinguish input registration from earned consequence. Short duration and one game limit transfer.

### J2 More spectacle is not monotonic improvement

Kao (2020), The Effects of Juiciness in an Action RPG. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.entcom.2020.100359), [publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1875952118300879).

Four conditions, n=3,018; medium/high feedback outperformed none/extreme on studied experience and behavior measures. Extreme was deliberately excessive. Abstract and original reproduced text inspected; no universal optimum follows. Decision: choose a coherent feedback hierarchy rather than maximize effect quantity.

### C1 Social influence can cause market outcomes

Salganik, Dodds and Watts (2006), Experimental Study of Inequality and Unpredictability in an Artificial Cultural Market. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1121066), [original paper and methods](https://www.princeton.edu/~mjs3/salganik_dodds_watts06_full.pdf).

Randomized music market with 14,341 participants and parallel worlds. Social information increased inequality and unpredictability; product quality still mattered. It is not a videogame-sales experiment. Decision: treat experience, discoverability and social transmission as distinct commercial factors; neither predict a guaranteed hit nor dismiss quality's contribution.

### C2 Quality signals and game sales

Kraemer, Weiger and Heidenreich (2025), Do All Stars Shine the Same. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2024.115034), [publisher original](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0148296324005381).

Econometric analysis of 1,642 games plus post-hoc consumer research estimates nonlinear review–sales relationships, attempting to separate persuasion from shared quality. Causal interpretation rests on modeling assumptions. Indexed original methods/discussion inspected; retrieval intermittent. Decision: deliver a credible early experience and truthful promise, while keeping market measures separate from internal design grades.

## Formal and structural lenses

### S1 Dominance and conditional choices

Cheng and Börgers, Dominance and Optimality, author manuscript 19 November 2025. [Original](https://public.websites.umich.edu/~tborgers/DandO.pdf).

Formal results connect dominance and optimality under stated utility/situation assumptions. Decision: inspect context-conditioned alternatives across multiple consequences. A tiny nominal disadvantage does not automatically make an option practically meaningful. This is a choice-analysis tool, not an enjoyment experiment.

### S2 Information value

Blackwell (1953), Equivalent Comparisons of Experiments. [Original DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177729032).

Formal comparison of information sources for decision problems; checked against an accessible formal restatement when publisher full text was unavailable. Decision: test whether a timely cue permits a different useful response. It does not prescribe maximal disclosure or certify visual attention.

### S3 Opportunity cost

Bellman (1957), A Markovian Decision Process. [Publisher-hosted introduction](https://iumj.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/abstracts/56038_abs.pdf).

Dynamic programming accounts for immediate consequences and future state value. Decision: evaluate existing heat/energy/cooldowns through context-dependent save/spend value. The mathematical principle does not justify adding a resource meter or waiting period by itself.

### S4 Future control and emergence

Klyubin, Polani and Nehaniv (2005), [Empowerment](https://researchprofiles.herts.ac.uk/en/publications/empowerment-a-universal-agent-centric-measure-of-control/); Juul (2002), [The Open and the Closed](https://jesperjuul.net/text/openandtheclosed.html).

The former formalizes action-to-future-state influence; the latter conceptually distinguishes rule emergence and authored progression. Neither validates a fun score. Decision: make motion change useful future possibilities and combine consistent rules. Empty space can offer many reachable states without compelling play.

### S5 Matchups and pacing

Omidshafiei and colleagues (2019), [Alpha Rank](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-45619-9); Booth (2009), [The AI Systems of Left 4 Dead](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf).

Alpha Rank is formal/computational analysis of strategic populations, including non-transitive interactions. Booth is a primary practitioner account of intensity-based pacing. Decision: inspect matchup/context differences and preserve pressure/recovery opportunities using the existing director. Neither source supplies a universal balance target or encounter timer.

### S6 Consequences under risk

Kahneman and Tversky (1979), Prospect Theory. [Original DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/1914185), [original-paper mirror](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Behavioral_Decision_Theory/Kahneman_Tversky_1979_Prospect_theory.pdf).

Risky-choice evidence motivates attention to reference points and consequence distributions. It supplies no universal videogame loss coefficient. Decision: distinguish reversible positional errors from irreversible progression loss, while retaining stakes and avoiding manipulative loss pressure.

## Evaluation methods

### E1 Construct validity

Jacobs and Wallach, [Measurement and Fairness](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.05511).

Methodological framework from computational measurement. Decision: explicitly separate the intended experience from its proxy. A reliable verb counter is still not a validated enjoyment instrument; a useful rubric can make bounded claims without a universal score.

### E2 Production testing and synthetic play styles

Gillberg and colleagues (2023), [production game-testing case study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11105); Holmgård and colleagues, [procedural personas](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06881); Bergdahl and colleagues, [automated testing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15819).

These primary research reports support automated exploration and differentiated test policies, while highlighting deployment gaps. Decision: begin with interpretable production-route scripts and add costly learned agents only when needed. Synthetic styles are instruments, not representative human samples.

### E3 Replay and state sequences

[Rapier official determinism documentation](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/determinism/); Chen, Cheung and Yiu, [metamorphic testing report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12543); [fast-check official model-based testing](https://fast-check.dev/docs/advanced/model-based-testing/).

Technical and methodological sources. Determinism is conditional on engine/version/initialization and whole-game ordering. Related-input and state-sequence tests can expose faults when full expected outputs are impractical. Decision: use actual owners and small laws, test the observer with counterexamples, and do not assert conservation where powered/damped arcade rules do not promise it.

### E4 Comparative experiments

Kohavi and colleagues, [Online Experimentation at Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/online-experimentation-at-microsoft/); Dwork and colleagues (2015), [adaptive data analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26250683/).

Experimentation methods support controlled comparisons and warn about adaptive reuse. Decision: pin baseline/candidate, pair relevant scenarios, keep held-out challenges distinct and treat repeated frames as correlated observations. The causal effect on simulated behavior is distinct from an effect on human experience.

### E5 Judge and objective failures

Zheng and colleagues (2023), [Judging LLM as a Judge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685); Gao, Schulman and Hilton (2023), [reward-model overoptimization](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/gao23h/gao23h.pdf); Krakovna and colleagues, [specification-gaming cases](https://deepmind.google/blog/specification-gaming-the-flip-side-of-ai-ingenuity/).

LLM evaluation has demonstrated biases; proxy optimization and specification gaming can satisfy metrics while missing intent. These are not direct game-enjoyment studies. Decision: bound editorial judgments, require observed evidence, test adversarial strategies, retain disagreement and never let an attractive score override a broken invariant.

## Research practice for future updates

For a new claim, keep the original source, type of study, manipulated/measured variables, result, important limitation, design decision and discriminating test. Do not turn an abstract into a full-methods claim. Do not call within-paper repetitions independent replication. Record null and conflicting findings. Prefer a small relevant evidence update to an ever-growing reading list without changed decisions.
