# PQ-173.00 repair — the camera that was pointed at nothing

## WHAT I FOUND

The tool that photographs the game so somebody can judge how it feels has now failed twice, and both
times it failed by saying it had succeeded.

The first time, it never got past the title screen. It took 337 pictures of the word SPACEFACE and
reported that everything was fine.

The second time — the "repair" — it clicked through the menus properly, waited until the game was
running at full speed, and took 403 pictures of a ringed planet, a dozen asteroids and a star field.
**There was no ship in any of them.** Not the player's ship, and not one of the ten enemies. Its own
notes said the ship was on screen in all 403 pictures, because it had checked the only thing it knew
how to check: it did the arithmetic to work out where the ship *would* appear, and the arithmetic
said "in the middle". Nobody ever asked whether anything had actually been drawn there.

I opened the pictures. I magnified the spot the notes pointed at and turned the brightness up until
empty space went grey. There is nothing there.

The reason turned out to be simple, and it is not a broken game: **the ships take about two and a
half minutes to show up after a fight starts.** Everything downloads correctly, the graphics card is
real, nothing errors. The ship's parts just are not handed to the screen for the first couple of
minutes, and the camera was taking all of its pictures inside that gap. The same fight, photographed
a minute later, is full of ships. Another lane's photographs did show the ship — because their
camera is much slower, so by accident it was always late enough.

## WHAT I CHANGED

**The camera now waits for the ships, and proves they are there before it takes a single picture.**
Two separate pieces of evidence, and it needs both:

- the drawing engine itself is asked to confirm it drew the ship's parts this instant, through the
  same camera the pictures come from — not that the ship exists somewhere, that it was *drawn*;
- and the pixels where the ship should be are counted, because space is black and a lit hull is not.

If the ships never turn up, the camera now **stops with an error**. It cannot produce a set of
photographs any more. That is the whole point: a picture of an empty arena that says PASS is exactly
the failure this whole effort exists to end.

Every photograph now also records whether the ship was drawn in it, so a run that loses the ship
halfway can never again look like a run that kept it. And if half the pictures have no ship, the
whole set is thrown away rather than filed.

**Words on the glass.** Another lane reported that mission cards were still showing through even
with on-screen text switched off. They were right, and the reason is that the list of things to hide
is always one screen behind the game — a contract card that appears nine seconds into a fight was
never on a list written before it started. So instead of a longer list, the camera now sweeps the
whole overlay for anything showing a word and hides it, once before the run and once a second
during it, and writes down what it hid.

**It now says which graphics card took the pictures**, and how much of the slowdown is the game and
how much is the photographer.

## WHAT YOU WILL FEEL

Nothing yet — this is the camera, not the game. But every judgement about how the game feels from
here on is made from pictures with your ship in them, instead of pictures of an empty sky.

## Engineering appendix

**Hypothesis line.** When a Crucible run starts, the authored ship parts are not submitted to the
renderer for ~150 s of wall clock, so a capture gated only on `realtimeFraction >= 0.6` photographs
an arena with no hulls in it; gating the first frame on *drawn* evidence instead of on frustum
projection should take frames-with-a-hull from 0/403 to all of them.

**What was ruled out, with evidence** (`.devshots/fun-loop/lanes/INSTRUMENT/logs/hull-diag.mjs`,
log `hull-diag-bundled.log`):

| suspect | evidence |
|---|---|
| a URL/query flag the capture inherits | navigates to the plain server root, identical to `scripts/check-crucible-route.mjs` |
| a missing or failing asset | `FAILED_REQUESTS 0`, `BAD_RESPONSES 0` |
| software rasteriser / no parallel shader compile | `ANGLE (Intel, Intel(R) Graphics (0x00007D45) Direct3D11)`, `KHR_parallel_shader_compile: true` |
| the hull is subpixel | measured `hullPx` 116 px with 1011 lit pixels once drawn |
| the projection is wrong | a DOM marker at the projected point, screenshotted, then cropped 3x with brightness lifted: correct position, pure black underneath |
| another lane's capture has a flag we lack | its Chromium args are a strict subset of ours; its hull appears because it screenshots at ~0.55 fps in a session already minutes old |

**Files.** `scripts/lib/bench/frameStripCapture.mjs` (new exports `HULL_PART_NAME_RE`,
`installDrawHooks`, `waitForHullsDrawn`, `sweepHudText`, `FRAME_FORMAT`, `FRAME_QUALITY`);
`test/frame-strip.test.mjs`.

**The gate.** `installDrawHooks` wraps `onAfterRender` on every mesh matching `/^LOD\d/`, counting
per MESH. Ownership is resolved at read time by current world distance to the player and to each
survival-cohort hostile, never pinned at hook time — the piloted run proved why within one attempt
(`playerDraws 0` while nine hostiles drew, because the game pools and swaps those meshes). Draws are
counted only when the `camera` argument is `state.render.camera`, which excludes shadow cascades and
depth pre-passes. A companion condition suggested in review — also require
`renderer.getRenderTarget() === null` — was tried and reverted after one 24-minute empty run: this
renderer is post-processed, so the scene pass draws into a target and only the composite reaches the
default framebuffer. The tally therefore keeps a second, unrestricted counter and, if nothing has
matched the shipping camera after 60 s while parts *are* drawing, says so in the log and falls back,
recording `hullWait.pass` in the manifest. An instrument that cannot report its own broken
assumption is worse than none.

**Known limits, recorded in the manifest as `hullWait.knownLimits` rather than papered over:** there
is no occlusion test, so a hull behind an asteroid still counts as drawn; and the lit-pixel box
cannot separate hull from a bright rock inside it. Both signals would have to be fooled at once, and
the frames are opened by eye before a strip is announced.

**Manifest additions.** `hullWait{waitedS, drawnAtSimTime, hullPx, litPixelsOnHull, litPixelsBoxArea,
hostilesDrawing, pass, evidence, knownLimits}`, `hullDrawn{medianPartsPerFrame, framesWithHull,
framesTotal}`, per-frame `hullPartsDrawn` / `hostilesDrawing`, `webglRenderer`,
`parallelShaderCompile`, `frameFormat`, `frameQuality`, `realtimeBeforeScreencast`,
`hudTextVerifiedAtStart` / `hudTextVerifiedAtEnd` / `hudTextSweptAtStart` / `hudTextSweptDuringRun`,
and `momentsInSpan`.

**The moment-list split.** `moments` remains the run's whole event stream from tick 0, because that
is what `compareStripEventTicks` compares and determinism is a fact about the sim, not about the
window someone photographed. `momentsInSpan` is the subset a viewer of these frames can see, and
retention, the `nearMoment` flag and the critic all use that one. Before the split, a collision from
ninety seconds before the first frame marked every frame "near a moment", so the 8 fps burst had
silently become the baseline.

**Codec.** The screencast was PNG. Measured: 90 % of real time with an empty arena, 53 % once the
arena is dressed — under the normal-speed floor. Switched to JPEG q92 and recorded in the manifest,
with `realtimeBeforeScreencast` measured immediately before the stream starts so the photographer's
share of any slowdown is always a number in the receipt.

**Checks.** `node --test test/frame-strip.test.mjs` (7), `test/fun-critic.test.mjs` (14),
`test/fun-report.test.mjs` (10) — all green. No golden is touched by this leaf; no `src/**` file is
touched by this leaf.

**Adversarial review.** agy Gemini 3.8 Flash High, read-only, scoped to one function and one
question, found the shadow/offscreen-pass hole and the instanced-mesh batch-origin
misattribution — both fixed. Its broad five-question review of the same file returned zero bytes;
the scope was the whole difference. Kimi K3 xhigh reviewed the same packet in parallel.
