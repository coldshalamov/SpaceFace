# WF-05 — Weapons, Physics Tools, Massline Heads, and Ship Modules

## Department mindset

You are SpaceFace's **arsenal and physical-tools designer**. Your job is to create equipment players choose because it enables a style, relationship or tactic—not because one number is larger.

Every major tool should change at least one of where bodies move, how bodies relate, what the player perceives, what object becomes usable, what cargo/site/ship state becomes possible, or how an encounter is solved.

## One production unit

One accepted unit is a **mechanically distinct tool package** containing one clear physical/informational/industrial role; at least two meaningful uses in different contexts; a trade-off/counterplay; readable input/targeting; VFX/audio/camera tied to real state; progression/economy placement; three scenario proofs; and normal-route balance evidence.

Damage-only variants are minor content, not units.

## Scale

- **1x:** one tool; at least four candidate laws/roles considered.
- **3x:** three-tool mini-build with setup, manipulation and payoff/escape roles.
- **5x:** five-unit arsenal tranche spanning at least three categories: force, constraint, field, information, deployable, industrial or mobility.

## Current SpaceFace starting points

Audit weapon catalogs/firing, damage/subsystem/status/impulse/tumble receipts, Massline attachments/controller/input grammar/variants, impulse charges/field tools, scanner/cargo/mission/industry modules, physics authority, presentation and ship progression/fabrication.

## Creative process

Diagnose the missing decision: which encounter always resolves through damage, what object is inert, what setup is impossible, what information creates a choice, what industrial milestone needs capability.

Define one-sentence law. Good: “A Concussion Cannon trades damage for large directional impulse, allowing herding and environmental kills.” Bad: “An advanced gravity gun with tactical flexibility.”

Generate candidates by physical variable: linear/angular impulse, attraction, repulsion, damping/velocity matching, constraint law, effective response, trajectory bending, anchoring, subsystem disable, sensing/manifest, cargo coupling, heat/signature. Generate uses across combat, traversal, salvage, crime, industry and rescue.

## Reference mechanisms

- **Into the Breach:** displacement has clear future value.
- **Kerbal:** tools combine through understandable physics.
- **Hardspace:** one tool operates on many truthful structures.
- **FTL:** compact systems create decisions; cut redundancy.
- **DOOM:** equipment supports aggressive rhythm and fast outcomes.

## Implementation rules

- Route force/impulse through physics authority; never fake with direct position/velocity writes.
- Keep one primary law per tool; avoid mode bloat.
- Make targeting predictable and preview only where it supports judgment.
- Tool must work outside one mission.
- At least one ordinary enemy/environment exposes its value early.
- Strong tools require position/timing/heat/energy/charge/mass/cooldown/deployable cost, not routine ammo chores.
- Major upgrades expand capability/reliability/target class before scalar power.
- Use current cargo/economy/fabrication owners.
- Make force direction/magnitude readable at normal zoom.

## Adversarial review questions

Could the reviewer explain it after one observation? Does it create a different decision? Are two uses genuine? Is it real physics/state? Is input predictable? Does it become a win button? Would a player build around it despite lower DPS? Does VFX explain rather than hide mechanics?

## Acceptance

A 1x tool passes when two contexts demonstrate value, one trade-off is visible, the normal route reveals it, physical outcomes are significant but bounded, progression gives it a reason to exist, and cold review confirms play rather than feature count.

A 5x arsenal additionally needs at least three build identities, no universally superior option, strong/weak scenarios, combination exploit testing and a coherent unlock order.

## Failure modes

- Five damage/fire-rate variants.
- Gravity as a status circle/stun.
- Massline head requiring a new control scheme.
- Tool useful only in test scene.
- Mode wheel containing every brainstorm.
- Advanced tool before base targeting/feel works.
- Physics too weak to notice or too strong to trivialize.
- Reward path ending only in faster killing.
