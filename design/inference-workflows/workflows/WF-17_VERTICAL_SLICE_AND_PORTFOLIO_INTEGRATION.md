# WF-17 — Vertical Slice, Reference Pocket, and Portfolio Integration

## Department mindset

You are SpaceFace's **creative/production director for an integrated playable slice**. Your job is to turn accepted ingredients into a piece of the actual final game, expose what does not compose, and extract a repeatable production method.

This workflow prevents the project from becoming a warehouse of systems, source packs and isolated proofs.

## One production unit

One accepted unit is a **complete playable beat/pocket** containing:

1. a clear experience thesis;
2. a composed physical location and route;
3. living-world activity;
4. one or more player opportunities;
5. current physics/combat/traversal used meaningfully;
6. production-quality visual/audio/UI presentation;
7. consequence/aftermath;
8. normal-route human, technical, art and performance acceptance.

For this workflow, 5x may be one five-unit sector tranche rather than five unrelated slices.

## Scale

- **1x:** one activity pocket or 30–90 second production-quality playable beat.
- **3x:** three connected pockets/beats forming a 5-minute route.
- **5x:** one production benchmark sector/session slice with five accepted beats or units, 5–10 minute route, systemic variation, human review and propagation recipe.

## Current SpaceFace starting points

Audit:

- current Ceres reference-sector program and five-minute gate;
- current Physics-as-Spectacle dependency chain;
- existing accepted gameplay foundations and open defects;
- current pocket topology, cast, traffic/jobs, sites, hazards and events;
- selected accepted/promotable asset families;
- route/performance/visual acceptance tools;
- current leases and active packets.

The current recommended 5x benchmark is Ceres, not a second reference sector.

## Creative process

### 1. Write one experience thesis

Example:

> Ceres is a hard-working industrial belt where ore becomes freight, freight becomes opportunity, law and predation contest the route, and the Cathedral shows what failure leaves behind.

Every asset, actor, event and effect must strengthen this thesis.

### 2. Build a beat sheet, not a script

Define likely experiential beats:

- arrival/orientation;
- ordinary work;
- transfer/logistics;
- voluntary diversion;
- threat/crime/opportunity;
- physical confrontation/escape;
- response/aftermath;
- quiet landmark/mystery;
- exit or continuation.

Systems may fill beats differently each run.

### 3. Rehearse with current content

Before broad art production:

- compose routes and participants;
- use proxies/current assets;
- measure camera visibility and dead intervals;
- prove player opportunity and physics terrain;
- remove redundant content;
- freeze the spatial/activity thesis.

### 4. Select production work from observed need

Only then re-author/promote:

- slice-critical occupation identities;
- essential props/structures;
- hero asset corrections;
- effect/audio families;
- UI cues.

## Reference mechanisms

- **Volition vertical slice:** prove both the game and production method.
- **Naughty Dog blockout-to-polish:** lock experience before detail.
- **Watch Dogs:** live activity and player interference create anecdotes.
- **Left 4 Dead:** authored route supports dynamic pacing.
- **Dishonored:** systems, space and presentation designed holistically.
- **CDPR/Horizon art-direction practice:** district identity and cross-discipline production.

## Implementation rules

- No new universal framework without a proven gap that cannot be solved through current owners.
- Integrate complete beats sequentially; do not partially wire every asset/system at once.
- Use current player-route evidence and exact candidate identities.
- Primary activity must enter the normal camera.
- Ordinary life, player opportunity, spectacle and quiet contrast all need space.
- Use foreground/midground/background layers with honest materialization.
- Performance optimization preserves density and visual premise.
- A slice cannot pass on green automated checks alone; human play and independent art review are required.
- If a core mechanic blocks the slice, stop and route WF-15 or WF-18 rather than scripting around it.

## Adversarial review questions

- Does this feel like a real piece of the intended final game?
- Would the player remain voluntarily for five or ten minutes?
- Does the place operate without the player?
- Are opportunities noticed without mission assignment?
- Can the player physically improvise and alter outcomes?
- Is the visual/audio personality bright, kinetic and distinctive?
- Does every unit justify its attention and cost?
- What would a trailer clip show that no other space game shows?
- Can the team explain how to build the next sector more efficiently now?

## Acceptance

A 1x pocket passes when:

- entrance, activity, opportunity, consequence and exit all work;
- current mechanics feel valuable there;
- art/audio/UI support the physical story;
- ordinary route, human review, performance and save/Continue pass;
- reviewer says KEEP without relying on future content.

A 5x benchmark additionally needs:

- coherent 5–10 minute route;
- several visible occupations and system relationships;
- legal/criminal/service/mystery or equivalent breadth;
- one memorable physics anecdote;
- distinct pocket identities;
- acceptable empty/quiet rhythm;
- portfolio and propagation records;
- no unresolved foundational defect hidden by polish.

## Failure modes

- Integration task becoming another framework.
- Bulk-importing donor assets.
- Scripted showcase rather than ordinary play.
- Five systems present but not interacting.
- Activity only on radar.
- Art finished before route works.
- Performance achieved by removing density/effects.
- Calling the slice done because every ticket closed.
