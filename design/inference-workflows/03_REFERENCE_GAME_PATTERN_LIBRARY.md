# Reference-Game Pattern Library

This library exists to improve creative reasoning without turning SpaceFace into a collage of borrowed surfaces. A reference is useful only when the agent extracts the **mechanism** that creates a player experience, identifies the production conditions behind it, and translates that mechanism through SpaceFace's existing verbs, owners, camera, fiction, and performance envelope.

**This library is an attractor — handle accordingly.** These same ~16 games appear
throughout the workflow docs; agents that read them before ideating converge on
them, and repeated runs average toward "competent Freelancer-with-tethers" instead
of natively SpaceFace-weird. Countermeasures, enforced elsewhere and repeated here:

- the **repo-native divergence pass** (02, Phase 4) never reads this file;
- at least one accepted candidate per slate traces to **no** reference game;
- the board flags any reference used 3+ times in 30 days — **rotate away from
  flagged references** or state why the mechanism is genuinely load-bearing;
- record references used per unit (`inference-record.mjs --refs`), or rotation
  cannot bind;
- a mechanism may be transferred; a *cast list, roster, or scenario* from any
  reference (or from these docs' own examples) may not.

## How to use references

For every selected reference, write four lines:

```text
Experience produced:
Mechanism and production method:
What must not be copied:
SpaceFace translation:
```

Do not write “make it like X.” Name the transferable mechanism.

---

## Freelancer — legible authored space adventure

**What it does well:** clear local navigation, visually distinct systems, readable factions and traffic, approachable combat, destinations that support a broad career fantasy.

**Transferable mechanisms:** local regions have immediately readable route and landmark structure; traffic and law reinforce place identity; stations, planets and lanes are understood as useful destinations rather than scattered props; the player can shift between trade, combat and exploration without changing games; presentation is colorful and inviting rather than physically literal.

**Do not copy:** 2003-era movement constraints, exact lane system, mission-board repetition, or its visual surfaces.

**SpaceFace translation:** keep the legible economic/navigational foundation but let momentum, Massline, fields, collision, piracy and industry create the distinctive play.

## Endless Sky — content grammar and causal continuity

**What it does well:** large content breadth built from reusable data grammars; locations, missions, traffic, factions and player assets remain connected.

**Transferable mechanisms:** a stable content vocabulary allows many authors to add coherent material; places expose different services, politics, traffic and story conditions; missions reference durable world nouns rather than isolated encounter scripts; player progression creates a sense of accumulated capital and regional access.

**Do not copy:** text-heavy delivery, crew/payroll chores, ammunition hygiene, or a flat remake of its systems.

**SpaceFace translation:** use current data catalogs and owner seams to multiply authored sectors, jobs, incidents, site states and ledger threads while keeping the action physical and nonmodal.

## EVE Online — economy and conflict as content generators

**What it does well:** resources, logistics, risk, law and ownership produce reasons for players to move and collide.

**Transferable mechanisms:** value moves physically or conceptually through routes; safer and riskier space have different opportunity structures; destruction, hauling, industry and territorial control are economically connected; aggression rules and response need to be understandable; economic systems are most interesting when they create conflict and decisions.

**Do not copy:** MMO-scale complexity, spreadsheets as play, long travel, opaque skill systems or market dependence on thousands of humans.

**SpaceFace translation:** every industrial chain should create visible traffic, theft opportunity, response, aftermath and infrastructure goals. Economy work must end in new agency and world transformation, not merely a larger credit balance.

## No Man's Sky — controlled procedural breadth

**What it does well:** a small team scales visual and world variety through procedural tools while retaining art-direction control.

**Transferable mechanisms:** author the aesthetic grammar, then generate within it; separate functional families, variation parameters and curation; use production tools to accelerate repetition, not to avoid artistic judgment; populate generated environments through dedicated placement and simulation layers; keep a tight feedback loop between artists, programmers and the playable result.

**Do not copy:** enormous procedural scope, resource-grind structure, planetary surfaces or its aesthetic.

**SpaceFace translation:** accepted asset families, state variants, composition recipes and sector identities become controlled production inputs. Every generated candidate still faces normal-camera review and can be cut.

## Outer Wilds — curiosity as an objective system

**What it does well:** visible anomalies, partial understanding and environmental relationships cause voluntary exploration without conventional quest pressure.

**Transferable mechanisms:** show something strange or incomplete within perception range; let observation produce a usable hypothesis; make knowledge change where the player can go or what they can do; preserve discoveries in a map/ledger without forcing reading; connect several clues physically and spatially.

**Do not copy:** time loop, archaeology structure, Nomai aesthetics or knowledge-only progression as the whole game.

**SpaceFace translation:** unusual traffic, wreck evidence, fields, routes, broken infrastructure and planetary operations should lead the player through physical investigation and optional ledger synthesis.

## Subnautica — signals, biome identity and useful fear

**What it does well:** environmental identity, strong silhouettes, audio, resources and danger turn navigation into emotional geography.

**Transferable mechanisms:** every region has a recognizable material, light, sound and hazard identity; resources and threats are distributed according to ecology; signals provide direction while the route itself creates discovery; deeper access is earned through capabilities; ordinary traversal alternates safety, awe and threat.

**Do not copy:** survival meters, underwater movement, crafting grind or biome surfaces.

**SpaceFace translation:** sector families need distinct traversal, traffic, hazard, material and opportunity rules. A player should recognize Ceres, Helios and Tethys by what happens there before reading a label.

## Hardspace: Shipbreaker — one physical verb with production rules

**What it does well:** cutting, tethering, pressure, systems and salvage create many outcomes from a coherent physical premise.

**Transferable mechanisms:** one core verb operates on many truthful substructures; asset production is designed around gameplay operations; physical mistakes generate consequences rather than arbitrary failure; UI, audio and VFX explain material and system state; content rules protect the premise during production.

**Do not copy:** first-person work shift, full arbitrary mesh cutting, corporate satire or slow labor pacing.

**SpaceFace translation:** Massline, impulse, fields, component states, cargo and collision must work on visibly truthful bodies. New ships/sites should be authored with useful anchors, sockets, failure states and aftermath from the beginning.

## Kerbal Space Program — readable physical experimentation

**What it does well:** players form hypotheses, build, test, fail visibly and learn from understandable motion.

**Transferable mechanisms:** physical outcomes are predictable enough to learn but rich enough to surprise; trajectory and state readouts expose useful information without solving the task; failure creates comedy and knowledge; parts and systems combine into player-authored solutions.

**Do not copy:** orbital realism, construction complexity, aerospace UI or simulation scale.

**SpaceFace translation:** release arcs, anchor selection, velocity, field direction and line load should be legible; assistance removes keyboard friction but preserves player decisions.

## DOOM (2016) — forward momentum and fast combat resolution

**What it does well:** every system rewards decisive movement and prevents passive attrition.

**Transferable mechanisms:** enemies have clear tactical roles; ordinary threats resolve quickly; resources and finishers push the player into action; arenas contain movement loops and threat hierarchy; feedback makes every hit and kill readable.

**Do not copy:** glory kills, gore aesthetic, FPS arena structure or exact resource loops.

**SpaceFace translation:** light enemies should be numerous, manipulable and disposable; difficulty comes from formations, projectiles, position and combined roles rather than health-bar length.

## Left 4 Dead — authored spaces plus dynamic pacing

**What it does well:** authored routes support variable pressure, temporary quiet, event escalation and memorable group situations.

**Transferable mechanisms:** a director manages intensity, not every action; authored encounter spaces contain explicit opportunities and fallback states; pacing alternates pressure and breathing room; reusable enemy roles combine differently according to context; dynamic systems are constrained by authored intent.

**Do not copy:** four-player structure, zombie hordes or invisible rubber-banding.

**SpaceFace translation:** sector pockets define cast, routes, terrain and opportunities; current directors/events vary pressure inside those boundaries. Quiet space becomes contrast, not default emptiness.

## Watch Dogs 2 / Legion — lived-world anecdotes and group context

**What they do well:** ordinary NPC personalities, activities, reactions and multi-actor situations produce unscripted stories the player can disrupt.

**Transferable mechanisms:** actors have current activities before the player arrives; incidents reassign roles and reactions rather than replacing the whole world; group behaviors preserve participant context; the player can escalate, help or exploit a situation; unpredictability is bounded by readable roles.

**Do not copy:** crowds of pedestrians, social hacking or city-scale simulation.

**SpaceFace translation:** miners, haulers, escorts, patrols, tenders and salvors form short causal scenes. Prefer live participants, interruption, response and aftermath over dedicated mission actors.

## Dishonored — holistic spaces and multiple solutions

**What it does well:** level geometry, AI roles, powers, story and objectives support several coherent approaches.

**Transferable mechanisms:** encounter spaces are designed around verbs and routes; objectives expose several physical or social solutions; environmental storytelling and gameplay routes reinforce one another; systems are combined deliberately rather than merely co-located.

**Do not copy:** stealth taxonomy, first-person powers or mission structure.

**SpaceFace translation:** a convoy, checkpoint, wreck or refinery operation should support interception, evasion, force, theft, protection and observation where current systems make those approaches honest.

## FTL — feeling first, systems second

**What it does well:** small systems combine into tense decisions; features are altered or cut when they do not produce the intended experience.

**Transferable mechanisms:** every system creates decisions under pressure; encounters are compact and legible; consequences propagate across systems; production protects the intended emotional rhythm rather than feature completeness.

**Do not copy:** node map, crew management, pause-and-command combat or random event cards as the main game.

**SpaceFace translation:** when a mechanic technically exists but feels wrong, simplify or remove it. A small expressive physical grammar outranks a large catalog of weak modes.

## DUST 514 / modular environment pipelines — coherent multiplication

**What it does well:** modular standards generate many locations while preserving functional and visual structure.

**Transferable mechanisms:** create modules around actual gameplay dimensions and routes; establish a shared manufacturing language; vary composition, state and ownership rather than building every piece from scratch; measure kit coverage and repetition.

**Do not copy:** exact modular grids or shooter map assumptions.

**SpaceFace translation:** cargo, repair, mining, law, salvage and construction kits should be composed differently per pocket and supplemented by hero forms only when identity requires them.

## Into the Breach — consequence legibility

**What it does well:** force, position and future consequences are visible enough that failure feels earned.

**Transferable mechanisms:** display the relevant future physical result; make displacement as important as damage; let the environment and objectives create tactical value; keep information hierarchy clean.

**Do not copy:** turn-based determinism or grid combat.

**SpaceFace translation:** release prediction, force vectors, target states, cargo risk and environmental danger should be readable enough that players can plan physical tricks without the game playing for them.

## SUPERHOT / physics sandboxes — rules becoming spectacle

**What they do well:** a few clear laws create immediately understandable improvisation.

**Transferable mechanisms:** preserve a simple relationship between input and physical outcome; dramatize motion and causality; make ordinary objects tactically meaningful; let replays or aftermath expose what happened.

**SpaceFace translation:** avoid helpers that secretly own movement or outcomes. Use camera, trails, impact, deformation, debris and audio to make real physics exciting.

---

## Selection rule

Use no more than four external references in one unit. At least one must inform the **player experience**, one the **production method**, and one the **review criterion**. The accepted result must still be unmistakably SpaceFace: top-down, bright kinetic arcade-industrial science fiction, a living economy, physical improvisation, optional story depth and persistent industrial growth.
