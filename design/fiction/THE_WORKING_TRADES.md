# THE WORKING TRADES
## Six Profession Dossiers of the Open Lane
### Companion to the Working Light — Lane Guild of Tethys

*Haulers carry mass. Couriers carry promises. Patrols carry drawers. These six carry the seams between them.*

The Code tells you what a lamp means. This volume tells you who is sweating under the lamp. It does not re-sing Mira Bluepack’s bulk-haul, Kess of the Span’s priority-mail, Warden Keel’s customs-scan, or Rell of the Moisture Column’s ore-tally. Those trades already have faces on every Helios board. What follows are the trades that make their work possible — or clean up after it fails.

If you paint yourself as one of these without the kit, the belt will know. Motion first. Lamps second. Paint never.

## 1. The Surveyor
### Crew name: *surveyor* · Slang: *reader*, *dark-hand*, *seam-sniff* · Insult: *lamp tourist*, *pulse-peeper*, *chart thief*

### Who flies it

**Ness of the Cold Pin** runs the long crab out of Tethys when the insurance desks want a corridor proved empty of “unexpected mass.” Callsign on open band is often just **PIN-3**. Habit: she names every uncharted rock after a person who still owes her coffee. The first seam she ever filed still answers, in her log only, to *Uncle Harrow, two cups outstanding.*

### Hull

**`ship_ranger`** — the Ranger. Long-endurance cruise, quiet field-sail manners, utility spine deep enough for pulse packs without turning the boat into a barge. Surveyors who cannot afford a Ranger fake the trade on a Hitch (`ship_kestrel`) and die of range. Ness will not share a chart with a Hitch that claims her work.

### Silhouette

What no hauler carries: a **sensor spine** stepped along the dorsal line from midships to a half-ship past the stern, ending in a cool **survey pin** on a slender boom that can crab ninety degrees off the nose. Port and starboard **array paddles** — flat, slightly warped ovals — sit high on the flanks so dust does not kiss them first. Under the chin: a **range-mast cluster** of three thin rods in a triangle, not a drill. Spare **gel drums** for pulse housings ride in a open rack aft of the cockpit, never painted the same colour twice. No magnet arms. No plate jaws. If you see jaws on a “surveyor,” you are looking at a salvor practicing honesty.

### Paint and markings

Base is **ash-grey with a cold-blue strip** along the sensor spine — the strip is paint over cable raceways, not fashion. Wear: **matte scrub on the boom tip and paddle leading edges** where micro-dust from other people’s cones sandblasts the gel. Stencils: `CHART-LIVE`, sector glyph, and a Guild-readable **survey licence** on the starboard cheek — Concord form when she works Ref 44-C corridors, hand-stamped Drift claim-route number when she works for shift bosses who do not care about drawers. Licence example on Ness’s hull: **SV-TETH-4419**. Under the bridge lip, chalked and never cleaned: *Harrow still owes.*

### The Working Light they show

They live in **Surveying — “Reading the dark”**: slow crab, **scanner pulse rings** as brief shell-flashes, side lamps dim, **single cool survey pin** on the boom. No work floods. No amber load-strobe unless they are ferrying cores home and then they should admit mass like anyone else.

They **fake** transit-empty clean burn when a client wants the chart “unadvertised.” They **neglect** return chevrons — surveyors are not home under rock — and they skip escort pins unless hired as eyes for a column. Quiet-adjacent readers run colder still: pulse only, pin nearly invented. A surveyor who floods a face is either lost or lying.

### Routine

Hour 0–1: spine-wake at the berth, coffee that tastes of sealant, log yesterday’s pulse ghosts.  
1–3: transit light-hull to the assigned dark, no amber.  
3–8: crab the grid. Pulse. Wait. Pulse. Log. Rename a rock. Eat cold protein while the array cools.  
8–10: one slow pass along any wreck whisper — mark, do not cut.  
10–11: stack green final into whoever paid. Offload cores under tally if Meridian bought the map.  
11–12: sell one unofficial coordinate to a Free waystation kid “for filter money,” then sleep with the pin still warm in the dream.

### What they carry / off-books

Carry: pulse cores, spare gels, sealed chart wafers, a single self-defense hardpoint that has not been fired since the last lean cycle.  
Off-books: **pre-file seam coordinates**, wreck headings one shift early, “market research” on who is cutting loud. They will not sell a live claim stripe off a Drift face — that is how you lose whistle rights forever — but they will sell *which* face is about to open.

### How they react to a stranger closing

They keep working the crab until your aspect says intercept. Then a short hail: *PIN-3 reading, hold wide of the ring.* If you ignore the ring, they go dark on lamps and bright on legs. They do not scatter like couriers; they **slide** — always keeping the boom between you and their soft belly. Hands are asked only for tow if the sail dies. Guns are a last grammar.

### Divergence by faction

| Flavour | What you see |
|---|---|
| **Concord-licensed** | Blue-white spine strip exact; licence stencil machine-cut; pulse cadence metronome-steady; Ref 44-C annex plate under the pin. Paint almost clean — dust is evidence. |
| **Meridian-contracted** | Gold pin gel over the cool survey white; company stripe fresh; data umbilicals branded; they invoice for every extra pulse ring you make them fire. |
| **Drift-collective** | Orange dust welded into paddle seams; claim-route numbers hand-cut; pin is miner-amber glass, not cool white; they survey for the shift, not the desk. |
| **Reach-adjacent** | Pin present, pulse too loud, wear on the gun lips not the paddles; sometimes a removable red scar under a panel. Chart wafer racks empty — they are ranging, not mapping. |
| **Free Frontier** | Faded cyan over primer patches; pin hand-wired; neighbour-wave double-flash when they pass a known hull; licence is a waystation chalk number that changes when the roof leaks. |

## 2. The Salvor
### Crew name: *salvor* · Slang: *boner*, *second-cut*, *umbrella* · Insult: *grave-rake*, *plate jackal*, *post-murder clerk*

### Who flies it

**Bram of the Second Cut** works cold hulls from the ash lanes out toward Charon when the first crews have already taken the easy scrap. Habit: he keeps a **single fastener** from every wreck that paid — strung on a wire behind the pilot seat until the wire sings in thrust. The seat rattles like a pocket of teeth.

### Hull

**`ship_drifter`** — the Drifter. Multirole enough to weld, haul, and discourage argument; cargo for plate without the mule’s solemn mass; utility mounts for the gear that makes the trade legal-looking. Ironbacks are for living rock. Atlas hulls are for living cargo. Salvage is a conversation with the dead on a hull that can still leave in a hurry.

### Silhouette

Bolted truth: **hooded amber salvage umbrellas** — work floods with deep cowls — on articulated arms over the dorsal and port midships, aimed *down* at the corpse, never out at the lane. **Jaws**: a pair of hydraulic plate-shears on the starboard bow knuckle, blades notched from old alloys. **Tether reels** at the hips, lines bright when live. Aft: a **scrap cradle** open-backed like a ribcage, clamps for irregular plate. Port flank: **drum stack** of cutting gas and seal foam, chained, never faired. No claim stripe unless Drift already filed the wreck as worksite. If the umbrellas are off and the guns are warm, the Code does not protect the story you tell later.

### Paint and markings

Base **soot-brown over old freighter grey**, scorch fans under the umbrellas where sparks kissed hull for years. Wear: **bright metal on the jaw knuckles and cradle lips** — the places that rub wreck and berth alike. Stern clamp points polished from yard dockings. Stencils: `SCRAP-OPS`, `MEN DOWN-RANGE`, and a **yard licence** when honest — **SL-VSTA-208** on Bram’s starboard jaw housing. Meridian-insured jobs add a gold invoice barcode that never quite survives the next spark bath. Reach-adjacent hulls stencil nothing and wear red under removable panels.

### The Working Light they show

**Salvaging — “Picking the bones”**: relative hold on cold hull, **amber salvage umbrellas**, cutter sparks, tether lines bright, scrap cloud. Weapons cold if they want the Guild story.

They **fake** “hull open” repair corners to keep traffic wide while they strip a contested wreck. They **neglect** load-strobe until the cradle is full — then a lazy amber heartbeat that does not match true hauler yaw. They almost never show return chevrons; home is a yard, not a claim. Distress they know by sound: they answer red-white only if the math says salvage rights later, not charity.

### Routine

0–1: yard brief, which wreck is still “legal enough.”  
1–2: transit empty, clean burn, umbrellas stowed flat.  
2–3: hold station, umbrellas out, first cut on the easy skin.  
3–7: second cut — bulkheads, cable runs, anything that still holds a serial. Bram adds a fastener to the wire.  
7–8: cradle full, irregular amber strobe home.  
8–10: unload under tally lamps if the yard is honest; under dust if it is not.  
10–12: grind slag off the jaws, drink something that pretends to be water, argue with Meridian about what “intact black box” means.

### What they carry / off-books

Carry: cutting gas, foam, tethers, plate blanks, a tagged black-box locker that is *supposed* to go to Concord when the wreck wore blue-white.  
Off-books: **unlogged serial plates**, personal effects that “fell out,” warheads that “were already safed,” Quiet-grade sealed crates found in false bulkheads. Bram will trade spare gel and foam to Free tenders. He will not trade a live Concord flight recorder where Warden Keel’s cousins can hear the channel.

### How they react to a stranger closing

They keep the umbrellas on and the cut going until your vector is rude. Hail is flat: *Second Cut working bones, give the spark cone.* If you close hot, umbrellas kill, tethers stay, guns wake. They do not scatter; scattering drops the payday. They will ask a stranger for a **hand on a tether** if the wreck starts to tumble — and they will remember if you help, and price you later.

### Divergence by faction

| Flavour | What you see |
|---|---|
| **Concord-licensed** | Umbrellas factory-amber; piracy-adjacent fine risk painted as caution stripes; black-box locker sealed and *visible*; blue licence plate readable at customs range. |
| **Meridian-contracted** | Gold barcode, clean company stripe on a filthy trade; invoice pulse on the mast when unloading; umbrellas timed like a premium depends on them — because it does. |
| **Drift-collective** | Claim paint if the wreck is filed worksite; miner-amber umbrellas ugly and correct; scrap goes to the forge that feeds the station, not a Tethys boutique. |
| **Reach-adjacent** | Red combat gels still in secondary housings; umbrellas late or cosmetic; jaws oversized; wear map wrong — gun-port polish, not cradle rub. |
| **Free Frontier** | Patch primer, cyan neighbour marks; umbrellas one dead lamp honest; they share foam with anyone whose distress was real; licence is a handshake and a chalk hash on the cradle. |

## 3. The Tender / Repair Rig
### Crew name: *tender*, *repair rig* · Slang: *patch boat*, *weld-wife*, *plate nurse* · Insult: *spark tourist*, *bandage barge*, *hull-open romantic*

### Who flies it

**Sola Patchline** holds soft station in the Helios–Tethys approach when someone’s mule ate a rock the charts swore was fiction. Habit: she **stencils the date of every exterior weld** on the plate itself in tiny white numbers, then photographs nothing — the dates are the log. Clients find their own hulls speaking calendar at them for years.

### Hull

**`ship_mule`** — the Mule. Stable under load, belly deep enough for **spare-plate racks**, slow enough that weld crews trust the relative drift. Rangers survey. Wasps threaten. Mules hold still while people stand on the skin of the void.

### Silhouette

Trade kit: **port-side plate rack** — vertical slots of curved hull skin, colour-mismatched, clamped like books. **Starboard welding boom** with twin articulations and a cluster of suit-lamp petals. Dorsal: **umbilical drum** and a soft-dock collar that never quite matches any one berth standard. Ventral: **magnetic work-shoes** tracks — scraped rails where suited bodies walk the underbelly. Four **static red “men at work” corner lamps** at the open bay lips. A temporary **white “do not push” bar** can swing across the client’s drive when Sola is inside their plume math. No survey boom. No salvage jaws. The mule’s freighter pods stay, but one row is always plates, not ore.

### Paint and markings

Base **shop-primer grey with safety-yellow bay lips**. Wear: **hand-polish and boot-scuff on the ventral rails and bay sills** — human traffic, not micrometeor. Welding boom elbow raw metal. Stencils: `HULL OPEN`, `SOFT TARGET`, `DO NOT WAKE`, and licence **RP-HEL-117** on the plate rack frame. Sola’s personal mark: a row of date stamps climbing the rack like a vine.

### The Working Light they show

**Effecting Repairs — “Hull open”**: soft relative drift or soft dock, **static red men-at-work corners**, welding stars, suit lamps, one engine cold, white do-not-push bar when needed.

They show **Loading — “Mouth open”** when taking plate aboard. They **neglect** load-strobe even when plate-heavy — the Guild grumbles; Meridian sometimes surcharges. They **fake** nothing if they can help it; fake repair is a Sker classic and Free radios never forget the name. They answer distress with purpose: red-white is their job interview.

### Routine

0: coffee, inventory plates by alloy, curse the one rack that always jams.  
1–2: loiter on the pin near the stack, red corners dark, listening for the call.  
2–6: soft dock, corners on, weld, date-stamp, suit change-out, re-weld the weld that failed the tap-test.  
6–7: undock, client spine-wakes first, tender waits until plume is polite.  
7–9: restock at Helios or a Free waystation; trade off-books seal kits.  
9–12: sleep in the bay with the red corners unpowered, still tasting ozone.

### What they carry / off-books

Carry: plate stock, weld wire, seal foam, suit patches, temporary clamp sets, a medical kit that has set more bones than a station clinic.  
Off-books: **unserialized plate** for Free hulls that cannot pay Meridian rates, “borrowed” Concord-spec seal rings, stimulant tabs for shift-end clients who still have to stack green final. Sola will not armor a Reach spear and call it a freighter repair. She will patch a Free neighbour for filters and a story.

### How they react to a stranger closing

They keep welding until the stranger’s wake is a problem. Hail: *Patchline hull-open, do not bounce me.* Free culture treats the mark almost sacred; Sola expects that courtesy even from Concord. If you close like a weigh-slip, red corners stay on and a rear hardpoint wakes without drama. They will **ask for a hand** — hold a plate, watch a cone — from anyone who hails soft.

### Divergence by faction

| Flavour | What you see |
|---|---|
| **Concord-licensed** | Yellow lips regulation-bright; dates stamped in approved font; spare plates serialized; blue seal on the soft-dock collar. |
| **Meridian-contracted** | Gold stripe, invoice mast-light, plate rack inventory QR; they repair insured hulls first and send others a polite denial. |
| **Drift-collective** | Heat-stained orange rack, plates scavenged from claim scrap; weld dates in grease pencil; they fix what keeps the shaft fed. |
| **Reach-adjacent** | Red corners on a timer, guns live under a tarp, plate rack half-empty (sold), scorch on bow not bay. |
| **Free Frontier** | Cyan neighbour wave hardwired next to red corners; primer patches; they leave the bay open while they eat so the lane can see the honesty. |

## 4. The Escort / Screen
### Crew name: *escort*, *screen* · Slang: *borrowed shadow*, *pin-dog*, *wedge* · Insult: *cargo tourist*, *gun without a hold*, *rent-a-wake*

### Who flies it

**Jex of the Borrowed Shadow** sells offset geometry to whoever’s mule is worth more than Jex’s pride. Habit: **never holds the same slot twice in a week** — port-high Monday, starboard-low Tuesday — so anyone ranging the formation learns nothing useful. The principal hates it. Jex lives.

### Hull

**`ship_wasp`** — the Wasp. Fighter mass, fighter manners, cargo measured in regrets. Escorts who buy mules are haulers lying to themselves. The traffic already knows this silhouette as convoy glass; the trade is the pin, not the paint.

### Silhouette

What the cargo does not carry: a **client-facing escort pin** housing on a short mast above the cockpit — small repeating flash aimed *in* at the principal, not out at the lane. **Hardpoint fairings** clean and often re-gelled. **External tankette** under the starboard wing root for long column burns (haulers forget escorts need reaction mass too). **No load pods.** No plate rack. A slim **sensor ear** on the port wing for watching the cone the mule is blind to. Some screens add a second pin aft for line formations. If the pins face *you*, believe motion: you are the problem in the geometry.

### Paint and markings

Base depends on paymaster; common independent is **matte slate with a single bright pin-mast**. Wear: **leading-edge wing burn and gun-lip polish** — combat wear map, honest. Clamp wear almost absent (they rarely berth long). Stencils: `SCREEN`, `NOT CARGO`, contract number of the week, and when Concord-warranted **ES-SCN-90** on the pin housing. Meridian cargo-screens wear gold stripe shamefully fresh. Free wedges stencil a cyan double-tick by hand.

### The Working Light they show

**Escorting — “Borrowed shadow”**: offset slot, **escort pin toward client**, **no load-strobe of their own**, principal shows its own mass language.

Concord warrant-screens: **blue pin**. Meridian cargo-screens: **gold**. Free neighbour-wedges: **cyan double-tick**. Drift shift-screens: **miner-amber pin** on the outside of the claim.

They **fake** patrol sweep if the contract wants intimidation without a customs drawer. They **neglect** spine-wake ceremony when undocking with a nervous principal — bad habit, costs friends at Helios. They never should show amber heartbeat; a Wasp that strobes loaded is either stolen timer or doomed costume.

### Routine

0: contract brief, slot plan, Jex already rewriting the slot plan.  
1: form on undock, pins live, principal load-strobe or empty as truth allows.  
2–8: hold geometry through the lane; watch cones; say nothing on open unless Reach math appears.  
8–9: peel at the berth threshold — escorts do not stack the principal’s final.  
9–10: loiter for reverse leg or take walk-away pay.  
10–12: sleep light; re-gel the pin; refuse to drink with the cargo crew who think friendship is free screen tomorrow.

### What they carry / off-books

Carry: ammunition, spare gel, a sealed contract wafer, almost no cargo.  
Off-books: **threat assessments** sold to the next principal, “lost” Meridian convoy timings, Free favours banked as neighbour duty. Jex will trade a full tankette of mass for a quiet berth. Will not sell the principal’s true mass while still on the pin — that is how screens stop getting hired.

### How they react to a stranger closing

Depends on the cone. Soft close: pin stays on client, short hail *Borrowed Shadow, hold wide.* Hard close: pin still client-facing while the nose turns to you — **guns face you, pins face the client.** They do not flinch first; flinching sells the mule. They scatter only if the principal dumps and runs; then the contract becomes a different animal. Hands are not asked. Hire is asked — sometimes mid-threat, shameless.

### Divergence by faction

| Flavour | What you see |
|---|---|
| **Concord-licensed** | Blue pin metronome; regulated blue-white; warrant stencil; formation geometry textbook; they cite while they cover. |
| **Meridian-contracted** | Gold pin, polished Wasp, company stripe; will not form on a silent freighter (no load-strobe, no screen — post–Span-Hold religion). |
| **Drift-collective** | Miner-amber outside pin; dust on wings; they screen the claim edge, not the brand; whistle network matters more than paint. |
| **Reach-adjacent** | Pin aimed wrong; red under panels; load-strobe costume on a second hull nearby; timer perfect, turn rate knife-sharp. |
| **Free Frontier** | Cyan double-tick hand-wired; volunteer wedge; pin fails and they shout on open instead; *fly easy* while the guns are live. |

## 5. The Prospector
### Crew name: *prospector* · Slang: *one-crew*, *claim-stake*, *solo seam* · Insult: *hobby cutter*, *orphan barge*, *Drift without the brothers*

### Who flies it

**Cal of the One Claim** works rock the Collective has not filed yet — or filed and forgotten in a lean cycle. Habit: **sleeps in the suit** on the rack behind the seat, helmet open, so the wake-up to a shifting face is thirty seconds, not three minutes. The cabin always smells like someone else’s fear sweat. It is usually Cal’s.

### Hull

**`ship_pelican`** — the Pelican. Real mining arms without the Ironback’s industrial sermon. This is the point: prospectors are **not** Drift Collective barges and not Moisture Column shift-rigs. One crew, one seam bet, two mining mounts and a hold that has to be enough. Ironbacks (`ship_ironback`) are what you buy when the claim stops being a prayer. Cal still prays.

### Silhouette

Prospector kit on a Pelican frame: **short survey wand** (not a full Ranger spine) bolted dorsal-forward for the find, and **working arms** for the cut — scoop size honest, not parade. **Claim-stake launcher** on the port cheek: fires a paint-marker spike with a radio tick. **Spare filter drums** external on the starboard hip — independents die of dust before they die of pirates. **No** multi-shift bunk blister, **no** collective tally mast. Return chevron is often **hand-painted metal**, one diode dead on purpose because Cal spent the money on filters. Compared to Rell’s world: smaller dust sphere, lonelier channel, uglier coffee.

### Paint and markings

Base **heat-stained orange-brown over whoever owned it last**, often with Free cyan scuffs. Wear: **bow and belly micrometeor**, arm knuckle polish, filter-drum rub rings where vac-gloves spin the latches every hour. Stencils: a personal claim glyph, `SOLO`, sometimes a scraped-out Collective number Cal “inherited.” Licence when they bother: **PR-CER-55** or a Free chalk circle. Under the cockpit: *not a barge.*

### The Working Light they show

**Surveying** while hunting: cool pin, slow crab, pulse rings soft.  
**Working a rock face — “Blind cone”** when cutting: work floods, red flank cone strobes, arms out, dust confession.  
**Returning heavy — “Home under rock”** when the bet pays: load-strobe plus return chevron toward *their* dock, not yours.

They **fake** empty lights when the hold is half-full of something they do not want weighed. They **neglect** perfect cone discipline when exhausted — which is how prospectors become incidents. They envy Collective whistle and do not always earn it.

### Routine

0–2: transit light to the dark edge of a known field.  
2–4: read, pulse, stake if the tick sings.  
4–9: cut the face alone; talk ore numbers to nobody (habit stolen from column crews).  
9–10: if full, chevron home; if empty, one more rock, always one more rock.  
10–11: unload at a Free pad or a Collective dock that pretends not to notice the solo glyph.  
11–12: service filters, sleep in the suit, dream of an Ironback they cannot afford.

### What they carry / off-books

Carry: filters, stake spikes, emergency foam, a hold of whatever the seam gave, a weapon that is mostly for argument.  
Off-books: **unfiled stakes**, ore under false tally, maps sold to Ness’s cousins, filter stock to other solos. Cal will trade a half-hold of dirty ice for a week of air. Will not sell a live Collective stripe location to Reach — that is how solos get a whistle *against* them.

### How they react to a stranger closing

If mid-cut: **keep working**, cone lit, short snarl on open — *One Claim blind, go wide.* If surveying: flinch earlier than a barge; solos know they look like prey. If the stranger hails kind (Free style), Cal may ask for a **tally witness** or a tow. Scatter is real: prospectors boost ugly and leave stakes behind like dropped teeth.

### Divergence by faction

| Flavour | What you see |
|---|---|
| **Concord-licensed** | Rare; blue survey tag on the wand; cutting only in permitted belts; cone strobes regulation; they file before they cut or they starve of permits. |
| **Meridian-contracted** | Gold stake ticks that report to a desk; clean chevron timers; ore pre-sold; arms better than the hull. |
| **Drift-collective** | Not truly solo — even a “one-crew” under Collective charter shows claim paint lit by floods and answers whistle; orange true, dust true, brothers on the channel. |
| **Reach-adjacent** | Floods without beam (pinning bait); red scar; stake launcher fires scrap, not paint; cone is a costume. |
| **Free Frontier** | Cyan neighbour marks on filter drums; cone optional until someone dies; they wave; they share filters when lean; the insult *hobby cutter* is said with affection until the air runs out. |

## 6. The Lighter / Tug
### Crew name: *lighter*, *tug* · Slang: *apron dog*, *berth-nudge*, *soft shoulder* · Insult: *bumper car*, *station flea*, *mass without a route*

### Who flies it

**Pim Berth-Hand** works the Helios apron and the Tethys trade-hub shoulders where Atlas hulls remember they cannot dance. Habit: **counts thruster ticks out loud** on open mic when lining a heavy — *seven, eight, nine, kiss* — so the big ship’s pilot can match without pride. Controllers hate the chatter. Big hulls live because of it.

### Hull

**`ship_mule`** again, but the silhouette lies differently than Sola’s. Same freighter bones; **push work**, not plate nursing. (Heavy-yard tugs sometimes grow into Atlas frames — `ship_atlas` — when the berth is a cathedral. Pim’s world is the everyday mule-tug that keeps the stack from becoming Span-Hold with extra steps.)

### Silhouette

Trade truth: a **bow push-cradle** — padded vertical ribs and contact pads, scuffed to white — replacing any pretense of a pretty nose. **Side nudge keels** low on both hips, long skids with replaceable polymer shoes. **Aft winch tower** for soft-line assists into clamp range. Dorsal: **apron beacon mast** with steady work-white, not survey cool. No mining arms. No escort pin. Cargo pods often **empty cages** used as ballast trim. The bridge sits high enough to see over another mule’s back. If you mistake this for Mira Bluepack’s bulk-haul, watch the nose: haulers do not wear a cradle that has kissed a hundred strangers.

### Paint and markings

Base **high-vis apron yellow over scarred grey**, station-mandated in Helios, ignored elsewhere. Wear: **cradle face and hip shoes continuous abrasion** — the honest map of the trade. Belly clean compared to miners; nose destroyed compared to couriers. Stencils: `APRON`, `TUG`, berth-group code, licence **TG-HEL-12** on the cradle upright. Hash marks painted for each season without a crushed pylon. Pim has seventeen. The eighteenth is superstition.

### The Working Light they show

Mostly **Approach / Final — “Stacking”** language for the *client*: they help another hull keep green final honest. Themselves: **soft white apron beacon**, thruster ticks, rarely a load-strobe (ballast is not cargo in the Guild’s heart, though Concord scanners argue).

They show **Loading/Unloading** adjacency when transferring a pod from Atlas to shore lighter. They **fake** heavy burn only as a joke that controllers do not find funny. They **neglect** spine-wake beauty — undock is a shove, not a ceremony. Escort pins are not their dialect; if they “screen,” it is with mass and nerve, not borrowed shadow.

### Routine

0: apron brief, which heavies are late, which clamps are sulking.  
1–4: meet inbound mass at the stack edge, cradle kiss, tick-count, walk them to the pylon.  
4–5: reverse the empty that thinks it can self-berth.  
5–8: same work outbound — undock corridors are where pride causes filings.  
8–9: winch a dead thruster mule that should have hired Sola yesterday.  
9–10: refuel, replace a hip shoe, argue with Meridian about tug surcharge.  
10–12: sleep in the cradle’s shadow on the hull, counting ticks in the dream until *kiss*.

### What they carry / off-books

Carry: spare polymer shoes, soft-lines, ballast drums, a boarding hook for stuck clamps, almost no market cargo.  
Off-books: **priority undock slots** whispered for Free friends, “lost” clamp time sold back to Meridian captains, spare shoes to Drift mules that ground their own berths. Pim will not nudge a dark-running loaded hull into Helios without waking a customs cousin — Span-Hold still sits in the Guild’s throat.

### How they react to a stranger closing

On the apron: they **keep working** and expect you to yield like the Code says mass and final deserve. Hail is practical: *Berth-Hand on the kiss, hold.* If you cut final, they flinch the heavy first and curse you second. They will **ask for a hand** — a second tug, a line, a thruster mirror — without shame. Scatter is rare; you cannot scatter a cradle contract. Distress they treat as neighbour duty: Free taught them, Helios pays them.
