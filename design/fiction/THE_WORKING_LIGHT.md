# THE WORKING LIGHT
## Visual Status Code of Working Spacers
### 3rd Revised Edition — Lane Guild of Tethys

*What you show is what you claim. What you claim is what they shoot at.*

---

## 1. Origin and Authority

Before the Code, a ship told you what it was doing the old way: by getting in your way.

Haulers ran dark or ran loud. Miners cut without marking the work cone. Patrols sat silent until they lit you. Couriers flew like ghosts until they hit something. In the third lean cycle after the Helios–Tethys corridor opened to bulk ore, a Meridian insured column called **SPAN-HOLD** (not the courier who wears that callsign now — the original liner) was cut in half by a Drift cutter that never saw the load-strobe because the hauler’s lamp bank had been “temporarily” sold for reactor seals. Forty-one souls. Three holds of water ice and refined metals. One customs gate that spent six shifts arguing over whose ledger owned the wreck.

That was the **Span-Hold Incident**. Meridian paid the claims. Concord filed Ref 44-C annexes until the drawers groaned. The Drift Miners Collective, who had already been painting claim markers on rock faces for generations, told both of them to stop inventing language and start using the belt’s.

The **Working Light** is what came out of that mess: a visual status grammar for anyone who has to live near someone else’s burn. It is not Navy doctrine and it is not Syndicate insurance copy. It is the Lane Guild’s codification of practices already half-lived on Ceres claim routes, Helios approach stacks, and the long cold runs past Pallas Drift.

**Who enforces it**

- **Concord** cites it at checkpoints under Ref 44-C “corridor legibility.” Fines for dark-running under load in controlled space. They do not chase every wink-out past security 0.4.
- **Meridian** prices it. An insured hull that fails to show load-strobe loses coverage on collision claims. Their escorts will not form a cargo-screen around a silent freighter.
- **Drift** enforces it with claim law: unmarked cutters get no whistle from the other rigs when Reach comes hunting. The belt does not care about your paperwork; it cares whether you made yourself readable.
- **Free Frontier** posts the Code on waystation bulkheads and shrugs. If you ignore it and die, the neighbors write down your name once and move on.
- **Reach, Quiet, and anyone with a weigh-slip open** treat the Code as a menu of costumes.

**What it superseded**

The old practice was **“manifest on request”** — radio only, lights optional, trust the registry. Stubborn hulls still run it: old independent skippers out of Io Reach who call lamp-codes “Concord jewelry,” Choir processions that refuse “profane commerce signals,” and Vael traffic that answers with clause-numbers instead of colours. On the frontier, some Free rigs still show only a single white running lamp and a wave on open channel. They are not wrong about freedom. They are wrong about surviving a dense approach stack.

## 2. The Signals

Every entry below is what an observer **sees**, what it **means**, who is **required** to show it, who **cheats**, and what it **costs** when you get it wrong.

### Commissioning / Undocking — *“Waking the spine”*

**See:** Dock clamps still kissing the berth. Running lamps cold, then a sequential spine-wake: bow lamp, midships, stern, one beat each. Thrusters feather in short greys, not a full plume. Transponder rises from dock-idle to live. On big stations (Helios, Tethys Trade Hub) a yellow berth-arc on the pylon matches the ship’s wake sequence.

**Means:** Commission complete; hull is under its own power and about to depart. Not yet in transit. Do not cross the undock corridor.

**Required of:** Any vessel leaving a registered berth in Concord- or Meridian-controlled space. Drift outposts expect it if they have a pylon; a Belt Outpost with one tired lamp will settle for a single double-flash bow.

**Cheat:** Smugglers undock dark and “wake the spine” only after the first turn out of scan cone. Reach hulls sometimes mimic the sequence while already under way — a fake commission to look like local traffic.

**Cost of wrong:** Clip a pylon or another undocking mule and Meridian invoices both of you. Concord files deviation before you clear the stack. On a claim dock, the other rigs will remember you as the one who almost took the shaft-crew with you.

### Transit Under Load — *“Heavy burn” / load-strobe*

**See:** Steady forward running lamps. **Port and starboard amber load-strobes** at a slow, even cadence (the “loaded heartbeat”). Plume is longer, cooler-looking on thermal, mass drag obvious in the turn. Cargo hatches sealed; no magnet-arm silhouette. On Meridian columns, a thin gold convoy pin-light on the dorsal mast.

**Means:** Hold is full or near-full. High mass. Long stop. Do not cut in front. This is the signal that makes Mira Bluepack readable three sectors out as bulk-haul.

**Required of:** Haulers and miners returning heavy in insured or claim-adjacent space. Couriers with sealed bulk may show a faster amber if the contract is priority-mail (Kess of the Span’s kin).

**Cheat:** Empty hulls strobe amber to look less like scouts. Raiders strobe amber to look like fat targets… or like boring targets, depending on the math. Quiet runners sometimes strobe with one lamp dead so the cadence looks “honestly worn.”

**Cost of wrong:** Fail to strobe under real load in a customs net and Warden Keel’s kind cites you for false manifest risk. Strobe under empty in Reach country and the pack assumes you are bait or blind.

### Transit Empty — *“Light hull” / clean burn*

**See:** White or cool-blue running lamps only. **No amber load-strobe.** Snappier yaw, shorter plume, higher pitch burn. Some independents fly a faint ventral “empty bar” — three dim whites in a row that mean *hold open for hire*.

**Means:** Low mass, quick to dodge, not worth a full interdiction if you are counting cargo. Or: looking for a contract.

**Required of:** No one, strictly. Guild recommendation after Span-Hold: *show empty if empty, so the loaded ones stay legible.*

**Cheat:** Loaded narcotics and stolen goods love clean burn. Quiet prefers it. A Reach scout will run empty-lights while weighing your tonnage on passive.

**Cost of wrong:** Concord scan that finds mass without strobe is “non-compliant manifest.” Meridian may void your next premium. Drift will assume you are claim-jumping light and watch you harder.

### Approach / Final — *“Stacking”*

**See:** Load-strobe (if any) drops to half-cadence. **Bow approach lamp** steady bright white or soft green. Lateral thrusters tick in rhythmic corrections. Speed bleeds in steps, not a panic brake. In Helios stacks, a green “final” on the nose for the last berth alignment.

**Means:** Committed to a station, claim dock, or field approach. Blind cone forward. Give way or take the fine.

**Required of:** Anyone entering a controlled approach corridor. Patrols use a blue-white Concord variant; Free waystations accept any steady bow lamp if you call ahead.

**Cheat:** Raiders “stack” toward a hauler as if sharing final, then cut across. Smugglers approach with green final while actually lining a black-market contact.

**Cost of wrong:** Cut final on Tethys and you eat a toll plus a filing. Cut final on a Drift unload and you eat a cutter beam argument.

### Working a Rock Face (Cutting) — *“Blind cone”*

**See:** Hull holds station relative to the rock. **Work floods** (harsh white or miner-amber) on the face. Mining beam or cutter glow. Magnet arms or vac-scoops deployed. Debris sheet and micro-glitter. Thermal bloom on the bow. Often a **red work-cone strobe** on the flanks: *do not enter this arc.* Drift rigs add a claim-paint stripe lit by the floods so the filed mark reads at a glance.

**Means:** Extracting. Sensors half-blind from dust and bloom. Beam is hot; crew is watching the seam, not the lane. Rell of the Moisture Column’s people live in this light for whole shifts.

**Required of:** Collective and insured claim work. Independent cutters in Free space “should.” Choir will not mark a rock they call consecrated — avoid those faces.

**Cheat:** Reach “miners” light a face with floods and no beam to pin a hauler into a bad turn. Quiet never floods; they cut dark and accept the risk.

**Cost of wrong:** Enter a marked cone and you are the incident. Fail to mark and the next rig through your dust cloud will not mourn you. Loud greedy cut raises attention — pirates interdict bright work.

### Holding Station on a Patrol Beat — *“On the pin”*

**See:** Near-zero relative drift at a charted pin. **Rotating or sweeping scan lamp** (Concord blue-white; Free soft cyan; Meridian gold “toll eye”). Formation partners hold wedge or line offsets. No cargo silhouette. Engine heat low, sensor heat high.

**Means:** Watch, not transit. You are in a measured box. Customs-scan boats (Keel-Watch types) will light a secondary narrow beam when they paint you.

**Required of:** Concord patrols in controlled corridors. Meridian escorts at column holds. Free neighbor-watch at waystations. Drift “shift screens” at claim edges.

**Cheat:** Reach pickets hold like patrol with stolen blue gels over lamps. The gel burns uneven; the sweep stutters.

**Cost of wrong:** Blow through a Concord hold and Ref 44-C writes itself. Linger in a Free hold without hailing and the neighbors assume Reach.

### Loading — *“Mouth open”*

**See:** Relative dock or tender contact. **Hatch aperture light** (warm interior spill). Crane, umbilical, or magnet-transfer arm moving. Load-strobe *off* or irregular. Brief vent puffs. On ore pads, a brown-orange dust halo.

**Means:** Hold changing. Ship is soft — hard burn risks the transfer crew and the seal.

**Required of:** Registered cargo ops at hubs and refineries. Claim-side load to a mule from a barge.

**Cheat:** “Loading” forever is a Quiet trick: hatch glow, no mass change, waiting for a better lane. Reach uses fake umbilicals as boarding covers.

**Cost of wrong:** Hard-burn away mid-load and you buy the cargo *and* the lawsuit. Fake-load near a Concord gate and the scan team gets interested in your seal codes.

### Unloading — *“Spilling the count”*

**See:** Same as load, but often with **tally lamps** — Drift orange blink synced to ore-tally ticks; Meridian gold invoice pulse on the mast. Dust and scrap glitter on miner returns. Empty bar may come up mid-op as mass falls.

**Means:** Delivering. The moisture column, the refinery, the trade desk is eating your hold. Rell’s gimmick made flesh.

**Required of:** Anyone offloading at a desk that issues a stamp. Free waystations care less about lamps than about whether you share filters when lean.

**Cheat:** Unload-signal while jettisoning evidence. Reach “unloads” into a second hull under dust cover.

**Cost of wrong:** Mismatched tally lamps vs. actual mass is how Meridian starts an audit and Drift starts a claim fight.

### Returning Heavy — *“Home under rock”*

**See:** Load-strobe *and* a **return chevron** — two amber lamps stacked or a chevron stencil lit on the bow. Heading toward home station or claim dock, not the field. Plume heavy. Sometimes a tethered chunk glowing dull on a Massline: the Code treats the chunk as part of the load.

**Means:** Miner cycle RETURN: field to home, hold full. Do not offer “empty hire.” Do not assume they will dodge well.

**Required of:** Drift Collective shift rules. Guild recommendation for any bulk return through contested lanes (Charon, Io, Pallas).

**Cheat:** Raiders return-chevron toward a station to look like local barges, then peel to the ambush lane.

**Cost of wrong:** No chevron, and a tired shift-screen may treat you as outbound claim-jumper. Fake chevron into a Concord stack and you get the customs net for free.

### Fleeing / Distress — *“Breaking the pattern”*

**See:** **Distress:** alternating red-white global flash, often with a stutter if the generator is wounded. Cargo dump petals if they are running light to live. Smoke, asymmetric plume, tumbling debris. **Flee without formal distress:** hard plume, no polite cadence, load-strobe dies mid-beat, sometimes a single screaming forward lamp.

**Means:** Not working. Not stacking. Get clear or render aid. Rescue craft answer red-white; Reach answers the dump petals.

**Required of:** Distress is optional until it is not — Guild and Concord both say show it if you want a legal rescue claim. Free Frontier treats the flash as a neighbor duty.

**Cheat:** Fake distress to bait Samaritans (old Reach classic). Quiet almost never flashes; they just go dark and leave.

**Cost of wrong:** False distress in Concord space is a filing that follows your registry. No distress when you needed it and the rescue craft (and the rep that comes with being saved) never finds you.

### Surveying — *“Reading the dark”*

**See:** Slow crabbing flight. **Scanner pulse rings** visible as brief shell-flashes. Side lamps dim; a single cool survey pin on a boom or mast. No work floods. Couriers on sealed runs (Venn of the Sealed Manifest’s cousins) may survey cold: pulse only, lamps near-dark.

**Means:** Mapping, seam-finding, wreck sniffing, or “market research.” Not extracting yet. Lower threat than a cutter, higher interest than a hauler.

**Required of:** Licensed survey in Concord charts. Everyone else does it because blind flight through dense rock is expensive.

**Cheat:** Patrols “survey” your hold with the same pulse language. Reach survey pin while actually ranging a spear.

**Cost of wrong:** Pulse too loud near a Quiet door and you get counted. Pulse on a Choir shrine-lane and you become dissonance.

### Salvaging a Wreck — *“Picking the bones”*

**See:** Relative hold on a cold hull. **Amber salvage umbrellas** (work floods hooded downward). Cutter sparks, plate glow, tether lines bright. Scrap cloud. No claim stripe unless Drift already filed the wreck as a worksite.

**Means:** Recovery, not murder-in-progress — *if* the umbrellas are on and weapons are cold. Looting a faction wreck under lights still costs you reputation if witnessed; the Code only makes the *work* legible, not moral.

**Required of:** Licensed scrap at yards. Guild courtesy everywhere else so passing traffic does not read you as a kill still in progress.

**Cheat:** Reach strips under red combat lamps and calls it salvage after. Quiet takes the black box and leaves the umbrellas dark.

**Cost of wrong:** Concord witnesses loot without umbrellas and files piracy-adjacent. Drift witnesses and prices your hull on the same scales as the scrap.

### Effecting Repairs — *“Hull open”*

**See:** Relative drift or soft dock to a tender. **Static red “men at work” corners** on the open bay. Welding stars, suit lamps, plate edges glowing. One engine cold. Sometimes a temporary white “do not push” bar across the drive.

**Means:** Soft target by necessity. Do not bounce wake off them. Free Frontier treats this almost sacred; Reach treats it as opportunity unless the neighbors are watching.

**Required of:** Any yard ops. Self-repair in the lane is Guild-strong advice.

**Cheat:** “Repairs” with hot weapons under a tarp. A classic Sker trick: weld-stars on a timer, guns live.

**Cost of wrong:** Hit a marked repair and every Free and Drift radio for two sectors learns your name. Fake repair near a Concord patrol and you eat a scan you will not enjoy.

### Escorting a Hull That Is Not Yours — *“Borrowed shadow”*

**See:** Escort holds **offset slot** (wedge or line) relative to the principal. **Escort pin**: small repeating flash toward the client, not toward the lane. Principal shows its own load or empty code; escort shows **no load-strobe of its own** unless it carries cargo (rare). Concord warrant-screens use blue pin; Meridian cargo-screens use gold; Free neighbor-wedges use cyan double-tick; Drift shift-screens use miner-amber pin on the outside of the claim.

**Means:** That fat mule is not alone. The pin says *I am watching the cone around them.*

**Required of:** Hired escorts, column screens, claim shift-guards. Independent “I’ll fly with you” friends on the Frontier still try to show a pin if they own a second lamp.

**Cheat:** Raider rides escort pin on a stranger to close distance. Smuggler pins a decoy hauler while the real sealed run goes dark wide.

**Cost of wrong:** Lose the pin and the principal looks unescorted — Reach math improves. Fake the pin and when the real screen arrives, you are the problem in the formation.

## 3. Legibility Rules of Thumb

As the crews say them:

- **If it’s cutting, it’s blind.**
- **Amber heartbeat means mass. Respect mass.**
- **Green final is a promise. Break it and the stack breaks you.**
- **Empty lights lie more than loaded ones.**
- **Red-white is not a negotiation.**
- **A patrol that doesn’t sweep is not a patrol.**
- **Dust is a confession. Bright dust is a loud confession.**
- **One dead lamp is honest wear. Two dead lamps is a story.**
- **If the chevron points home, don’t offer them a side job.**
- **Pins face the client. Guns face you.**
- **Quiet doesn’t forget a loud code.**
- **Weigh-slips don’t care what colour you painted.**
- **Ref 44-C is a drawer. The Code is a habit.**
- **Wave on the Frontier. File in Helios. Count in Sker.**

## 4. Divergence by Faction and by Class

Same signal. Different soul. **This is how you read who is flying, not only what they claim to do.**

### Meridian corporate hauler (mule-class bulk, insured column)

Paint: clean syndicate gold-and-slate, company stripe fresh enough to shame you. Load-strobe is **factory-timed gold-amber**, never missing a beat — the heartbeat of someone whose premium depends on it. Approach green is crisp. Escort pins are gold, escorts themselves polished Wasps in cargo-screen. Plume is efficient, not romantic. They will toll you politely while perfectly lit. Coldburn Rey’s sort of competition hates them for the stamp, not the lamps.

### Independent / Free Frontier working hull

Paint: faded cyan, patch primer, waystation stencil half-scraped. Load-strobe **works when the bulb works** — cadence slightly slow, one side dimmer. They invent kindness: a soft cyan “neighbor wave” double-flash when they pass friendly. Escort is a volunteer wedge, pins hand-wired. Distress is sincere and a little late. They still say *fly easy* on the channel while their empty bar advertises hire.

### Drift / frontier claim rig (Pelican barges, Mule ore-runs, Moisture Column kin)

Paint: heat-stained orange-brown, claim numbers hand-cut, dust welded into every seam. Work floods are **ugly and correct**. Red cone strobes are bright enough to anger poets. Return chevrons are painted metal, not fancy diodes. Load-strobe is miner-amber, slightly irregular with generator load when the beam was just running. They will not chase your aesthetics. They will defend a marked cone like it is the shaft that feeds the station — because it is.

### Concord military / customs patrol (Wasp and heavier)

Paint: regulated blue-white, seals readable, no poetry. Hold-station sweep is a **mechanical metronome**. Scan secondary beam is narrow and rude. They cite Ref 44-C whether your lamps match or not; the Code is evidence in their drawer, not their religion. Commission spine-wake is perfect. They do not show load-strobe — a patrol that strobes amber is either stolen or doomed.

### Crimson Reach raider mimicking a hauler

Paint: once-mule greys with **wrong wear** — scorch on the bow where a freighter scorches on the belly; red Reach scar-marks under removable panels. Load-strobe too fast (predator impatience) or too perfect (stolen Meridian timer still on factory default while the hull is filth). Plume hot for the “mass” they claim. Escort pins face *you*, not a client. Weigh-slip chatter on an open band ruins the costume. When the math turns, the amber dies mid-beat and the spear lights.

### The Quiet sealed runner

Almost no Code. When forced near Tethys or Pallas traffic, they show **minimum legal**: one running lamp, occasional clean-burn whites, never a full load-strobe even when heavy. Sealed-cargo couriers may run a single violet-gel pin so dim you invent it. They prefer you saw nothing. A Quiet hull that suddenly speaks full Working Light is already someone else’s problem.

### Ascendant Choir transit

They reject commerce colours. Procession lamps in ritual magenta-pink intervals. Hold becomes “chorus ring,” not patrol pin. If they mark anything, it is Pattern, not Guild. Do not expect amber. Do not enter their ring to teach them the Code.

### Vael passage

Clause-lights: cool teal lattices that do not map cleanly onto amber/green/red. They will be legible to each other. To you, they read as “not human work.” Do not assume empty. Do not assume distress. The accord is older than your lamp gel.

### Class notes in one breath

- **Hauler:** mass language — strobe, plume, turn rate.
- **Courier:** speed language — clean burn, priority amber if sealed-mail, little dust.
- **Miner:** cone and floods — if you only learn one dialect, learn this.
- **Patrol:** sweep and hold.
- **Escort:** pin and slot.
- **Smuggler:** absence and almosts.
- **Raider:** costume until the weigh.
- **Rescue:** red-white authority; they ignore your pride.

## 5. Forgery, Spoofing, and Counter-Practice

**How a raider fakes a hauler’s loaded strobe**

Steal or buy a Meridian timer pack. Wire it to two amber lamps on the flanks. Paint a company stripe in a hurry. Fly the lane at hauler speed with a slightly heavy plume (dump reaction mass if you must). Optional: drag a scrap sled on a dim tether to fake return mass.

**Tells**

1. **Cadence vs. motion:** real loaded mules *yaw late*. A light raider with perfect amber still corners like a knife.
2. **Thermal lie:** empty holds stay cooler amidships; faked load often shows hot drives and cold cargo belly.
3. **Wear map:** freighter micro-impacts live on the nose and belly; fighters wear on the wings and gun ports.
4. **Escort geometry:** real screens face outward from the client. Fake escorts stare at prey.
5. **Dust:** real ore returns carry a brown halo; clean fake-load in a belt is a joke.
6. **One perfect lamp bank on a filthy hull:** factory heart, corpse body.
7. **Radio:** Reach counts and weighs; Meridian invoices; Drift complains about shifts. The wrong voice under the right lights is fatal to the con.
8. **Dead secondary systems:** no hatch heat history, no berth-wear on clamp points, fresh gel on old housings.

**Counter-practice**

- Concord: paint + passive mass + registry. Ref 44-C does not need to believe you; it needs a surcharge.
- Meridian: insurance ping — account standing vs. observed mass.
- Drift: another rig’s eyes. The belt is a rumor engine with floodlights.
- Free: ask on open channel. Liars hate short questions.
- Working rule: **believe the motion first, the lamps second, the paint never.**

## 6. What It Looks Like at 3am

Helios approach, late shift. The stack is three mules and a courier. Someone’s port strobe has been half-dead for a month; the heartbeat stutters every seventh flash and nobody has filed the repair because the seal kit costs more than the fine. A Concord sweep ticks across the corridor like a bored metronome. Warden Keel’s cousin on the customs boat drinks something that was coffee at the start of the cycle.

Out on Ceres, a Pelican holds the face with floods the colour of old bone. The red cone strobes have gel burns. Dust makes a soft sphere that eats stars. On the open channel, somebody mutters ore numbers to nobody — moisture column habit. The return chevron on a homebound mule is missing one diode; they fly it anyway. The claim mark on the rock is scuffed where a beam kissed paint.

Pallas way out, a Quiet runner shows one white and a lie. A Reach hull practices amber two lanes over, timer perfect, turn rate wrong. Free Frontier kids on a waystation roof — if a station can be said to have a roof — watch the pins and take bets on which light is honest.

Nobody recites the Guild edition. They say: *heavy burn, give him room.* They say: *cone’s lit, go wide.* They say: *that empty’s too quick.* They say: *red-white, wake the rescue.*

The Code is not a hymn. It is the set of habits that keep Span-Hold from happening every week. Lamps fail. Gels bleach. Timers drift. People still die in the dark between signals.

But if you are working, you show the work.

If you are heavy, you admit the mass.

If you are blind on a face, you warn the lane.

And if you paint a hauler’s heartbeat on a raider’s ribs, someone out here has been counting longer than you have been lying — and the weigh-slip is already open.
