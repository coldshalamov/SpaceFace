# LANE FURNITURE
## The Marks, Buoys and Platforms of the Open Corridor
### Companion to the Working Light — Lane Guild of Tethys

*Nobody photographs a tally post. Everybody adjusts their burn around one.*

The Code tells you what a hull claims. The Trades tell you who is sweating under the lamp. This volume is the clutter: the small iron and plastic that turns empty rock into a *lane*. Helios stacks, Ceres claim routes, Tethys junction shoulders — the furniture is denser than the charts admit and uglier than the tourist feeds allow.

If you treat any of these as empty scenery, you will learn motion the expensive way.

---

## 1. Claim Mark
### Formal: *claim mark* · Slang: *stake*, *face tick*, *paint tooth* · Insult: *hobby spike*, *orphan stripe*, *paper rock*

### Physical form (for the modeller)

A **1.8 m spike body** driven into rock or welded to a seam face: hexagonal shaft, 0.28 m across flats, steel-grey core with a **0.9 m radio tick capsule** at the tip — a blunt cylinder, slightly fatter than the shaft, one flat antenna ring and a **paint-marker nozzle** that once fired fluorescent claim-dust. Overall height from rock to tip: **2.4–2.7 m** when seated true; many lean 5–12° after a beam kiss or a bad drive.

**Primary mass:** the shaft and the **base flange** — a 0.6 m disc with four rock bolts, two often missing or sheared. **Bolted on:** a **claim plate** (0.4 × 0.25 m) with raised numerals, hung on two bolts on the lee side of the face; a **single miner-amber gel lamp** in a caged housing mid-shaft; a **spare gel puck** wired with tape to the flange (or long gone). **Hangs off:** a short **tether loop** of scarred cable for suit handholds; sometimes a faded **flag streamer** of heat-cloth, half-burned. **Bent or missing:** the antenna ring is often **crushed flat on one side** where a Pelican’s scoop brushed it; one rock bolt hole shows **torn metal** from a jump-claim fight; the paint nozzle is frequently **capped with slag** after the first cut bloom.

Independent stake-launcher spikes (Cal of the One Claim’s kin) are thinner — **1.4 m**, no flange plate, just a barbed root and a tick that dies in a season.

### Light

**Steady miner-amber claim pin**, low intensity, with a **single soft double-tick every eight seconds** — the belt’s way of saying *filed, still warm*. When floods from a working face hit the plate, the stripe reads as a stripe; the lamp is only the night signature. **Low cell:** the double-tick stretches to twelve, then twenty; amber goes **brown-orange** and the radio tick weakens before the lamp dies. A dead claim mark is still law if the plate is readable; a bright mark on an empty face is either a new stake or a costume.

### Surface

Base paint: **heat-stained orange-brown over raw bolt steel**, Collective orange where the shift cares. **Scorch** on the face-side of the shaft from work floods and cutter bloom. **Scoured** on the lane-facing edge by dust sheet from other people’s cones. **Faded** plate numerals where gloves and suits rub the read. Stencils: hand-cut **claim number** (example: **CL-CER-7-441**), sometimes `FILED` and a shift boss hash. Prospectors stencil a personal glyph and scrape out last year’s Collective digits.

### Age and neglect

**Breaks first:** antenna ring, gel cage latch, rock bolts, then the tick capsule seal. **New:** orange true, plate sharp, lamp cage unpainted metal still bright. **Twenty years:** flange half-buried in weld-dust slag, plate numerals filled with grit, one bolt replaced with a **wrong-diameter scrap pin**, lamp housing cracked and **rewired with freighter lamp cord**, shaft painted over thrice so the orange is archaeology.

### Who services it

**Drift shift bosses and the rig that filed it.** Service: whenever a cutter works that face — wipe plate, swap gel if the tick is weak, re-torque bolts. **Skip:** antenna cosmetics, streamers, anything below the plate if the radio still pings. Free solos service theirs with a kick and a prayer. Concord almost never; Meridian only if the ore is pre-sold to their desk.

### What it means to a pilot

**Someone’s bread is in that rock.** Do not cut the face. Do not park in the dust cone. If the plate is lit and a red work-cone is up nearby, go wide or become the incident. An unmarked rock with a fresh spike is a prospector bet — still not free ore. A mark with **no plate and a screaming bright lamp** is Reach bait or a joke; believe motion.

### Faction variants

| Hands | Hardware difference |
|---|---|
| **Drift Collective** | Heavy flange, hand-cut plate, miner-amber ugly and correct; spare gel taped on; dust welded into every seam. |
| **Prospector / Free** | Thin spike, barbed root, cyan chalk on the shaft, tick that fails; neighbour-wave gel sometimes stolen from a waystation. |
| **Meridian-contracted claim** | Gold barcode sticker over the plate edge; factory amber timer; plate machine-cut; bolt heads painted gold once, then forgotten. |
| **Concord-permitted belt** | Blue licence wafer clipped under the plate (`PR-…` or claim annex); lamp cooler white-amber; they fine dark marks harder than weak ones. |
| **Reach costume** | Bright lamp, no dust on the flange, plate numbers that do not match any shift log; red scar paint under a removable strip on the shaft. |

## 2. Lane Pin
### Formal: *corridor marker* / *lane pin* · Slang: *side stick*, *pass post*, *Ref stick* · Insult: *jewelry*, *Concord toothpick*, *drawer buoy*

### Physical form (for the modeller)

A **9 m vertical spine** of tubular alloy, 0.22 m diameter, planted in a **1.2 m hexagonal base drum** (depth 0.8 m) ballasted with slagcrete. At **4 m** and **7.5 m**: two **vane fins** — thin plates 1.6 m long, 0.4 m deep — set at **120°** with a third vane that is often **not a fin at all**: a **flat unpainted repair plate** bolted after a strike, edges raw. **Primary mass:** base drum and lower third of the spine (batteries and ballast). **Bolted on:** a **pass-side chevron housing** (0.5 m) that points *which side is legal*, a **speed band** of three small lamps in a vertical strip, a **Ref 44-C annex plate** (0.3 × 0.2 m) on the drum. **Hangs off:** cable loops for service suits; sometimes a **dead bird** of tangled foil from someone’s bad dump. **Bent or missing:** upper vane **twisted 30°** from a mule wake; speed-band middle lamp **empty socket**; annex plate **half-sheared** where a tug cradle kissed it.

### Light

**Concord blue-white** for the spine-tip **hold pin** — a slow **mechanical sweep** when the pin is also a patrol reference, or a **steady bright tip** when it is only corridor furniture. **Pass chevron:** soft green on the legal side, dim red ghost on the forbidden (many Free crews only notice the green). **Speed band:** cool white pulses in **group-of-three / pause** for standard corridor limit; **group-of-two** for reduced; **long single** for “crawl / stack adjacent.” Grammar borrowed from Working Light legibility, not Navy parade. **Low cell:** sweep stutters (the tell of stolen gels on raider pickets too); chevron green dies before tip white; speed band drops to one lamp that **lies** about the limit — trust the stencil on the drum if the band is sick.

### Surface

Base: **regulated blue-white over slate**, scoured to bare metal on the **lane-facing lower 3 m** by thruster wash. **Scorch fans** on the leeward vane from someone’s hard plume. Stencils: **corridor ID** (example: **LN-HEL-TETH-04**), `PASS PORT` / `PASS STBD` chevron paint, speed numeral, Concord seal, and a service date in approved font when someone cared. Meridian-adjacent pins on toll approaches add a thin **gold stripe** around the drum lip.

### Age and neglect

**Breaks first:** speed-band sockets, vane hinges, annex plate fasteners, then ballast seals (drum lists). **New:** gel crisp, vanes matched, sweep metronome-true. **Twenty years:** one vane a **flat scrap plate**, tip lamp rewired to a freighter running-lamp module, drum stencils layered until `PASS` reads as a ghost, base **pitted with micro-impacts** and one bolted-on **bumper ring** of scrap tire-polymer from apron culture.

### Who services it

**Concord corridor crews** out of Helios and the Tethys Customs Gate, on a schedule that assumes peace. Cycle: quarterly gel and bolt pass; annual ballast check. **Skip:** vane symmetry, tourist-facing paint, any pin past security 0.4 unless a Span-Hold-class filing forces a drawer open. Free waystations “service” pins with a cyan splash and a shouted channel note. Meridian pays for pins that protect insured columns and ignores the rest.

### What it means to a pilot

**Which side, how fast, who owns the drawer.** Pass on the green chevron side. Match the speed band or eat a filing in Helios / a surcharge near Tethys. A pin with a **sweeping** tip means a patrol beat is married to this post — you are in a measured box. A pin with **two dead lamps** is a story; give it room and check open channel. Reach loves a perfect pin next to a bad turn rate.

### Faction variants

| Hands | Hardware difference |
|---|---|
| **Concord** | Blue-white exact; annex plate readable at customs range; sweep metronome; machine stencils. |
| **Meridian toll approach** | Gold drum lip; invoice QR under the speed band; chevron timer factory-gold-amber hybrid. |
| **Drift claim-edge “pin”** | Shorter (6 m), miner-amber tip, no Ref plate; pass side painted in grease; means *claim traffic this side*. |
| **Free Frontier** | Faded cyan over primer; hand-wired tip; speed band one lamp honest; neighbour double-flash jury-rigged into the chevron housing. |
| **Reach spoof** | Stolen Concord gel burned uneven; sweep stutters; vanes wrong wear (gun-scorch not wash-scour). |

## 3. Tally Post
### Formal: *weigh-point* / *tally post* · Slang: *count stick*, *gold tooth*, *hold bite* · Insult: *invoice spike*, *thief’s confessional*, *Span-Hold’s cousin*

### Physical form (for the modeller)

A **6 m tower** on a **3 m square platform deck** (deck thickness 0.25 m) with open grating and two **stub bollards**. Primary vertical is a **1.1 m diameter hexagonal drum** rising **4 m** from deck — the **scale house** — topped by a **1.5 m mast** with **tally lamps** and a **gold invoice pulse** housing. Overall height ~**6.2 m**. **Primary mass:** scale house (sensor guts, battery, sealed ledger brick). **Bolted on:** a **boom arm** 3.2 m long with a **mass-sensor yoke** (two pads like blunt tongs) that swings over a hold or mule back; a **thermal hood** over the yoke; cable tray down the boom with **missing cover plates** on the last third. **Hangs off:** a **chain of inspection tags** on a ring; a **soft-line coil** for holding a nervous hauler. **Bent or missing:** yoke pad **one side worn concave**, the other replaced with a **flat unpainted plate** after a strike; boom **droops 8°** on tired hydraulics; deck corner **crumpled** where a cradle mis-kissed.

### Light

**Meridian gold tally lamps** — short **invoice pulses** synced to weigh ticks when live (think unloading’s “spilling the count,” fixed in place). Idle: a **slow gold heartbeat** every four seconds on the mast so the post remains legible as *weigh furniture, not salvage*. When counting: **faster gold blink** paired with a secondary **cool white confirm** on the yoke. **Low cell:** gold goes **brassy and late**; confirm white dies first; a sick post still bites holds but the blink **desyncs** from the desk — pilots learn to watch the boom motion, not the poetry.

### Surface

Base: **syndicate slate with gold-and-slate company stripe**, too clean on new posts, shamefully clean on busy ones where money flows. **Scorch** under the yoke from thruster wash of mules holding station. **Scoured** deck grating from boots and magnet shoes. **Faded** gold stripe on the boom elbow. Stencils: `WEIGH`, `HOLD OPEN FOR COUNT`, desk code (example: **WT-TETH-MTS-19**), barcode that never quite survives dust, and sometimes a hand-scratched **crew tally hash** of how many lies the post caught this season.

### Age and neglect

**Breaks first:** yoke pad faces, boom hydraulic dampers, mast gel, ledger-brick cooling fans. **New:** stripe fresh, yoke matched pads, pulse factory-timed. **Twenty years:** one pad a **scrap plate**, boom with **three colours of repair paint**, stripe worn to primer on the lane side, ledger brick **externally clamped** with cargo straps after an internal mount failed, gold lamps mixed with **one miner-amber refugee bulb** from a Drift trade.

### Who services it

**Meridian desk techs** from Tethys Trade Hub and insured corridor contractors. Cadence: after every major column, or weekly, whichever bills better. **Skip:** deck cosmetics, tag chains, the soft-line (haulers bring their own pride). Drift will not maintain a Meridian post; they maintain **ore-tally lamps on rigs** instead. Concord cites false tallies; they do not polish gold.

### What it means to a pilot

**Your hold is about to become a number.** Slow early. Show honest load-strobe or clean burn that matches mass — mismatch is how audits start. Do not cut the boom arc. Empty hulls that “accidentally” pass a tally post still get remembered. Free captains use the post as a rumour engine: who paid, who argued, who dumped petals before the bite.

### Faction variants

| Hands | Hardware difference |
|---|---|
| **Meridian** | Gold pulse true; barcode; company stripe; yoke logo-embossed once. |
| **Concord customs weigh** | Blue-white mast, no gold stripe; Ref plate; scan secondary beam mount beside the yoke. |
| **Drift refinery mouth** | Shorter post, miner-orange drum, tally blink synced to ore ticks not invoices; boom built from claim scrap. |
| **Free waystation “count”** | Platform only, hand scale, cyan splash, lamps borrowed from a neighbour; honesty is social. |
| **Quiet-adjacent avoidance** | No post — absence is the furniture; if you see a dark Meridian post with fresh scorch, someone counted what they should not. |

## 4. Whistle
### Formal: *distress relay* (unlicensed) · Slang: *whistle*, *scream can*, *neighbour bell* · Insult: *ghost yelp*, *bait tin*, *no-licence hymn*

### Physical form (for the modeller)

Nobody’s standard. Common Free build: a **2.2 m scavenged fuel drum** (1 m diameter) clamped to a **rock spur or dead pylon stub** with **cargo straps and three unequal chains**. Atop the drum: a **0.7 m jury mast** of welded rebar and a **lamp cluster** in a shopping basket of wire. **Primary mass:** the drum (battery junk, relay coils, someone’s spare suit air as “emergency” that expired). **Bolted / strapped on:** a **wide-band antenna** bent from a survey paddle fragment; a **hand crank** that does nothing (superstition); a **plaque** of hull plate with scratched names. **Hangs off:** prayer ribbons of cyan cloth, empty gel tins, a **single boot** nobody explains. **Bent or missing:** antenna **S-curved** from a wake; one chain **replaced with polymer line**; drum lid **warped** and held with a clamp that once belonged to a clamp set on Sola Patchline’s mule.

### Light

**Distress grammar without a hull:** when triggered or when its watch-circuit “thinks” it heard need — **alternating red-white global flash**, often with a **stutter** because the generator is wounded by design. Idle: a **dim cyan neighbour double-tick** every ten seconds — Free handwriting on Working Light. Some whistles only wake when a **red-white** passes within their deaf radius; then they **echo** the distress for two minutes and die. **Low cell:** red dies first; white becomes a **lazy grey blink**; cyan double-tick becomes a **single accidental wink**. A silent whistle is still a landmark; a screaming one is either truth or Reach’s oldest classic.

### Surface

Base: **whatever the drum was** — freighter grey, apron yellow, claim orange — under **patch primer and Free cyan scrawls**. **Scorch** around strap points from hurried welding. **Faded** sun-side paint if near a bright station shine. Stencils: hand-painted `WHISTLE`, waystation chalk hash, arrows toward help, and names of the dead the relay once answered. No licence number; any stamped number is a joke or a theft.

### Age and neglect

**Breaks first:** straps, antenna feed, red gel, then water intrusion in the drum. **New (as these get):** cyan bright, flash painful, plaque empty. **Twenty years:** drum **rust-through patches** foamed shut, mast a **third generation of rebar**, gels mixed colours, plaque so full of names the new ones go on the rock, chains half polymer, **boot still there**.

### Who services it

**Whoever lives nearby.** Free Frontier kids, Drift shift screens who want whistle rights both ways, tenders who answer red-white for a living. Cadence: after each real rescue — or never. **Skip:** licence, upkeep schedules, false-distress filters (those cost money). Concord dismantles whistles in controlled stacks and then wonders why corpses go unfound past the chart edge.

### What it means to a pilot

**Neighbour duty lives here.** If it is flashing red-white, treat it as distress until motion proves bait — approach with umbrellas cold and eyes open. If it double-ticks cyan, you are in Free manners country: wave, do not dark-run rude, answer if you can. A whistle bolted to a **fresh wreck** is either mercy or a snare; check for amber umbrellas and gun heat.

### Faction variants

| Hands | Hardware difference |
|---|---|
| **Free Frontier** | Cyan double-tick idle; drum culture; open build; names on plaque. |
| **Drift** | Miner-amber idle tick, red-white on trigger; claim-route welded base; they expect you to answer if you want cone courtesy later. |
| **Concord “rescue beacon”** | Not a whistle — sealed, licensed, blue plate, steady tip with rare red-white; no boot. |
| **Meridian insured lane** | Gold-tagged emergency buoy, invoice for false trigger; no straps, all fairings. |
| **Reach bait** | Perfect red-white, no cyan idle, scorch under a “new” drum, gun-lip polish on nearby rock mounts. |

## 5. Cold Locker
### Formal: *bonded cache* / *unmanned locker* · Slang: *ice box*, *clip cache*, *short bond* · Insult: *pirate pantry*, *Quiet’s fridge*, *hold without a spine*

### Physical form (for the modeller)

A **4 m hexagonal drum** (face-to-face 1.8 m) mounted on a **9 m spine** of lattice truss, clipped to rock by a **root clamp** and two **outrigger legs** (one often shorter). Overall: drum at mid-spine so the mass hangs **like a tick on a wire**. **Primary mass:** the drum and its **internal rack frames**. **Bolted on:** a **seal collar** and hatch with **three dogs**; a **bond lamp ring** around the hatch; a **solar / trickle petal** (one petal bent); a **registry wafer slot** under a barred window. **Hangs off:** a **cargo net remnant** with one frozen crate silhouette still suggested by dust; a **broken magnet puck**. **Bent or missing:** one lattice bay **crushed inward** from micro-impact; hatch dog **replaced with a welded scrap bar**; outrigger leg **sheared and cabled**.

### Light

**Bonded storage language:** a **steady cool-white ring** around the hatch when sealed and paid; **slow amber load-tick** (half a hauler’s heartbeat) if mass is inside and the bond is live; **green confirm** flash when a contracted hull is authorized to open (stacking kinship — a promise of approach). Empty honest lockers show **three dim whites in a row** on the drum belly — the empty bar, grounded. **Low cell:** ring goes **uneven**; amber tick **misses every third**; green dies and owners cut with codes in the dark — or cut without codes and make enemies.

### Surface

Base: **shop-primer grey with safety stripe** (yellow or Meridian gold). **Scorch** on the hatch lip from hurried torch work. **Scoured** spine on the lane side. **Faded** bond stencils where gloves work the dogs every week. Stencils: `BONDED`, `SHORT TERM`, desk or Free hash (example: **BX-CER-88** or chalk `FILTERS INSIDE — ASK`), serial on the wafer window, and `NOT SALVAGE` which fools no salvor with empty umbrellas.

### Age and neglect

**Breaks first:** hatch seals, bond ring gels, outrigger feet, wafer reader. **New:** stripe sharp, lattice true, ring even. **Twenty years:** drum **dented in two faces**, spine with **sistered beams**, seal foam crusted at every seam, bond ring **one dead arc honest**, interior rack **half missing** (sold), exterior still stencilled as if full.

### Who services it

**Meridian bond clerks** by contract cycle; **Free waystation co-ops** by rumour and padlock culture; **Drift** for filter and plate caches near refinery mouths. Cadence: when a bond expires or a complaint files. **Skip:** lattice paint, petal alignment, the “not salvage” courtesy. Quiet services nothing and still empties them.

### What it means to a pilot

**Mass without a mule.** Do not treat it as free scrap if the ring is white and the stencil is live — Meridian and Free both price thieves. An amber-ticking locker is someone’s short-term hold; loitering looks like weighing. A locker with **umbrellas dark and hatch dogs wrong** is already a crime scene or a Quiet receipt. Authorized open: show your approach lamp manners, soft station, no hard plume on the spine.

### Faction variants

| Hands | Hardware difference |
|---|---|
| **Meridian bonded** | Gold stripe, wafer reader, invoice pulse on a tiny mast, serials everywhere. |
| **Free co-op** | Cyan marks, mechanical padlock culture, empty bar honest, neighbour notes chalked on the drum. |
| **Drift shaft cache** | Orange dust, shorter spine, miner-amber tick, holds filters and plate more than luxury cargo. |
| **Concord evidence locker** | Blue seals, no amber mass language, cameras on the root clamp. |
| **Quiet use** | Minimum light, false empty bar, hatch that looks factory while the wafer is a lie. |

## 6. Ash Pin
### Formal: *memorial mark* · Slang: *ash pin*, *dead stick*, *name iron* · Insult: *tourist tear*, *guilt spike*, *unmaintained hymn* (said quietly)

### Physical form (for the modeller)

A **3.5 m slender pin** — often a **cut spar from the dead hull** itself — set in a **1 m poured or foamed base** on the wreck’s last rock or free-floating on a **minimal ballast plate** chained to debris. **Primary mass:** the spar base. **Bolted / wired on:** a **name plate** (0.5 × 0.3 m) with punched or welded letters; a **single lamp** in a cage; offerings — **fasteners on a wire** (salvor habit), gel tins, a child’s toy clamp, a strip of company stripe cloth. **Hangs off:** prayer streamers, a **tethered helmet** sometimes, frozen. **Bent or missing:** spar **leaned by the explosion that made it necessary**; plate **half-melted** on one corner; lamp cage **empty** more often than not; ballast chain **one link wrong alloy** and proud of it.

Nobody moves them. That is the rule. Nobody maintains them either — that is the cost.

### Light

**Not commerce.** When someone still cares: a **slow white breath** — on for three seconds, off for nine — like a spine-wake that never finishes. Some Free pins add a **cyan double-tick** on the anniversary shift. Concord memorial arrays (Helios keeps a formal one) use **steady cool white** in ranks; field ash pins are lonelier. **Low cell / dead cell:** darkness. The pin remains. A pin that suddenly runs **red-white** is not memorial — something living is using the dead for a stage.

### Surface

Base: **hull-native paint** of the lost ship — mule grey, Wasp slate, Pelican orange — scorched at the break. **Scoured** by years of dust without a crew to wipe it. **Faded** names where gloves touch for luck. Stencils: names, date, sometimes `SPAN-HOLD` as a word that still means forty-one souls and a drawer full of annexes; claim numbers of the living who refused to forget; `DO NOT MOVE` in three handwritings.

### Age and neglect

**Breaks first:** lamp, streamers, then the plate fasteners; the spar outlasts all of it. **New:** name sharp, lamp breathing, offerings fresh. **Twenty years:** plate **illegible except to fingers**, spar **pitted**, base foamed over meteor nicks, offerings a **strata of rust**, lamp long dead, and still the lane bends a little around it because someone told someone once.

### Who services it

**No roster.** Free neighbours replace a gel when guilt or love spikes. Salvors leave a fastener and do not take the plate. Concord formal arrays have grounds crews; field ash pins do not. Meridian may invoice a memorial on a brochure and never visit. Reach that steals a name plate earns a kind of attention no weigh-slip covers.

### What it means to a pilot

**Slow your mouth.** Give wide berth if traffic allows — not for physics, for the channel. Do not salvage the pin. Do not use it as a lane pin or a claim stake. If you must hold station, hold with repair manners: soft, visible, no joke. New pilots from Helios learn the Memorial Array as architecture; out past Ceres they learn ash pins as **habits of the living**.

### Faction variants

| Hands | Hardware difference |
|---|---|
| **Free Frontier** | Cyan streamers, open offerings, breath-lamp when someone can spare a cell. |
| **Drift** | Spar from the lost rig, claim-paint stripe on the base, names of the shift; dust allowed to bury the foot. |
| **Concord array** | Ranked pins, blue-white steady, machine letters, grounds path — Helios’s clean grief. |
| **Meridian** | Rare gold name inlay on insured disasters; most corporate dead get a desk plaque in Tethys instead. |
| **Reach** | Empty bases where plates were pried; sometimes a red mark left as insult; the lane remembers. |

## Closing — Reading the Clutter

| Furniture | First silhouette tell | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Claim mark | Spike on rock + plate + amber tick | Stay out of the face; cone may be next |
| Lane pin | Tall spine + vanes + pass chevron | Take the green side; match the speed band |
| Tally post | Platform + boom yoke + gold mast | Slow, honest mass language, no boom-cut |
| Whistle | Strapped drum + ugly mast | Answer red-white; wave at cyan |
| Cold locker | Hex drum on long lattice spine | Bond is law until the ring lies |
| Ash pin | Thin spar + name plate + quiet | Do not move it; do not make it a joke |

Stations are where the holos point. Furniture is why the open corridor is not empty. Span-Hold taught the Guild to make hulls legible. The belt already knew: if you cannot read the small iron between the docks, you are not ready for the lamps on the ships.

Believe the motion first. The buoy second. The brochure never.
