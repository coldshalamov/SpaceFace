## Findings (ranked)

### F1 — Claim lapse-ability is keyed to the owner row's mint-time role, not the posting's demand class — arm posts on non-arm rows mint immortal claims — `MECHANISM_VERIFIED` — impact M, effort M, risk medium

Evidence: `sectorClaimStamp` (`src/render/assetResidency.js:827-839`) tests `metadata.role` on the *stored* row (`SECTOR_ARM_CLAIM_ROLE = /warm|runway|prewarm|armory|predicted/i`) to decide whether the new sector union mints a lapse-able `sectorClaimAts` entry or an immortal `sectorLiveClaims` member. But the stamp's semantic — "did the arming context lapse?" — is a property of the *post*, not the row. Three reachable mislabel vectors, all verified in code:

- **`reclaimWarm` resident union** (`:507-517` → `unionOwnerSector` `:898-937` → `sectorClaimStamp`): a warm arm re-posting a key already held by a `runtime-cache`-minted row on the shared session owner (`runtime.defaultResidencyOwner`, `assetLoader.js:710/1294`) unions `sectorId` under the *row's* `'runtime-cache'` role → **live claim**. Probe (injected clock): mint `role:'runtime-cache'` on owner O, then `reclaimWarm(key, O, 'sector_alpha')`; arm dies; at t+130 s `prepareSectorExit('sector_alpha')` still retains the entry — the dead arm's claim qualifies the exit-warm cohort forever. Same arm on an arm-minted row correctly lapses (retained 0).
- **Decode-cache warm retains** (`assetLoader.js:856-871`): every boundary-scoped load that completes with an arm role posts `residency.retain(cacheKey, runtime.decodeCacheOwner, { role: 'decode-cache', sectorId: options.sectorId, decodeWarm: true })` — `'decode-cache'` is non-arm → **live claim per warm-purpose load**. This is the dominant vector: `sector-prewarm` (`renderer.js:19190`), `sector-predicted` (`renderer.js:6948`), `crucible-roster-warm`/`survival-roster-prewarm`/`save-envelope-decode-runway` (`:21530/:22130/:20590/:6656`) — all carry `residencyOwner` + `sectorId`, so each arms two claims: a correct lapse-able one on the arm's own row, and an immortal duplicate on the decode-cache row that survives the arm's retraction.
- **`reclaimWarm` request branch** (`:524-537`): `state.requests` scan unions + stamps `request.metadata` under the *request's* mint role. Reclaim consults any active same-key request under the owner — including a non-warm (`'runtime-cache'`) request racing the arm — so the in-flight union propagates a live claim at commit (`retain` seed `:424-441` carries `priorLive` verbatim). Probe: `beginRequest(role:'runtime-cache')` → `reclaimWarm(key, owner, 'sector_delta')` → `commit()` → sector_delta retained on exit-warm at t+130 s.
- (`retain`'s merge path `:406-408` is the same shape — `unionOwnerSector` stamps by `existingMetadata.role` for every re-posted `sectorId`/`sectorIds` member.)

Mechanism the player sees: none directly — this is over-coverage, not a pop. The dead claims keep the entry qualified on `hasLiveSectorClaim` (`:1962-1970`) so **every exit-warm mint** (`prepareSectorExit`/`rotateSector` and deferred twins) re-retains entries whose only demand was a retracted arm — cohort dilution + mint work scaling with accumulated dead claims across a long session — and `entryCorridorRank` (`:1060-1072`) keeps ranking the entry in-corridor when the dead sector sits in the prediction list, so under byte pressure (`sortSoftEvictionCandidates` `:1074-1088`) a stale-claimed entry outranks an honest no-corridor entry of the same lease weight → the honest file evicts first → cold re-decode on its next need (pop-adjacent, second-order). `pruneLapsedSectorClaims` can never reach these members — live claims are definitionally `sectorClaimFresh` (`:850-856`) — so they live exactly as long as the shared row (session, or until invalidation).

Fix sketch: key the stamp to the *posting's* class, not the stored row's — thread a `claimWarm`/purpose flag (or test the *posting* metadata's `role`/`decodeWarm`) through `unionOwnerSector(entry, metadata, sid, stampRole)`, `reclaimWarm` (resident branch is definitionally arm-side — reclaim is the warm consult), and the retain merge/seed paths. The decode-cache call site already carries `decodeWarm` in the same metadata — route on it. Symmetric note: a non-arm post on an arm-minted shared row currently mints a lapse-able claim over live demand — **unreachable on today's call graph** (every non-arm `sectorId` post rides a dedicated owner: boundary rows, `decodeCacheOwner`, `bootstrapResidencyOwner`; shared-owner `sectorId` posts are all warm-role) — but the fix must classify by the post in *both* directions or the latent hole becomes live.

Effort M (the flag must thread four sites + the request-metadata propagation; the seed propagation already handles mixed `priorAts`/`priorLive`). Magic-frame impact M — bounded over-coverage with a real ordering/bytes distortion under pressure; errs anti-pop. Risk medium — mis-routing in the other direction would re-create the W135 under-coverage bug.

### F2 — `decodeCacheOwner`/`bootstrapResidencyOwner` live-claim accrual — well-traveled assets claim every served sector forever — `MECHANISM_VERIFIED` mechanism / `UNVERIFIED` residual — impact L, effort S–M

Evidence: independent of the F1 mislabel, *correctly-labeled* live posts accumulate without bound. `decodeCacheOwner` unions `options.sectorId` on every boundary-scoped serve (`assetLoader.js:860-870`); `bootstrapResidencyOwner` (`partsLibrary.js:10240-10245`) mints a permanent `'bootstrap'` (non-arm) claim on hardcoded `'sector_helios_prime'`; `retainLibraryPlan` boundary rows (`:10577-10580`) claim their mount sector for the boundary's lifetime (correct — dies at release). A shared hull part served under N sectors holds N live claims for the session — each still qualifies it on exit-warm and corridor rank. Mostly *intended* (served-sector claims are honest return-visit signal, and `decodeServed` weight-2 already encodes the priority); the residual is that a serve's sector claim never de-escalates even when the sector is provably dead (not in corridor, departed long ago). Probe-ground answer to the prompt's (a): a live row never claims a sector it *should have released* in the mounted-boundary case (release kills the row) — only shared rows accumulate, and over-coverage is the strictly safer failure side (under-coverage is what W135-F2 fixed: pops from evicted live demand). No fix recommended beyond F1's flag route; if accrual ever shows as index bloat in `diagnostics().sectors`, cap the union per row or lapse *served*-sector claims independently of live-demand ones.

### F3 — `sectorClaimFresh`'s missing-stamp back-compat is a forward-compat hole — `UNVERIFIED`
