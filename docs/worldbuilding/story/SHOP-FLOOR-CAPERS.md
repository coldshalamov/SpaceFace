# SpaceFace — Shop-Floor Capers

> **Scope:** the operational register the corpus rationed itself out of. This is the
> Ritchie idiolect + Stanhope self-implication + Theo rambling-sincerity layer the
> LITERARY-AUDIT's Section IV named as the missing strut. Every recurring NPC gets
> a *private caper they think is the real story* — a scheme whose mechanics reveal
> character, whose failure reveals the system, and whose humor is never a quip
> pasted on but a byproduct of a person being specific about their own corner of the
> rot.
>
> **Discipline:** the audit's cardinal rule holds. No "this is the point." No author
> name-drops. No Marvel meta-zingers. The funny and the pitiful live in the same
> place: a person with a scheme, doing the arithmetic, unaware (or dimly aware) that
> the arithmetic is the confession. Leonard's test on every line: *if it sounds like
> writing, it's wrong.* These are people talking about their work. Their work is the
> crime. They don't know that. Or they know and they do it anyway because the
> alternative is the deck.

---

## The principle: everyone thinks they're the protagonist

The eight canonical contract-actors (plus the iter-07 cast) are not a conspiracy. They are eight people running eight private capers, each one convinced their scheme is the real story and the rest of the sector is scenery. The schemes intersect — Kessler's skim feeds Drift's moisture-loss feeds Mira's code-swap feeds the Pit's air — but no one actor sees the intersection. Each sees their own ledger. Each believes their ledger is the load-bearing one. Each is a node who thinks they're the protagonist. That mistake is where the comedy and the pity both live, and it is the Ritchie structural-irony engine: the audience sees the collision the characters don't.

The comedy rule (Stanhope + Scorsese): every joke about the scheme must also be a confession of the schemer's complicity. The joke that doesn't cost the teller something is a quip, and a quip is what we cut.

## KESSLER — the weigher's daughter

**The caper:** Kessler has been saving the skim for nineteen years to buy his daughter a berth on a Core station where the air is warm. He has the number. The number is precise. The number assumes the skim continues at the current rate for six more years.

**What Kessler thinks the story is:** a father's quiet sacrifice. The long con of love, measured to the gram, that nobody sees because the scale is where Kessler hides it.

**What the story actually is:** Kessler is the rate. The skim doesn't continue "at the current rate" independent of Kessler — Kessler *is* the rate. The six years will become twelve because the daughter grows up breathing Tycho air (18 degrees, yellow-white) and the Core berth's price inflates faster than the skim compounds, and Kessler will keep thumbing the seal because the number is the only prayer he has, and the number is structurally incapable of arriving, and Kessler knows this on the nights he counts it, and thumbs the seal again in the morning because the morning is when the next load comes across and the load has to be weighed and Kessler is the one who weighs it.

**The joke that is a confession (Stanhope):**
> "I'm saving for her berth. Nineteen years. The number's almost there. Six more, she's on a Core station, warm air, full spectrum. She's nine. She thinks I weigh cargo. I do weigh cargo. I weigh it twice — once for the column, once for her. The column balances. Her column doesn't. Her column's been six years from balancing for nineteen years. I'm not bad at math. The math is fine. The math is the only honest thing on the scale. The scale is the lie. The lie is what I'm saving. I'm saving the lie for her. She'll breathe warm air on a lie I thumbed out of a seal, and she'll never know, and that's the gift. That's the whole gift. The gift is that she never finds out her father is the reason the air where she grew up tasted like chalk."

**Where it lands:** B1 (Tycho scale, ambient), B6 (the Slow Gray — Kessler's hands are the symptom the graffiti names).

## VOSS — the map of sealed shafts

**The caper:** Voss files exhausted claims because Voss is building a private map of every shaft the system has sealed and re-sealed. The map is for the day Voss goes back into the sealed shafts alone and works the played-out veins that were never played out, only filed that way.

**What Voss thinks the story is:** the last honest prospector, keeping the only map that remembers what the claims office forgot, one shift away from the vein that changes everything.

**What the story actually is:** the shafts Voss means to reopen include Shaft 7, which is not exhausted of anything a miner would want. What Shaft 7 has is a sealed hopper, a double-pass weld, and the reason the Pit's air went bad. Voss does not know this. Voss will find out, or Voss will not. The map does not have a column for what is actually in the shafts. The map only has coordinates. Voss has spent nineteen years building the most accurate map in the sector of a route that leads, unknowingly, to the crime that's killing the air Voss breathes while drawing it.

**The joke that is a confession (Theo — rambling, regionally-specific, sincerely wrong):**
> "People ask why I file 'em exhausted. I file 'em exhausted because they are exhausted. You work a vein down to the dregs, you file it, you move on. That's the job. The job's honest. What I do after the job — that's mine. I keep the markers. I keep the coordinates. I keep which shaft got sealed by which supervisor on which shift, because the supervisors lie and the rock doesn't. The rock knows where it is. I just write it down. Nineteen years of writing it down. I've got a map that'd make the claims office weep. Every played-out shaft in the belt, cross-referenced with who filed it and why, and the why is always a lie, and the lie is always 'exhausted,' and 'exhausted' means 'someone wanted it closed.' Shaft 7's on my map. Shaft 7's been on my map since year 3. I'm going back into Shaft 7. I'm going back into all of them. The vein's there. I can feel the vein. The vein's always there. The vein's been there for nineteen years and I've been mapping my way back to it and the air's been getting worse the whole time and I never once put the two together because the two don't go together, do they? The vein's the vein. The air's the air. They don't go together."

**Where it lands:** B1 (Hollow Station claim post, ambient), the slow realization across the midgame that Shaft 7 is on Voss's map and Shaft 7 is where the recycler grid was sealed.

## MIRA — the ticket that's always two jobs away

**The caper:** Mira swaps manifest codes at the Bourse relay to fund a one-way passage offworld — one ticket, to a system where no one has heard of Bourse or the Quiet or her name.

**What Mira thinks the story is:** the escape artist, two jobs from freedom, running the only honest dishonesty in a dishonest house.

**What the story actually is:** the thing that keeps Mira at the relay is not the money, which is enough. It's the suspicion that the moment she stops swapping codes, the system will notice she existed. She cannot prove this. The suspicion is the cage. The two-jobs-away number holds, the ticket stays bought but unclaimed, and Mira is a woman whose escape route is a door she built herself and will not walk through because walking through it would confirm the system can see her, and being seen is the thing she's been hiding from for four years by being useful.

**The joke that is a confession (le Carré — short, acidic, muttered):**
> "Two more jobs. Two more and I'm gone. I've been two more jobs from gone for four years. The ticket's real. I bought it. It's mine. I just haven't picked it up. Picking it up is a signature, and signatures are how the system knows you moved, and I'd rather be the woman at the relay the system needs than the woman at the gate the system notices. You notice me, you can file me. You file me, I'm in the column. I'm not going in the column. The column is where people go who stopped being useful. I'm useful. I swap the codes. The codes keep me here. The here keeps me useful. The useful keeps me out of the column. Two more jobs."

**Where it lands:** B1 (Bourse relay, ambient), B5 (the Free Captains branch routes through Mira's freight system — the player is now in Mira's channel).

## ROOK — the tally as confession in installments

**The caper:** Rook coordinates bounty tags from a booth at Cinder and keeps a private tally of every double-bill, convinced the tally is leverage he'll one day trade to Concord for a pardon and a badge and a booth at a Core station where the air is warm.

**What Rook thinks the story is:** the player of the long game, holding the house's receipts, waiting for the hand where he cashes them all in.

**What the story actually is:** the pardon is a fantasy. Concord does not pardon ledgers; it absorbs them. Rook's tally is not leverage. It is a confession written in installments, and the installment plan never ends, because the next name comes across the board every morning and the name has to go somewhere and Rook's booth is where names go. Rook knows this on the nights he counts the tally. He double-bills again in the morning because the morning is when the next name comes.

**The joke that is a confession (Stanhope — the logical argument that indicts the arguer):**
> "People think I'm dirty. I'm not dirty. I'm precise. Every tag I double, I log. Both payers, both cuts, my fee, the date, the signature. I've got the cleanest books in the sector. Cleaner than Concord. Concord shreds. I file. My tally is the only honest record of what this booth does, and what this booth does is decide whose problem a body becomes, and I've been deciding that for eleven years, and the tally proves I decided it correctly every time. That's leverage. That's my pension. One day I hand Concord the tally, they see how clean the books are, they give me a badge and a booth where the air is warm, and I stop. That's the plan. The plan's sound. The plan's been sound for eleven years. The plan requires Concord to read the tally and see a pension. Concord will read the tally and see a confession. I know this. I've known this since year two. I keep the tally anyway. The tally is the only thing I've ever built that goes the direction I pointed it. The direction is down. I know. The plan's sound."

**Where it lands:** B2 (the Elroy double-bill is Rook's booth; the player doesn't see this until B7), B7 (Rook's tally appears in the Kurtz figure's ledger as one of the columns the Kurtz figure copied).

## SLATE — the column of failure points

**The caper:** Slate runs a side business in bad welds — not for the skim, but for the *inventory*. Every ship Slate patches twice is a ship Slate knows the failure point of. The list is Slate's retirement: one day the right ship comes through with the right owner and the right insurance, and Slate's second pass is the thing that turns a repair job into a salvage claim, and the salvage claim is the score.

**What Slate thinks the story is:** the patient metallurgist, holding the deck, waiting for the one break that pays.

**What the story actually is:** the right ship has not come through. The list grows. The failure points accumulate. Slate has become a man who knows exactly how every hull in the sector will break and who is waiting, with the patience of a man who has nothing else, for the one break that pays — and the break that pays is a death, because the salvage claim requires a wreck, and the wreck requires a hull to fail at the point Slate welded it to fail, and Slate knows this, and Slate is waiting for it, and the waiting is the patience of a man who has decided that one death is his retirement and has filed that decision under "metallurgy" because "metallurgy" is a word that doesn't cost him the thing "murder" would cost him.

**The joke that is a confession (Gilligan — competent procedure, the one lethal detail):**
> "It's not sabotage. It's metallurgy. Metallurgy is patient. So am I. Every hull I patch twice is a hull I know. I know where it bends. I know where it goes. I know the pressure spike that takes it. I'm not causing the spike. The spike happens on its own. The universe provides spikes. I just know which seam the spike finds, because I welded that seam, second pass, narrower, the way I weld. The weld's good. The weld holds to the third spike. Always has. The fourth is where it goes. Nobody pushes a second-pass hull to the fourth. But somebody will. Somebody always does. And when they do, I'll be here, and the salvage claim comes through my yard, and I quote the replacement, and the column balances, and I retire. That's the plan. The plan doesn't require me to do anything. The plan requires me to wait. I'm good at waiting. I've been good at waiting for twenty years. The column's never been wrong."

**Where it lands:** B3 (the shipyard, the VARIANCE ADJUSTMENT hull the player walks past, Slate's double-pass seam is in the player's new ship).

## DRIFT — the pension the exchange forgot to fund

**The caper:** Drift records ore deliveries at Meridian Exchange and logs the shortfall as "moisture loss," his own account showing the difference deposited under a subsidiary code. Drift tells himself this is the pension the exchange never funded.

**What Drift thinks the story is:** the honest man forced into one dishonesty by a dishonest scale, bounded, the books to be balanced one day when he pays it back.

**What the story actually is:** Drift has been paying it back, in his head, for sixteen years. The principal has never decreased. The interest is the thing Drift doesn't call interest, because calling it interest would make him Kessler, and Drift is not Kessler, Drift is a man with a pension the exchange forgot to fund. He will retire on the moisture loss. The moisture loss will retire him. These are the same sentence and Drift has not noticed.

**The joke that is a confession (already authored, B1 chapter extension — the ramble).** The shop-floor voice is the joke. The joke is that Drift narrates his own crime as a weather event and the narration is the only thing keeping the hours even, and the narration is accurate, and the accuracy is the confession, and Drift does not hear it as a confession because he's too tired to hear it as anything but the shift.

**Where it lands:** B1 (the extended chapter beat), the moisture-loss entry is the audit-threshold mechanism the whole Silt economy rests on.

## HALE — the sacrament (the caper is the absence of one)

**The caper:** Hale has no caper. Hale initials the second fine because REF 44-C requires it and Hale has never, in seventeen years, failed to initial a legally required entry. The priest does not look in the hold. The priest administers the sacrament. The hold is someone else's department.

**What Hale thinks the story is:** the honest man. Seventeen years of unbroken signatures. The regulation says what the regulation says. Hale does what Hale does. The system is not Hale's problem. Hale is not the system.

**What the story actually is:** Hale's integrity is load-bearing for the structure that's suffocating forty thousand people, and he is — by every standard he was handed — innocent. Remove Hale's adherence and the double-fine apparatus collapses. The villainy routes through the honest man. Hale has no caper because Hale is the caper: a man whose adherence *is* the corruption, whose innocence is the mechanism, and whose tragedy is that nothing he can do from inside his standards would stop any of it, because his standards are the thing doing it. The graffiti on his scanner housing — *HALE INITIALED BOTH. GATE 3. YEAR 7. THE SECOND ENTRY IS FILED UNDER REG 44-C. GO LOOK.* — is the only accusation the system can't file under a different code, because the accusation *is* the filing. Hale initialses the maintenance request to paint over it.

**The non-joke that is the confession (Bacigalupi — the dissonance is the joke, and it's not funny):**
> "The manifest says titanium. The scan says 12,400 kilograms. The seal is Concord ALA. Under REG 44-C, I don't break Concord seals. If the Pit is short of Silt this cycle, they can file an appeal with Logistics Oversight. I don't change the numbers on the sheet. I just sign it."

Hale is describing the recycler catalyst grid the player is moving in B0. Hale doesn't know it's a recycler catalyst grid. Hale doesn't break Concord seals. The "Silt" appeal goes to Vale's division. This is not a breakdown. This is the system. The joke is that there is no joke. The joke is that Hale is telling the truth and the truth is the crime and Hale is the only person in the sector who is not lying and his honesty is the most lethal thing in the room.

**Where it lands:** B0 (Hale's gate clears the player's first cargo), B4 (the Patrol Authority branch files through Hale's gate), all chapters (Hale's signature is the thread).

## QUINN — the second ledger

**The caper:** Quinn changes credits at the bar on Outpost 9 and keeps a second ledger — *his* ledger, not the house ledger — under the till. Every short count goes into it. The number climbs. The climb is the only thing in Quinn's life that has ever gone the direction he pointed it.

**What Quinn thinks the story is:** the underground man, three years from buying the bar, then the station, then his name off every Pit hierarchy that ever held it. The player is a mark in his long game.

**What the story actually is:** the station isn't for sale. The hierarchy doesn't sell names back. Quinn will die behind that bar with the ledger two-thirds full and the number still climbing, and the number will be the most honest thing in the room. Quinn's skim created a windfall that made the people above him richer and the people around him poorer (the Vael pullout → MTS fills at worse rates → commissary loses its subsidy). Quinn still doesn't know he caused it. He thinks he's three years from buying the bar.

**The joke that is a confession (Scorsese — the laugh at the wrong thing, the self-deception performed as confidence):**
> "You want to know the secret? The secret's arithmetic. Every stack I count, I count twice. Once for the house, once for me. The house column balances. My column climbs. The climb's the only direction anything in my life has ever gone. Three years, I buy the bar. Five, I buy the station. Ten, I buy my name back off every list that ever held it. The arithmetic's sound. I checked it. I check it every night. It never changes. It's the only thing that never changes. You know what else never changes? The air in here. Same air since I started. They say it's getting worse. I don't taste it. I've been breathing it so long it tastes like mine. That's the secret too. Everything in here is mine. The bar's mine — I mean, not yet, but the way the column's going, it's mine. The air's mine. The ledger's mine. The count's mine. You're mine, long as you're changing credits at my bar. Three years."

**Where it lands:** B1 (Outpost 9 bar, ambient), the Quinn windfall appears in the Kurtz figure's ledger annotated *unintended*.

## The through-line (the collision the audience sees, the characters don't)

Kessler thumbs the seal → the variance feeds Drift's moisture-loss → Drift's subsidiary code is the same column Mira's code-swap hides in → Mira's reroutes put cargo on ships Slate welds → Slate's failure points determine which ships Rook's bounties clear → Rook's double-bills remove witnesses to Hale's clearances → Hale's clearances move the recycler grid out of the Pit → the Pit's air gets worse → Kessler thumbs the seal again to save his daughter from the air his thumb is helping kill.

Eight capers. Eight ledgers. One economy. Nobody sees the loop. The loop sees them. The loop is the game.

The comedy is structural (Ritchie): the audience, by B6, can see the collision none of the eight can see, because each is looking at their own column. The pity is Dostoyevskian: each is doing the only arithmetic available to a person in their position, and the arithmetic is correct, and the arithmetic is the crime. The horror is Bacigalupi: this is not a heist. This is a business model. It prints every cycle. It has no villain. It has eight people being specific about their work.

- **Barks:** the "joke that is a confession" blocks are ready to become hail/contact-dialogue lines in `barks.js` CONTACT_VOICE_REGISTERS, gated to the right contact.

- **Encounters:** the Theo/Drift ramble (B1 chapter extension) is already structured as a monologue a contact delivers; it can become a depth-h encounter's `primaryLine`.
- **Post-ending chains:** the "what the story actually is" reframings are the post-ending mission premises — the player who chose E meets these people again, still running their capers, still not seeing the loop.

The capers are the strut. They do not replace the canon. They give the canon's eight functions the interiority the audit demanded, and they do it in the operational register the corpus rationed itself out of, and the humor is never bolted on — it is the sound of a person being specific about the crime that is their job.
