**SPACEFACE — NPC ECOLOGY (THE INVISIBLE NETWORK)**

They do not meet. They do not coordinate. They do not need to. The Pit taught them all the same lesson and they learned it at the same time in the same place under the same wardens. The network is not organized. It is fluent.

---

**The graph:**

KESSLER (scales, Tycho) logs weight. DRIFT (ledgers, Meridian Exchange) records yield. What Kessler under-weighs, Drift logs as "moisture loss." The difference between the two numbers lives in a subsidiary code that neither of them owns on paper. Neither has ever spoken to the other. They worked adjacent shafts in the Pit for eleven months. The math transfers.

VOSS (mining claims, Hollow Station) files claims exhausted. SLATE (hull patches, Pit shipyard) does bad welds on the machinery that worked those claims. The equipment fails. The claim gets filed exhausted before the real crew can document the yield. Voss never needs to know which patch Slate cut twice; Slate never needs to know which claim Voss will file. The timing is enough. They spent three years in the same cell block.

MIRA (sealed freight, Bourse) routes the crate. QUINN (currency, Outpost 9) changes the credits that were supposed to pay for the crate. By the time the original buyer files a dispute, the crate has new seals and the credits have become house scrip. Neither Mira nor Quinn touches the other's part. They were on the same work crew for two years. They don't talk anymore.

ROOK (bounties, Cinder) tags the target twice. HALE (customs, Gate 3) clears the same target for transit between tags. The target thinks they're paid up because they are: they paid Rook once. Rook took the payout from both clients and wrote the second payout into a log that Hale never opens because the log is formatted to pass Hale's scanner handshake without being read. They found this out by accident in year four. They don't talk about it. The format still works.

---

**The graffiti that connects them:**

Most graffiti is solo testimony. A few are notes between nodes.

*THE WELD KNOWS THE CLAIM KNOWS THE WEIGHT.* (Slag Bay 4, Pit shipyard — written after Slate's third bad patch collapsed the hopper on a claim Voss had already filed. The cargo was already logged by Kessler. Nobody filed charges. The graffiti appeared during maintenance shutdown.)

*MIRA NEVER TOUCHES THE CRATE. THAT'S HOW YOU KNOW WHAT'S IN IT.* (Inside cargo hold, Bourse freight annex — the signature isn't there, which is itself a signature.)

*ROOK OWES ME—* (Comms panel, Cinder booth — the sentence stops. The writer is either paid or not able to finish it. Neither possibility is better than the other.)

---

**What the player learns:**

Not through cutscene. Not through a log dump. Through repetition.

The player works a Kessler job and the weight is wrong. They work a Drift job and the shortfall matches. They work a Voss job in the same sector as a Slate repair job and the timing connects without anyone explaining it. By the time the player has run enough contracts, the network is visible without ever being named. The graffiti doesn't explain it either. It just confirms: someone else saw this too. Someone else survived long enough to write it down.

The network is a language, not a pact. Nobody swore anyone in. The Pit is where they all learned the grammar; the system is where they speak it.

---

**The structural reveal:**

The structural center is MIRA (the physical pivot) and HALE (the systemic anchor). Mira is where the extraction side hands off to the laundering side — without her code-swap, the physical transaction breaks. Hale is the thread that ties every contract together legally: his blind adherence to REG 44-C validates the whole structure. Hale is not corrupt. The non-corrupt person is the mechanism the corrupt system runs through. The villainy routes through the honest man, which is the only villainy you can't arrest.

The underlying transaction is the theft of the Pit's primary atmospheric recycler catalyst grid.

The catalyst grid weighs 12.4 tons. It was pre-loaded with high-grade Refined Silt — the catalytic slurry that lines the recycler channels and keeps CO2 from accumulating. This is the weight that went missing in Contract 47-A. The player's first job was carrying the stolen recycler in the manifest slot labeled "TITANIUM ALLOY 12400KG." Every job they've taken since is a continuation of that contract. The graffiti has always known this.

The theft of the grid with its Silt load is what triggered the Slow Gray in the Pit's lower decks. Without the primary recycler, the degraded backup Silt couldn't clear the CO2. The workers got slower. The ore throughput dropped. The ATMO DEBT score fell below Vale's threshold. The maintenance allocation cut. The backup Silt degraded to Chalk. The Pit has been breathing Chalk air ever since. They called it being tired. The work was hard. The work was not why.

— Kessler under-weighs the *ongoing* ore skim (sub-tonne rounds, cycle after cycle — each one "moisture loss," each one under the audit threshold). The 12.4-tonne grid was never his to hide. That left the Pit on a clean manifest under a legal code.
— Drift balances the Meridian Exchange ledger against the under-tonne losses Kessler feeds him — "shipping loss," "moisture loss," the slow bleed that never trips the audit  
— Voss files the shaft where the recycler was housed as "claim exhausted — sealed" *(after* the grid left legally; the seal is the cover-up, the filing is the paper trail that makes the shaft's emptiness look like geology instead of policy)
— Slate collapses the shaft hopper with a double-pass narrow weld, burying the stripped vault so nobody goes looking for what the legal transfer already moved
— Mira re-registers the ongoing skim's freight as "INDUSTRIAL COMPONENTS" under code VALE-ALA-47A — riding the *clean* authorization the grid's transfer already established, laundering the small thefts inside the paperwork the big one made respectable
— Hale clears customs at Gate 3 without opening the container (REG 44-C: sealed cargo, double fine paid) — for the skim and the grid alike, because the seal is Concord ALA and Hale has never broken a Concord seal
— Quinn converts the Helios Prime buyer's payment to house scrip, exits the audit trail
— Rook double-bills a bounty on the Pit's chief engineer (one Elroy, maintenance division) who filed a report when the recycler disappeared. The player took this contract in B2. "BOUNTY COLLECTED." The second tag was Rook's. The player only saw the first.

Eight people. Two transactions. The first — the 12.4-tonne grid — was legal, signed, filed, and never needed hiding. The second — the ongoing sub-tonne skim that rides the first one's paperwork — is the one that needed a network, and the network grew it out of proximity and mutual utility and twenty years of the same prison teaching everyone the same lesson. They are not colleagues. They are rhymes. The big theft made the small one possible. The small one is the one that pays every cycle.

The structural center is the gap between the two. The grid left once, lawfully, and the Pit's air went with it. Everything since has been eight people skimming the runoff and the system that laundered the skimming because the skimming fit a code that was already on the books. The horror isn't the heist. The horror is that there was no heist. There was a filing, and then fourteen years of petty larceny nested inside the filing's paperwork, and none of it — not the grid, not the skim — was ever illegal.

The punchline lands when the player sees the combined log: no cutscene, no music sting. The log just sits there. The 12.4 tons in B0's first contract is the same 12.4 tons the algorithm reallocated is the same 12.4 tons on the legal manifest is the same weight of the recycler catalyst grid that the Pit's lower decks stopped running on in year 3. The grid was never stolen. It was transferred. The player was the courier in a transaction that was lawful at every signature. They were in the story before they knew it was a story, and the story was a form in triplicate.

---

**What arrived at Helios:**

The grid arrived at Helios Prime in year 3, on schedule, manifest correct, seals intact. It was logged into the Core maintenance depot. It was the wrong grid.

Not wrong as in defective. Wrong as in Helios already had a better one — a newer revision, installed two cycles prior, that the reallocation order's author hadn't cross-referenced because cross-referencing installation status against the transfer queue was somebody else's department. The Pit's primary recycler catalyst grid — 12.4 tonnes of high-grade Silt-loaded hardware, good for forty years of service — was shelved. Put in a warehouse. Filed under "SURPLUS — PENDING INSTALLATION." The installation never came. It never came because nobody at the Core ever needed it, because the Core's air was already perfect, because the Core's air had been perfect the whole time.

The Pit has been breathing Chalk for fourteen years over an object that has spent those fourteen years gathering dust on a shelf three rungs above the people who built it.

Somewhere in the Helios maintenance system there is a ticket. Filed year 3, cycle 2. A functionary — name redacted, title junior — wrote: *"Newly received industrial component (12.4t, manifest VALE-ALA-47A) smells faintly of machine oil in bay 7. Requesting ventilation review."* The ticket was assigned. The ticket was resolved: *"Ventilation adequate. Odor consistent with transit. No action."* The ticket was closed. The ticket number is in the system. The grid is still in bay 7. The smell dissipated in a week.

Forty thousand people went gray, and the only record at the destination is a smell complaint.

The graffiti that knows this, if it exists, the player has not found yet. It would read, if it existed: *THE AIR AT HELIOS IS GOOD. IT WAS GOOD BEFORE YOU GOT THERE.* It would be written in a hand that worked the Core maintenance depot in year 3 and transferred out in year 4 and never went back. It may not exist. The hand may be dead. The bay still smells faintly of machine oil in summer cycles when the ventilation runs hot. Nobody has filed a ticket about it since year 3, cycle 2.

---

**Non-Negotiables:**

- No NPC ever references another by name in dialog or contract text.
- No contract ever explicitly names the connection.
- The ecology is only visible in aggregate, across multiple playthroughs or close attention.
- The graffiti confirms; it does not explain.
- The player is never rewarded with a revelation cutscene. The reward is understanding, which is cold.
