# SpaceFace — The Le Carré Layer

> **Scope:** scaffolding, not voice. This document and `DOSTOYEVSKY-LAYER.md` are the
> only places in the corpus where author names appear. Body prose, sheets, and chapters
> **never** name-drop. The corpus is *already* structurally Le Carré — Vale is the
> Smiley-becomes-Karla's-mirror move, the eight-node ecology is the Circus-as-machine,
> Hale is the honest administrator whose adherence *is* the corruption. This layer
> deepens and *names* what is already there, then adds the one element the corpus lacks:
> the intelligence operation running *underneath* the player's career, which recontextualizes
> every prior beat.
>
> The Dosto layer remains intact. The two substrates interlock: guilt-as-physiology is
> what the operation *uses*; the double is what the operation *produces*. This layer
> adds the handler, the mole, the cast-role, and the controlled-leak — the Le Carré
> grammar of *people believing they are protagonists while being operated.*

---

## I. The themes (canonical for this world)

### `the_handler_and_the_cast`
**In the source:** Control and Karla run agents who do not know they are agents. The agent believes they are acting on personal motive; the handler has arranged the motive. The discipline is that the handler never tells the agent the real objective, because the agent who knows the objective performs it wrong.

**In SpaceFace:** the "selector" (`PROTAGONIST.md:131`) is a Concord Intelligence handler. Wren's personal quest — the fragment, Callum, the coordinates, Lida — is real to Wren and *simultaneously* the cover for a mole hunt. Wren was selected because his personal motive (find Lida) is unimprovable cover: a man searching for his sister will go places a Concord operative cannot, ask questions a Concord operative cannot ask, and carry a piece of evidence (the fragment) that the mole has been paid to suppress — all without knowing he is the lure. The handler did not create Wren's motive. The handler *cast* Wren for already having it. This is the Drummer Girl move: the actor is chosen for the truth of their feeling, then placed where the feeling is useful.

**Where it lands:** the entire B-thread. The reveal is staged (see §III) so that the player learns the operation existed only at B7, and then re-reads B0–B6 with the new lens — every "the system filed this" becomes "the operation placed this."

---

### `the_mole_and_the_mirror`
**In the source:** Karla is Smiley's mirror — the brilliant adversary on the other side, who believes the opposite of what Smiley believes and is the more effective for it. Bill Haydon is the mole inside the Circus, charismatic, ideologically convinced, betraying his own service because he believes it deserves to be betrayed.

**In SpaceFace:** two new figures.
- **Brandt** — the Karla mirror. Defected from the same Concord Intelligence class as the handler (Marsh), now runs the Quiet's intelligence cell. Brandt believes Concord is too corrupt to survive and that the Quiet (the gap-fill the system created and refuses to acknowledge) is the only honest actor left, because the Quiet's business *is* the system's true business. Brandt is the adversary who is right about the diagnosis and wrong about the cure.
- **Aldiss** — the mole. A senior Concord Intelligence analyst, planted inside the Reading Room (the Concord Intelligence salon), leaking Vethari-evidence file status to Brandt for fourteen years. Aldiss is not ideologically convinced in the Haydon register. Aldiss is *weary*. Aldiss saw the Vethari evidence, concluded the system would never acknowledge it, and decided the file should be passed to someone who would. The betrayal is the slow, tired, paperwork-grade treachery of a man who lost faith one file at a time.

The player is bait for the mole. The fragment's release into civilian logistics is the controlled leak that will surface the mole's response — because Aldiss, who has kept the Vethari file "UNRESOLVED" for fourteen years, will move to recover or reclassify any fragment that leaves the system, and the move exposes the channel.

**Where it lands:** the entire Thread-A corruption and Thread-B search recontextualize as the bait-and-channel of a mole hunt. The player's career is the operation.

---

### `controlled_leak_and_the_honey_trap`
**In the source:** the controlled leak is intelligence released *deliberately*, in a known channel, to see who acts on it. The honey-trap is the asset cultivated to draw a target out.

**In SpaceFace:** the second Vethari fragment, planted in Wren's hold six months before B0, is a controlled leak. Concord Intelligence (Marsh) released it into civilian logistics *knowing* Aldiss's channel would flag it. The "density mismatch packed where a competent tech would find it" was packed there so Wren would open the crate — because a courier who never opens the crate can't be followed, and a courier who does is a courier the mole will come to believe is a threat or an opportunity. Wren is the honey-trap. The fragment is the leak. Aldiss is the target. The Quiet (Brandt) is the channel.

The sophistication: the leak *also* works as a genuine whistleblower release, which is why Wren's "whistleblower" reading (`PROTAGONIST.md:133`) is so convincing. It was *designed* to read that way — to Wren, to the player, and to Aldiss, who must not realize the leak is controlled until his response has been traced.

**Where it lands:** B0 (the pre-loaded cargo — now readable as the operation's opening move, not just the system's pre-filing); B2 (Elroy's death — now readable as either the operation's collateral or its cover, since Elroy's report is *also* a thread that leads to the mole); B5 (Callum's buyer — now the node where the civilian channel and the intelligence channel converge).

---

### `the_salon_and_the_grubby_work`
**In the source:** Le Carré's lifelong contrast: the elegant, library-quiet policy salon where decisions are made in educated voices, vs. the grubby fieldwork (the dead drop, the smudged contacts, the informer paid in drink) that the salon both depends on and refuses to acknowledge.

**In SpaceFace:** **sophistication alongside the seediness.** The corpus is currently 95% grubby (the Pit, the Slow Gray, Kessler's thumb, Quinn's bar). This layer adds the *salon* — the layer where decisions get made in rooms with good air.

- **The Reading Room** — a small Concord Intelligence salon tucked behind the Logistics Oversight offices on Helios Prime. Not a war room. A reading room: bound inquiry reports, a long table, coffee that doesn't taste like an apology, three analysts who speak in the measured cadence of people who have never raised their voices because they have never needed to. The Reading Room is where the mole hunt is authorized. The Reading Room is where Aldiss sits, writing the assessments that keep the Vethari file "UNRESOLVED." The Reading Room is the place where the system's worst outcomes are produced by people who would be offended to be called corrupt.
- **The Routers** — the Quiet's intelligence cell that Brandt runs. The mirror of the Reading Room: also small, also measured, also speaking in educated voices — but the Routers' salon is a converted cargo hold in the Veil Nebula with the best air in the outer sectors, and the Routers' business is the Quiet's actual business (the ATMO TOKEN brokerage, the gap-fill). The Routers are the Quiet's *policy* layer — the layer that decides which sectors breathe and which don't, by deciding which tokens to broker at markup. Brandt runs both the intelligence cell and the policy.

The contrast is the point. The Reading Room and the Routers are the same room on opposite sides of the river. Both are full of intelligent people doing intelligent work that produces the Slow Gray in the lower decks they never visit. The Pit is downstream of both.

**Where it lands:** a new layer of scenes — Helios Reading Room (sophistication), Veil Routers (sophistication-on-the-other-side), both readable as the *decision layer* the existing grubby cast executes without knowing exists. The player who reaches B7 and reads the Kurtz figure's ledger can now see the line that runs *from* the Reading Room *through* Vale's authorizations *through* the eight-node ecology *to* the Pit's air.

---

### `betrayal_as_love_the_human_layer`
**In the source:** Le Carré's deepest insistence: the betrayal is *personal*, always. Haydon's betrayal of the Circus is also his betrayal of Smiley's wife (the affair was the cover *and* the wound). The Drummer Girl's Kathy is in love with the operative she's been cast against. The intelligence layer is always also a love/friendship/loyalty layer, and the damage is done there, not in the policy.

**In SpaceFace:** the handler-mole hunt runs through personal bonds.
- **Marsh and Brandt** were classmates in the same Concord Intelligence cohort. They defected in opposite directions in the same year, over the same piece of evidence (the first Vethari fragment, fourteen years ago — the same fragment Callum later stole and Wren later carried). They have not spoken since. The mole hunt is also Marsh hunting the mirror Marsh became by not defecting. The operation is the letter they never wrote each other.
- **Aldiss** was Marsh's mentor in the Reading Room. Aldiss taught Marsh to write the assessments. Marsh learned the form well enough to recognize, twelve years later, that Aldiss's last fourteen assessments had been written to keep a file unresolved. The mole hunt is the student hunting the teacher. Aldiss knows Marsh is hunting. Aldiss has not run, because running would confirm what the file already says, and Aldiss is too tired to confirm anything.
- **Wren** is the human layer for the player. Wren was not cast against his will. Wren was cast *for* his love (Lida) — the handler used the truest thing about Wren as the operational lever. The player, at B7, has to decide how to feel about having been operated through their character's deepest wound.

**Where it lands:** the personal layer is what makes the intelligence layer land. The Cold War between Marsh and Brandt is also a friendship that ended over evidence neither could publish. The mole hunt is also a student finding the teacher. The fragment is also the reason the player's whole quest exists.

## II. The two new characters and two new groups

### Characters (sheets: `sheets/characters/marsh.md`, `sheets/characters/brandt.md`)

- **Marsh** — Concord Intelligence handler, Reading Room. The "selector." Senior, controlled, has never raised their voice in the line of duty because the Reading Room doesn't raise voices. Marsh authorized the controlled leak that put the second fragment in Wren's hold. Marsh is running the mole hunt against Aldiss. Marsh was Brandt's classmate and Aldiss's student. Marsh does not appear on-screen until late (see §III) — the operation runs through comms popups, reference codes, and the careful absence of any name on the things that are clearly being done by someone. *(Marsh has no gender pronoun in canon — same discipline as Vale. "Marsh" is a surname. The handler is a signature on an authorization the player has to look very hard to find.)*

- **Brandt** — defected to the Quiet, runs the Routers cell in the Veil Nebula. The Karla mirror. Brandt believes the Quiet is the only honest actor because the Quiet's business *is* the system's true business. Brandt receives the mole's file-status updates and uses them to keep the Vethari evidence suppressed for the Quiet's purposes (the Quiet's ATMO TOKEN brokerage profits when sectors fail; acknowledging alien life would destabilize the allocation system the brokerage feeds on). Brandt was Marsh's classmate. They defected in opposite directions over the same fragment.

### Groups (sheets: `sheets/groups/reading-room.md`, `sheets/groups/routers.md`)

- **The Reading Room** — Concord Intelligence salon, Helios Prime, behind Logistics Oversight. ~3 analysts. The decision layer. Bound inquiry reports, a long table, good coffee. Where the mole hunt is authorized. Where Aldiss sits.
- **The Routers** — Quiet intelligence cell, Veil Nebula, Brandt's operation. The mirror salon. Also ~3 analysts. Also measured voices. The Quiet's policy layer — decides which tokens to broker, which means which sectors breathe. Brandt's mole channel terminates here.

(Plus one **implanted NPC**, no new sheet needed — **Aldiss**, a name attached to an existing ambient role: the senior Reading Room analyst who has been writing the Vethari "UNRESOLVED" assessments for fourteen years. Aldiss is the mole. Aldiss is documented inside the Reading Room group sheet and the Marsh/Brandt sheets, not as a standalone character sheet, because Aldiss never appears on-screen — Aldiss is the file the operation is closing.)

## III. The Drummer Girl thread — staged reveal

The thread is designed so that the player cannot perceive it on a first playthrough and cannot unsee it on a second. Every beat from B0 to B6 has a "civilian" reading (what the player sees) and an "operation" reading (what the handler was doing). The two readings coexist; neither is false.

| Beat | Civilian reading (Thread A/B) | Operation reading (Marsh's mole hunt) |
|------|-------------------------------|---------------------------------------|
| **B0** | Cargo pre-loaded; player already in the ledger; crime-before-the-criminal | Controlled leak deployed. Fragment enters civilian logistics through a channel Aldiss's mole-network is paid to monitor. The "transponder ID the ship had before" is the channel. |
| **B1** | Manifest re-categorizes; the system's first mercy | The re-categorization is the mole's first response — Aldiss's network reclassified the cargo to flag it for retrieval. The graffiti `REDISTRIBUTED TO THE HIGHEST BIDDER` is civilian testimony that the operation reads as confirmation the channel is live. |
| **B2** | Elroy killed; filed as bounty collected | Elroy's report is *also* a thread to the mole (it questions the year-3 transfer). The double-bill through Rook's booth — "one client was Vale's intermediary" — now has a third client the player never saw: the operation, which let the kill happen because Elroy alive was a competing thread. The cruelty is operational, not just systemic. |
| **B3** | Comms popup "your recent work has been noted" | The popup is the operation's first acknowledgment that the bait is working. "Noted" = the mole took it. The popup is not from Vale — it's from the Reading Room, forwarded *through* Vale's relay to look like Vale. |
| **B4** | Three doors, one administrator | The branch choice is also the operation's channel-selection: which faction the player runs through determines which intelligence surface the mole has to use to approach the fragment. All three doors are monitored. |
| **B5** | Callum, the data chip, the unpunished man | Callum's "man who paid well and didn't give a name" — the buyer of the first fragment, fourteen years ago — was Brandt, via the Quiet. The chip Callum gives Wren traces to the same REF 44-C facility because the facility *is* the channel Aldiss uses. Callum has been an unwitting node in the mole network for fourteen years. |
| **B6** | Player's own graffiti; the Slow Gray; Phase 3 | The settlement offer (`D. VALE / ADMIN / PRIORITY`) is now readable as the operation trying to formalize Wren's status before B7 — because the operation needs Wren to reach Ashfall with the fragment, and formal status keeps the routing clean. |
| **B7** | The Kurtz figure; the ledger; the choices | **The reveal.** The Kurtz figure's ledger contains a page the player can find: a fourteen-year correspondence in Concord Intelligence internal format, between the Reading Room and the Routers, routed through a dead-drop in the ATMO TOKEN brokerage. The Kurtz figure was the dead-drop. The Kurtz figure went off-grid eleven years ago because *they realized they were the channel* and could not live as the channel. The "wrong mercy" (Dosto layer) was the Kurtz figure's refusal to keep being the conduit. The mole is named in the ledger: ALDISS. The handler is named: MARSH. The player's whole career was the operation. |

The reveal at B7 is **not a cutscene**. The reveal is a page in a ledger. The page sits there. The player reads it or doesn't. The game does not point to it. The five endgame Choices now each carry an additional Le Carré weight: the player who knows about the operation can choose to (A) join Concord knowing it ran them, (B) join the Quiet knowing Brandt was the buyer, (C) loop knowing the loop serves the operation, (D) take the ledger knowing the ledger is the operation's file, (E) take the next contract knowing the operation will re-run with a new bait. None of the choices lets the player confront the operation. The operation is the system. You can't arrest the system.

## IV. How chapters carry the layer (without naming it)

Same discipline as the Dosto layer: **enacted, never announced.** No chapter says "this is an intelligence operation." The chapter shows:
- A **comms popup with a forwarding chain that doesn't quite make sense** (the popup routed through a station that has no reason to be on the chain).
- A **reference code that appears on a document it shouldn't** (the mole's signature on the wrong file).
- A **graffiti line that knows about a transaction the player hasn't made** (civilian testimony that the operation reads as channel-confirmation).
- A **page in a ledger, at B7, in Concord Intelligence internal format** (the reveal, sitting there).

The sophistication scenes (Reading Room, Routers) appear as **optional discoverable content**, never as mandatory cutscenes — the player who finds them earns the second reading; the player who doesn't has the civilian reading intact. This is the Le Carré contract with the reader: the second reading is the reward for attention, and the first reading is never invalidated.

## V. What this layer deliberately does NOT do

- **Does not make Wren a secret agent.** Wren never knows he is bait until B7 (if he reads the ledger), and even then the knowledge doesn't give him agency over the operation. Wren was always a civilian. The operation used his civilian status. That's the wound.
- **Does not let the player "win" the intelligence game.** The mole hunt's resolution is not the player's to force. Aldiss is closed (or not) by Marsh, off-screen, regardless of the player's choice. The player is the bait, not the hunter.
- **Does not invalidate the civilian reading.** Every beat works on first playthrough without the operation layer. The Le Carré layer is the *reward* for the player who replays or reads closely, not a tax on the player who doesn't.
- **Does not name Le Carré in body prose.** Only this scaffolding doc carries the name and the character references (Smiley, Karla, Haydon, Control, Kathy). Chapters and sheets cite this doc by theme id.
- **Does not displace the Dosto layer.** The two interlock: the Dosto `crime_without_punishment` is *how the operation keeps its bait functional* (a bait who collapses is a bait who stops moving); the Dosto `the_double` is *what the operation produces* (the player's pre-existing filing is also the handler's cast-sheet). The layers are substrates, not competitors.
- **Does not soften the corpus.** The salon has good coffee and bound inquiry reports and three analysts who have never raised their voices — and the Slow Gray still kills forty thousand people downstream of their measured, educated decisions. The sophistication is not a reprieve from the seediness. The sophistication *is* the seediness, one floor up, in better air.
