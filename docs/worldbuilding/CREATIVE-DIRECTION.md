*The showrunner's bible. Written in one night by the new creative director, after reading every
file in this folder and every line the player actually sees. This document is the authority for
voice, casting, canon arithmetic, and the production pipeline. It does not replace
`vibe/vibe-CANONICAL.md` (the house voice), `LITERARY-AUDIT.md` (the protected lines), or
`stylistic-influences/` (the measurement shelf). It sits above them and tells the team how to use

## 1. The thesis

Every story this game tells runs on one sentence:

> **The paperwork was correct, and the air is still gone.**

Not "institutions are bad" — that is the theme-tag version, and it fails the Patterson filter on
sight. The thesis is narrower and worse: every document in this world is accurate, every signature
is legal, every filing is procedurally correct, and forty thousand people are breathing chalk
anyway. The horror is administrative. The truth survives only in records no institution keeps:
graffiti, a dead man's ledger, a 3.1-kilo crate at the bottom of a manifest.

If a beat cannot be traced back to that sentence, it is not a SpaceFace beat.

---

## 2. The voice

The house voice is not a blend of the eight influences. Blending is how you get beige. The house
voice is a **casting**: each influence owns a register, a zone, a set of characters. The synthesis
is structural — a collision machine built out of paperwork, narrated in short sentences, set in an
economy of air, with silence at the bottom and home at the far edge.

Ten rules, in order of how often they get broken:

1. **End on the number.** The hard fact is the last sentence. The soft abstraction after it is the
   enemy. `It weighs 0.01 tonnes.` Full stop. The reader decides what it weighs.
2. **If it sounds like writing, rewrite it.** No sentence may be poster-ready. No character
   narrates their own emotional state. No one says what the scene just showed.
3. **The joke and the confession are the same sentence.** `The previous owner defined 'fine' as
   'hasn't killed anyone yet,' which is also how I define most of my relationships.` That is the
   only kind of funny we make. Every joke about death is also a confession.
4. **Documents re-mean.** An early clean line is re-read as operational after a later fact, with no
   tutorial sentence and no invalidation of the first reading. That is the only twist mechanism we
   use. We do not do reveals. We do re-filings.
5. **Nobody is the villain.** The machine runs on honest offices: the priest who does not look in
   the hold, the director who initials the recommendation, the dockmaster who stamps the chit.
   Damage is done by people who would be offended to be called corrupt. Do not give anyone a
   mustache.
6. **Guilt is physiological.** The file says closed; the body disagrees. Tremor, flinch, taste,
   sleep. Never a confession scene, never therapy voice, never the words "I feel guilty."
7. **Scarcity has a beneficiary every quarter.** For every collapse, ask: who booked the gain this
   cycle? If the answer is "no one" or "the system," the beat is not finished. There is a desk.
   There is always a desk.
8. **Everyone thinks they are the protagonist, and everyone is wrong.** Every recurring crook has a
   private caper they believe is the real story. The laugh and the pity live in their wrongness
   about which game they are in.
9. **The edge is home.** The farthest station smells like the Pit. There is no exotic exit, no
   outside, no alien wonder. Travel changes the paint, not the room.
10. **The Vethari are always off-frame.** No flashback shows them. No cutscene renders them. They
    exist as a shadow on a field, a sound through a hull, dried filaments on a fragment, a suicide
    performed as arithmetic. The moment we show one, we lose them.

## 3. The eight registers

The influences are not blended; they are **zoned**. Each owns territory. Crossing a zone border
without a contrast cut is a fireable offense.

| Register | Owns | Zone law |
|---|---|---|
| **The paperwork tradecraft** (le Carré) | Thread B, Marsh/Brandt/the salons, every document that re-means | Delayed re-meaning only. No omniscient spy. Betrayal is always personal. |
| **The terminal register** (McCarthy) | The Vethari, the crawlspace, Elroy's flicker, the endings | Airless. No jokes, no caption, no sermon. End on the number. Silence must cost the character, not the author. |
| **The shop floor** (Leonard) | The eight-node ecology, the Pit, bars, docks, card tables | People talk. Wit is residual under pressure, never quota. Gesture builds character (a thumb on a seal, a second count under UV). |
| **The balance sheet** (Bacigalupi) | The Silt economy, MTS, ATMO DEBT, the commodity layer | Scarcity is written through price, body, or denial — never monologue. Every collapse has a recurring beneficiary with a name. |
| **The physiology of guilt** (Dostoevsky) | The player's body, Quinn's second ledger, the doubles, punishment refiled | The system steals the collapse that should follow the crime. The double is a staffed future, not a clone twist. |
| **The edge** (Conrad) | Ashfall Reach, the Witness, the journey outward | The far place smells like the start. Discovery is recognition. Ideals are downstream of cargo. |
| **The honest office** (Greene) | Hale, Vale, Inspector Dree, every decent face of the machine | Integrity runs the machine. No conversion scenes, no corruption monologues, no mustaches. |
| **The collision engine** (Ritchie) | Structure, not dialogue: the eight struts, the object path, the convergence | Several small plan-engines forced into one room by a shared clock. The object follows the price path. One scheme must succeed too well. |

The two registers that must never share a paragraph: **terminal** and **shop floor**. A Ritchie
strut's plan may fail upward *into* a Vethari beat. No one may quip *through* a Vethari beat.

## 4. The casting

Each recurring character carries exactly one dominant register. This is the bold direction: not an
amalgam, a commitment. When you write a character, you write their register and no other.

**Wren (protagonist).** Cast as the handler's asset: recruited through the truest thing he feels,
and the truest thing is Lida. His surface voice is the card table — fast, precise, calibrating,
never charming on purpose. His silence is the maintenance shaft: sound meant detection. Three
registers (comfortable / working / cornered), already canon in `story/PROTAGONIST.md`. He never
monologues. The past comes out as details that cost him nothing: a flinch at a frequency, reading
air by taste. He is *Lord Jim* if Jim never told anyone — the guilt is the story he does not tell
about the day he was twelve.

**Director Vale (antagonist).** The honest office, one floor up. The algorithm recommends; Vale
initials. No face, no first name, no scene. Vale's power is that Vale determines which log is
official. Vale is never given dialogue beyond one line ("Good work. Keep it clean."), and that
line is the exact sentence the Pit's wardens used at quota. That is the whole performance.

**Hale.** The priest of the honest office. Protected — do not touch, do not deepen, do not give
him a crisis. He is built from what he declines to do. Any scene where Hale "realizes" something
is a scene that gets its author reassigned.

**The eight-node ecology (Kessler, Voss, Mira, Rook, Slate, Drift, Quinn, + Hale as anchor).**
The collision ensemble. Each has a strut — a private caper they think is the real story — and each
is a node in a machine none of them can see. Kessler: residual duty, saves anyway, the number is
the prayer. Quinn: the underground man's spite ledger; he thinks the player is a mark in his long
game. Drift: the honest man and his one bounded dishonesty, compounding for sixteen years. Rook:
the referee fantasy; his tally is a confession in installments. Slate: patience as pathology; he
knows how every hull in the sector will break. Voss: the map obsession; the sealed shafts are his
retirement and one of them is Shaft 7. Mira: the ticket bought but unclaimed; the suspicion is the
cage. Their full voices live in `story/NPCs-CANONICAL.md` and `story/SHOP-FLOOR-CAPERS.md`.

**Marsh (the selector/handler).** The handler who casts for existing motive. The evidence points
one way and Wren trusts evidence over paranoia; that trust is the leash. Marsh never appears
plainly. Marsh's register is the forwarding chain.

**Brandt.** The defector mirror. Suppresses not from malice but to protect the Quiet's ATMO TOKEN
brokerage — the adversary who is right about the diagnosis. Cast against Marsh: two services, one
method.

**The Witness of Ashfall (formerly "the Kurtz figure").** The player's staffed future — the
possible self, kept by the ledger. **Ruling 6 renames him in voice; the entity id `npc_kurtz`
stays as plumbing.** In prose and player-facing text he is "the Witness" or "the Ashfall Witness."
His mutter is the only speech in the game permitted to be unhinged, because it is a filing system
talking to itself.

**Elroy.** The crime engine inverted: the system stole the punishment from the people his report
was right about, and billed him for the postage. His report was wrong on exactly one detail — the
authorization existed, one floor up — and that detail is what made it mortal. Innocent harm,
filed correctly.

**Inspector Sula Dree** *(new — see §9).* The machine's decent face, and the one the player can
actually talk to. She processed Wren's arrest nine years ago as a junior sweep officer; she has
since been promoted into REF 44-C country and believes the file every time. Her decency is not a
disguise. That is the horror.

**Lien (MTS declining-sectors desk)** *(new).* The recurring beneficiary made nameable. Not a
villain: a desk officer whose fund holds the short position on the Pit's air, whose quarterly is
defended by the Pit's recyclers never being fixed. Lien also *sells the Pit its emergency Silt at
markup.* The man who profits when you cannot breathe is the same man who sells you the breath.

**Pell (Special Custody)** *(new).* The head of the sub-office whose budget exists because the
Vethari secret exists. Faceless by Vale's discipline: a title and a renewal date. Pell's whole
career is keeping the file at UNRESOLVED — because INERT closes the office and ACTIVE HAZARD would
require telling someone. The creditor of the cosmic horror.

**Wex (Helios Bay 7 depot manager)** *(new).* The punchline that is also the indictment: the man
who shelved a 12.4-tonne catalyst grid by Thursday because Helios already had a better one, and
who filed the lounge smell ticket. Not stupid. Not cruel. Just three rungs up and Thursday.

**Spence** *(new).* Outpost 9's other fixture. The only living soul who knows Quinn's second
ledger exists — he watched Quinn build the compartment under the till eleven years ago. He has
never said a word. The not-saying is the friendship. He is the shop floor's silence, and he is
where the player learns what Quinn's arithmetic is actually for — never from Spence's mouth.

**Ivo** *(new).* Pit maintenance, third shift. The hand that paints over the graffiti. Possibly —
never confirmed, never denied — one of the hands that writes it. His sheet presents the evidence
both ways and resolves nothing. The wall is a leaked database; whether Ivo is the leak or its
janitor is the question the game refuses.

**Lida.** Off-frame. Forever. She exists as a nine-year-old who drew university buildings, a set of
coordinates that match nothing on file, and a weight that never leaves the manifest. No rescue, no
corpse, no answer.

**The Vethari.** The terminal register as a species. The ledger-civilization whose punishment loop
is the system's mirror at species scale. Always off-frame. Their one permitted act on record is a
suicide performed as arithmetic, and it is protected text.

## 5. The comedy law

The joke budget is set by the stakes of the room.

1. **The shop floor breathes.** Bars, docks, scales, weld bays, card tables: dry wit, fast and
   confessional, is not only allowed — its absence is a defect. A Pit worker who never gets to be
   funny about suffocating isn't stoic; he's unwritten.
2. **The terminal zones are airless.** The Vethari, the crawlspace, the silo, Elroy's flicker, the
   Witness's ledger room, the endings. No jokes. Not one. The absence of wit there is the craft.
3. **Contrast cuts, never blends.** The game's signature move is the hard cut from the shop floor
   to the terminal register: the laugh that ends in the smell ticket. Forty thousand people went
   gray and the only record at the destination is a complaint that the new lounge air smelled of
   machine oil for a week. The horror is twice as heavy wearing the clown's nose — but the nose
   and the horror are never in the same sentence.
4. **No joke quotas.** Humor is generated by situation, object path, and a character's wrongness
   about which game they're in. If a scene needs a joke inserted, the scene's structure is wrong.
5. **Every joke about death is also a confession.** The kill feed gag, the reactor joke, the
   after-action deadpan: each one indicts the speaker. If it's only funny, cut it.
6. **Epic and funny are the same budget.** The contrast between the strut and the silo is where
   the game's tone lives. Protect both halves or lose the whole.

## 6. Canon rulings (binding)

These settle every contradiction found in the corpus tonight. They are law. Files that disagree
are bugs to be fixed, not variants to be honored.

**R1 — The master timeline.**

| When | Event |
|---|---|
| Year 3 (14 years before B0) | The Pit's recycler catalyst grid (12.4t) leaves legally under `VALE-ALA-47A`; Slate welds the Shaft 7 hopper shut (paid via three cutouts, told it was mold remediation); the REF 44-C investigation opens on the first fragment; Brandt defects to the Quiet; the Pit's air begins to die. |
| 9 years before B0 | The vault job. Callum sells the first fragment into Brandt's channel; it is returned to custody (the closed loop). Wren is arrested at 22, ships on penal transport **CPV-2214** ("the Can"), escapes on day 43, is frozen ~4 years. |
| 5 years before B0 | Wren is revived at 26, receives the Tessera, begins working alone. |
| 6 months before B0 | Marsh's channel plants the **second fragment (3.1 KG)** in Wren's hold via the 15%-above-market job. The fragment has been in the hold **six months — not five years.** |
| 6 weeks before B0 | Elroy files his report: the Shaft 7 hopper empty, "decommissioned without authorization." True on the Pit's local records; false one floor up, where `VALE-ALA-47A` sits under REF 44-C above his clearance. That error is his death sentence. |
| B0 | Contract 47-A. B2: Elroy dies, double-billed through Rook's booth. |

Wren is 31 (awake 27, frozen 4). In-sector 19 years: arrived at 12. Lida was 9; she would be 28.
The investigation is 14 years old and has never been closed. The reunion line is
**"It has been nine years"** — not seven, not fourteen. Fourteen belongs to the grid, the
investigation, and the defection. Nine belongs to the vault, the sale, and the arrest.

**R2 — The codes.** `REG 44-C` is the customs/logistics **regulation**: Hale's double fine, the
seals, the Concord Logistics License. `REF 44-C` is the **case-file reference**: evidence custody,
sealed investigations, Vale's authorization chain. The near-identical codes are the deliberate
tell — one apparatus, two prefixes, and the player who notices has learned to read. No file may
conflate them, and no file may *explain* them. The split is shown, never taught.

**R3 — Kessler's numbers.** Kessler has been on the scale **nineteen years**. The accuser's wall
says **TWENTY-TWO MONTHS. LOOK IT UP.** Both stand: twenty-two months is the window the accuser
could prove. The gap between the provable window and the actual career is the testimony form's
whole point. The wall's numbers are evidence, not omniscience.

**R4 — The Tycho variance review.** Under review for **fourteen cycles**. Total missing:
**22 tonnes. 12.4t of it in cycle 1.** The number twenty-two belongs to the tonnes, not the
cycles. (The review predates the player's run; Contract 47-A is merely its newest entry.)

**R5 — Fees and air.** The Pit's berth fees have been collected for **nineteen years**. The air
has been poison for **fourteen**. The two numbers may share a paragraph but never a clause: the
fees are older than the crime they failed to prevent.

**R6 — The Witness.** The Ashfall ledger-keeper is "the Witness" / "the Ashfall Witness" in all
prose and player-facing text. The Conrad name survives only as the entity id `npc_kurtz`
(plumbing, like the `chapter-01-CANONICAL.md` filename) and in fenced design notes. Same ruling
retires "Marlow" from `sheets/worlds/s9-ashfall.md`.

**R7 — The loop.** Choice C is the loop, everywhere, with no residual death language: the jump
completes; the player emerges at the Pit's docking bay; the graffiti is the same; the contract is
still pending. The wormhole is not an exit. Death is an exit, and the system does not grant exits.
`SYMBOLISM-MOTIFS.md`'s "This is the player's own death" and "not coming back through an airlock"
and `ENDGAME-B7-REDESIGN.md`'s "the book closing itself" are the last three mercy-leaks; they die
tonight. The kill feed's `+1 UNKNOWN` at the loop's close is **an entry with no name, and the
count continues** — not a death certificate.

**R8 — Vale sighting order.** B0 authorization line = first. B3 comms popup = second. B4
"V. DIRECTOR, ACTING" clearing-station field = third. B5 `VALE HOLDINGS LLC` registry = fourth.
B6 settlement message = fifth. Chapters count accordingly, with no "if the player counted" hedges.

**R9 — The Free Captains door.** The B4 "Free Captains" contract board door is the **Free
Frontier**'s front — a brokers' compact of independent captains with no customs arm of its own, so
the Quiet vets their freight (Mira processes their runs) and the Reach recruits their bankrupt
graduates (hence "Reach-adjacent"). Three answers, one truth. This simultaneously resolves the B4
contradiction and gives the live `faction_free` its canon: the Free Frontier is the sector's
gig-economy hall, and its tragedy is that it is the only faction whose members all believe they
are temporary.

**R10 — The orphan five.** Five live factions have no canon: the Understory, the Archive, the
Verge-Layers, the Pitborn, the Fulfillment. Provisional framing (to be developed in the expansion,
marked PROVISIONAL): the **Verge-Layers** are ruled *not* a Vale superpower — the "observer
lattice" is what the Reading Room's channel looks like when the HUD starts trusting you; Vale's
power remains the filing, never magic protocol. The other four get one-line canon sketches in
`orgs/factions-CANONICAL.md` and remain open for development.

**R11 — Elroy's cargo.** The medicine was real: recycler-catalyst reagents and inoculant stock
consigned to the Pit's lower decks, misfiled as contraband by the same refile that killed him. The
kill feed's second line — *They were carrying medicine* — is the refile catching up with the
cargo. This is why the line matters.

**R12 — The grid's dates, one object.** Year 3: paper transfer (`VALE-ALA-47A`), the Shaft 7
hopper emptied and welded shut. The grid racks at Helios Bay 7; install abandoned after a week;
shelved fourteen years. Six weeks before B0: Elroy finds the Pit's hopper empty and files his
report. B0: the grid is issued out of Bay 7 consigned home to the Pit as "TITANIUM ALLOY"; the
player hauls it; it is skimmed en route; it never arrives. Four events, one grid, and the
paperwork was correct at every step.

**R13 — The fragment's custody.** The first fragment: recovered 14 years ago (investigation
opened), vault-stored, stolen 9 years ago, sold to Brandt's channel, returned to custody — the
closed loop. The second fragment: held in long-term stowage (Pell's line item), planted in Wren's
hold 6 months ago by Marsh's channel. The player's crate: `UNCLASSIFIED COMPOSITE — 3.1 KG`,
`PERSONAL EFFECTS — DO NOT TRANSFER`. The manifest string is exact; prose that rounds to "3
kilograms" is a bug.

**R14 — The B0 route.** Contract 47-A runs **Helios → the Pit**, exactly as
`chapter-00-cold-start.md` and `chapter-01-CANONICAL.md` say: pre-flight at Helios (the crate
pre-loaded out of the Bay 7 warehouse), arrival at the Pit (mass zero, payment withheld,
`THEY KNEW THE MASS.` on the Pit airlock). The grid's full path: **year 3** it leaves the Pit for
Helios (paper transfer `VALE-ALA-47A`), is racked at Bay 7, install attempted and abandoned after
one week (the smell ticket) because Helios already had a better one; **year 17 (B0)** it is issued
as `TITANIUM ALLOY` consigned *home* to the Pit — a repatriation somebody authorized — and is
skimmed in transit (the escorts, the cargo drone, the unknown vessel). It never arrives. Its
current whereabouts are unfiled. The empty Bay 7 rack the player finds later reads
`RECEIVED YEAR 3 / INSTALL PENDING / ISSUED YEAR 17` — the issue week is the week the player
hauled it. **The runtime currently docks B0 at Helios (`narrative.js`, `campaignData.js`) and the
spine overlay agrees with it: both are the outliers.** The two chapter files and the dockmaster
logic are canon. Runtime alignment is logged for the story-wire program; it is a mission-data
change, not a copy change.

**R15 — The protagonist's runtime backstory.** Canon: Wren, the prison transport, four years
frozen, the Tessera settling a debt. Runtime's "KAEL," the "galactic prison colony" six-month
stint, and the named prior crew of the Tessera are superseded. The Tessera's prior owners are
**deliberately unnamed** — a palimpsest of names nobody remembers. Player-facing text is rewritten
to canon in the same pass that fixes everything else.

## 7. The kill list

The second writer gets fired tonight, per file, in the fix passes. So the team recognizes him,
these are his tells — every one drawn from the corpus:

1. **"This is the point."** Any variant. "The absence of comeuppance is the point." "That's the
   point." Ration: zero. The scene is the point or it isn't.
2. **The soft abstraction after the hard number.** "It weighs 0.01 tonnes. It might weigh
   everything." The second sentence is always the wound. End on the number.
3. **The author caption.** "This is the Dosto inversion the whole game is built on." "The humor is
   Bacigalupi." If the material needs its patron named, it hasn't achieved him. Cut the caption,
   keep the material.
4. **The self-exegesis.** Delivering a reserved line and then annotating it: "*I was carrying
   it.* Three words. The 'it' is the killing and the cargo and the air—" No. The reader keeps the
   ambiguity; that is what they are for.
5. **The writers-room with the door open.** "the next thirty hours," "highest-leverage single UI
   moment in the game," beat numbers in body prose, "load-bearing." Design talk lives in fenced
   `DESIGN RATIONALE` blocks at the foot of the file, or nowhere.
6. **The borrowed bit.** Near-verbatim comedian material with the name filed off. We do not cover
   other people's songs.
7. **The maxim wall.** Graffiti is testimony: fact, name, number, cut off. "KESSLER. SCALE 4.
   TYCHO. TWENTY-TWO MONTHS. LOOK IT UP." — yes. "Clean means no witnesses left." — no. When the
   wall reaches for theme, convert it back to fact.
8. **The duplicated couplet.** "[X] is always the same. Only the [Y] changes" gets one canonical
   home per formulation. Everywhere else, write the specific version: what is the Pit, in *this*
   sector, actually made of.
9. **The inspirational speech.** "Make something of it." "We can do this." No one in this world is
   performing for an audience. The contracts don't care who you are.
10. **The poster line.** If a sentence would work on a loading screen or a hoodie, it goes.

The full clinical diagnosis lives in `LITERARY-AUDIT.md` §III. The anti-pattern shelf lives in
`review/author-packets/anti-patterns.md`. Both apply.

## 8. Protected lines

`LITERARY-AUDIT.md` §I is incorporated by reference and is absolute. The boring artifact. The
priest who does not look in the hold. "It fit two children if neither of them moved." The Vethari
suicide as arithmetic — including the "Only the X. Only the Y." cadence, which is load-bearing
there and only there. "Everything tastes like the apology that wasn't made." The eight tells. The
clean contracts. The reactor joke. The Callum reunion dialogue. "Paranoia is free and evidence
costs something." The kill feed that only counts to one. The graffiti as leaked database.

These lines are load-bearing. A revision that "improves" them is the failure mode this whole
program exists to prevent. Distinctive is fragile. Do not polish the cold lines.

## 9. The expansion program

The corpus tonight: ~150k words of creative stack, 67 entity sheets, B0–B7 authored, five endings,
a player route that is partially wired. The program grows it — never by lore islands, always by
reweighting what exists. Minimum 15% growth in every category, measured in the closing report.

**Characters: 17 → 23.** New, fully cast (§4): **Lien** (the beneficiary), **Pell** (the
creditor), **Wex** (the punchline), **Inspector Sula Dree** (the decent face), **Spence** (the
silence), **Ivo** (the hand). Each gets a sheet, a voice law, and a reason to exist that traces to
the thesis.

**Factions: 8 → 9 canon + 5 provisional.** The **Free Frontier** gets its canon (R9) and a sheet.
The orphan five get provisional sketches. The Reach's thin sheet is rebuilt.

**Places: 11 worlds → +4 station sheets.** New station sheet type: **Outpost 9** (Quinn's bar),
**Gate 3** (Hale's checkpoint), **the clearing station** (B4's hub), **Helios Bay 7** (Wex's
depot). Stations are where the game actually happens; they rate their own sheets.

**Ships: 3 → 5.** The **Variance Adjustment** (the palimpsest hull from B3) and **CPV-2214, "the
Can"** (the prison transport) get sheets.

**Story: new documents.**
- `story/CONTEMPORARY-HISTORY.md` — the universe right now: what every faction, desk, and crook is
  doing *this cycle*, fourteen years into the Slow Gray. The present tense the storyline walks
  through.
- `story/SIDE-STORIES.md` — the collision engine formalized: the cross-cutting threads (the
  unknown vessel, the graffiti hand, the re-routed crate, the second client, the smell ticket, the
  drawer) specced as side stories that cross B0–B7, each with its mission derivation.
- `production/CUTSCENE-SCRIPTS.md` — eight cutscenes written as video-generation prompts: shot
  lists, lens and light law, artifact text on screen, audio. The Vethari stay off-frame in all of
  them. That is law, not preference.
- `production/ART-PROMPTS.md` — image-generation prompts: character headshots, places, ships,
  artifacts, multi-angle turnarounds, all in the house's visual register (body-cam, 60% spectrum,
  interrogation-bright or ambush-dark, no clean hero shots).
- `STORY-PIPELINE.md` — the pipeline itself (§10).

**Chapters: B0–B7 fixed and deepened.** Every second-writer line in tonight's audit reports gets
its surgery. Every expansion opportunity that reweights existing beats gets written: Elroy's
report as a findable artifact, the drawer's mechanical readings, the Vale sightings as a silent
collectible, the futures ticker pricing the player's violence, Slate's Shaft-7 job, the 14-cycles
message in Wren's own syntax.

**Player-facing: aligned to canon.** The runtime is the truth the player gets; tonight it
diverges from canon in twenty places (the audit's list). The fix pass brings `narrative.js`, the
endings, the barks, the flavor packs, and the splash into canon — Kessler's wall, the epilogue's
loop, Vale's one line firing for everyone, the witness renamed, the splash's marketing voice
replaced by registry lines. All fixes verified with the owning checks.

## 10. The pipeline

The story stack, in the order of authority and production:

1. **Worldbuilding** (`vibe/`, `orgs/`, `contracts/`, `story/ATMOSPHERIC-ECONOMY.md`,
   `story/SECTOR-GRADIENT.md`, `sheets/`): what the world *is*. Economy first — the Silt, the
   tokens, the desks — because the thesis is economic.
2. **Contemporary history** (`story/CONTEMPORARY-HISTORY.md`): what the world is *doing* — the
   present-tense pressures of the current cycle. This is new tonight; it was the missing layer
   between the setting and the plot.
3. **The storyline** (`story/STORY-STRUCTURE.md`, `STORY-SPINE-NARRATIVE-OVERLAY.md`,
   `chapter-*.md`, `ENDGAME-B7-REDESIGN.md`, `THE-WORLD-AFTER.md`): the dual-thread novel —
   Thread A happens to everyone; Thread B happens to Wren; the side stories cross through both
   (`story/SIDE-STORIES.md`).
4. **Missions** (`src/story/campaign47a/`, `src/data/missions.js`, plus the derivations in
   `story/SIDE-STORIES.md`): missions are *derived* from the storyline. A mission that cannot name
   its chapter beat and its side-story thread does not get built.
5. **Cutscenes & art** (`production/`): derived from the storyline, specced as generation prompts,
   constrained by the register law (off-frame Vethari; terminal zones airless; contrast cuts).
6. **Player-facing text** (`src/data/narrative.js`, barks, flavor, endings, HUD): the last mile.
   The audit protocol (`stylistic-influences/AUDIT-PROTOCOL.md`) runs on every string. Comms stay
   under twelve words unless the surface is long-form. Graffiti stays testimony.

A change enters at the highest layer it belongs to and flows down. Nobody patches a lower layer
that contradicts a higher one; you fix the higher layer or you file the lower one's divergence as
a bug.

## 11. Writing workflow

Every line of new prose passes, in order:

1. **Draft free.** Find what you're saying. Mark uncertainties `[CHECK]`.
2. **Register pass.** Whose zone is this? Does the line obey that zone's law? (§3)
3. **Number pass.** Does it end on the hard fact? Delete every soft abstraction that follows one.
4. **Voice pass.** Cover the speaker's name. Could only this character say it, this way?
5. **Audit pass.** Run the beat through `stylistic-influences/AUDIT-PROTOCOL.md`: surface tags,
   device tests, Patterson filter. Name the *work* you tested against in the PR note — never in
   the string.
6. **Kill-list pass.** §7. Then sleep on it and read it aloud. If it sounds like writing, rewrite
   it.
