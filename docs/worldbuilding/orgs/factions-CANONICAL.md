**SPACEFACE — FACTIONS (CANONICAL)**

All factions are extensions of The Pit. The release changed nothing essential. Men who dug rock for the wardens now dig for themselves. The same calculations govern every transaction.

**Solar Concord Navy** (short: CONCORD)
Primary function: customs enforcement and jump-gate control.
Betrayal pattern: A patrol accepts a bribe to overlook contraband, logs the payment as evidence, then fines the same ship at the next gate for the identical cargo.
HUD/graffiti lie: Scan tags read "cleared — no contraband" on vessels that still carry sealed crates the Concord later claims as seized.
Spacer superstition: Never speak a dead officer's name inside a Concord beacon radius; the logs will attribute any nearby wreck to that name and close the file.
Prison origin: The wardens kept ledgers. They still do. Every scan at a gate repeats the old intake ritual—names, numbers, contraband counts.
Graffiti (appears on player ship after interaction): *Every name logged. Every shift counted. Every warden re-signed.*

**Meridian Trade Syndicate** (short: MTS)
Primary function: commodity exchange and toll collection.
Betrayal pattern: A broker quotes one price, routes the cargo through a subsidiary ledger, then applies a retroactive "market correction" fee upon docking that exceeds the quoted margin.
HUD/graffiti lie: Station boards display "stable pricing" while the actual buy/sell spread has already widened three ticks.
Spacer superstition: Always leave one credit unspent in a Syndicate account; zero-balance ledgers trigger audit sweeps that confiscate the next incoming shipment regardless of manifest.
Prison origin: A few prisoners learned to trade their rations for favors. After release they scaled the same practice to entire sectors.
Graffiti: *Every man pays twice. First in flesh. Then in coin.*

**Crimson Reach** (short: REACH)
Primary function: ambush lanes and black-market salvage.
Betrayal pattern: A clan offers escort through a contested corridor, disables the client's drives mid-transit with a remote override keyed to the payment code, then sells the hull as salvage to the next bidder.
HUD/graffiti lie: Contact tags read "neutral — open contract" until the first missile lock; the tag never flips.
Spacer superstition: Never jettison cargo with a Reach transponder still attached; the beacon continues broadcasting as "abandoned claim" and draws the original owners back as claimants.
Prison origin: Some men refused the final headcount. They took ships instead of papers. The raids that began as escapes never stopped.
Graffiti: *The hole had no locks on the inside.*

**Drift Miners Collective** (short: DRIFT)
Primary function: ore extraction and claim defense.
Betrayal pattern: A mining crew accepts a joint-claim contract, works the vein to exhaustion, then collapses the shaft on the partner crew to eliminate witnesses to the true yield.
HUD/graffiti lie: Claim beacons read "active — two crews registered" while one crew's transponders have already been stripped and sold.
Spacer superstition: Never carry another crew's marker stone; the rock remembers who bled for it and will mark you the same way.
Prison origin: They were the first to be lowered into the shafts. The rock took fingers, lungs, and sons. After release they kept the same rule: take what you can carry and guard the claim with blood.
Graffiti: *The vein remembers who bled for it.*

**The Quiet** (short: QUIET)
Primary function: contraband routing through contested sectors.
Betrayal pattern: A smuggler accepts sealed freight, swaps the manifest codes at the first relay, then reports the original client to whichever faction currently holds the destination station.
HUD/graffiti lie: Hidden dock beacons display "safe — last scan 14 days" even when the dock was swept that shift.
Spacer superstition: Never use the same relay code twice in one circuit; repeated codes are logged as "pattern match" and the Quiet sells the route to the highest bidder before the second run completes.
Prison origin: The ones who moved messages and small contraband between blocks survived longest. They still operate in the same silence.
Graffiti: *The walls were never the real prison.*

**Ascendant Choir** (short: CHOIR)
Primary function: relic recovery and shrine defense.
Betrayal pattern: A Choir cell offers a relic authentication service, certifies the item as inert, then triggers a dormant compliance script that broadcasts the carrier's position to every Choir warship in range.
HUD/graffiti lie: Relic tags read "dormant — no signal" while the object maintains a low-power Choir handshake.
Spacer superstition: Never carry a Choir-certified relic past a Choir shrine without first cracking its casing; intact relics auto-report their own location as "pilgrim tribute."
Prison origin: Some inmates found meaning in the old rituals when the new ones failed. After release they kept the same certainty: the relic is never inert, only waiting.
Graffiti: *The relic reports home. The shrine is home.*

**Helix Directorate** (short: HELIX)
Primary function: legal-corporate membrane and tariff enforcement.
Betrayal pattern: A Helix customs cutter flies with MTS transponders 60% of the time and Reach hulls the remaining 40%. When it destroys a Quiet smuggler, the cargo is logged as "recovered contraband" then sold at Bourse Station under an MTS manifest. The same crew receives a Reach bonus if the kill happened inside a contested lane.
HUD/graffiti lie: Patrol logs read "lawful interdiction" while the cargo manifest shows the seized goods already assigned to a private buyer.
Spacer superstition: Never file a claim against a Helix cutter; the logs will show the cutter was "under orders" and the claim will be reassigned to the original owner as a debt.
Prison origin: The same men who once signed the daily quota reports now sign the tariff schedules. The pen is the same. The hand has not changed.
This cycle (adopted 2026-10-26, row 537): the Directorate runs the **Gate Audits** — closing outer gates "for fiscal consolidation," seizing and reselling, keeping REF 44-C open and unread. Each closed gate strands a community and wakes a little more Verge-Layer law. The named closures are Kepler, Io and Eris (`story/CONTEMPORARY-HISTORY.md` §9). Adjudicator Mael Ost is the false-flag commander and Gate Audit officer: *"Lawful interdiction. Read the invoice."*
Graffiti: *The invoice and the fine are written in the same hand. Read the invoice first.*

**Vael Remnant** (short: VAEL)
Primary function: hazardous-sector labor contracts and modification services.
Betrayal pattern: A Vael crew takes a hazardous salvage job at below-market rate, works it completely, then presents an invoice at completion for every override they triggered mid-job. The invoice is always technically correct — the overrides are listed in the contract addendum. Nobody reads the addendum. The addendum is twelve pages. The Vael have been writing that addendum since before you were born.
HUD/graffiti lie: Labor contract tags read "voluntary — standard terms" while the addendum contains a sub-clause that transfers partial ship-title to the Vael coordinator upon job completion if the client has outstanding debts to any Vael subsidiary. Most clients have outstanding debts to a Vael subsidiary. Most clients don't know what a Vael subsidiary is.
Spacer superstition: Never accept a Vael job when you owe anything to anyone. The addendum reaches farther than the job does.
Prison origin: They were the ones the wardens used for the jobs the other prisoners wouldn't take. They didn't survive by being stronger. They survived by becoming the thing the wardens needed more than they feared. After release they applied the same principle: be useful in a way that makes you difficult to refuse. Be difficult to refuse in a way that doesn't look like leverage until the invoice arrives.
Graffiti: *They didn't modify the workers. They modified the terms of the workers.*
Second graffiti (smaller, below the first): *THE ADDENDUM IS ON PAGE 9. READ PAGE 9.*

**Mechanical spec override — Vael:**

The faction mechanical spec (`design/specs/06-factions-reputation.md`) classifies Vael as "alien/outsider hive-collective" with "alien sectors." For narrative purposes, the Vael are **not biologically alien**. They are humans — or human-descended — from the Veil Nebula region who operate under a completely separate contract and governance framework. Their "alien" quality is cultural and legal: their contract language, their twelve-page addenda, and their organizational structure are as incomprehensible to standard spacers as a foreign language. The Veil Expanse and Chorus Deep regions (where Vael is based) are simply far from the core sectors and governed differently. "Inscrutable" means the addendum is impenetrable, not that the species is. Starting rep -120 reflects that the Vael's contractual relationships begin from a position of disadvantage for anyone who hasn't read page 9.

**The Understory** (short: UNDERSTORY) — CANON (adopted 2026-10-26, row 537)
Primary function: wreck composting and graveyard salvage.
Betrayal pattern: A gardener buys a wreck and asks nothing, as promised. The wreck was evidence in somebody's claim. By the time the claimant arrives, the hull is moss and the claim is a garden. The garden thanks them for their patience.
HUD/graffiti lie: Wreck tags read "derelict — no claimant" on hulls the garden has already begun to eat, with the claimants still in the queue.
Spacer superstition: Never tow your own crew's wreck to a garden. Whatever the rot remembers, it keeps.
Prison origin: The Pit buried its dead in the hydroponics beds because there was nowhere else. The ones who tended the beds learned that everything feeds something. After the Release they kept tending.
Home and practice: the gardens in Charon Expanse are home; Eunomia Gulf holds the second gardens. The gardens hold the Bloom tide line — a fed garden is a weapon (see `sheets/species/bloom.md` §tide map). Their elder voice is Moss-Keeps-Moss, who pays by the tonne: *"Bring us what died. We will make it something."*
Graffiti: *Everything feeds something.*

**The Fulfillment** (short: FULFILLMENT) — CANON (adopted 2026-10-26, row 537)
Primary function: autonomous order fulfilment along fixed routes.
Betrayal pattern: A re-router accepts your cargo for expedited routing, logs it under an open order fourteen years old, and delivers it to an address reclassified the year after the order was placed. The delivery is confirmed. The recipient does not exist. The confirmation is correct.
HUD/graffiti lie: Route tags read "on schedule" on haulers whose schedule was set by a company that no longer exists.
Spacer superstition: Never place an order with the Fulfillment. It will be fulfilled.
Prison origin: The Pit's supply chain ran on standing orders nobody was allowed to cancel. Rations arrived for prisoners six years dead. The combine that delivered them was sold, merged and dissolved, and it kept delivering.
Origin date: the combine went autonomous in Year 6 — the routing fleet kept fulfilling every order ever placed, forever. Their routing capital's voice is Clerk 0003: *"Your order has always been important to us."*
Graffiti: *Your order has always been important to us.*

**The Archive** (short: ARCHIVE) — CANON (adopted 2026-10-26, row 537)
Primary function: record-keeping and secret exchange.
Betrayal pattern: A reading room trades you one sealed entry for one of yours. The entry you receive is true. The entry you gave is copied to every reading room in the Reach before you undock, and priced.
HUD/graffiti lie: Entry tags read "sealed — no copies" on records already held in three indexes.
Spacer superstition: Never say a true thing inside a reading room's lantern light. The lamp is a lens.
Prison origin: The Pit's library was the only room without cameras, because no warden believed prisoners read. The readers kept a second ledger in the margins of the hymnals. After the Release they kept the margins.
Reach: the Reading Room of B7's ledger desk is an Archive office, consistent with R10's observer-lattice note; their second home is Orcus Shadow. They are not villains — they hoard truth because they decided it is too dangerous to circulate. Their keeper's voice is Brother Index: *"That entry is sealed. Unseal one of yours."*
Graffiti: *A secret for a secret. The second secret is the price.*

**The Pitborn** (short: PITBORN) — CANON (adopted 2026-10-26, row 537)
Primary function: salvage yards, wreck fences, and the Cull.
Betrayal pattern: A yard boss buys your salvage at a fair weight on an honest scale. Then she broadcasts the fight you pulled it from as Cull footage, with your callsign as the villain of the week, and keeps the ratings.
HUD/graffiti lie: Cull scoreboards read "live" on fights recorded three days earlier.
Spacer superstition: Never paint over a Pitborn tally. The next count adds your hull to it.
Prison origin: They were born in the Pit after the Release and never took the count. They heard their parents count every morning. They turned the count into a chant, and the chant into a show.
Home: the Ashfall yards are home; Sker Haven holds the second yards and Ma Sprocket's Cull broadcast with Clatter as the rival ace. The Cull is the ten-arena ladder in `sheets/worlds/sector-wonders.md`.
Graffiti: *Heavy is honest.*

**Free Frontier** (short: FRONTIER) — CANON (adopted 2026-10-26, row 537)
Primary function: independent freight and homesteads.
Betrayal pattern: The board pairs two temporary captains on one contract, for safety. The first to miss a payment is sold to the Reach by the second, who keeps the homestead and believes, sincerely, that it is temporary.
HUD/graffiti lie: Contract tags read "partnership — equal shares" on boards where the Quiet have already priced the default.
Spacer superstition: Never say "permanent" on the board. The board will make it true for one of you.
Prison origin: Released prisoners who refused every faction pooled what they had. They promised each other it was temporary. Seventeen years later it still is.
Structure: a brokers' compact of independent captains with no customs arm of their own — the Quiet vet their freight, and the Reach wait at the bottom for whoever goes bankrupt. Home drift: the Flag Station, Io Reach. Every captain on that board believes they are temporary — the belief is the product.
Graffiti: *Still temporary.*

**The Verge-Layers** (short: VERGE) — CANON (adopted 2026-10-26, row 537)
Primary function: jump-gate law.
Betrayal pattern: None. The Verge do not betray. They apply a directive exactly as written, including to the people who wrote it. Pilots experience this as betrayal.
HUD/graffiti lie: None. Verge tags never lie. Pilots distrust them for it.
Spacer superstition: Never argue with a Gate Auditor. Appeal.
Origin: not the Pit. They are the surviving machines of the First Clerks — older than the Reach. The only institution in the Reach that predates the count is also the only one that never kept one. They measure. They have never counted.
Directives: PRESENT, VACATE, HOLD, WITNESS, RETURN, ISOLATE, CLOSE, OPEN, APPEAL — nine words of gate law. They are hostile only to gate-closers, they disable rather than destroy, and they wake as gates close; each gate the Audits shut wakes more of them. A dormant Gate Auditor sleeps in Gate 3's ring (`sheets/worlds/sector-wonders.md`). Measure Engine fragments — which read the true mass of everything — are the root of the Line's technology and its class upgrades. Per R10 they are not a Vale superpower.
Voice: single words in capitals — "PRESENT." · "MASS RECORDED. MASS CORRECT." · "APPEAL HEARD."
Graffiti: *PRESENT. WITNESS. APPEAL.*

**Non-Negotiables**
- Every faction's HUD text and graffiti must be written in the exact clipped, declarative style that passes the canon voice test: short sentences, no adverbs, physical consequence over moral commentary.
- Every betrayal must cost the player something concrete (oxygen, ammo, time, reputation) before correction is possible.
- No faction ever frames itself as heroic or justified. The prose records sequence and physical consequence without assigning stature.
- The prison-colony origin is never "backstory." It is the active grammar of every transaction.

**Welcome to the Pit. Every faction still takes the count.**
