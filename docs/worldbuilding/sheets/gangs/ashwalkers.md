## Captain Corvus (the reclaimer)

**Voice:** *The monologuer who believes every word.* Corvus talks like a man delivering a final statement to a tribunal that doesn't exist. Long, rehearsed, full of justifications that have been polished across twenty years of reuse. Corvus is not lying — Corvus has simply told the story so many times that the story has become the truth, and the truth has become the story, and neither bears much weight against the salvage logs.

**The scheme:** the reclamation caper above. The Ashwalkers strip condemned sectors. Corvus keeps a private ledger of "what was reclaimed, from whom, and what they deserved." The ledger is the documentation of a man who needs to be the hero of his own story badly enough to write the documentation. The ledger is also, accidentally, accurate — which makes it useful to anyone who wants to know what Concord has condemned and when.

**The strut:** Corvus thinks the player is a rival reclaimer sent to poach a claim. The player is not. Corvus is wrong about what game they're both in (the Ritchie beat). The Ashwalkers' salvage maps intersect the operation's cartography by coincidence. Corvus will sell the player a chart, warn the player off a sector, or open fire depending on which version of the story Corvus is telling that day.

**Coercion hook:** the player who needs to reach a condemned sector (a Thread-B lead, a Shaft-7-style anomaly, the Ashfall approach) has to deal with Corvus, because Corvus claims it. The claim is not legal. The claim is enforced by six ships and a rehearsed speech.
