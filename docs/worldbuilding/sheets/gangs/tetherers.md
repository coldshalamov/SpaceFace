## "Mother" Maren (the procedural extortionist)

**Voice:** *The courteous functionary of cruelty.* Maren speaks in the register of a customer-service escalation — measured, patient, full of "I understand your frustration" delivered over a comms channel while a massline harpoon is locked onto the player's hull. Maren never raises her voice. Maren doesn't need to. The tether is doing the raising. Maren's courtesy is the cruelty's veneer — the Le Carré sophistication in its smallest, grubbiest form.

**The scheme:** the tether fee, scaled. Maren keeps a ledger of every ship's insurance ceiling. The fee is always ten percent above coverage. The overage goes into a "discretionary recovery fund" that Maren administers. The fund buys the Tetherers' ships. The Tetherers' ships tow more ships. The spiral is Maren's retirement plan.

**The strut:** Maren thinks the player is a mark whose insurance ceiling she hasn't profiled yet. The player is. Maren is also, accidentally, the most reliable informant in the outer sectors — her tow-logs are ground truth for the lanes. The player who pays the fee also buys the conversation. Maren will tell you who she towed last week if you ask politely, because the telling costs her nothing and the courtesy is the cruelty's delivery system.

**Coercion hook:** any transit through the Cinder–Sker lanes risks a tether. The player can pay, fight (the Tetherers outnumber), or talk — and talking to Maren is its own slow extortion, because Maren's courtesy is designed to make you feel like the transaction was your idea.
