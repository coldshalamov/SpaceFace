## The ship's history (the unreconciled weight)

The Tessera was given to Wren to settle a debt. The debt was owed by a cargo-hauler captain to the salvage crew that revived Wren after the prison-ship freezing. The ship was the settlement. The ship has a reactor that works and a hull that holds and a transponder ID that has been through enough owners that the registry entry is a palimpsest of names nobody remembers.

The ship's prior owners are not named in the canon. This is deliberate. The ship's history is the system's history — a layering of filings and routes and cargo manifests that the ship carried before Wren and will carry after. The ship does not belong to Wren. The ship belongs to the registry. Wren is the current operator. The system files the ship under the ship. The operator is whoever the system filed under the prior transponder ID, six weeks before Wren's first contract.

The cargo hold has carried, in order: titanium alloy (the 12.4t that was the recycler grid), industrial components (that became surplus redistribution), administrative records (sealed, three years), and — for the entire game, at the bottom of the manifest, never moving, never selling — `UNCLASSIFIED COMPOSITE — 3.1 KG [PERSONAL EFFECTS]`. The second fragment. The ship has carried it for five years. The ship will carry it into every endgame choice.

## The ship as character

The Tessera is the corpus's quietest character. The ship does not speak. The ship's HUD lines speak for it — the STABLE LOAD that won't clear, the CARGO: STABLE that ends the game, the OPERATOR: UNKNOWN that marks Phase 3. The ship's recycler breathes the air Wren breathes. The ship's transponder files the routes Wren flies. The ship's manifest carries the weight Wren carries. The ship is the body. The player is the ghost. The ghost haunts the body. The body keeps the score.

The superstition from `SPACER-SUPERSTITIONS.md`: *Never name a ship after a living person. If the ship is destroyed, the name survives the person or the person survives the ship. Either way, something is haunted.* The Tessera is named after no one. The Tessera is named after a tile in a mosaic — a small piece of a larger pattern, which is what a courier is in the system. The ship's name is the system's name for the ship's function. The ship has no name of its own. The ship has the function.
