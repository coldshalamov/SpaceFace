## The mirror salon

The Routers operate out of a converted cargo hold in the Veil Nebula. The Vael built their own recyclers; the Quiet, brokering ATMO TOKENS, can afford to run the same. The result: an outer-sector salon with the best air in the reaches, run by a defected Concord Intelligence officer, staffed by three analysts who speak in the same measured cadence as the Reading Room. The hum of the nebula instead of coffee. The same long table. The same bound inquiry reports. The mirror is exact, and that is the point.

## The dual function

The Routers serve two functions, and the gullible reading (that they serve only the first) is the cover for the second:

1. **Harm reduction.** The Routers broker ATMO TOKENS from surplus inner-sector stations to underwater outer-sector stations. Without the Routers, outer sectors fall into the Slow Gray faster. This is real. The Routers save sectors.
2. **Revenue and policy.** The brokerage is also the Quiet's primary revenue stream, and the brokerage gives the Routers a structural incentive to keep the system failing — because a sector that recovers stops buying tokens at markup. The Routers' policy layer decides which sectors breathe by deciding which tokens to broker. The harm-reduction argument is the cover for the policy. Both are true.

This is the Le Carré move: the adversary who is right about the diagnosis (Concord is too corrupt to survive) and wrong about the cure (the Quiet's brokerage profits from the disease it treats). Brandt has lived inside both truths for fourteen years and has stopped noticing the contradiction.

## The mole channel

The Routers are the receiving end of Aldiss's leaks. The dead-drop is the ATMO TOKEN brokerage — file-status updates encoded in token-transfer metadata, readable only by someone who knows the encoding. The Kurtz figure was the dead-drop's physical node for three years (before going off-grid upon realizing they were the conduit). The channel has been running for fourteen years. Marsh's mole hunt is the operation to close it.

The Routers do not know the second fragment in Wren's hold is a controlled leak. Brandt believes (or believed, until B7) that the fragment's release into civilian logistics was Aldiss's response to a new Concord policy — a genuine leak, not a bait. The operation depends on Brandt not realizing the leak is controlled until Aldiss's response has been traced. The reveal at B7 (the ledger page) is the moment the channel becomes visible — to the player, and, in the fiction, to whoever else reads the ledger after.
