## Quick facts (lifted from prose canon)

- **Age 31.** In this part of the galaxy 19 years. Stopped trying to find home 11 years ago; started again 6 months ago.
- **Ship:** *Tessera* — reactor "fine" for three owners, transponder ID a palimpsest, cargo manifest carries one permanent entry: `UNCLASSIFIED COMPOSITE — 3.1 KG` (the second fragment, unsellable, unjettisonable, treated as PERSONAL EFFECTS).
- **Origin:** agricultural world, one city, one university. Age 12 when a Vethari ship blotted the sun.
- **Captivity:** 9 weeks on the Vethari ship. Escaped through a maintenance shaft during a grief cycle. A Vethari broke its own neck so the shared field wouldn't transmit his position — the old ledger-precision applied one last time.
- **Sister Lida:** age 9 when he last saw her. Hidden in the grain-silo crawlspace ("designed for one adult technician; fit two children if neither of them moved"). Fate unresolved. The game refuses to answer.
- **After:** 12 years on stations, learned the language from dock workers, hacking from open diagnostic ports, cards from Vethari grief-cycle calibration. Ran with Torren's crew (the boss killed by a syndicate; "the man who shot him ordered a drink afterward and charged it to Torren's tab"). Paired with Callum. The vault job. The first fragment. The betrayal. Arrested, frozen 4 years, revived at 26 with partial left-hand function loss and full memory. Given the Tessera to settle a debt. Working the outer industrial belt 5 years. Six months ago: the second fragment, planted for him to find.
- **What he carries:** the memory of a Vethari killing itself so he could live. *"He doesn't call this hope. He calls it a data point. He collects data points."*
- **The artifact:** boring. 3kg. Dark gray with greenish tint. Non-human metallurgy, dried filament traces. No power. *The physical object is insignificant. The chain of custody is the intelligence.*

## Design note

Wren is the thread through the systemic story (Thread A). Thread A works on anyone. Wren is the one person in the system who would look at a piece of alien hull plating and see a way home instead of a filing problem. His personal story never merges with the systemic one. The two share a bureaucracy (REF 44-C), not a cause.
