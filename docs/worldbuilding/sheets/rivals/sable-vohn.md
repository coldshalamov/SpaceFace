## The rivalry

Sable and the player compete for the same contracts. The player who takes a contract Sable wanted finds she took the next two in retaliation. The player who beats her to a board finds she's already at the next one. She is not hostile — she is *competitive*, which is colder, because hostility at least admits the other party matters. Sable treats the player as an obstacle the way the system treats a courier: as a routing problem.

The rivalry has emotional weight because Sable's scheme rhymes with Wren's (family buyback vs. family search). The player who learns Sable's motive — late, through the comms popups she doesn't know are leaking her payment routing — recognizes the structural rhyme. Two people running the same arithmetic for different sisters, neither of whom will arrive, because the system is calibrated to keep them running.

## Coercion hook

The player who needs a specific contract (a Thread-B lead, a faction-chain mission) and finds Sable has it must out-bid her, out-race her, or trade. Sable trades in information — her tow-log contacts, her board-arbitrage sense. The trade always costs more than the contract. Sable designed it that way.
