## The wild card

Pek is the player's unreliable counterweight. Pek will sell the player information that is accurate, incomplete, and slightly oversold, in that order. Pek will accept a job from the player and complete it for someone else simultaneously, and both parties will be satisfied because Pek is good at the work. Pek is not a traitor — Pek is a *pluralist*, in the same way the system is a pluralist (it serves everyone; it is loyal to no one).

The comedy is real but bounded. Pek is funny the way the system is funny — the humor is in the recognition that the chaos is structural, not personal. Pek has survived the Pit, the Tetherers, the Choir, and three marriages by being the most interesting person in the engagement envelope. The "wild card" is not a quirk; it's a survival strategy that has worked so long it has become a personality.

## Coercion hook

The player who needs a thing Pek has (the tow-log fragment, the Choir certification) must catch Pek, and catching Pek is the work — Pek is always leaving. The player who hires Pek gets the work done and a new problem started. The player who shoots Pek inherits Pek's debts, marriages, and the Choir misunderstanding. Every option costs.
