Golden pin `0b33dde1` untouched by this audit. Mechanism probes below were run against `createAssetResidencyRegistry` headlessly with an injected `now` clock (`options.now`) — stale/fresh claims are simulated deterministically, not by wall-clock waits.

`saturated: false` — two material mechanism-verified defects remain in the W134 claim-stamp machinery: the gate is applied on only two of the four exit-warm mint sites (the ungated rotate seam re-covers the lapsed cohort on every normal transition), and claim staleness conflates "arm died" with "arm never re-posts" (live consumers stamp once at admission, so the gate drops still-mounted entries after ~120 s of dwell). ## Findings

### 1. Seam-asymmetric claim gate — the ungated rotate mints the exact cohort P2 just excluded — `MECHANISM_VERIFIED` — impact M–H, effort S, risk low

Evidence: `hasLiveSectorClaim` (`src/render/assetResidency.js:1898-1905`) is required by `prepareSectorExit` (`:1922`) and `prepareSectorExitDeferred` (`:1967-1968`), but `rotateSector`'s warm-mint sweep (`:1875-1880`) and `rotateSectorDeferred`'s (deferred twin in the same file, same filter shape) qualify candidates on `row.nonPlayer > 0` alone — no claim freshness check anywhere on the rotate path.

Mechanism: on a normal sector transition the ordering is `sector:exit` → gated `prepareSectorExitDeferred` mint (drops stale-claimed rows) → `sector:enter` → `rotateSectorDeferred` warm mint (`queueResidencyWarmMint`, `renderer.js:19641+`). The rotate mint releases the gated hold (`assetResidency.js:1955-1962` equivalent release on the rotate side) and re-sweeps the *same* `sectorOwnerIndex` rows — ungated — so every entry whose claim lapsed rides the hold anyway. Net effect: **on the dominant transition path the P2 exclusion is a no-op** — the full ungated cohort (pre-W134 behavior, including dead session-owner unions) is what the warm hold carries; the gate only determines which rows are held during the travel gap between the two mints.

Probe (injected clock, 10 live-boundary claims on sector X aged 130 s): `prepareSectorExit('X')` retained **0/10**; `rotateSector` on the identical registry emitted **10** `asset-retained` warm-previous-sector events.

Fix sketch: hoist the qualifying predicate into one shared `exitWarmQualifies(entry, row, sid, includePlayer, nowMs)` consumed by all four mint sites (both prepares + both rotates). Effort S. The rotate path keeps its own release/retarget semantics — only the filter is shared. Pair with Finding 2's stamp-semantics fix so the gate doesn't spread the reverse defect into the rotate seams.

### 2. Claim staleness conflates "arm died" with "arm doesn't re-post" — mounted consumers stamp once, never refresh — `MECHANISM_VERIFIED` — impact M–H, effort M, risk medium

Evidence: stamps refresh only on a re-claim path — `unionOwnerSector` `has()` early-out (`:845-847`) and new-member add (`:857`), `reclaimWarm` request-scan union (`:513-517`), retain-mint seed (`:416-431`). But the consumers that matter most retain exactly once: `retainLibraryPlan` (`src/render/partsLibrary.js:10558-10585`) retains each plan file with `sectorId` only inside the admission loop (`:10303/:10311` — a mounted boundary never re-retains); the decode-cache settle retain (`assetLoader.js:859-870`) is one-shot; the per-poll warm re-posts in `renderer.js:5412-5464` cover only runway/pending picks under `decodeCap`, not settled mounts. `SECTOR_CLAIM_STALE_MS = 120000` (`:798`) vs a settled-sector dwell of any real length.

Mechanism: after ~2 minutes parked in a sector (normal gameplay dwell — parked while docked, trading, idling in a menu), *every mounted entry's* sector claim is stale even though its owner row — the live demand — is still registered and unreleased. Two consequences:

- **Exit-warm under-coverage**: `prepareSectorExit(…, {includePlayer:true})` on `save:restoring` (`renderer.js:18802-18815`) is the one gated mint with no following rotate to heal it. Probe: cohort of 10 live-boundary claims aged 130 s → retained **5/15** in a mixed cohort (only the claims minted <120 s ago survived), and **0** for the stale half — the still-mounted stations/gates/dressing, i.e. the heaviest re-decode set, get no teardown-gap protection. Same hole on non-rotate exits (menu/game-over/new-game reset paths that publish `sector:exit` without a following `sector:enter` on the departed sector). If the cache-cleanup sweep (`releaseUnreferencedCacheOwners*`, `:1628-1707`) or byte pressure evicts during the gap, the restore/return frame re-decodes — a late pop-in burst.
- **Corridor-rank demotion**: `entryCorridorRank` skips lapsed claims (`:997-1009`), so a soft-only entry whose corridor-sector claim lapsed sorts with rank −1 (`sortSoftEvictionCandidates`, `:1011-1025`) — evict-first even while that sector is still the corridor's live leg. Under byte pressure the soft budget picks genuinely-current-sector files for eviction ahead of foreign ones — re-decode pop on the next approach.

Fix sketch: the claim should not lapse while the row's *purpose* is live demand. Options: (a) `sectorClaimFresh` treats rows whose role is not warm-purpose (inverse of `WARM_PURPOSE_RESIDENCY_ROLE = /warm|runway|prewarm|armory|predicted/i`, `assetLoader.js:120`) as live regardless of stamp — a live owner's row presence *is* the claim; keep stamps enforcing session-owner/soft rows, which is the cohort P2 actually targeted; (b) exempt `sid === currentSectorId` in `hasLiveSectorClaim` (the departing sector's live claims are definitionally armed until teardown); (c) refresh stamps at read for sids still in the corridor (self-healing, cheaper than (a) but retains the other holes). Recommend (a) — it is the semantic fix — with (c) as belt. Effort M, mostly the audit pass over role strings.

Risk: re-widens the exit-warm cohort toward pre-P2 over-inclusion; mitigated because stamps still expire for soft/session-owner rows (the dead-arm case) — those keep pruning correctly.

### 3. Prune only fires on new-member adds — same-sid re-posts keep lapsed members in the union + index — `MECHANISM_VERIFIED` — impact L, effort S, risk low

Evidence: `unionOwnerSector` `has()` early-out (`:845-847`) refreshes the re-posted sid and returns *before* the lapsed-member prune (`:849-855`) — a row that re-posts its existing membership (the dominant live path, every ~0.25 s for armed rows) never sweeps its stale remainder. `sectorOwnerIndex` retains dead `sid → entry` rows; exit sweeps iterate and freshness-filter them (correctness holds — `hasLiveSectorClaim` still drops them — but the index bloats and the sweep does wasted work on exactly the hot seam). Probe: after a new-member add on a 2-member lapsed union, `diagnostics()` shows `sectors: ["Y"]` only and `verifyLedgerConsistency` is clean — the prune path itself works when reached; the issue is reachability.

Fix sketch: run the prune inside the `has()` early-out too (e.g. `if (nowMs - metadata.lastSectorClaimPruneAt > SECTOR_CLAIM_PRUNE_INTERVAL_MS)` — amortized, one extra stamp on the metadata), or sweep members during the same stamp refresh since the scan is already O(members).

### 4. Scalar `metadata.sectorId` survives prune — stale emit reads + a born-live resurrection path — `MECHANISM_VERIFIED` — impact L, effort S, risk low

Evidence: the scalar is set-if-null at `:858` and never re-pointed or cleared when `unionOwnerSector` deletes members (`:853` prunes the Set + `sectorClaimAts` + index row only). Two bounded consequences: (a) emit/diagnostic reads of the scalar (`:460` retain emit `ownerMetadata.sectorId`, diagnostics rows) report a sector the union dropped — cosmetic staleness; (b) resurrection: a request-metadata union seeded only via the scalar (`request.metadata.sectorIds` not yet a `Set`, e.g. an arm that never re-posted) — at commit, `retain`'s seed branch `:422-425` mints the scalar's sector into the owner's `sectorIds` *without a stamp* → `sectorClaimFresh` missing-stamp rule treats it as live (`:810-817`) → a sector whose claim died >120 s ago comes back qualified on the gated sweeps. Window is narrow (scalar must name a pruned member at commit time) but the mechanism is real.

Fix sketch: when the prune deletes the member the scalar names, re-point the scalar to any surviving member (or delete it); alternatively stamp scalar-seeded members at union time so seeding can't mint born-live dead claims.

### 5. Born-lapsed members on request commit — `priorAts` propagates stale stamps verbatim — `MECHANISM_VERIFIED` mechanism / `UNVERIFIED` impact — effort S

Evidence: `retain`'s mint seed copies `priorAts` as-is (`:422-425` `new Map(priorAts)` + stamp-only-if-missing). A request pending >120 s (deep ambient queue under decodeCap, or an armed request that sat while the player idled) commits with stamps older than the window → owner-row members born lapsed → skipped by `entryCorridorRank` and the gated sweeps until a future re-post. For arms that stay alive this self-heals at the next re-post (~0.25 s); for arms that committed and died, born-lapsed is semantically *correct*. Residual exposure is narrow — the committed-and-still-live-dweller case is the only miss, and Finding 2's fix (live-role rows don't need stamps) subsumes it.

Fix sketch: at commit, clamp propagated member stamps to `max(at, mintMs - SECTOR_CLAIM_STALE_MS)` or stamp missing members unconditionally — S, and cheap.

### 6. Per-poll write spend — non-finding (measured)

`reclaimWarm` re-post on the `has()` early-out path: **0.17 µs/call** (one `Map.has`, one `Map.set`, stamp refresh); `unionOwnerSector` new-member add: **~0.1 µs/call** (Set add + index add + stamp). At the measured re-post cadence (~240 calls/poll for an armed sector's runway cohort) this is ~40 µs/poll worst case — well under the 3.22 µs/file reclaim baseline and invisible next to the decode work it protects. The prune scans are O(members-of-row), bounded by union breadth (small). No finding.

`test/` contains zero references to `sectorClaim`, `hasLiveSectorClaim`, `unionOwnerSector`, or the `prepareSectorExit*`/`rotateSector*` gate split — the seam asymmetry in Finding 1 and the lapse semantics in Finding 2 have no regression coverage. The registry is headlessly importable with an injectable clock (this audit's probes ran that way), so a test is cheap to add once the fixes land.
